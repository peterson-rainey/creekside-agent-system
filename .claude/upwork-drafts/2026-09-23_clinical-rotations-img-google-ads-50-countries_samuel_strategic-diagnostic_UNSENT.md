Across 50 countries, the country sending the cheapest form submissions is often the one where the fewest students can pay for a US rotation. Google can't see that. If the form fill is the only conversion it gets, Smart Bidding buys more of the cheap ones, and the blended cost per lead can look fine while several countries lose money.

We've seen a blended number hide a losing country on a Google account for an ecommerce store selling in the US and UK. In July, non-brand ROAS across both countries was 1.50x, which described neither of them. The UK was at 0.42x on about a third of the spend, and the US was at 2.00x.

So the real work here is getting the rotation purchase back into Google Ads with its value, then judging each country on it. We haven't run medical education. What carries over is reading an account country by country and measuring the sale, not the lead.

1. I've been in paid ads for six years.
2. The largest Google budget on our book this past year was a reverse mortgage lender at $65,682 in its biggest month. I led that account, with a specialist on our team running it day to day.
3. Five of our Google accounts had months in that range this past year, and two stayed in it for most of the year.
4. Multi-country is the thinnest part of our record. The widest was a 49-country Shopping test running alongside an ecommerce client's US campaigns, so 50 at once. It returned 0.65x in about a week and came off.
5. The US and UK store. Each country had its own Search and Shopping campaigns and budget, and the US carried about two thirds of the spend. That separation is why the split above was visible at all. For you I'd start with a few country tiers by cost per paying student rather than 50 campaigns too thin for bidding to learn, and split a tier out once its numbers justify it.
6. A bankruptcy firm on Google, case study attached. We split the account by case type, gave Orange County its own campaign to test that market alone, and moved budget to the markets and keywords that converted. By the firm's own weekly read, call quality held steady while leads went from 117 to 229 on about $1,400 more spend and cost per lead fell 42%, from $86.09 to $50.29.
7. Which conversions are primary. Whether sign-ups and purchases carry the click ID back. Spend and cost per paying student by country. Search terms checked against the leads who actually bought. Whether branded search is flattering the blended numbers.
8. By cost per paying student and revenue per country, not cost per lead. Two caveats. Google won't import a click-based offline conversion more than 90 days after the click, so a student who buys later gets judged in your own records. And small countries need grouping until they have enough purchases to read.
