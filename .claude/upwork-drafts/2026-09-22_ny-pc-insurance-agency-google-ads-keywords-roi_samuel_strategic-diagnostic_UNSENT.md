## PROPOSAL

For a P&C agency, the keyword list usually matters less than the searches it's actually paying for. Broad terms like "car insurance" or "home insurance NY" also match people looking for their current carrier's claims line, a policy login, or an agent job, and each of those clicks costs the same as a real quote request.

So the first pass is the search terms report, not the keyword list. Anything that spent without producing a quote either becomes a negative or moves into its own ad group with a budget set on purpose. That usually frees up money before a single new keyword goes in.

ROI is the harder part, because Google only knows what you tell it a lead is worth. If every quote form counts the same, bidding treats a one-car auto quote the same as a commercial package or a landlord policy on a six-unit building, and it will happily buy the cheapest leads. Splitting campaigns by line of business, with each line valued closer to what it actually pays the agency, shows which lines earn their spend and points bidding at the valuable ones.

New York adds a wrinkle. Clicks in the city and upstate can cost very different amounts, so one statewide budget lets the expensive areas eat most of it. Worth seeing how the account splits by location before touching bids.

The closest account we've run to yours is a bankruptcy law firm, another regulated, consumer-facing lead market. Restructuring its campaigns by market took leads from 117 to 229 and cost per lead from $86 to $50. That case study is attached.
