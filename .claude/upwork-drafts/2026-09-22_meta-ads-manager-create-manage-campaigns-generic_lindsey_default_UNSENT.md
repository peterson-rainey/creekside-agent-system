## PROPOSAL

Have you run Meta ads for this business before, and do you still have access to that ad account? I ask because the old data usually answers half of the first month's questions: which parts of the spend turned into sales, and which ones only looked cheap.

The clearest example I have is a survival-gear store I run on Meta. For about six weeks this summer it ran in the US and the UK side by side. The UK was the cheaper market to reach, about $27 per thousand impressions versus $41 in the US. It also brought back $0.59 in sales for every dollar spent, while the US brought back $3.79. Judged on reach costs alone, the UK looked like the better place for the budget.

I learned that lesson first with my own money. When I was building the e-commerce business I later sold, the only number that mattered was what landed in the bank account, not what Ads Manager called a result. That's still how I read every account.

After 10+ years across dental, skincare, meal prep and e-commerce accounts, the ones that keep improving change one thing at a time. A new hook, a new offer or a new market gets tested on its own, and the rest is left alone long enough to read it, because big edits send Meta back into learning. On a smaller budget that matters even more, since five tests running at once rarely give any of them enough data to call.
