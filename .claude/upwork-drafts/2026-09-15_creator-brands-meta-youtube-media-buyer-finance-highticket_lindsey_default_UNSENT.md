I don't have a restriction and recovery story to walk you through, so prevention is where I put the work: every rejection logged with the claim that triggered it, income and return claims checked before an editor ships them, and more than one admin on every ad account and page, since a restricted profile can take down accounts where it's the only user. Budget changes don't send an ad back to review, but edits to creative, targeting, the optimization event or the billing event do, so I scale winners through budget and launch new angles as new ads.

On cost per booked call, I don't have a high-ticket number to give you. The closest is an implant group whose Meta leads went to a call center: $64,747 over about three months for 2,558 leads at $25.31, which is cost per lead, not per booked call.

I pulled spend back to what the team could work and added qualifying questions to the forms, knowing cost per lead would go up. Since then I ask what the booking side can handle before I raise a budget.

When leads are cheap and nobody books, I first check whether the leads are real and whether anyone reaches them while they still care: duplicate or junk submissions, how fast the first call, text or email goes out, and whether booking sits right on the thank-you page. Then bookings by ad, because cheap leads that don't book often trace back to a hook promising something the call doesn't deliver. Then the optimization event.
