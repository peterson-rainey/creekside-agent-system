HIGH-TICKET ACCOUNT

On a new high-ticket store, an inactive purchase conversion is more often a duplicate than a broken tag. Shopify's Google channel creates its own purchase action, an older GTM or GA4 import keeps sitting in the account, and whichever one isn't primary looks dead. So the audit starts by matching every purchase action to the orders it should be counting.

1. Personally audit and manage?
Not me alone, and you should know that up front. The audit and daily management sit with one Google Ads specialist on our team, named to you before any access is granted. I set strategy and sign off on the testing plan and major changes. Those two are the only people with access, as users on accounts you own. Nothing gets built in an account of ours.

2. Active Google Ads accounts
15 spent under our team in the last 30 days, about $74,000 combined. That is team-wide, not a personal count.

3. Closest high-ticket account
A US luxury mattress brand on Shopify Plus, still running. About $6,000 a month on Google, list prices $1,995 to $3,495, roughly $2,260 per tracked order.
Starting problem: Shopping showed prices about $599 above the site, because Shopify automatic discounts don't reach Merchant Center as a sale price. Cold non-brand keywords were buying clicks at around $19 with a Quality Score of 1. Checkout micro-events were inflating conversion value.
Changes: sale price overrides on every variant, size-first titles, the $19 keywords paused, micro-events moved to secondary so one purchase action is primary.
Results, last 90 days: branded search drove 4 purchases and $9,045 in revenue on $1,995. Non-brand Search and Shopping drove zero and are paused. It was rebuilt in mid-July, so it's young, but you should hear the weak half from me.
For a non-brand number: a Shopify electronics brand at about $560 per order ran non-brand Search at 4.39x on $7,992, June 12 to August 31.

4. Broken or just quiet?
Two checks. Does the tag fire: run a test checkout through Tag Assistant and read the Google tag diagnostics. Should a paid click have bought: pull Shopify orders whose landing URL carries a gclid for the same dates. Orders there with nothing in Ads is breakage. No orders there is volume: at a 1% conversion rate, 60 paid clicks a week predicts less than one sale. If the action is a GA4 import, check the link and the key event setting too.

5. Products on a limited budget
Rank by gross margin in dollars, not price. Cut anything with feed errors, weak price competitiveness in Merchant Center, or thin stock. Then do the math: at your CPC and a 0.5 to 1% conversion rate, how many sales does the budget buy a month? If the answer is three, advertise a handful of products, not forty, so one of them reaches a readable number. Margin-band custom labels keep that grouping in the feed.

6 and 7. Search, Shopping or PMax
Likely Standard Shopping on the shortlist plus tight Search on high-intent product terms, and no PMax at first. PMax learns on purchase volume a new store doesn't have, and it blends brand and remarketing into its return, which hides whether cold traffic works. What would change that: 30+ clean purchases a month with verified value, strong price competitiveness, and search terms showing demand at your price. With those, PMax earns a controlled test against Shopping.
