Before anything else: we don't have a Shopify store we took from zero sales to steady sales on Meta that I can show you, and we've never run ads for art or prints. Better you hear that from me now than find it at the examples.

What we do have is a high-ticket furniture brand we ran on Meta, and what it showed is probably the most useful thing I can tell you about your last campaign.

Most people don't buy something that costs a few hundred dollars on their first visit. On that account, retargeting people who had already been to the site was about a sixth of the spend and brought in nearly a third of the sales Meta reported. Retargeting always looks a bit better than it is, since some of those people would have bought anyway. But it shows where the buyers were: people who had already looked, then saw the brand again.

That changes how I'd read a campaign that sold nothing. Three things I'd check in what you ran:

1. What it was optimized for. Meta needs roughly 50 of that result a week to get out of learning. On a small budget at your price point, a campaign optimized for purchases rarely gets close, so it can spend for weeks and never learn who buys.
2. Whether anyone saw a second ad. If all the money went to people who had never heard of you, the ones who were interested never saw you again.
3. Shopify orders against what Meta reported. Meta's click window tops out at 7 days, so someone who clicked, thought about it for two weeks, then bought wouldn't show up as a Meta sale. It's possible the campaign sold something and didn't get credit. It's also possible it really sold nothing. Either way, you want to know which.

On creative, that same account split its ads by product. One product returned 2.36x. Another returned 1.36x. A third brought in $444 on $1,111 and was cut after 11 days. A campaign with no product focus spent $5,264 and brought in $424. Same brand, same months. With prints I'd expect the same thing: a few images will sell and most won't. The faster you find out which, the less the test costs. So the first tests would be single prints and small collections shown framed on a wall, not a scroll through the catalog.

In fairness to that account, its best month was driven by a 50%-off sitewide sale and by retargeting, not by ads to new people. It's also no longer a client.

Before any money goes in, our dedicated conversion tracking specialist sets up purchase tracking on Shopify with order values passed through and confirms it's recording. On the Google Shopping side, one thing to check early: Shopify's automatic discounts don't reach Google as a sale price, so Shopping ads can show a higher price than your site does.
