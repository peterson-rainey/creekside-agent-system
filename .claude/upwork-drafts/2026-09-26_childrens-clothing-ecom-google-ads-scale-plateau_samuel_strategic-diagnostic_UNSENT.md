## PROPOSAL

When a store's Google Ads stall after years of growth, the account-level ROAS usually hides the reason. Branded searches, people already typing your name, often return 20x or more, and they get averaged in with the campaigns meant to find new customers. Performance Max also serves on your brand terms unless brand exclusions are set. So the blended number can look healthy while the non-branded campaigns are held to a target only brand traffic can reach, and that caps how far they can scale.

**What I'd check first**

Brand vs non-brand. Brand exclusions on Performance Max, brand Search in its own campaign, and a separate ROAS for each. That gives the real starting point for scaling decisions.

Tracking before any big moves. If the store is on Shopify, the Google & YouTube app sets add to cart and checkout started as conversions by default, next to purchase, so bidding can treat a cart like an order. A GA4 purchase import running alongside the Google Ads tag can also count the same order twice. Our tracking specialist would confirm each order reaches Google Ads once with its value, and that GA4 revenue lines up with the store's own numbers.

Where the room is. For each product and campaign, we separate impression share lost to budget from share lost to rank. Products losing share to budget while holding a healthy non-brand ROAS are where spend goes first. Products losing to rank need feed, price or bid work before more money.

The feed. US Shopping ads require age group, gender, color and size on kids' clothing, and "toddler" (1 to 5) and "kids" (5 to 13) are separate age groups. Titles also need the terms parents actually search, like "toddler" or "3T".

**How scaling works from there**

A high target ROAS keeps Google out of auctions it would win at a slightly lower return, so we lower it in steps rather than all at once, since big setting changes restart learning. Budget goes to the products with proven room, one test at a time, judged on non-branded return so brand sales don't flatter the results. Every two weeks you get a short written read: what changed, what we learned, what we'd do next, plus a live dashboard.

**Examples**

A store selling laptop screen extenders, which we ran on Google for about ten months at roughly $8,000 a month. Non-branded Shopping returned 5.5x and non-branded Search 5.3x over that stretch, while branded Search ran at 28.7x. That gap is why brand gets split out before anything else gets read.

A survival gear store we built on Google this summer, around $8,500 a month, with Shopping at 2.4x since July.

Both run below where a store your size likely spends, and we haven't run kids' clothing on Google yet.
