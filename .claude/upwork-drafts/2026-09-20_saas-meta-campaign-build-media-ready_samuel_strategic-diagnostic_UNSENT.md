Meta gets very good at finding whatever event you tell it to find, and for a SaaS campaign that choice decides more than the creative does. Optimize for signups and it finds people who sign up, including the ones who never activate or pay.

The catch is volume. An ad set needs roughly 50 results a week to get out of the learning phase, and a new campaign rarely produces 50 paid conversions a week on day one. So launch usually optimizes for trial starts or demo requests, while the events further down (activated, paid, upgraded) get sent back to Meta through the Conversions API from the start. Once enough of those build up, optimization moves down the funnel to the event that actually makes money. Skipping that step is how an account ends up with a cheap cost per signup and flat revenue.

On the creative: every ad added to a live ad set restarts learning, so we'd load the strongest set at launch and test in planned rounds instead of dripping new ads in each week. Meta's built-in creative test takes two to seven ads at a time. Winners get picked on cost per activated or paid user where the data allows, not on CTR.

Structure starts lean. One prospecting campaign with broad targeting, letting the creative do the qualifying, plus a retargeting campaign for site visitors and stalled trial users. Retargeting is reported separately so it doesn't make new-customer cost look better than it is. Tracking setup goes to a dedicated tracking specialist on our team.

The closest example is a B2B SaaS company selling referral software to home service contractors. Inbound leads and ARR both doubled in the six months after their seed round. Across the team that's $20M+ in managed spend and 200+ account audits.
