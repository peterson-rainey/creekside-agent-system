## PROPOSAL

On an online store, the account-level ROAS in Ads Manager is often the number hiding the problem, because it averages the campaigns making sales with the ones quietly losing money.

One store our team runs on Meta, a survival-gear brand, ran the UK alongside the US this summer. Over those weeks the whole account read 2.32x. Split by country, the UK spent $12,454 for 106 purchases, 0.59x. The US spent $14,731 for 719, 3.79x. The UK was also the cheaper audience to reach, about $27 per thousand impressions against $41, and still the far worse place to sell. The blended number described neither country.

So the useful cut starts with splitting results the way the money actually moves: by country, by product, by prospecting versus retargeting, and by new customers versus past buyers. Retargeting and repeat buyers almost always post the best-looking ROAS, and plenty of those people would have ordered anyway, so budget gets judged on what brings in new customers.

Before scaling anything, we'd confirm the Pixel and the Conversions API both send purchases with order value, and that Meta's purchase count roughly matches your store's orders. If those two disagree, every optimization decision after that is built on the wrong number.

Structure stays lean so each ad set gets close to the roughly 50 purchases a week Meta wants before it exits learning. New creative gets tested two to seven ads at a time, and changes get batched, since adding ads or switching bid strategy restarts learning. Our Meta specialist runs the account day to day, with me on strategy, and you get reports every two weeks plus a live dashboard you can check anytime.

Setup is $1,500 one-time, which covers that tracking check and the account build. Management is 20% of ad spend with a $1,500 monthly minimum, on a 90-day minimum, since the first month mostly proves the tracking before there's enough data to move budget with confidence.
