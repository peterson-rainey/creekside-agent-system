# Brickell Digital / Ava / True Fitness -- Meta Ads, 3 brands
Profile: Samuel Rainey | Style: strategic-diagnostic | Status: UNSENT | Drafted: 2026-09-04

---

Three brands, three different Meta problems. The risk in this hire is someone treating it as one channel job with three budgets.

B2B AI SaaS is the one that quietly burns money. The optimization event is a demo request, and at enterprise/F500 deal shapes the volume is nowhere near what Meta's delivery system needs to learn. We run a B2B SaaS account in our own book right now that has spent roughly $18K over the last twelve months and produced three recorded conversions. Traffic is not the problem there. 1.4% CTR, $2.28 CPC, $31.88 CPM, all healthy. The problem is that the account is optimizing toward an event that fires three times a year. That account should never have been asked to buy demos. It should have been buying a mid-funnel event with real volume and letting sales close from a warm list.

B2C AI SaaS is the opposite problem. Volume is there, so Meta will happily optimize toward the cheapest possible signal, installs, signups, trial starts, and quietly fill the top of the funnel with people who never activate. The fix is a purchase-or-activation event upstream in the pixel and a shallow event used only as a learning-phase crutch, then removed. I have run this exact ladder on a consumer app: 2,662 installs with cost per install cut from $7.36 to $3.90 through campaign restructuring and creative rotation, not bid tinkering.

Straight answer on the agency: our proof selling to consumers and to small business owners is deep, and our proof selling paid media services to other agencies is thin. If that piece is make-or-break, someone with that specific book is a better fit. If it sits behind the two SaaS brands, I would run it off your own client list as named accounts and lookalikes rather than interest targeting, and I would expect it to be the slowest of the three to prove out.

What "full ownership" means on my side: separate ad accounts and pixels per brand so signal never cross-contaminates, one shared naming and UTM convention so the three roll into a single view, weekly written reporting against a primary KPI each brand actually agrees on, and I own the account structure decisions. I do not need to own creative. You have a team for that. Where I add value is telling that team which angle to make next and why, based on what the account is actually rewarding, not on a creative brief written in a vacuum.

One timing note worth flagging since you are hiring in September. Across our Meta book, Q4 CPMs run roughly 2.3x their off-season level at flat spend. That is not a reason to hold budget. It is a reason to have the account structure, events and creative rotation settled in October rather than debugging them in November when every impression costs more.

Two things I would want before quoting properly:

What is monthly Meta spend across the three brands today, roughly. Are we talking closer to $10K/month combined or closer to $50K? And is that split evenly or is one brand carrying most of it?

For each brand, what is the single event you would fire me over? Demo requests, activated users, closed revenue? I ask because the answer usually differs from what is currently set as the conversion event in the account, and that gap is the first thing I would fix.

Background: six years running paid media, US markets throughout. I sit inside a team that runs Meta and Google across a full client book, so you get me as day-to-day owner of the channel with an analyst bench behind the account. Happy to walk the three accounts with you and give you a real read on structure before you commit to anyone.
