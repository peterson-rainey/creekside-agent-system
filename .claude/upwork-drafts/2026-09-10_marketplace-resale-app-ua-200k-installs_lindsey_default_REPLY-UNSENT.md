200,000 in 90 days is about 2,220 installs a day from day one. The only install economics I can speak to from our own book put cost per install at $3.90 after restructuring, down from $7.36. Price 200,000 entirely through paid auction at the better of those two numbers and you are at roughly $780,000 of media in the window, about $260,000 a month. That is approximately what our entire client book spends across all platforms. It is not an argument that the target is wrong. It is an argument that paid auction cannot be the instrument that delivers it, and if the plan assumes it will, the budget conversation is going to arrive in week three instead of week one.

The first two weeks make it harder, not easier. A new app campaign spends through a learning period where cost runs high and volume runs low before either settles, and the standard reflex is to raise bids to force the pace. That buys installs from the cheapest available attention, which is the cohort that opens once and leaves. You would hit a download number and fail the retention number in the same quarter.

Your retail chain is the part of this that isn't auction priced. A shopper already standing at the counter buying a pre-owned watch is the exact user the app needs, the acquisition cost is production and staff incentive rather than media, and the inventory side of the marketplace comes from the same footprint. I can't tell you what volume that channel carries until I see your store count, foot traffic, and transaction counts, and I'd rather build the plan off those numbers than guess at them. What I can tell you is that it has to carry a serious share, with paid layered on to extend reach and to build lookalike audiences off the users retail produces.
