## 3. Max allowable CPL and CAC at a $249 to $299 price, and the scaling gates

Cannot give you a real number without your gross margin, and I would rather bracket it than invent one. Here is the arithmetic I would run with you on the consultation.

Start with contribution margin per unit, not price. Take your selling price, subtract landed COGS, fulfillment and shipping, payment processing, and a returns reserve. Consumer hardware at this price usually lands somewhere between 30% and 45% gross. At a $274 midpoint that is roughly $96 per unit at 35%, roughly $123 at 45%.

You cannot spend the whole contribution margin on acquisition or you run a business that grows and makes nothing. For a single-SKU hardware product with no repeat purchase, I would cap acquisition at 50% to 60% of contribution. That puts maximum allowable CAC around $48 to $58 at 35% margin, and around $62 to $74 at 45%.

Now the part that decides your whole media plan. Maximum allowable CPL is max CAC multiplied by the rate at which a waitlist signup actually becomes a buyer. If 10% of your list converts at launch, your allowable CPL is roughly $5 to $7. At 25% it is roughly $12 to $18. Single digits to low teens. That is the entire reason I pushed back on optimizing to free email signups in the proposal: at your price point there is no room for a $30 lead that converts at 8%.

Two things move those ceilings upward and both are in your favor. Modular means attachments and add-on modules, so if a station buyer predictably buys one or two modules later, you are underwriting acquisition with contribution from the whole basket rather than one unit, and allowable CAC can move meaningfully. And a refundable deposit is not just a better optimization event, it repriced the funnel, because a deposit taker converts at a far higher rate than an email and that raises allowable CPL directly.

Gates I would want cleared before scaling spend, all of them on the same product and offer:

Tracking integrity first. Pixel firing once, no duplicate events, deposit or intent event matching platform-reported numbers within about 10% of what your own backend shows. Nothing else means anything until this is true.

Then, at minimum: one audience-and-angle combination delivering leads at or under your calculated max allowable CPL for two consecutive weeks, not one good week. Landing page converting at a stable rate you have measured rather than assumed. At least 50 to 100 conversion events on the winning setup, because below that you are reading noise. A measured waitlist-to-deposit or deposit-to-purchase rate from real people, not an assumption. And frequency and CPM stable rather than climbing while cost per result holds.

Fail any of those and the answer is fix the input, not add budget. I will tell you to stop. That is the job.

## 4. Next 60 to 90 days with Prototype 2 still being finalized

Test demand and language. Do not test production polish.

What I would test. Which buyer you actually have, and this is the big one, because a modular handheld station reads very differently to a commuter who wants portability, a collector who wants the dock and the modules, a PC-handheld owner solving a specific docking problem, and a parent buying a gift. Those are four different products described four different ways at the same price. Then the angle underneath each, whether the hook is portability, modularity and future-proofing, price against the obvious alternative, or a specific frustration with what they own now. Then price and offer framing at $249 versus $299, including whether a refundable deposit outperforms a plain waitlist, which is a real question and worth knowing before launch. Then the landing page: one clear headline test, and whether a configurator or module picker lifts intent or just adds friction. And I would want the waitlist segmented by declared intent from day one, so the list has structure instead of being one undifferentiated blob at launch.

What I would specifically not spend heavily on yet. High-production creative or a hero launch film, because Prototype 2 is not final and you will be re-shooting anything that shows the hardware. Rough prototype footage, static renders and screen-recorded UI outperform polish at this stage anyway and cost nothing. No broad awareness or reach buying, which is where prelaunch budgets usually die, and our own accounts back this up: across two consumer-product accounts, everything we ran on awareness, engagement and traffic objectives spent about $2,850 and recorded zero conversions. No catalog or dynamic product ads, because there is no catalog and no purchase history to feed them. No server-side CAPI build before there is volume worth deduplicating. No paid influencer or whitelisting spend yet, and I will note we do not have partnership-ad results to point to. No expansion beyond one country. And no scaling spend on a winning CPL until the waitlist-to-buyer rate is measured, because a cheap lead against an unknown conversion rate is not a validated funnel.

## 5. Structuring a $2,000 first 30 days

One boundary before the plan, so there are no surprises later. $2,000 works as a validation tranche and I am happy to structure it. It is below our threshold for an ongoing management engagement, which starts at $5,000 a month in ad spend. So treat this as the test that tells us whether there is something here, priced and scoped as a test, with management as a separate conversation once volume justifies it.

Now the honest framing of what $2,000 buys. At the CPMs we actually see on new consumer-product accounts with no pixel history, call it $20 to $35, $2,000 gets you somewhere in the range of 60,000 to 100,000 impressions and roughly 900 to 1,400 clicks. At a 10% to 20% page conversion rate that is maybe 90 to 280 waitlist signups. That is enough to rank messaging angles and read landing page performance. It is not enough to validate CAC, and anyone telling you otherwise is selling you something.

Week one, zero dollars on media. Tracking and the page. Pixel installed with standard events, deposit or intent event defined and firing, UTM convention set so every click is attributable by angle, GA4 connected, and the landing page live with one headline variant against a control. Getting this wrong costs you the entire budget in unreadable data, so it happens before spend, not alongside it.

Media split across the remaining weeks. Roughly $1,300 to cold prospecting in a single campaign, and I would run four to five ad sets split by messaging angle rather than by audience. At this budget audience is not the variable worth testing, because slicing $1,300 across interest stacks starves every one of them below the point of learning. Broad targeting with the angle as the variable gets you a readable answer. Roughly $350 to retargeting, switched on in weeks three and four once there is enough traffic to build from, and I expect this to look far better than cold, the way it did on the camera account, so I will be careful not to let it flatter the overall number. Roughly $350 held in reserve, unallocated on purpose, to push behind whichever angle actually moves rather than spreading it evenly on day one.

What you get at day 30. A ranked list of angles by cost per signup and by page conversion, a read on which buyer segment responds, landing page conversion measured rather than guessed, a clean tracking setup you keep regardless of what happens next, and a written recommendation that is either a specific scaling path with numbers attached, or a clear statement that the data does not support more spend yet and here is what to fix first. That second outcome is a real possible answer and you should want a plan that can produce it.
