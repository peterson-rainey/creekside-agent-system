## PROPOSAL

For a premium pet brand on Shopify, the ROAS Meta reports won't tell you how hard you can push spend. What will is what a new customer is worth over the first few months against what it costs to win one. Much of pet spend is repeat, so Retargeting and Loyalty show big ROAS on orders that were coming anyway, and the average hides how Prospecting is doing.

**How the scaling ceiling gets set**

We'd tag first-time buyers in Shopify and exclude existing customers from Prospecting in Meta. The ceiling comes from contribution margin on the first order (after shipping, discounts and fees) plus repeat orders over 60 to 90 days. For example, a $60 order at 30% contribution is $18, so paying $30 for a customer only works if repeats cover the other $12. Prospecting is judged against that ceiling alone, Loyalty on repeat rate and AOV, and MER guards the whole account. LTV takes months to read, so the ceiling starts conservative and moves as cohorts mature.

**Tracking and creative**

Our tracking specialist would first confirm the Pixel and Conversions API dedupe into one purchase with its value. Each ad then gets tagged by format, angle (pet in use, owner talking, close-up, problem solved) and opening hook, so results are judged by idea instead of by ad.

**Where our Meta record sits**

A consumer camera brand went from about $4,800 a month in February to about $20,000 in May, and CPM went from $12 to $32 across those months. Several things changed, so that isn't spend alone, but increases come in steps.

A replacement parts store we took over was running 11 campaigns at about $15,600 a month before we started. Tracked cost per conversion went from $34.72 that month to $19.89 in April and $22.91 in May, with 7 campaigns, spend at $13,200 and $11,600, and CPM flat near $8. Other variables moved too. We haven't run a pet brand.

A Meta specialist on our team runs it day to day, our tracking specialist owns tracking, and you get a live dashboard plus a written report every two weeks.
