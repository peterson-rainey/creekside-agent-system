## Draft

Most peptide stores stuck in Merchant Center are fighting two different problems with one set of fixes. Misrepresentation is a judgment about whether the business is verifiable, and COAs, research-use positioning and clean policies answer it. Healthcare and Medicines is a call about what the product is. No amount of trust signals clears it if Google still reads a specific item as an unapproved substance.

That split decides the whole plan, so it is where I would start.

1. Separate the flags by item. Sort every SKU into clean, fixable (naming, images, descriptions, landing page copy), and likely never approvable regardless of wording. Some compounds land in that last group, and knowing it in week one is far cheaper than learning it on the fourth review.
2. Audit the site the way the reviewer sees it. Misrepresentation is judged from the live domain, not the feed: the business name matching across Merchant Center, footer, terms and card descriptor; contact details reachable without checkout; shipping and refund terms that match checkout; and any benefit or dosing language anywhere on the domain, including old blog posts and reviews. One forgotten article can hold the account.
3. Request review once, after fixes are verified and documented. Each review judges what is live that day, and spending one on a half-fixed site makes the next harder.
4. Launch on the clean group only, then add SKUs in small batches. Pushing the full catalog right after approval puts the account you just recovered back at risk.

I would build Shopping first, with custom labels for margin and approval risk, and bring PMax in only for SKUs that have already proven stable.
