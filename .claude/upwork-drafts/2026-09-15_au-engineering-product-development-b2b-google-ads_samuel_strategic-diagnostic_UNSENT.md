## PROPOSAL

On a small B2B account, the expensive mistake usually isn't the cost per click, it's who the clicks come from. Searches for product development or engineering design services pull in inventors with an idea and no budget, students, job seekers and people shopping for cheap CAD work, alongside the companies you actually want. If their form fills count as conversions, automated bidding learns to find more of them, and on a thin budget a few wrong lessons use up the month.

So the audit starts with what counts as a lead, then reads the search terms report against the enquiries your team would quote on, which is where the negatives come from. Google's default location setting also shows ads to people outside Australia who have only shown interest in it. Target CPA on a handful of monthly conversions tends to throttle delivery, so manual CPC usually controls cost better until volume builds. Broad match on niche engineering terms drifts toward courses, jobs and how-to searches. On the landing page, we'd check whether the form separates a funded project from an idea, since one field there often does more for lead quality than a bid change.

The closest account our team manages is a commercial paving and striping company selling to property managers and general contractors, where Search clicks average over $17. In one market, on near-identical spend over the same months, Search delivered leads at $131 and Performance Max at $289, even though PMax clicks were about five times cheaper. Last month we also audited an industrial equipment manufacturer's account where support requests sat in the same conversion as quote requests, so cost per lead looked better than it was. None of our accounts is an engineering firm, and all are in the US.

Hands-on here means a dedicated Google Ads specialist runs the account day to day, including search terms, negatives and bids, while our conversion tracking specialist owns tracking and I stay across strategy and walk you through the audit findings. So I won't claim I personally make every change. We're a small US team rather than a solo freelancer, and our afternoon is your morning for calls.

Roughly where does monthly spend sit, closer to A$3,000 or A$15,000? Audit advice only helps inside the budget you're working with. And today, does a conversion mean a qualified enquiry, or every form fill and call?
