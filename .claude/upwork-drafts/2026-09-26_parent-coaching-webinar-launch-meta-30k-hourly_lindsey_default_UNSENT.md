Picky,

When an open enrollment closes, do you know which ads first brought in the parents who bought? Standard attribution counts a click for 7 days at most, so when a parent who registered a month earlier joins on the last day of cart, Ads Manager credits whichever ad they touched in the final days, or none. The ads that found them can look like the weakest spend in the launch report, and cutting them on that read leaves the next launch a smaller list to sell to.

**Past the click**

The largest account I run spends close to $100,000 a month on Meta for a cosmetic dental practice. When its qualified-lead rate fell, the cause was in the CRM: booked patients weren't being marked, so Meta wasn't getting the booking data it learns from. That account taught me to check every step after the click before touching an ad that's working.

**Webinars and creative**

A kids' life coaching company our team runs Meta for holds a recurring webinar. Registration ads go live a few days before each date and come off on the day, with separate campaigns for different angles. Running my own e-commerce business taught me the cheapest click isn't always the one that sells, so I'd brief your editor on the angles that bring in people who buy, not just register.

You'd have a live dashboard to check any day, plus a written read every two weeks on what changed, what caused it, and what I'd test next.
