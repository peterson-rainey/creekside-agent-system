Flat roof work and homeowners pull in different directions in the Google auction, and that is worth sorting out before any budget goes live. Most flat roof stock in a city sits on commercial buildings, condo blocks and attached garages, so a search like flat roof repair returns a mixed pool of property managers, condo boards and homeowners. Bid that theme while the copy speaks to homeowners and you pay commercial level clicks for calls you may not want, or you quietly filter out the higher ticket work. Which of the two is happening shows up in the search terms report inside a week.

The bigger split is repair against installation. They share a keyword theme and almost nothing else. Repair is urgent, low ticket, often a leak, and the searcher calls whoever answers first. Installation is a considered replacement with a long deliberation window and many times the job value. Put them in one ad group under one smart bidding target and the cheap urgent leads dominate the data, the algorithm optimises toward them, and replacement work starves. Separating those two is usually the highest impact structural change in a roofing account.

Then the channel question, which has a number behind it. Our team runs a Virginia asphalt contractor, exterior property work sold to owners, the closest shape to roofing on our book. Over the last twelve months that account spent $48,568 on Search for 255 leads at $190 each, and $30,418 on Performance Max for 112 leads at $273 each. Performance Max clicks were six times cheaper, $2.82 against $17.31, and its leads still cost 43 percent more. Google will push a new account hard toward Performance Max because it launches fast. On an account with no conversion history to aim it at, that is not where the first sixty days belong.

The ramp on that account is worth seeing too, because it was not a straight line. The first two months ran $2,984 for 5 leads, about $600 each. Month three landed at $161. The cheapest month of the year was $112 in March, and cost per lead then drifted into the $200s across the summer and stayed there even in a month when spend was cut by nearly half. Paving is seasonal, so that reads as demand and competition rather than a budget ceiling. Which of the two you are looking at is worth knowing before you plan spend.

Tracking decides the rest. Smart bidding is obedient rather than intelligent, and it buys more of whatever the account calls a win. Count every form fill and contact page view and Google will find the cheapest person who does those things, and the dashboard will look healthier than the calendar. Roofing closes on a call or an inspection booking, so those have to be the money events from day one.

Two things specific to Calgary. Local Services Ads are worth checking early. Roofing runs as an LSA category in parts of Canada as I understand it, but I would confirm the category and Calgary eligibility with Google rather than take my word for it, and screening takes time to clear. On that same asphalt account Local Services returned 6 leads at $18 each against $190 on search. Six leads is not a forecast, but it prices on a different mechanism and sits above the paid results. Second, Calgary sits in hail country, and roofing demand there moves in storm driven spikes that also pull in out of town competition bidding the same terms. A budget spread flat across twelve months is the wrong shape for that market.

Where we are thin. No roofing company on our book and no Canadian local service account, so the currency, the competitive set and the LSA specifics are new ground. The structure problem is not.

Week one would be tracking and structure rather than ads. Conversion actions tested end to end, call tracking live, Local Services submitted in parallel, search split by repair against installation and by service line, geography drawn to the drive radius you actually want. Then launch narrow on exact and phrase, read search terms daily through month one, and expand only on evidence.

You would not run this day to day. The account sits with a dedicated Google buyer rather than being handed off after signing, and you get the search terms and spend breakdown in writing whether or not the month reads well. For scale, about $65,000 a month across our active Google accounts, six years on the platform. The asphalt write up is attached.

On structure, we bill a percentage of ad spend rather than hourly, so nobody is paid to be slow. Onboarding is a fixed fee covering the build and the audit. I can put specific numbers against both once I know the budget level.

Two things before quoting properly.

What monthly budget range are you weighing, and what can a booked roof cost you before the math stops working?

How does the work split between repair and replacement today, and which of the two do you want more of?
