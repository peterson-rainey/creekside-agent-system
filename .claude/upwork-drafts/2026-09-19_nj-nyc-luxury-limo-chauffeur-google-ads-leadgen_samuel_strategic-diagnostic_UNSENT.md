The person booking a JFK or Newark pickup is often searching from Chicago or London the week before they fly in, not from New Jersey. Google's default location setting ("presence or interest") will reach them, which is right for airport arrivals. It's wrong for everything else, because the same setting shows your corporate and hourly ads to people who are only reading about NYC. So the first split we'd make is by geography: airport arrival campaigns allowed to reach travelers before they land, and corporate, hourly, events and NJ to NYC campaigns limited to people actually in the tri-state area.

The second split is by value. One lead might be a single airport drop-off, another a full-day Sprinter for a corporate group. If Google is told to maximize leads, it will find you more of the cheap ones, because those searches are easier to win. That's why campaigns get separated by service and airport, and why completed reservations need to go back into Google Ads with their fare attached. Once bidding sees revenue instead of form fills, it can tell an Escalade account client apart from a one-off ride.

How we'd structure it: one campaign per airport (EWR, JFK, LGA), with Teterboro on its own small budget so private aviation searches don't get starved by airport volume. Then corporate and executive, hourly/as-directed, vehicle-type (Escalade and Sprinter/group), NJ to NYC routes, and brand kept separate so it doesn't flatter the non-brand numbers. Negatives start with rideshare, rental, shuttle, AirTrain and transit, driver jobs, "cheap" and "how to get to" searches, then grow weekly from the search terms report.

Tracking calls and bookings: calls from ads run through a Google forwarding number. If your own number sits in the call asset, those calls can't be imported or tied back to a keyword later. Quote forms capture the click ID and pass it into your reservation system, so when a quote becomes a completed ride, it's uploaded back to the campaign and keyword that produced it. Reporting then reads spend, calls and quotes, cost per lead, completed reservations, revenue. GA4 and GTM are handled by a dedicated tracking specialist on our team, not whoever builds the ads.

On experience: we haven't run a limo account. The closest work is high-value local service where the phone decides everything. For a personal injury firm, $56.6K in Google spend produced 619 conversions at about $91 each, which worked out to roughly $950 per case on total spend. The second number is the one the owner ran the business on. For a paving contractor, Performance Max bought clicks at $3.00 against $14.53 on Search at near-identical spend, but Search delivered leads at $131 versus $268. A report that stopped at clicks would have moved budget the wrong way. Across the team that's $20M+ in managed spend and 200+ account audits.
