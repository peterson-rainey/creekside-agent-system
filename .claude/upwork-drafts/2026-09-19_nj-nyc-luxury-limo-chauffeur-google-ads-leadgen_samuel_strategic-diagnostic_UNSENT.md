The person booking a JFK or Newark pickup is often searching from Chicago or London the week before they fly in. Google's default location setting ("presence or interest") will reach them, which is right for airport arrivals and wrong for everything else, because it also shows your corporate and hourly ads to people just reading about NYC. The first split is by geography: airport campaigns allowed to reach travelers before they land, and corporate, hourly, events and NJ to NYC campaigns limited to people actually in the tri-state area.

The second split is by value. One lead is a single airport drop-off, another a full-day Sprinter for a corporate group. Told to maximize leads, Google finds more of the cheap ones. Bidding only learns the difference when completed reservations go back into Google Ads with their fare attached.

In practice that's one campaign per airport (EWR, JFK, LGA), with Teterboro on its own budget so private aviation isn't starved by airport volume. Then corporate and executive, hourly, Escalade and Sprinter/group, NJ to NYC routes, and brand kept separate so it doesn't flatter the non-brand numbers. Negatives start with rideshare, rental, shuttle, transit and driver jobs, then grow weekly from search terms.

Calls from ads run through a Google forwarding number. If your own number sits in the call asset, those calls can't be imported later. For quotes, the form captures the click ID. If your reservation software can store it, each completed ride gets uploaded back to the campaign and keyword that produced it. That's something we'd confirm first. GA4 and GTM go to a dedicated tracking specialist on our team.
