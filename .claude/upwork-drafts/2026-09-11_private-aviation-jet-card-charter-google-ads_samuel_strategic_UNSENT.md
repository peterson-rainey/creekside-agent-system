A Jet Card buyer and a one-off charter inquiry can cost the same as a form fill and be worth very different amounts once they reach Salesforce, which is why legacy structures misallocate so quietly. The clearest case we ran was Google for a reverse mortgage lender spending around $60K a month. Its largest campaign was a broad Performance Max build, and on cost per form fill it sat within about 10% of a much smaller age 62+ campaign. Once funded loans were matched back from their CRM, the age 62+ campaign was producing funded deals at $827 each against $5,588 for the broad one. We rebuilt the conversion signal around the lender's pricing-qualified stage instead of the form, shifted budget toward the campaigns with the best pre-qualified to pricing-qualified ratio, paused the underperformers, and agreed a floor of 100 pricing-qualified leads a month at $600 each.

Lead quality is the honest part of that story. The rebuild exposed two things form fills had hidden: loan officers were firing the qualification event more than once per lead, and many funded loans closed after Google's 90-day upload window, so bidding on funded deals alone was never going to work. After tracking was verified one-to-one against the CRM in July, Google's qualified-lead numbers came in below what its form volume implied, and budget moved to Meta in $5,000 steps where pricing-qualified leads were running under $400. The lesson I would bring here is to import a Salesforce stage that happens inside that window as the primary conversion, then let charter and Jet Card each earn budget on it separately. Is the pipeline already passing GCLIDs on new leads, or is that part of what is still maturing?
