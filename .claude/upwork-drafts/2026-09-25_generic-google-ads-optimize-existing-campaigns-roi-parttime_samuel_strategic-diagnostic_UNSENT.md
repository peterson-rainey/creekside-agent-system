Most Google accounts have a headline number that cannot be improved, because a large share of it was never acquisition in the first place. Branded search sits inside the account total. People typing the company name were going to buy anyway, they convert cheaply, and they lift the blended figure until it stops describing anything you can act on.

Here is the size of that distortion with real numbers. An ecommerce account on our book ran branded and non-branded Search alongside each other from November 2025 through August 2026 on near identical budgets. Branded spent $25,496 and returned 28.74x, at a $0.35 click and $16.90 per conversion. Non-branded spent $27,183 and returned 5.31x, at a $1.26 click and $104.25 per conversion. Six times the cost to reach someone who did not already know the brand. Search across that whole account blends to 16.47x, which is correct arithmetic and a useless management number. Tuning against it tells you to spend more on people already searching your name.

**The second place the report points the wrong way**

Campaign type. A paving contractor our team runs today had a Search campaign and a Performance Max campaign live over the same four months at nearly the same spend, roughly $5,750 against $5,640. Performance Max bought clicks at $3.00 against Search at $14.53 and delivered 1,879 clicks against 396, so on any view built around click cost or click volume it was the stronger campaign. It produced 21 conversions at $268 each. Search produced 44 at $131. Cheaper traffic, more expensive customers. I cannot tell you from that data why it happened, only that the click metric pointed the opposite way from the outcome metric for four straight months.

**What the first ninety days covers**

Split the last ninety days three ways before touching a setting: branded against non-branded, campaign type against campaign type, and conversion action against conversion action. The third one governs the other two. If form starts, page views or taps on the phone number are set as primary, Smart Bidding is buying the cheapest of those, and the account will look like it is getting better while revenue sits flat.

Then two numbers most accounts cannot produce on request. What a genuinely new customer costs at today's spend, and what that cost does when spend goes up. If it holds flat there is headroom and the answer is budget. If it bends, more budget makes the report worse, and the constraint sits upstream in the offer or in the search terms you are already paying for.

**Where restructuring carried the result**

A bankruptcy firm in Orange County went from 117 leads to 229 while cost per acquisition fell from $86 to $50.29, on campaign restructuring by market rather than added budget. A naturopathic clinic our team runs today had previously run Google Ads on broad wellness terms that brought traffic and few patients. On a $1,500 monthly budget the account has produced 560 conversions in the last twelve months at $40.29 each. Both are lead generation rather than ecommerce, so weight them accordingly if you sell products.
