## DRAFT

SAAS

Do you know what share of your paid subscriptions in a given month comes from people who had already trialed or installed before they ever saw the ad? On an interview product I would expect that share to run higher than most subscription categories, because demand is bursty and tied to job search cycles, so a lot of any month's signups are returners arriving on their own schedule.

It matters because reactivation and retargeting pools convert cheaper than anything else in an account, which holds blended cost per subscription down while true new-subscriber cost drifts upward. A dormant user returning through an ad is real revenue, but it is not new revenue, and the platform counts it the same either way.

I deal with this on a meal subscription account I run, by building acquisition campaigns that hard exclude every prior purchaser so the cost of a genuinely new customer is measured on its own. It sits at $25 with 4.52x ROAS. Different category, and your post is clear that category alone does not qualify, so I put it forward as the mechanic rather than the credential.

On the case you asked for, the gaps first. I ran the Meta side of a referral platform SaaS post seed, not the search side, so I have no SaaS search case of my own to offer. I also cannot give you a clean acquisition cost comparison from it, because what I have is leads and ARR, not cost per paid subscriber. What I can give you is roughly $2,000 a month across six months, with inbound leads and ARR both doubling in that window.

Given the export, the first thing I would settle is how much of the mobile side is modeled rather than observed, since that is where platform numbers and billing records stop agreeing. Every keep or cut call after that depends on which conversions count as new. Briefing creative at volume is the easy part once the counting is settled.
