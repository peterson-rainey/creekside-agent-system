## PROPOSAL

The risk with video-led Meta campaigns is that good video makes the cheap metrics look great. Left to itself, Meta will spend the budget on views and engagement, because that's where video wins, and none of it is a lead.

We saw exactly that on a small local account last month. Two campaigns, same audience, same launch day, only the objective different. The engagement campaign got nearly twice the impressions and not one recorded enquiry. The leads campaign brought in 124 at $15.77 each on roughly the same spend. Across the Meta accounts our team runs, only about 3% of campaigns are set to optimize for video views, even though most of the creative is video. Everything else optimizes to a lead or a sale.

So before anything launches, three things get checked:

1. What counts as a lead. If the pixel fires when someone opens a form instead of when they submit it, Meta learns to find form-openers. The lead event should fire once, on the real submission, and go through the Conversions API as well so iPhone tracking gaps don't starve it.

2. Where the lead lands. Instant forms are cheap and fast but pull in more tyre-kickers. A form on your site costs more per lead and usually converts better. On a dental implant account we ran, our team added qualifying questions, accepted a higher cost per lead, and cut spend when the call center needed room. Lead volume is only worth buying if someone can follow it up.

3. How creative gets judged. Since the assets come from your side, the most useful thing we can hand back is which hooks and formats actually produced leads, not just which ones held attention, so each new round of shoots is aimed at what converts.

Audiences start broad, then retargeting on video viewers and site visitors gets layered in once the pixel has enough volume to work with.

On budgets: the accounts our team runs range from a local practice spending around $3,800 a month to a single aesthetics account spending just under $100,000 a month on Meta (all USD).

One thing to be upfront about, since your post asks for independents: we're a small agency, not a solo freelancer. The usual worry with agencies is that someone senior sells the account and a junior runs it. That isn't how we work. A senior Meta specialist on our team, one who runs accounts at that scale, would set up and run yours and be the person you deal with directly, with me on strategy. The upside of a team is cover, so nothing stalls when someone is away.

You'd get a written update every two weeks plus a live dashboard you can check any time. Our working day falls overnight in Melbourne, so changes are usually done before your team starts.
