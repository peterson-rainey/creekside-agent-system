ONCOLOGY

Registrations from oncology HCPs are easy to buy and hard to verify, and LinkedIn, Meta and Google differ enormously on that second part. LinkedIn is the only one of the three that takes specialty, title and seniority as actual targeting inputs, which is why it's on your list. Meta doesn't expose employer or job title as a targeting input, so oncologists aren't natively addressable there, only reachable through matched lists like NPI derived files or creative that self-selects. Inferred, never verified. Google Search catches clinical intent at the moment of the question, guideline updates, molecular profiling, differential diagnosis. Narrow volume, real intent.

Measurement is the first decision, not channel mix. An NPI on a form isn't verification either, since the registry is public; it needs a cross-match on name and state, or a credentialing vendor. Grade each channel on verified on-target registrations after that check, not on blended cost. Otherwise Meta looks like the cost winner while filling the list with off-target signups, and with fellows, pharmacists and nurses all in scope, HCP is broad enough to hide that for months. LinkedIn needs the same check, since clinician profiles go stale.
