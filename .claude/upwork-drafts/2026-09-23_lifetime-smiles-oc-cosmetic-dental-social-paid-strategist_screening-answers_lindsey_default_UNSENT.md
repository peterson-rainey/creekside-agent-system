## Answers (paste-ready)

**1. A healthcare or local-service client you helped grow, with real numbers.**

The closest one to you is a veneers practice I run Meta for now. The most useful number I have from it is a side by side rather than a single figure. Two campaigns launched the same day, one on the leads objective using forms, one on engagement aimed at DMs. Over the same stretch this summer the engagement campaign delivered 55,242 impressions and 1,533 clicks for $2,139 and produced 6 conversions. The forms campaign delivered 24,784 impressions for $3,430 and produced 90 leads at $38.11 each. Across ninety days that account sits at 186 conversions on $15,453.

Before that I ran Meta for a dental group feeding form leads straight into a call center. Across the whole engagement that was 2,558 leads at $25.31 each. They pulled back because the call center could not keep up with the volume, not because the cost per lead moved.

One caveat, since you asked about booked appointments specifically. Those are leads. I do not have a verified cost per booked appointment for either practice, because in both cases the booking step sat outside the ad platform. That is the same gap you have today, which is my answer to your next question.

**2. One specific thing you would change about our Instagram or website right away.**

Your website, and it is close to a five minute fix that is currently costing you everything downstream. You are running the PixelYourSite plugin, but there is no pixel ID in it, so nothing fires. I checked before writing this.

In practice that means every visitor you have already paid to send there is unattributable, no retargeting audience is building from traffic you have already bought, and Meta has no conversion signal to optimise against, so it is guessing at who to show your ads to. The plugin is already installed, so this is creating the dataset, pasting the ID in and verifying events land. Same day.

The second half is bigger and worth saying before you hire anyone. Your Schedule Appointment button hands patients to a separate booking site that reports nothing back. Until that is wired, you can measure leads but not booked appointments, which is the exact thing you have said you want reported. That one is an integration, not a setting, and I would scope it in week one rather than discover it in month three.

**3. Your recommended starting monthly ad budget for a practice like ours.**

On Meta, $5,000 to $6,000 a month to start.

The reasoning rather than just the number. At roughly $38 a lead, which is what the comparable veneers practice actually produces, $5,000 buys somewhere near 130 leads a month if your costs land anywhere close to that. That is enough volume to get through the learning phase quickly and to judge lead quality in month one instead of month three. Below about $3,000 the data arrives too slowly to make decisions with and you end up paying for a learning period twice.

I would split it roughly 70/30 cold to retargeting at the start and let the numbers move it from there. Retargeting gets meaningfully cheaper once the pixel has a few weeks of history, which is another reason the pixel comes first.

I would not raise the budget until the booking side reports back. Scaling spend while you still cannot see which leads became appointments only buys more of something you cannot measure.

**4. Your approach for the first 30 days.**

Week one is measurement. Pixel and dataset created, events mapped to what actually matters, form submissions and calls separated from page views, and the booking integration scoped so we know what it takes. Nothing launches on top of broken tracking.

Week two is the audit and the market. Your pages, your existing content and anything already boosted, then your Orange County competitors, what they are running, what they lead with and where the gaps are. Ads in this category are public, so this is research rather than guesswork.

Week three I build. Lead forms and the cold campaign off your existing before and after library, with creative sorted into distinct angles rather than one pile, so we learn which story pulls rather than which image did.

Week four is launch and first read. Retargeting switches on once the pixel has history behind it. I hold the DM campaigns back deliberately until the lead side has enough data to judge them against, for the reason in my first answer.

By day thirty you should have a measurement layer you trust, a competitor picture, live campaigns and a first cost per lead worth acting on. What you will not have yet is a reliable cost per booked appointment. That depends on the booking integration, and I would rather tell you that now than promise it and miss.
