Asphalt is one of the few categories where the click price alone can end a new account before the work is any good. On the paving account our team currently runs in Virginia, search clicks on the core service terms cost $17.37 each over the last twelve months. At that price a brand new account does not get many chances to be wrong, which is why what happens in the first sixty days matters more here than in most industries.

Here is what that looked like on a real account rather than in theory. Twelve months, $79,094 spent, 373 leads, a blended $212 cost per lead. But the first two months of that account ran at roughly $460 a lead on $3,683 of spend. By month three it was at $199, and the best twelve day stretch of that account, in early March, ran 35 leads at $119 each after the twelve days before it had returned 14 leads at $174. Same account, same market, two weeks apart. Nothing clever happened in between. It was search term mining and negative keywords, which is unglamorous and is where the early money is either saved or wasted. A new advertiser should budget for that ramp deliberately instead of concluding in week three that Google does not work.

The second thing worth knowing before you launch. That same account ran Search and Performance Max side by side in one metro, Charlottesville, over the same five months on almost identical spend. Search spent $5,752 and returned 44 leads at $131 each. Performance Max spent $6,090 and returned 21 leads at $289 each. Performance Max clicks were five times cheaper, $2.71 against $14.53, and its leads were more than twice as expensive. Google will push a new account toward Performance Max because it is easy to launch and it fills the impression graph quickly. On a paving account with no conversion history to feed it, it mostly buys you traffic that was never going to call.

The third is what you let the account count as a conversion. Smart bidding is obedient rather than intelligent. It buys more of whatever you tell it a win is. If a brand new site counts every form fill, every phone tap and every contact page view, Google will find you the cheapest available version of a person who does those things, and the dashboard will look healthier than the calendar. Paving quotes close on a call or a site visit, so the build needs the phone call and the qualified quote defined as the money events from day one, and ideally the closed jobs fed back in later.

How our team would run this. Week one is tracking and architecture, not ads. Conversion actions defined and tested end to end on the new site, call tracking live, Search separated by service line so driveways and lot work are not bidding against each other, and geography drawn against a drive radius you actually want to serve. Then launch narrow on exact and phrase, read the search terms daily for the first month, and expand only where there is evidence. Local Services Ads are worth setting up in parallel for a contractor, because they price on a different mechanism than the auction. Performance Max comes later, if at all, once there is conversion history to give it something to aim at.

For context on scale, we run roughly $65,000 a month across 13 active Google accounts, which includes the paving account above. Six years on the platform.

Three things I would want before quoting properly, and I would rather ask than assume.

What monthly budget range are you weighing, and what can a booked job cost you before the math stops working? At a $17 click a paving account needs a number to bid to.

What is the service mix and how far will you travel? Residential driveways, commercial lots, sealcoating and striping have very different lead values, and the split changes the build more than the budget does.

Is the new site already carrying analytics and call tracking, or is that part of this? Getting that wrong in week one costs more than anything else on the list.
