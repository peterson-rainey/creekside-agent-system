## PROPOSAL (paste-ready)

When a Meta account stops working, the first question is one the account's own numbers cannot answer: did your performance change, or did the auction?

Across the Meta accounts our team runs, spend held roughly flat from September through December last year, about $198K, $208K, $214K and $200K a month. Blended CPM across those same months went $22.39, $24.45, $33.78, $33.90. One account we ran, a high-ticket dental and aesthetics practice holding between $90K and $115K in spend every month for a year, went from $29.39 in September to $59.52 in November and $67.59 in December, then settled back to $28.95 by March. Spend did not move in either case. The cost of reaching people did, and then it came back down on its own.

That is the part that is genuinely hard to see from inside a single account. One curve will not tell you whether it is yours or the market's, and pausing good campaigns in November is an expensive way to find out.

The second thing that reads as a drop tends to be self-inflicted, and it comes out of the creative testing itself. Adding a new ad to a live ad set is a significant edit in Meta's terms and sends that ad set back into learning. So does changing bid strategy or the optimization event. Ship variations one at a time and the ad sets never climb out of it. So tests get batched, two to seven at a time, inside the campaign that already holds the delivery learnings, and the structure stays lean enough that each ad set gets near the fifty or so results a week Meta wants before it settles.

The third is measurement, which is where pixel and CAPI work earns its keep. Both should fire on the same action sharing one event ID so they deduplicate instead of double counting, and Meta's reported conversions should be reconciled against what your CRM or store actually recorded. Standard attribution credits a click for seven days at most, so anything closing later is never attributed at all, which quietly changes what the number in your report is even measuring.

On scope I would rather be straight than agreeable. Paid media on Meta is what I would own, along with the tracking underneath it. CRM and automation work we do, mostly in HighLevel, with Zapier or Make only where there is no native connection. Email we run as well, though I have no email performance numbers I would hold up as proof. We have a landing page developer and a conversion tracking specialist on the team, but no before and after conversion rate from a page rebuild that I would put in front of you. SEO and organic content are not ours, and you would be better served hiring those separately.

Our Meta specialist runs the account day to day with me on strategy. Reporting is every two weeks plus a live dashboard you can open anytime. Onboarding is $1,500 one time, covering the tracking audit and the account build, and management is 20% of ad spend with a $1,500 monthly minimum.
