## 1. Provide past examples of success specifically in my niche

I have never run ads for an AI consulting and integration firm, and I would rather tell you that directly than stretch a case study to fit.

The closest is a B2B software company selling referral automation to home service contractors. I ran the Meta side. Contractors didn't know the category existed, so Meta's job was to build the demand and search picked up what it created. Over six months their inbound leads and ARR both doubled. That period also followed a funding round, so the credit is shared, and it ran on a few thousand dollars a month per platform.

The part that transfers just as directly is the shape of the sale. I ran Meta for a dental implant practice where a case sold in the upper $30Ks and every lead went to their call center. Over my last 90 days there, $64,746.78 brought in 2,558 leads at $25.31 each. Different industry, same economics as yours: nobody buys from the ad, they agree to a conversation, and the campaigns have to be judged on which conversations actually happen.

I also run Meta for a B2B CRM software company at the moment, so selling software to business owners is current work, not a one-off from years ago.

## 2. Explain a time you were unsuccessful in an ad campaign and how you turned that into a positive

In August a local dental practice I ran had its budget split across two campaigns, one built for leads and one for engagement. The leads campaign spent $1,955 and produced 124 leads at $15.77 each. The engagement campaign spent almost the same, $1,846, and produced no tracked leads at all.

What made that worth something is that both ran in the same account, in the same month, against the same kind of audience, which is about as close to a controlled test as an ad account gets. It turned something people usually argue about into a number: an engagement objective optimizes for people who engage, and Meta will keep finding them for as long as you pay.

I use that pair now whenever a smaller budget is about to be spread across awareness and leads. My answer is to put the budget behind the event that produces a conversation first, and widen only when there is enough volume to read a second campaign honestly. On your account it would mean proving out lead volume before any budget goes to pure awareness.

## 3. Describe your recent experience with similar projects

Recent work has mostly been Meta lead generation for businesses whose sale needs a conversation first:

- A dental implant practice, lead forms feeding their call center, cases in the upper $30Ks. That one wrapped up this summer.
- Local dental practices running on a few thousand a month each, where the question is which campaign type deserves the budget rather than how to scale.
- A B2B CRM software company, running now.
- Further back, a B2B software company selling referral automation to home service contractors, where Meta built category-level demand.

I run email alongside Meta for a couple of clients too, which matters when the decision takes longer than a click.

On one of those accounts the weekly performance report was drafted by an AI agent that queued its notes in my voice for review instead of posting them. That is the closest I have been to the customer side of what you sell, and part of why the work interests me.

## 4. How do you use metrics to inform your strategy?

Three habits, in order.

First, I split before I average, because blended numbers hide the half that is losing. On the implant account, over the same ten weeks, the Spanish-language campaign in one city brought leads at $17.38 while the English campaign in that same city ran $25.36. Averaged together that gap disappears, and so does the decision that follows it.

Second, I judge a campaign on the event that makes the money, and I check that event outside the ad platform. On that account it meant checking the CRM for who actually scheduled a consultation, rather than counting form submissions in Ads Manager. For you it would be the calls that turn into scoping conversations.

Third, I give a change enough volume to be readable before judging it. Meta needs roughly 50 results per ad set per week to leave the learning phase, so with a lean budget I run fewer things with real budget behind each, instead of five tests that never reach a verdict. You would get an update every two weeks: what changed, what it did, and what I am doing next.
