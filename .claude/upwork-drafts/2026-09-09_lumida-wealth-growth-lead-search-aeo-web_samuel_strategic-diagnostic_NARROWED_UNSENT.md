## PROPOSAL (3,959 characters, under the 5,000 limit)

A firm publishing this much research, with a podcast behind it, tends to get a flattering first read out of Google Ads and a confusing second one.

The mechanism is worth knowing before launch. Content builds name recognition, name recognition creates branded search, and branded search converts at a fraction of the cost of everything else. Turn paid search on and blended cost per acquisition looks excellent, because a good share of it is people who already heard the founder somewhere and typed the firm name. Paid gets credited for demand the content created. Then budget scales, branded inventory runs out quickly because it is capped by how many people already know you, and the marginal dollar moves to non-brand where the real price lives. The account looks like it stopped working. It did not. It ran out of the cheap half.

The size of that gap is worth seeing early. In an ecommerce account our team runs, branded and non-brand search ran side by side for ten months on nearly identical budgets, $25,496 and $27,183. Branded came in at $0.35 a click and $16.90 per conversion. Non-brand came in at $1.26 and $104.25. Same account, same window, roughly the same money, about six times the cost to reach someone who did not already know the name.

Your numbers will not be those numbers. The point is that it belongs on the report as two lines from week one, never one. Otherwise the day you scale is the day the account appears to break, and the conversation turns to ad quality when the actual subject is how much branded inventory exists.

The second thing to settle before launch is what the account is allowed to count as a win. Bidding trains on whatever you feed it. Feed it form fills and it gets very good at finding people who fill in forms, which in advice-led financial services skews toward the smallest and slowest inquiries you have. The alternative is to send the qualified opportunity back rather than the lead. The click identifier gets stored on the record at first touch, and once your team marks something qualified, that event goes back to the platform as the conversion, so bidding optimizes toward pipeline instead of volume. It needs a few weeks of history before behavior changes, which is why it is worth wiring before you need it rather than after.

On competitors, the paid version of that question is cheap and answers itself fast. Our team ran competitor-name campaigns in a dental implant account alongside that same account's brand campaign. Conquest came in at $64.24 per conversion against $136.96 on brand. The identical play in a second metro spent $381 and produced nothing at all. Both results sitting in one account is the honest version of that tactic. It is a market-by-market question, not a strategy.

Straight with you on scope, since your post covers more ground than we should claim. Creekside runs Google and Meta. There is no SEO service line here, no organic ranking or backlink results to point at, and no Webflow or conversion-rate-lift work to show you. On answer engines, nobody holds a multi-year record in a discipline roughly two years old, and anyone presenting one is worth a second look. So the useful offer is the search half run properly, while the rest of that list gets solved another way.

On the vertical, a financial advisory firm running retirement planning events sits in our book on both platforms today. That is a match on funnel shape rather than a claim about wealth management at your client tier, and there is no wealth firm on our list.

Two things would change the plan more than anything else. What range are you considering for monthly search budget, and is that a ceiling or a number you would size against what it returns? And what does your team already treat as a qualified opportunity, in their own words rather than the stage name in the CRM?

Happy to get on a call and go through the brand and non-brand split against your own search terms before you commit to anything.
