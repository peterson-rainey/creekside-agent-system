## ANSWERS

1. Describe your recent experience with similar projects

The closest recent match is a mortgage lender I ran Meta for over about a year. Financial services, lead generation. Spend scaled to just under $22,000 a month at its peak, and between April and May of this year cost per lead ran $82 to $59 while volume went 109 to 140, on slightly less spend than the month before.

That account also had a Google program running alongside it, handled by a specialist I work with, so the two halves were planned against each other rather than in isolation.

Overlapping with that, a dental implant practice: Meta instant forms feeding a call team, $64,746.78 spent between April 21 and July 15 of this year, 2,558 conversions at $25.31 each.

Both accounts have since ended. Neither was a Search, Display or YouTube seat, which I'd rather say plainly than stretch.

2. How do you use metrics to inform your strategy?

The number I scale on is cost per booked appointment or sale, not cost per lead. Meta optimizes toward whatever event it is told counts, so an account judged on form fills will keep finding people who fill in forms and the reporting will look fine while revenue sits flat. The check I run is against the CRM rather than the platform: how many of last month's leads reached a real conversation, and which campaigns those came from.

That moves decisions in both directions. On the dental account the leads were cheap and plentiful and I still cut daily spend by more than half, because the call team could not work the volume it was already getting. Cheap leads nobody calls are not a result.

Reporting runs every two weeks against a live dashboard, so what gets acted on is a trend rather than one bad week.
