## PROPOSAL

Before any creative is written for a research peptide brand, two things on the Meta side can shape what is possible. Meta can review the page an ad opens as well as the ad, and a category assigned to your data source can limit what a campaign can optimize toward. The second you can check in Events Manager today.

**Experience, straight**

We have not run Meta for a research peptide brand and have no peptide result to show. Our peptide work so far is diagnosis and strategy on Google rather than a managed account with results. One store we spoke with had a Google account reading Not Eligible on Search and Shopping even though a healthcare attorney had reviewed the site before launch, so I would not treat legal review as proof an ad will pass. On Meta we run ads for a practice offering cosmetic and aesthetic procedures, where Meta requires adult targeting and bars statements of inferiority about physical appearance. Separately, the account has spent $89,900 to $114,600 in every full month since October 2025 and is still running. That is spend, not a result.

**The check to run first**

In Events Manager, see whether a category has been assigned to your data source and whether any restriction notices exist. Meta can limit specific mid and lower funnel events or apply full restrictions. If the event we would optimize toward is limited, one option is a different event or objective, and I would tell you before any spend. If not, we would build few ad sets on broad targeting so each can reach the roughly 50 purchases a week where delivery settles.

**What compliant means here**

We would check the page against the same rules as the ad. Nothing in the ads would assert or imply a viewer's health condition, which Meta's personal attributes rule covers. For creative tests we would add new ads in planned batches, since each addition is the kind of edit that sends an ad set back into learning. We would not reword a rejected ad to get it through. Meta's drugs policy bars ads promoting "unsafe substances, products or supplements" and does not name peptides, so how Meta reads each product is Meta's call, and some products may never be advertisable.

**Availability and pricing**

We work Monday to Friday, US Central business hours, and we would confirm a start date after seeing the account. Structure is a review, then the build, then management with a written read every two weeks. Management is a percentage of monthly ad spend, 20 percent up to $30,000 and less above, with a $1,500 monthly minimum, plus a one-time $1,500 onboarding fee covering the review and build.

Has any account, Page or domain tied to the brand already been restricted on Meta, and is monthly spend closer to $5,000 or $25,000? The plan changes a lot between those two.
