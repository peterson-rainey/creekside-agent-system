## Answers

Q1. Please show me websites you personally redesigned and explain what you would change about my current website.

I haven't redesigned websites. I run the paid Meta side, so there's no site I can show you as my own design work. What I can do is look at your site the way I look at any page ad traffic lands on, and here's what I'd change before spending a dollar sending people to it:

1. Give the training program its own page. "Book Training" and "Purchase Product" look like buttons but aren't links, so there's currently no way to learn about or sign up for your course from the site. Course outline, price, dates, what students leave with, and a way to apply. A training ad needs somewhere to land, and right now it has nowhere.
2. Split clients and students. Everything sits on one page, and a woman booking a fill wants something very different from someone thinking about a lash career. Separate pages let each ad send people to the one that matches.
3. Keep what's already working and move it up. Your tiered pricing is clear, and reviews like "I've been a client for 8+ years" are exactly what a new client needs to see before booking. Right now they sit below a lot of text.
4. Quick fixes worth making in the redesign: the browser tab reads "perfect lashes website", the "get social" section has no links to your Instagram or Facebook, and "Purcahse" and "Intermeidte" are misspelled.
5. Tracking. I couldn't find a Meta Pixel on the site, and bookings finish on Vagaro, so the first thing I'd check is what Vagaro can report back to Meta. Without that connection, Meta can't see which ads turn into appointments.

The black-and-gold look itself I'd leave to a designer.

Q2. Show me 2–3 beauty or local service businesses whose Instagram/Facebook you have personally grown. What were their results?

I haven't grown organic followings. I don't run day-to-day posting, so I have no follower-growth numbers to show you, and none of the businesses below are lash salons. What I do is run paid Meta for local appointment-based businesses, and those results are measured in enquiries and bookings:

- A cosmetic dentistry and med spa: I run their Meta ads, which spent about $95,000 in the last 30 days.
- A dental implant group: between April 21 and July 15, 2026, the Meta campaigns I ran brought in 2,558 leads at $25.31 each.
- A local dental practice: last month, the lead campaign I ran brought in 124 enquiries at $15.77 each, on under $2,000 of spend.

Happy to walk you through any of these accounts on a call.

Q3. How do you use metrics to inform your strategy?

I pick the one number that actually means money for each goal and judge everything against it. For you that's two numbers: cost per booked appointment for lash clients, and cost per training inquiry for students. Likes, follows and reach get watched, but they never decide where the budget goes.

How that works in practice:
- First, make sure the numbers are real. Your bookings finish on Vagaro, so I'd confirm what Vagaro can pass back to Meta. If it can't report completed bookings, I'd track clicks through to booking and match them against new clients in Vagaro every week.
- Every week, I read each ad in order: do people stop on it, do they click, do they book. That tells me whether the problem is the creative, the offer, or the booking page, so I fix the right thing instead of just changing the ad.
- Before an ad launches, I set how much it gets to spend before I judge it. Then it gets cut or given more budget on the booking number, not on how it looks.
- Capacity matters as much as cost. On the dental implant group, we deliberately reduced ad spend so the call team could keep up with the leads coming in. Your version is chair time: when your Level 3 artists are booked out, budget should push what's still open, like Level 2 appointments or your training program.
- Once a month, I set targets for both numbers and report plainly on what's working and what isn't.
