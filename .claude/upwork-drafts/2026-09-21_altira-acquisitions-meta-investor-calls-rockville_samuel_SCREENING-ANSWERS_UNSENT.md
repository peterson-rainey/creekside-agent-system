**1. Describe your recent experience with similar projects**

The closest ones from this year:

A dental implant group where full-mouth cases averaged in the upper $30Ks. Meta lead forms fed a call center, averaging about $25 a lead across 2,558 leads from late April to mid-July. Separately, our team added qualifying questions to the forms, accepting a higher cost per lead for quality, and cut spend when the call center needed room.

A reverse mortgage lender on Meta at up to about $22K a month. The events ran form fill, pre-qualified, pricing-qualified, funded. Meta had put data-sharing restrictions on the account, so our tracking specialist got the events approved and confirmed with Meta before the June conversion campaigns were built on the pre-qualified step.

On real estate, beyond the land seller in my cover letter, we currently run Google for a Nashville company that buys houses direct from sellers.

**2. How do you use metrics to inform your strategy?**

Backwards from the booked call. Cost per lead is the easiest number to improve and the least useful on its own, so your weekly report would show cost per lead, cost per qualified applicant (by whichever answers you define as qualified) and cost per booked call, split by ad through the utm_content your funnel already captures.

Three rules sit on top of that. Meta wants roughly 50 of an event a week per ad set before it learns well, so campaigns optimize on Lead until the qualified event has that volume. Each Reel is judged on cost per qualified applicant, so a cheap one pulling mostly "just researching" answers gets cut. And stage ratios double as a tracking check: on the reverse mortgage account I expected pricing-qualified to run 10 to 20% of pre-qualified, and when the counts didn't fit, we didn't call the tracking done until it was traced.

It cuts the other way too. On the dental account our team checked the CRM for who booked a consultation and kept a campaign running even though its leads cost more, because they were booking.
