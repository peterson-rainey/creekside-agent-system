## PROPOSAL

Landscape lighting, pool cage lighting and permanent architectural lighting get bought three different ways, and that's usually where uneven lead flow comes from. People search Google for landscape lighting. Most homeowners with a screened pool cage don't think about lighting it until they see one lit up at night. Permanent lighting sits in between, and interest usually picks up heading into the holidays.

So I'd split the budget by how each service is bought, each in its own campaign so one doesn't eat the rest. Google Search takes landscape lighting. Permanent lighting runs on both: Search catches people who already know the product exists, and Facebook reaches the ones who don't yet. Search volume on permanent lighting terms can be thin in some markets, so we'd check it before committing budget and move that spend to Facebook if it's not there. Pool cage lighting lives mostly on Facebook, where a short night video of a finished job does the selling. Local Service Ads are worth checking too, if your category qualifies.

Two things make leads steadier month to month. First, counting calls. Google only counts calls from your ads as conversions when they come through a Google forwarding number, and without that it optimizes toward form fills and misses the phone leads. Second, Facebook lead forms get a question or two, like zip code and whether they own the home. That raises cost per lead somewhat in exchange for fewer wasted calls.

If permanent lighting is a big part of the business, those campaigns should be running and learning before the holiday rush, not starting in the middle of it.

Our Google and Meta specialists would run the accounts day to day, with me on strategy.

The attached case study is LawnValue, a Tennessee lawn care company our team ran on Google, Facebook and Local Service Ads. Their leads came and went before we started, and over 12 months the campaigns brought in 2,000+ leads.
