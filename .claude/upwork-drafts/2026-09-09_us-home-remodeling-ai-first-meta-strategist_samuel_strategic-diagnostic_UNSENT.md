## PROPOSAL (4,221 characters, under the 5,000 limit)

The gate you set is the part worth looking at first. Scaling past $5,000 once a funnel proves repeatable is the
right instinct, and $1,500 a month is about $50 a day, which is where that plan meets Meta's math. A remodeling
lead worth having usually lands somewhere between $80 and $250, so $50 a day buys single digit leads per week
across the entire account. Meta wants roughly 50 conversions per ad set per week to leave learning. The evidence
your $5,000 decision depends on is therefore very hard to produce at the budget that comes before it.

That is not an argument for spending more before you are ready. It is an argument for deciding what month one
is actually for.

At $1,500 you are buying signal integrity, not learning. One offer, one audience, one conversion event that
means something to your sales team. Creative testing gets deferred, because at that volume a losing variant and
an unlucky variant look identical, and calling one a winner is how accounts get scaled into a wall.

What you can read at that budget are the rungs above the conversion. Hook rate, then hold rate, then click
through, then form completion, then lead to appointment. Each has enough volume to be readable at $50 a day even
when cost per lead is still noise, and each failure points at a different fix. Weak hold with a strong hook is a
script problem. Strong click through with weak completion is a form problem, not a creative one. Leads arriving
and sales calling them junk is a message match problem, and the fix is usually to make the ad more
disqualifying, not more appealing. That ladder is what turns "we need more creative" into an actual brief, and
it is how the winning funnel gets found before the budget arrives to confirm it.

On qualified over cheap, one example of the tradeoff being taken deliberately. Our team ran a dental implant
account where Meta lead forms fed a call center, 2,558 leads on $64,747 over about 78 active days. Qualifying
questions were added to the form on purpose, cost per lead rose, and that was the intended outcome, because the
constraint was how many calls the phone room could make in a day, not how many leads Meta could deliver. Spend
was throttled to that capacity. Any funnel with a human step downstream has that ceiling somewhere, and it is
worth finding yours before spend scales into it. That account has since ended.

On measurement, a live example of a blended number lying. In a Texas gutter account in our book, account level
cost per thousand reads $39.05. Split by objective it is $47.77 on the campaigns actually chasing leads and
$6.74 on boosted posts sitting in the same account. Roughly an eighteen percent understatement of what lead
inventory really costs, invisible unless the report separates them. That account also keeps proven instant form
creative in its own campaign, separate from new concepts on their own budget, so a new angle is never judged
against a budget allocation it did not earn.

On the AI side, the useful parts are unglamorous. Scheduled pulls from Meta and Google land in one database
next to calls and CRM records, so questions get asked once across the whole funnel. A monitor runs every
morning against each account and checks pacing, KPI drift, frequency, and pixel and CAPI health, which catches
the tracking break that would otherwise burn a week of budget. Competitor research against the Meta Ad Library
runs on a schedule rather than as an afternoon someone loses, which is what makes creative angles a queue
instead of a scramble.

Straight with you on proof. No Meta remodeling result exists in our book. Home services results we can document
are largely Google side, including custom home construction where cost per lead went from $454 to $239. The
gutter account above is live Meta in a trade, and the dental one is lead forms into a phone room at volume.
Neither is remodeling, and I would rather say that than stretch something adjacent.

Two things worth knowing before a plan means anything. Is $1,500 a firm ceiling for the first stretch, or a
starting point you are open to sizing against the learning rate? And what does your team currently count as a
qualified opportunity, in their words rather than the CRM's?

## LOOM SCRIPT (target 3 to 4 minutes, three questions in their order)

**Q1. Competitor research and creative strategy.**
Ad Library first, and structurally rather than for inspiration. Filter to remodelers running the longest, since
duration is the only public proxy for what is working. Read for three things: the offer, the proof format, and
the first three seconds. Most remodeling ads open on finished kitchens, which is the contractor's pride and the
homeowner's least useful frame. What converts is the homeowner's problem, so damage, the in-home consult, the
moment somebody explains what a quote actually covers. On our side that research runs on a schedule against the
Ad Library rather than as an afternoon someone loses, so angles accumulate in a queue. The brief that comes out
is angle, hook, script, the shot list your crew captures on a job site, and what the editor cuts, in that order.

**Q2. Finding a winning funnel for a high-ticket local business.**
Walk the five rung ladder on screen: hook rate, hold rate, click through, form completion, lead to appointment.
Point at the budget math, roughly $50 a day, single digit leads a week, 50 conversions per ad set per week to
exit learning, so the conversion count is not readable yet and the rungs above it are. Say plainly that month
one is one offer, one audience, one conversion event, tracking verified, creative testing deferred until volume
can settle it. Then the gate to $5,000 is a rung that moved, not a cost per lead that wobbled.

**Q3. AI, automation and KPI tracking daily.**
Show the morning monitor: pacing, KPI drift, frequency, pixel and CAPI health, one account at a time, before
anyone opens Ads Manager. Show scheduled platform pulls landing next to calls and CRM records so lead quality
can be asked as one question instead of three exports. Be explicit that the judgment calls are not automated,
only the fetching, the checking and the flagging. Overclaiming here is easy and it does not survive week two.
