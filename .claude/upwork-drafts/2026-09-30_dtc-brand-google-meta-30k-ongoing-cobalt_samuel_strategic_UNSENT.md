At $30K a month across Google and Meta, the first thing I'd want to know is whether the two platforms are both taking credit for the same order. GA4, the Pixel and Google's conversion tag each count a purchase their own way, and when the combined platform-reported revenue runs well ahead of what Shopify actually booked, budget decisions get made on inflated numbers. That check comes before any bid or budget change.

**The account you asked about**

A survival gear ecommerce brand, running Google and Meta together. The problem: blended Meta ROAS looked fine at 2.32x, so nobody questioned it. When our team split performance by country over the same six weeks, the US was returning 3.79x and the UK 0.59x. The UK's cheaper CPMs ($27 vs $41 in the US) were pulling budget toward it, and the blend was hiding a market returning 59 cents per dollar spent.

What changed: we brought the split to the owner with the numbers side by side, and they chose to pause the UK. On the Google side, non-brand ROAS went from 1.50x in July to 2.61x in August. Roughly 45% of that lift came from the UK removal, so I wouldn't present it as a pure optimization win. Happy to walk through screenshots on a call with the brand hidden.

**How I'd run your two accounts**

Week one is tracking. Confirm CAPI is deduplicating against the Pixel (matching event IDs), check Enhanced Conversions on Google, and reconcile a week of platform-reported purchases against Shopify orders so we know how far off each platform is.

Then Merchant Center. Disapprovals, missing GTINs and weak titles quietly cap Shopping and PMax, so feed health gets checked before we judge those campaigns.

Google: Search split brand vs non-brand so brand doesn't inflate the account's ROAS, Shopping and PMax given separate budget lanes, weekly search term review and negatives. YouTube earns more budget only once we can see it moving branded search and direct traffic.

Meta: prospecting, retargeting and catalog kept distinct enough that retargeting isn't claiming sales prospecting created. Creative tests run one variable at a time with a spend threshold decided before launch, so "what won" is a real answer.

**Reporting**

You'd get a written update every two weeks: what changed, what happened, what's next, in plain English. There's also a live dashboard you can check anytime, and if tracking breaks, spend pacing drifts or ROAS drops, you hear about it that day, not in the next report.

One question: what are your current ROAS and CPA targets, and is that measured on first order or on repeat purchase value?
