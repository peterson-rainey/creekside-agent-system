## Proposal

When you say performance driven, is the number you are managing to the ROAS that Meta and Google each report on themselves, or a blended new customer cost across all three channels together? I ask because those two numbers disagree more often than they agree, and which one you pick decides what gets cut.

The reason it matters to me is a replacement parts brand I ran Meta for at about $15K a month. Going into Q4, ROAS fell from 7.7x to 4.3x on flat spend while click-through rate actually rose and CPMs climbed about 40 percent. The creative was getting better and the auction was getting more expensive at the same time. Cutting on platform ROAS that quarter would have killed the winners. I held them, CPMs normalized in January, and by April the account was at $19.89 per purchase and 8.57x, across roughly $124K in spend and 4,300 purchases while I had it.

I built and sold my own e-commerce business before I started doing this for other brands, and after 10+ years the same pattern keeps showing up. Google captures the demand Meta creates, so the two accounts have to be read together, not scored against each other. On a consumer camera brand I ran, retargeting looked about 15 percent cheaper per purchase than prospecting, but the warm pool was too small for shifting budget into it to change anything. Email is the third leg, and the first thing I check in Klaviyo is whether the site is actually passing Added to Cart events, because one of our stores had an abandoned cart flow firing on nobody.

I run Meta and email directly and sit on accounts where our Google team runs search and Shopping alongside me, including three e-commerce brands live on both right now.
