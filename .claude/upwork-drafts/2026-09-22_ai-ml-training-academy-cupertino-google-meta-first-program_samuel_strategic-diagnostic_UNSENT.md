## PROPOSAL

"NLP certification" on Google is largely a coaching search, so the acronym is the risk, not the topic. Keywords that spell it out ("natural language processing course", "NLP with Python") are safer, and negatives like practitioner, coaching and hypnosis go in before launch, not after the first report. Meta has the same trap: targeting an "NLP" interest can pull the coaching crowd, so build that audience from engineering and data interests.

For structure, one campaign per catalog area so each has its own budget, then ad groups by course. On a new account I'd start with phrase and exact match on course and certification terms and hold off on bare terms like "machine learning" or "data science", which draw students and job seekers. Broad match comes later, once conversion bidding has leads to learn from. Corporate L&D comes in phase two as its own campaign, with its own cost-per-lead target.

On pages, something in between: one template, four versions matched to the catalog areas, with a separate form for corporate inquiries.

Google goes first because it catches people already searching. Meta adds the career changer who isn't searching yet, through a free workshop or info session, plus retargeting. It starts once Google shows which area converts. Our team runs Meta for a company selling a certification to adults, built around a weekly free webinar, the closest shape to yours.

The usual blind spot is treating the form fill as the win. A grad downloading a syllabus and a working professional asking about a cohort look identical to Google unless enrollments get sent back. So setup covers GA4, enhanced conversions and call tracking, plus a way to mark which leads enrolled.

For the pilot, I'd run two catalog areas rather than four, at no less than $5,000 a month on Google, then shift budget toward whichever turns leads into enrollments cheaper.

Setup is $1,500 per platform, one-time, billed as each platform launches. Management is 20% of spend with a $1,500 per platform monthly minimum. We work on a 90-day minimum, then month-to-month, because a new account's first month mostly shows whether tracking works, not which area deserves more budget. Launch timing mostly depends on how fast tracking and pages are ready.

Happy to walk through it on a call. Is the pilot closer to $5,000 a month or $15,000, and does enrollment happen online or after a call with an advisor?
