## PROPOSAL

Most lead campaigns stop measuring at the form submit, which is how cost per lead can fall while the calendar stays empty. Google and Meta optimize toward whatever event you feed them, so if a booked appointment never gets sent back, they get very good at finding people who fill out forms and never pick up the phone.

The fix is mostly plumbing. Calls, form fills and booking requests get tracked as separate conversions, every lead carries its source into your CRM, and when someone books (or better, shows up), that status goes back to Google as an offline conversion and to Meta through the Conversions API. A few weeks into that, bidding shifts toward people who actually book, and you can see which campaign produced each appointment instead of guessing.

On channels, Google Search usually goes first because it catches people already looking. Facebook and Instagram work well as a second layer, remarketing to site visitors and then prospecting, but they create demand rather than capture it. Those leads need faster follow-up and a qualifying question or two on the form. Every qualifying question raises cost per lead, and it's usually worth it.

The other half of booked appointments happens after the ad. Our team ran Meta lead forms into a dental implant practice's call center at around $20K/month, 2,558 leads over 90 days at $25.31 each, and the real limit turned out to be how many leads the call center could work, so spend was pulled back to match. On a paving contractor's Google account, Performance Max clicks came in 5.4x cheaper than Search, but its leads cost $289 against $131. Case study attached.

Two things would shape the plan. What you sell and in which markets, and a rough range for monthly ad budget, including whether any of it is running today. Also worth knowing who follows up on a new lead and how quickly, since that decides how hard we can push volume.
