## Draft (verbatim)

Teeth extractions and dental implants look like one service line on a website and behave like two different buying decisions. An extraction is urgent and uncomfortable, so the patient searches on a phone and calls whoever picks up. A full-arch case gets deliberated for weeks and often starts as a referral from a general dentist, so it never passes through the funnel at all, even when the patient later looks you up by name. Funnels for oral and maxillofacial practices get built for the second patient and measured on the first, and then an audit comes back recommending better conversion when the real problem is two demand streams sharing one form.

That is where our team would start, before anything gets redesigned. Dynamic number insertion so calls carry their source into GA4, consult requests tracked separately from consults that actually happen, and referral traffic tagged so it stops getting credited to whichever page the patient landed on last. How far that reaches into your practice management system depends on which one you run and whether a BAA is in place, so that piece is worth scoping, not promising.

On implants specifically, we ran Meta for a full-arch practice across Roseville and El Dorado Hills: 2,558 patient leads at $25.31 each over 78 active days. Those are leads, not case starts. Their data lived in Salesforce and HIPAA limited what we could see of the scheduling system, which is much of why measurement comes first. Two other dental practices are on Google with us now, one of them on Meta as well.

Being straight about the rest: paid acquisition and the tracking under it is our lane. We are not an SEO shop and we do not build websites. On GEO we have no track record, and nobody holds a multi-year one in a category this new. If you bring in a separate SEO or web team we work alongside them fine, and the measurement layer makes their work legible anyway.

Curious what you have for call tracking today, and whether any paid budget sits behind the extraction and implant pages.
