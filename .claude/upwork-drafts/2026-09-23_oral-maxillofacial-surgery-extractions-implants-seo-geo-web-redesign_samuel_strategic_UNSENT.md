## Draft (verbatim)

Teeth extractions and dental implants look like one service line on a website and behave like two entirely different buying decisions. An extraction is urgent and uncomfortable, so the patient searches on a phone and calls whoever picks up. A full-arch implant case gets deliberated for weeks, talked over with a spouse, and often arrives as a referral from a general dentist who will never visit your site. Funnels for oral and maxillofacial practices tend to get built for the second patient and measured on the first, and then an audit comes back recommending better conversion when the real problem is two demand streams sharing one form.

That is where our team would start, before anything gets redesigned. Dynamic number insertion so inbound calls carry their source into GA4 and into your practice management system. Consult requests tracked separately from consults that actually happen. Referral traffic tagged so it stops getting credited to whichever page the patient landed on last. Get that honest first and a redesign has something to aim at.

On implants specifically, we ran Meta for a full-arch practice across Roseville and El Dorado Hills: 2,558 patient leads at $25.31 each over 78 active days. Their downstream data lived in Salesforce and HIPAA limited what we could see of the scheduling system, which is much of why measurement is where we push first. Two other dental practices are on Google with us now, one of them on Meta as well.

Being straight about the rest: paid acquisition and the tracking under it is our lane. We are not an SEO shop and we do not build websites. On GEO, nobody holds a multi-year track record in it yet and I would be skeptical of anyone claiming one.
