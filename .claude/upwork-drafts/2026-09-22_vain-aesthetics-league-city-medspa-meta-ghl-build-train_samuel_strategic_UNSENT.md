The first decision in this build is what Meta gets told counts as a win. If it's the form fill, Meta finds the people who fill out forms most cheaply, and for a med spa that tends to mean injectable price shoppers and people collecting free consults who never book. GoHighLevel can send a signal back to Meta when a lead moves to booked or showed in the pipeline, so Meta learns from people who actually come in. That only works if each lead keeps its Facebook details, so tracking goes in before any spend. At a smaller budget, bookings may be too few for Meta to learn from, which is one of the first calls we'd make together.

Two things belong in week one. Automated texts from a local number need A2P 10DLC registration, and carriers review your consent language and sample messages, so that starts before the ads do. Meta also requires cosmetic procedure ads to target 18 and older. Before-and-after shots are allowed under that, but promising a specific result within a set timeframe without a qualifier is not, so offer copy needs care.

On training, we'd split it. What your team can own early: working the pipeline, picking up conversations the automation hands off, judging which leads are real, and reading the few numbers that matter weekly, plus details like HighLevel only counting a lead as paid social when its link tag contains "fb_ad", case-sensitive. What stays with us until bookings come in steadily enough to read week to week: budget pacing, creative testing and the Meta signal setup, because those break quietly. Everything gets built inside Vain's accounts either way.

For results, our team ran Google and Meta for a three-location med spa, where conversion rate came in around double the industry average at the same cost per conversion and helped save the third location from closing. Today we run both platforms for a cosmetic dentistry practice that brings in north of 100 consultations a month.

On GoHighLevel, our team built the lead flows for a dental practice. We held spend until the flows and leads were confirmed, then raised it, and the pipeline traces a lead from its ad to a booked consultation. One lesson: message ads can flood an owner's phone through the automations, so qualifying questions go in the flow from the start.
