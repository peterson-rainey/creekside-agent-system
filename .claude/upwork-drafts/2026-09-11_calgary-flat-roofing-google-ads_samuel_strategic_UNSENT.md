Flat roof repair in Calgary is two different buyers typing the same words. A homeowner with a leaking inner-city infill and a property manager with a leaking strip mall both search it, and those are not the same job. Add installation, where one project is worth several repairs, and a single campaign ends up bidding the same on all three. The blended cost per lead looks fine while budget drifts toward the cheapest, least valuable calls.

So the first fix is structure, before keyword research. Repair and installation each get their own campaign and budget, so a high-value install is never competing with a patch job for the same dollars. Residential campaigns carry a negative list that strips out commercial, condo board and warehouse searches. Location is set to presence only, not presence or interest, so you are not paying for people outside Calgary reading about Calgary roofers. Calls count as leads only past a minimum length, so wrong numbers never inflate the results.

Our clearest example of this in the trades is a paving contractor on Google. Its original broad Search campaign ran $562 per lead before it was replaced with a service-specific build, which has since produced 207 leads at $193 each with a 9% click-to-lead rate. Performance Max in that same account converts at about 1%. A Tennessee builder saw the same kind of service-line split take cost per lead from $454 to $239, and that case study is attached.

To be upfront, we have not run roofing, and our local service accounts are in the US rather than Canada. Structure and tracking carry over directly. Calgary click costs and your competitor set get measured in the account rather than assumed.
