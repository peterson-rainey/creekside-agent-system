Supplement native usually breaks in one of two places, and neither one is the ad. Either the presell earns the click but the offer page can't close a reader who never went looking for a product, or the front-end CPA looks fine and nobody underwrites it against what a customer is worth at day 60 or 90. The fix is different in each case, so the first job is working out which one you'd be dealing with.

Before anything else, a straight answer to your first ask: our team has not scaled on Taboola or Outbrain, and we don't run a supplement account. Our book is Meta and Google, across ecom and lead gen. So I don't have native case studies to send, and I won't present a Meta account as one. Better you hear that now than on a call.

What follows is Meta, not native. The part that carries over is what decides whether native makes money: cold traffic, a page that has to do the selling, and a CPA judged against margin rather than the dashboard. One example from an ecom parts store our team took over: in the month before we started, cost per purchase was $34.72 at an $8.10 CPM. In April it was $19.89 at an $8.18 CPM, and in May $22.91 at a $7.75 CPM.

How the first 30 days would run:

Week 1, economics before traffic. First-order contribution margin, refund and chargeback rate, and the 60/90-day repeat curve if you have one. That sets the CPA you can actually afford to buy at, which is the number every scaling decision runs off. Server-side conversion tracking goes in the same week, so publisher-level data isn't guesswork.

Week 2, compliance and pages. Supplement advertorials get rejected on disease and outcome claims, so every angle gets checked against structure/function language before it goes to review, not after a rejection. Two or three presell angles, each with its own hook, all pointing at the same offer so the test reads cleanly.

Weeks 3 and 4, launch on one platform first with enough budget per angle to get a real read. Start with broad publisher coverage, then block or bid down publishers on cost per purchase, not CTR. You get a written brief each week: what won, what's next, and why.
