## ANSWERS

**1. Provide past examples of success specifically in my niche**

Straight answer first: we don't run ads for an AI or automation consultancy today, so the exact match doesn't exist and I won't dress one up.

Closest live work is an AI product company rather than a services firm. We run Meta for an AI medical scribe sold to clinicians, around $10,000 a month, and it's been with us over a year. The campaigns are split by specialty and by message, pain-point videos against feature-led ones, because in that market the specialty decides the objection more than any demographic does. Happy to walk you through that account on a call.

Closest example I can put numbers to is a B2B software company selling referral automation to home service contractors. In the six months after their seed round, inbound leads doubled, with the funding doing part of that work. Meta built awareness for a category contractors didn't know existed, and Google captured the searches that followed. That case study is attached.

**2. Explain a time you were unsuccessful in an ad campaign and how you turned that into a positive**

A paving contractor we run on Google. A Performance Max campaign and a Search campaign ran over the same four months at nearly the same spend, about $5,644 against $5,752. PMax bought clicks at $3.00 against $14.53, so on any click report it looked like the better buy. It wasn't. Search produced 44 leads at $131 each, PMax produced 21 at $268.

We went in expecting cheaper traffic to pull the lead cost down and it did the opposite. What came out of it is a comparison we now run on purpose instead of assuming: when both can run, give them comparable spend and a fixed window, then judge on cost per lead rather than cost per click. It's also why I'd want your qualified calls flowing back into the platforms early, because the same trap repeats one level deeper, where cheap leads look fine until you see which ones actually booked.

**3. Describe your recent experience with similar projects**

We have about a dozen Google accounts and fifteen on Meta live right now, nearly all US businesses, and most of them lead generation rather than ecommerce.

Three that sit closest to yours. The AI scribe account above, on Meta at roughly $10,000 a month. A paving contractor on Google, about $7,100 and 42 leads last month, where the job is keeping expensive clicks pointed at the work worth winning. And a clinic on Google where booked appointments come back from the CRM, so the campaigns are read on bookings rather than raw form fills.

That last one is the pattern I'd want here. A consulting sale gets decided on a call, not on a form, so the account needs to see that far down the line.

**4. How do you use metrics to inform your strategy?**

By settling first on which number the account is allowed to chase, then making sure the platforms can actually see it. Here that's a qualified discovery call, so the calls your team marks as a fit go back to Google through an offline import tied to the click and to Meta through the Conversions API. One limit worth knowing: Google won't import a conversion more than 90 days after the click, so anything slower than that gets judged in your CRM instead of the ad account.

After that it's mostly refusing to read averages. On one account last month, two campaigns ran side by side on similar budgets. The one optimised for leads brought 124 at $15.77. The one optimised for engagement bought nearly twice the impressions and recorded no enquiries at all. The account-level figure was $30.65 a lead and described neither of them.

Timing matters as much. Google reports conversions on the click date, so the newest month always looks worse than it will finish, and a Meta ad set needs roughly 50 results a week before its numbers mean much. Inside that window I change one thing at a time, so the next reading still means something.
