When a creative wins in your Meta account, can you tell whether the angle won or the product it happened to show? With every product in one prospecting campaign, Meta pushes spend to whichever item buys cheapest, and creative reviews credit the angle for what the product did.

**Two accounts, with the numbers**

A luxury furniture brand: with prospecting broken out by product line, one line returned 2.36x and another 0.40x, which I cut after 11 days. Monthly spend went from $24.9K in May to $40.4K in July, Meta-attributed revenue from $41K to $168K. To be straight about July, 59% of spend was a 50%-off sitewide sale, retargeting returned 7.92x, and cost per purchase rose from $171 to $267 while CPM nearly doubled. The sale and warm audiences carried the peak.

A US replacement-parts store at about $15K a month, a fix-first account rather than a scale one: I fixed the audit findings (ads were set to every language), retired six small inherited campaigns, and launched one new sales campaign with fresh creative. No single change gets the credit for what followed: cost per tracked purchase moved from about $28 in the inherited January to $18.73 in April, CPM flat near $8, then $21.43 in May.

**How I judge creative**

Building and selling my own e-commerce brand taught me the ad that stops the scroll and the ad that sells are often different ads. So I judge each creative on cost per purchase within one product, retire on a set rule, not feel, and brief new angles from what winners share.

**Google and billing**

Google sits with our Google specialist, with both platforms in one live dashboard and a written read every two weeks. I don't bill hourly. Management is 20% of each platform's monthly spend, stepping down at higher spend, with a $1,500 monthly minimum per platform, plus one-time $1,500 onboarding per platform covering a full account audit.
