Extended stay sold as weekly and monthly housing runs straight into Meta's Housing special ad category. That one matters, because inside it Advantage+ loses detailed targeting and lookalike audiences and gets forced to a 15 mile minimum radius. Reaching travel nurses or work crews as distinct audiences stops being something the platform will sell you at all.

Worth separating out the credit piece, since it usually gets conflated: Meta's credit category applies to businesses offering credit products, so it does not catch you. What matters is that building the offer around housing need rather than lodging is exactly what pushes the classification toward housing. Whether this trips comes down to how the page and the ads are worded, and that call has to be made before anything gets built rather than after a rejection. Google has its own housing restrictions and they are lighter, you keep radius targeting and remarketing that Meta takes away. Not zero, but not the same problem.

Performance Max is the riskier first move at a small budget in a small market. We run a Virginia asphalt contractor's account where Search and PMax ran in the same city across overlapping months on comparable spend. Search leads came in at $131. PMax leads came in at $289. That is one time-matched comparison rather than a law of physics, but the structural reason is worth knowing: PMax blends Search in with Display, YouTube, Gmail and Maps and reports the whole thing as one number, so you lose the ability to see which of them actually produced a booking. In Osceola the demand is someone typing "extended stay Osceola" or "weekly rate hotel I-35." Search puts the budget on that and nothing else.

The reporting you asked for is the real blocker, and it is worth being blunt about. Occupancy and booking impact instead of clicks requires call tracking and a booking conversion in place first. A 35-room independent takes most of its business by phone and walk-in, and none of that shows up on its own. Without it there is nothing to report except clicks dressed up to look like something more, which is usually why owners end up getting exactly that.

One targeting note: contractors and work crews are often booked in blocks by a foreman or a staffing coordinator rather than by the worker scrolling Instagram. That is a B2B buying motion, and it changes how the Meta creative would need to read if we run it at all.

Given the budget language, I would start on Google Search only rather than both platforms at once. It halves the management cost, it puts every dollar against people already searching, and Meta can come in once the housing classification question is settled and tracking is actually proving occupancy. Our rate is 20% of ad spend per platform with a $1,500 monthly minimum per platform while spend is under $5K, plus a one-time $1,500 onboarding per platform.

Two comparable local accounts. A naturopathic clinic in Sacramento, Google Search only, running about $1,350 a month and producing 100 conversions at $40 over the last 90 days. And the asphalt contractor above, which is the source of the Search and PMax comparison. We have not managed a hotel, and I would rather say that than imply otherwise. What we do know is small-budget local lead generation.

Roughly what range are you thinking monthly, under a thousand, one to three thousand, or higher? It decides whether one platform or two makes sense to start.
