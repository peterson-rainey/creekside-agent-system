Does your AudienceView checkout run on its own domain, separate from your main website? I ask because that one detail decides whether Meta ever sees a ticket sale. If the pixel only lives on your site, Meta learns from people who clicked "Buy Tickets," not people who paid, and it quietly optimizes toward browsers. Getting the purchase event, with the order value, firing from the checkout itself is the first thing I would check, before touching a single ad.

**What I've seen with ticketed launches**

With 10 to 20 shows a quarter, the other trap is giving every performance its own campaign. Each one ends up with too few sales for Meta to learn from, so everything stays stuck in learning. The accounts I've seen hold up keep a steady season-level campaign built on past buyers and lookalikes of them, then add a short push for each show as its date gets close, with the creative doing the show-specific work. A short clip from rehearsal usually beats a polished poster.

I've spent 10+ years in Meta and email, and I built and sold my own e-commerce business, so tying spend to actual revenue instead of clicks is how I think about every account. On one account, the client's contact form didn't talk to their analytics at all. I found it, and they swapped forms the next day so every submission could be tracked. A ticketing checkout on its own domain is the same kind of problem.
