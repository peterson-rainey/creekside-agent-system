## DRAFT

Quick question before anything else: when someone buys, does checkout happen on your own domain, or on a platform like Kajabi, ThriveCart or Stan? For a digital products business that one answer decides how much of your previous campaign data can be trusted. When the purchase happens off-domain, the Pixel often misses it or counts it twice, and Meta ends up optimizing toward whatever event it can actually see.

The reason I lead with it is that on the e-commerce accounts I've worked in, tracking breaks results more often than strategy does. On one consumer camera brand, campaigns optimized to add to cart and checkout were producing purchases at about $311 each, while campaigns optimized straight to purchase, same account, same months, came in around $61. Meta learns from the event you hand it.

1. Experience across both
10+ years on Meta, and I built and sold my own e-commerce business before doing this for clients. I run the Meta side myself. Google on our accounts sits with our search specialists, and our team manages roughly $250K a month across the two platforms, so each half gets someone who works that platform every day.

2. Digital products / e-commerce
My results are physical-product e-commerce, not digital products, and I'd rather say that plainly. Three I can put in writing: a spa parts retailer with roughly $125K of Meta spend over nine months at just under $29 per purchase. A furniture brand scaled from about $17K to just over $40K a month. And the camera brand above, 623 purchases at $93.58. What carries over is the purchase event and the creative testing. What changes is margin: with no shipping or inventory, you can usually afford a higher CPA than a physical brand, which widens what counts as profitable to scale.

3. Launching for an established business
Audit before building. Your previous data shows which audiences, angles and offers already converted, so new campaigns extend what worked instead of starting cold. Nothing profitable gets switched off until its replacement has proven itself.

4. First 30 days
Tracking verified first: Pixel, CAPI, GA4, GTM and Google conversions. On Meta, broad prospecting against a customer-list and retargeting layer, all optimized to purchase, with two or three genuinely different creative angles from your team rather than variations of one. On Google, brand and non-brand Search split apart. Shopping is mostly closed to digital goods, so keyword intent carries most of the weight there.

5. Daily monitoring
Daily: spend pacing, CPA against target, tracking health, disapprovals, and Google search terms. What I deliberately avoid is editing ad sets mid-learning, because a significant change resets Meta's learning and turns a working campaign back into a new one.

6. Personally managing
Yes on Meta, I'm in the account daily. Google is run by our search specialist, and you'd know exactly who that is from day one.

7. Monthly fee
20% of ad spend per platform, with a $1,500 monthly minimum per platform, so $3,000 a month across both until spend passes $7,500 on a platform. The rate steps down to 15% above $30K per platform and 10% above $60K. Onboarding is a one-time $1,500 per platform. Roughly where does your combined monthly spend sit today: under $10K, $10K to $30K, or above that?

On the 30 days: our agreements run a 90-day minimum, then month to month. The first month on a relaunched account is mostly learning phase and the first round of creative, so a day-30 verdict lands before the numbers settle. Better to say that now than at signing.

8. What's included
Strategy, campaign builds on both platforms, keyword and audience research, tracking setup and audit, daily monitoring and optimization, creative angle recommendations for your team based on what the data shows, and weekly reporting with a call whenever you want one.
