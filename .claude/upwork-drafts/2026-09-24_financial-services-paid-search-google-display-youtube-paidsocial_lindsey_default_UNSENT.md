## PROPOSAL

Quick question before anything else: does the product you're advertising fall under credit or lending? That one answer decides what the paid social half of this can actually look like, and it's worth settling first.

Worth saying up front: Search, Display and YouTube aren't mine, and neither are LinkedIn or X. I run Meta and email. On the account this question comes from, a mortgage lender I ran Meta for over about a year, the Google side sat with a specialist I work with, and that's the setup I'd expect here.

Those Meta campaigns sat in a special ad category. Age and gender locked, detailed targeting cut to a short approved list, no lookalikes, no exclusions, and a fifteen mile minimum radius with no ZIP codes. An awareness, consideration, conversion structure that leans on lookalike seeds and suppression lists does not exist in there. What worked instead was letting the copy qualify people: state the product's actual requirement plainly and let the wrong audience filter itself out.

The asymmetry matters for how you split the channels. Google's version of those rules drops age, gender, parental and marital status and ZIP codes, but radius and city targeting stay. So on a regulated financial offer the geographic precision lives on the search side, and social has to earn it through creative and message order.

On the Meta half of that account, April into May of this year went from $82 a lead to $59 while lead volume went 109 to 140, on slightly less spend than the month before. It peaked just under $22,000 a month.

One thing that would shape any recommendation: is monthly paid social spend closer to five figures, or still four?
