## DRAFT

Meta needs roughly 50 purchases per ad set per week to get out of the learning phase. The cheapest cost per purchase we have ever produced on a low-ticket consumer product is just under $10, and that was a repeat-purchase food product with a subscription behind it, which is an easier buy than a first book from an author without a cold audience. Borrow that number anyway, since it flatters your case rather than penalizing it. Fifty purchases a week at under $10 is roughly $400 to $500 a week for a single ad set, so on the order of $2,000 a month, and that buys the ability to read one variable. Comparing an audience against an audience, or a hook against a hook, takes more than one ad set. That's arithmetic, not a forecast. The rest of our low-ticket work sits well above that floor, from the high $20s to the high $80s per purchase.

Which is why the budget isn't really the decision. Margin per copy is. If a copy nets single digit dollars, even our best ever cold number doesn't clear it, and no test spend fixes that. Books that work on paid work because of what sits behind them. If the book alone has to pay for the buyer, that's worth knowing now rather than after 60 days of data nobody can read.

So the number I'd settle before any spend number: what is a buyer worth to you over the following year beyond that first copy, whether that's a list, a second title, a workshop, or coaching?
