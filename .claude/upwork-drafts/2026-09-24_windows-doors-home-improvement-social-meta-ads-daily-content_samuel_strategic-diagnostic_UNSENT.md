## PROPOSAL (paste as-is)

The number most likely to mislead you here is the one the account will report by default.

An account in our book ran exactly the mix you are describing, gutters in Texas, boosted organic posts sitting alongside lead campaigns inside one ad account. Over eleven recorded days in August it spent $5,567.56 for 142,589 impressions. Account level cost per thousand impressions reads $39.05.

Split by what the money was actually buying, the lead campaigns ran $5,363.26 at a $47.77 CPM. The boosted content ran $204.30 at a $6.74 CPM. Roughly 4 percent of the spend bought 21 percent of the impressions, and it pulled the average down far enough that the blended figure understates the real cost of lead inventory by about 18 percent.

That matters for windows and doors specifically, because the cheap half of that account is the half you are asking someone to produce every day. A daily video and a daily post will reliably produce the best looking numbers in the account, every month. If they report into the same line as the lead campaigns, the report gets harder to act on the better the content performs.

The fix is unglamorous. Content and lead generation get separate campaigns, separate budgets and separate reporting lines, and they get graded on different things. Content earns saves, shares and profile visits. Paid earns cost per booked in home estimate. Neither number is allowed to flatter the other.

The second thing worth settling before anyone writes a plan is that cheap attention and cheap customers are different purchases. A paving contractor in our book, homeowner trades, roughly the same shape of sale as a window replacement. Time matched, late November through mid April:

Search: $15,452.44, 1,026 clicks, 107.3 conversions, $15.06 per click, $143.97 per conversion
Performance Max: $14,702.12, 5,375 clicks, 64.7 conversions, $2.74 per click, $227.41 per conversion

Clicks came in 5.5 times cheaper on the channel whose conversions cost 1.6 times more. Anyone grading those two campaigns on traffic cost or engagement would have cut the profitable one. When a single job is worth thousands, that one call is the account.

On your requirements, straight answers.

Meta lead generation in home improvement trades, yes. The gutter account above is the worked example, alongside lawn, landscaping and residential construction accounts sold to homeowners on the same model. Windows and doors specifically, no. The home services results we can document are Google side, including custom home construction where cost per lead moved from $454 to $239 while monthly lead volume went from 15 to 60.

Organic growth and daily content production is the honest gap. There is no organic social growth portfolio to send you and no TikTok account in the book. Producing a video and a post a day is not the work our team does.

So if the monthly salary is buying one person for both halves, I am the wrong fit for one of them and would rather say that now than three paragraphs in. If the paid half can be scoped on its own, that is the half I want, and whoever you hire for content gets better direction from a paid account that already knows which angles convert.

Two things before any of this becomes a plan. What range is the monthly ad budget sitting in today, and is that a ceiling or a starting point? And what does your team currently count as a real opportunity, a measured window, a signed contract, or something earlier than both?
