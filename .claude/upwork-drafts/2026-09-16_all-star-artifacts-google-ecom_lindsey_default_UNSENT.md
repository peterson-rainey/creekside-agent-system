What can you afford to pay for one sale and still make money on it? When I was running the ecommerce business I built and later sold, that number was the only thing that told me whether a new channel was working. Everything else Google reports can look great while you lose money.

The first number that will mislead you is branded search. Most of your spend is on Meta, so a parent who sees your ad on Instagram and later Googles "All Star Artifacts" gets counted as a Google sale. If branded and non-branded sit in the same campaign, the test looks strong and tells you nothing about whether Google can find new customers on its own. Our team splits them from day one. Branded gets its own small campaign and its own line in the report, and the profitability call gets made on non-branded Search and Shopping only.

Here is how the first 30 days would look:

Week 1, tracking and feed. Purchase conversions with real order value, enhanced conversions, and a check that Google and your store agree on order count before any budget goes out. Merchant Center needs extra care for personalized products, since they get flagged when the feed shows a generic image and the page asks for a photo upload. The feed gets organized by sport and product type so Shopping can be split by what actually sells.

Week 2, launch. Search on high-intent terms like "custom football cards," "personalized sports trading cards," and "custom baseball cards for team," plus a Shopping campaign. Tight match types and a negative list built before launch (free templates, printable, card grading, official NFL cards).

Weeks 3-4, search terms reviewed at least twice a week. Collectibles searches drift into hobby and resale traffic fast, and that's where small test budgets disappear. Then a first read on non-branded CPA against your break-even CPA.

Reporting covers actual sales, CPA and profit by sport. It's also worth planning around the season. Team orders spike at season start and around end-of-season banquets, so budget room should be ready for those windows instead of a flat daily spend.

Holding Performance Max back is the right call. PMax will spend on your branded traffic and retarget your Meta visitors, which makes it look like it's working when it's mostly taking credit. It earns a spot once Search and Shopping have a clean non-branded baseline.

The closest account in our book is Aura Displays, a Shopify brand our team built on Google from zero with Search, Shopping and PMax. Branded search runs around 27x ROAS, and non-branded settled around 4-5x while cold spend scaled roughly 4x. When PMax was tested head to head against Shopping, Shopping won (5.47x vs 5.29x), so PMax was paused. That's why proving Search and Shopping first makes sense here.

Across our team we manage around $3.8M a year in Google and Meta spend, including several ecom brands running both platforms at once, which is where you're heading.
