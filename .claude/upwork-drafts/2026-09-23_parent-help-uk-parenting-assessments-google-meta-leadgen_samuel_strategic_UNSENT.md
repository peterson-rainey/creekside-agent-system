## PROPOSAL

In assessment work, the person who searches often isn't the one who instructs. A parent may find you first, but in a lot of cases the instruction and funding come through a solicitor or local authority. So the parent route and the professional route need separate campaigns, pages and forms, and cost per enquiry only means something once each enquiry is tagged by who is instructing.

Channels, and why. Google Search comes first. Solicitors and social workers looking for an independent assessor tend to search by the assessment itself, and that's the highest-intent traffic you can buy. Parents search differently, so they get their own campaigns and wording. Meta's job-title targeting tends to be thin, so for professionals we'd lean mainly on retargeting people who have already visited your site. For parents it's worth testing, with copy that describes the service rather than the viewer's situation. Performance Max waits until tracking is clean, otherwise it optimises toward form fills that never become instructions.

Reducing poor-quality enquiries. Searches around social work commonly pull in job seekers and students, so we'd expect the negative keyword list (jobs, salary, training, courses, free advice) to do real work from day one. The enquiry form asks who is instructing and whether proceedings are underway. That raises cost per enquiry a little and saves your team time on ones that can't go ahead.

Tracking from advert to referral. Forms carry the source of every enquiry into your CRM, and calls from ads run through a Google forwarding number, since that's the only way Google counts them. Each enquiry is marked when it becomes an instructed assessment, and only that outcome goes back to Google, never case details, so bidding learns which searches produce referrals without sensitive information leaving your systems.

First 30 days: audit the current accounts for wasted spend, set up GA4, Tag Manager and call tracking, review your Google Business Profile, and agree ad copy with your team for safeguarding sign-off before anything goes live.
Days 31 to 60: professional and parent campaigns live, search terms reviewed weekly, and separate landing pages for each audience, with turnaround times, assessor qualifications and a referral form for professionals.
Days 61 to 90: instructed-assessment data flowing back to Google, and budget moved toward what produces instructions, not clicks.

Evidence. Our team ran Google and Meta for a US personal injury firm. On Google: US$56.6K spend, 619 conversions at about US$91 each, a 9.6% click-to-conversion rate. Across all channels: 102 cases at about US$950 per case. That account has since ended. For a bankruptcy firm, restructuring the campaigns by market took leads from 117 to 229 and cost per lead from US$86 to US$50. Both case studies are attached. For a dental implant group we ran Meta lead forms into their call centre: US$64.7K spend, 2,558 leads at US$25.31 over 90 days. Our team added qualifying questions, accepted a higher cost per lead, and cut spend when the call centre needed room.

Where we're thinner: our client book is US-based, and we haven't run family-law or social-care services. Local search we handle through Google Business Profile (one client's profile went from position 42 to 13); we don't offer organic SEO as a separate service.
