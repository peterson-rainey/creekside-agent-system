When a business runs Google and Meta together, the most expensive mistake usually isn't inside either account. It's in how budget moves between them. Both platforms take credit for the same customer. Someone sees an Instagram ad, searches your name two days later, clicks a Google ad and converts. Meta counts it, Google counts it, and the reports add up to more conversions than the business actually got.

If budget follows platform-reported CPA or ROAS, it drifts toward whichever platform claims most aggressively, and that is rarely the one creating new demand. Branded search is the usual winner on paper, because it harvests people other channels already warmed up.

So before we'd move a dollar, we'd check three things:

1. Whether each platform is optimizing to the real money event (a sale, a qualified lead, a booked call) or to a shallow proxy like a page view or add to cart that makes the volume column look healthy.
2. How much of Google's reported performance is brand search versus new demand, and whether Performance Max is quietly buying your own brand terms.
3. What the CRM or store says actually happened, compared against what each platform reports. That gap tells you how much to trust each dashboard when splitting the budget.

The same check changes which numbers you judge a campaign on. On a contractor account our team runs, Performance Max bought clicks at $3.00 against $14.53 on Search over the same months at near-identical spend. Search still delivered leads at $131 versus $268. A report that stopped at CPC would have moved budget the wrong way.

On Meta, we ran lead forms into a dental group's call centre that produced 2,558 leads at $25.31 each over 90 days. Getting forms to feed a team that actually calls them back fast is where most of that result came from, not the targeting.
