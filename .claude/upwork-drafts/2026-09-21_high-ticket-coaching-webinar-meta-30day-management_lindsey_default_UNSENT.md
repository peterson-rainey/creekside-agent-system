## PROPOSAL (paste-ready, 340 words / 1,835 chars)

Do you know how much of the webinar the registrants from each ad actually watch? I ask because cost per registration only tells you what a hook costs. Watch time shows whether it promised what the webinar delivers, and an ad that brings in cheap registrants who leave before the offer looks like a winner in Ads Manager and a loser on the sales calendar.

It's also an early, honest signal. Watch time comes in well before sales do, and in far bigger numbers than booked calls, so a new hook can be read before its bookings add up. Reading it by ad means each registrant's ad has to be saved on their contact record, which I'd check first. From there I judge each ad in order: how long its registrants stay, how many book, how many show for the call, then what closes.

The closest funnel I've run is an implant dentistry group whose full-mouth cases averaged $36K to $38K and only closed after a consultation. I ran about $65K through their Meta account in just under three months, with leads going to a call center for booking. I added qualifying questions to the lead forms so people were screened before a rep ever called. For a webinar, the same lever is a question or two on the registration page, judged by what a booked call costs, not what a registration costs.

For new hooks, nothing gets dropped into the ad sets carrying the account, since a new ad puts a working ad set back in the learning phase. Hooks get tested in their own campaign on their own budget and judged on watch time first, then bookings. Budget on the live campaigns moves on that same read.

I run one account at just under $100K a month on Meta right now. Roughly where does yours sit, nearer $5K, $20K or $50K a month? That decides how big a slice testing can take.

There's a short video on my profile that walks through how I run accounts like this.
