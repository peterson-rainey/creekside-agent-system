**1. A paid campaign targeting US physicians or other healthcare professionals**

I haven't run a campaign aimed at physicians or other healthcare professionals, and I'd rather say that than dress up something else. The closest healthcare work I have is patient-facing: Meta lead generation for a dental implant practice, run alongside a colleague. The goal was implant consultations, using Meta lead forms that fed a call center. Over 78 active days ending July 15, 2026, it spent $64,746.78 and produced 2,558 leads on Meta's standard lead event, $25.31 each. The client took it in-house in July, so that's past tense.

I don't have a conversion rate or registration count for it, so I won't give one. On that account, qualifying questions were added to the form and the higher cost per lead was accepted. Separately, the call center couldn't handle the lead inflow at one point, so spend was cut back. It's patient-facing, so it isn't evidence that I know how to reach oncologists, and I don't want it read that way.

**2. Reaching US oncology consultants on LinkedIn and Meta**

I wouldn't build the plan on a profession filter, since I can't count on either platform giving me a clean "oncologist" option. I'd build it so it still works without one. I haven't run LinkedIn Ads, so I'll stick to what I'd do on Meta.

On Meta, I'd start with people your team has already verified. If you have at least 100 verified people, which is Meta's minimum for a lookalike source, they become the seed, and I'd exclude all existing registrants at the ad set level so you aren't paying to sign up members twice. I'd expect consultants, pharmacists and nurses to need different creative, so I'd separate them by ad set and test the professional or interest options Ads Manager shows for this audience against the list-based one. Verified share decides what stays, not clicks or registrations.

Creative would also do some of the filtering. It speaks to clinical practice, not the disease, since the disease draws patients and caregivers. And the registration step asks for what you verify with: role, specialty and license or NPI number. That will lower registration volume, which is why verified share is the number I'd judge by.

**3. Measuring and improving lead quality, not just volume**

I'd want quality defined before spend moves. Here that's a verified clinician in an oncology-relevant role, verified by your team against license or NPI number, plus role and specialty. If that status can be passed back by ad set or creative, I'd report cost per verified HCP next to cost per registration, because the two can point in opposite directions.

With whoever handles your site tracking, I'd send verification back to Meta as a standard event so it learns from verified people, not everyone who registers. Some health-related categories restrict custom events, so I'd confirm what's allowed in Events Manager first. If verified volume is too thin for Meta to learn from, I'd optimize on registration and judge every ad set by verified share, scaling or cutting on that.

To improve quality, I'd add qualifying questions (role, specialty, practice setting) and accept a higher cost per registration for them. I'd also compare Meta's registrations to your registration database before trusting a trend.
