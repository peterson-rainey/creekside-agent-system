Does the qualifying half of your registration form fire its own event back to Meta, or does the whole form count as one conversion? That single choice usually decides whether the room fills with business owners or with people who were simply curious about retirement.

Most event campaigns I have seen send Meta one signal, form submitted, and Meta does exactly what it was told. It finds the cheapest people who will submit a form. The qualifying answers still land in your spreadsheet, so the list reads fine at a glance, but the algorithm never learned which ones you wanted.

I ran Meta for a reverse mortgage lender where we moved optimization onto a pre qualification event rather than the raw lead. The first nine days cost about $2,100 and recorded one conversion, the part nobody warns you about. The next thirty active days came in near $107 across 66, and the seventy after that near $93 across 179. Two caveats before you read too much into that. The account under records conversions, so those are the campaigns where the event fired at all, and the event being counted changed underneath the chart, so it is not a percentage improvement. A cost per lead figure only means something once you know which event produced it, and I want that defined before a seminar goes live rather than argued about after one.

One more pattern from that account. The cheapest qualified leads came from retargeting people who opened the form and abandoned it, around $63 against $93 on the concurrent non retargeting campaign. Warmer audiences should be cheaper, so that is not a win on its own, but with fixed seat counts those abandoners are usually where your last seats come from. You would see that in a live dashboard, not only in the days before an event.

Is the monthly media budget under five thousand, between five and fifteen, or above that? It changes how many seminars are worth supporting at once.
