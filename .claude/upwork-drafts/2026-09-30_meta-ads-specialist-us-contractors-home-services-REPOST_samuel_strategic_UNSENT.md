**Where contractor lead quality slips on Meta**

Instant Forms are usually where contractor leads get cheap on Meta, and also where quality slips, because the campaign learns from whoever fills out the form rather than from who books the estimate. Meta's conversion leads goal is built for that, optimizing toward the leads your CRM marks as qualified, but it only runs on Instant Forms, needs at least 200 leads a month, and wants that qualified stage reached within 28 days. For a single electrician or roofer, 200 a month is often the real constraint, so the setup depends on each account's volume. Under it, qualifying questions on the form and a regular check of who actually booked. Over it, CRM stages sent back to Meta so it bids toward the good leads.

**1 and 2. Home service accounts on Meta**

Most of our home services results are on Google, but two ran real Meta campaigns. A lawn care company in Tennessee got over 1,000 conversions from Facebook ads over twelve months in 2022 and 2023, alongside its Google campaigns. That case study is attached.

The second is a gutter company in the San Antonio area that our team ran on Meta until September. Proven Instant Form ads had their own campaign, separate from new creative tests, financing-offer ads were tested against ads naming specific cities, and Cibolo, San Antonio and Austin ran as separate campaigns rather than one metro radius. I won't quote a cost per lead from it, since the account's recorded conversions don't include the Instant Form leads. It did show how boosted posts can distort a contractor's blended numbers: about $200 of boosted posts pulled the blended CPM down to $39.05, while the lead campaigns were really paying $47.77.

One more that isn't home services but is the same local lead problem: Meta Instant Forms feeding a dental implant practice's call center, 2,558 leads at $25.31 each in under three months. The more useful part was the tradeoffs: qualifying questions went on the form even though they raised the cost per lead, and spend came down whenever the call center needed room.
