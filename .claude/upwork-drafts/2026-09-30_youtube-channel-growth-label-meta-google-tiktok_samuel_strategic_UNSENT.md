Buying a million views is the easy part. The hard part is whether any of those views turn into subscribers and returning viewers, because that's what makes YouTube keep recommending the channel after you stop paying.

**Where I'd be upfront**

We haven't run campaigns for a record label, and TikTok ads aren't something our team manages today. If label experience or hands-on TikTok is the bar, I don't clear it, and I'd rather say so now than have you find out on a call.

**Where we can help**

Our team runs Meta and Google Ads every day, and a lot of growth budgets get wasted in the same few places:

1. Paying for cheap views that drop off in the first few seconds. YouTube counts them, but the algorithm reads them as a signal the video isn't worth pushing.
2. Sending Meta traffic to YouTube and never measuring what happens after the click. That handoff loses a lot of people, and most people running these ads never check how many.
3. Promoting every upload the same way. Usually one or two videos are the right entry point for a new viewer, and the budget should sit behind those.

**How I'd start**

First we'd look at your YouTube Analytics to find which videos already hold new viewers and turn them into subscribers. That becomes the ad inventory. Then we'd test paid views on YouTube against Meta traffic for each of those videos, and judge each one by subscribers and watch time gained per dollar, not raw views.

**One question**

What's your monthly ad budget, and what does "real growth" mean for you in 90 days? For example, is it a subscriber number, monthly views, or streams on a specific release?

Our team can do a lot at different budget levels, but the plan looks very different at $5K a month than at $50K.
