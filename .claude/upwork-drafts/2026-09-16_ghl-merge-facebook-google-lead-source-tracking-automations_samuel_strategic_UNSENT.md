Merging Facebook and Google into HighLevel only improves lead source visibility if HighLevel can tell where each lead came from, and Facebook is the strict side. When a Facebook or Instagram ad sends someone to a landing page, HighLevel only counts it as paid social if the link's utm_source contains fb_ad, lowercase. Tag it facebook or fb and it never shows up as paid social. Instant form leads sync through the Facebook integration, so that rule doesn't apply to them. Google is more forgiving, since the auto-tagged click ID is enough.

Calls and texts land under Others and hand-entered leads under CRM UI, so phone leads need call tracking to tie back to an ad.

We built our own lead intake on HighLevel this way. Click IDs and UTMs are saved to the contact at form submit, before the booking step, and follow-up sequences trigger off pipeline stage changes, not tags, so the pipeline also shows where leads stall.

I'd go tracking first, then the automations, then one dashboard with both platforms' spend next to the pipeline split by lead source, so the ads get judged on what leads do after they come in.

From there, stage changes can go back to Meta through HighLevel's Conversions API action, so Meta can optimize toward leads that move forward instead of form fills. Google takes an extra step. HighLevel's Google Ads action doesn't fire on stage changes, so those updates get uploaded separately and matched on the saved click ID. The tradeoff is fewer, later conversions to learn from, so that step waits for enough lead volume.

Tracking sits with our dedicated conversion tracking specialist and each ad account with a specialist on that platform, while I stay on strategy.

The attached case study, a law firm on Google and Meta, tracks results past the lead, down to cost per case.
