**1.**
The closest match is a two-location dental implant group where our team ran Meta lead forms feeding the practice's call center. We split campaigns by location and by language, added qualifying questions to the form to screen out poor fits, and accepted a higher cost per lead in exchange. When the call center needed room, we cut spend to match what they could handle.

Over 90 days that was $64,746 in spend and 2,558 leads at $25.31 each. In the final 30 days, Spanish-language leads came in at $12.99 against $22.79 for English.

Cost per booking I can't give you. Bookings lived in the practice's call center and CRM, and we never had them tied back to ad spend. That's the gap I'd want to close early, because it's the number that matters.

**2.**
Every offer ends in the same step: the cost estimator. That's the handoff into 1:1 care, and it lets you judge all four offers on one number.

1:1 nutrition therapy: most of the budget. Prospecting plus retargeting, optimized toward completed estimates and then bookings once there's enough volume.

Courses: their own campaigns, optimized to enrollments. Course buyers and course page visitors go into a retargeting audience that sees 1:1 ads, and every course ends with "your plan may cover working with a dietitian directly," linking to the estimator.

Corporate wellness: sold to HR and employers, mostly through outreach, not Meta. Once a company signs, employees get a tracked estimator link, so you can see which employers send patients into 1:1 care.

Physician referrals: not an ad problem first. A simple referral path for the office and a note back to the physician after the first visit. Ads can support that once the path exists.

**3.**
We watch what's actually running, not just what's trending: the Meta Ad Library for competitors and adjacent telehealth brands, the comments on popular nutrition posts to see what people are confused about, and search trends to see what's growing. Claude helps us summarize and sort that, and a person decides what's worth making.

Here's how I'd use one current trend. More people are on GLP-1 medications, and a common worry is losing muscle along with fat. A dietitian clip on what to eat to protect muscle while on one fits that well. The wording matters. Meta doesn't allow ads that assert or imply someone's health condition, so "What people on GLP-1s often miss about protein" works where "Are you on a GLP-1?" gets rejected. The ad would end with checking whether insurance covers visits, and your CEO approves it before anything runs.

**5.**
Claude is our main AI tool. We use it for research summaries, copy drafts, hook and caption variations, and reporting. We don't use AI to generate people, faces or voices.

Patient footage never enters any of our AI tools. It stays in your approved editing software or BAA-covered storage, and only authorized footage gets cut. Transcripts of patient footage count as protected too, so captions for testimonials are written inside the approved tool, not pasted into anything else. The BAA and NDA are signed before anyone sees a file.

On Meta: businesses classified as health and wellness can have health-related signals filtered out, and custom conversions that suggest a health condition may be blocked. So we keep the pixel off pages like your cost estimator, name events neutrally, and send "completed estimate" and "booked visit" server-side with no plan, condition or health details attached. If those events still get limited, we optimize to the strongest signal Meta will accept and judge results on bookings in your system, not on Meta's dashboard.
