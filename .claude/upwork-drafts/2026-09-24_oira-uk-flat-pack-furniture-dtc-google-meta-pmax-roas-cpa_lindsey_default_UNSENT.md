When someone sees one of your flat-pack pieces in a Meta ad, where do they actually buy it: on your website, or on the marketplace listing they already trust for delivery and reviews? I ask because with a strong marketplace presence, website ROAS alone can undersell what the ads are doing. I'd judge Meta on site CPA and ROAS alongside total sales across every channel, so you can see what's really paying back.

One note on scope: I run Meta, not Google Ads, and I'd work alongside whoever runs your Search and Performance Max from day one so both channels are judged on the same numbers.

Two e-commerce accounts I ran on Meta:

1. A replacement parts store spending $12K-$15K a month. In the month before I took it over, cost per conversion was $34.72 at about 5.6x ROAS. By April it was $19.89 at 8.57x with CPMs basically flat, and May held at $22.91 and 6.98x.

2. A luxury furniture brand, the closest match to you. About $105K through Meta over four months, with spend around $24K-$25K a month, then $40K in July, while CPMs nearly doubled. The sales campaigns ran about $92K of that at roughly $275 per conversion. I quote CPA there rather than ROAS because order values weren't recorded on all of that spend.

My audit always starts with tracking, then works outward:
- Is Meta receiving every purchase with its order value, through the pixel and the Conversions API, without double counting?
- How much spend goes to people already on your site versus new buyers, and what each costs.
- Which products and angles actually carry sales, and which are soaking up budget.
- Product pages and checkout on mobile. Flat-pack buyers want dimensions, assembly and delivery answered before they pay.

When I built and sold my own e-commerce business, the lesson that stuck was that bottom-line sales and platform ROAS rarely agree, so I report both side by side.
