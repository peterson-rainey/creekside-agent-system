Q1. What's the largest monthly Meta budget you've personally managed? What was the offer, and how did performance change while you managed it?

The largest is a cosmetic dental practice I run now, at close to $100,000 a month on Meta. The offer is veneers and smile makeovers, sold through Instagram message ads that lead to a booked consult. It was already spending at that level when I took it over, so I won't claim its growth. The biggest scale-up I ran myself was a luxury furniture brand this year, where monthly spend went from $24.9K in May to $40.4K in July. To be straight about July: 59% of that spend was a July 4th sale at 50% off, which returned 4.17x, and CPMs nearly doubled that month, from $42 to $79. So I read July as a sale window, not a new baseline.

Q2. Tell us about a time you caught a problem in an account before the client did. What did you notice, and what did you do?

While setting up lead tracking on one of my accounts, the tracking specialist I work with and I found the landing page leads couldn't be counted. The page was built in Squarespace, but the form inside it came from Constant Contact, and that embed doesn't integrate well with GA4. The client hadn't noticed, because leads were still reaching their email list. There was just no reliable way to count them against the ads. I sent three form options that fire a trackable submit event, and since they wanted to keep Constant Contact for email, I confirmed JotForm feeds leads straight into it and recorded a short Loom walking through it. They swapped the form the next day, and the tracking was set up right after.

Q3. Tell us about a time paid performance changed and the cause turned out to be outside Ads Manager. How did you figure it out?

Last month, a dental practice client messaged that leads had dropped hard over half a week and that Meta had sent one form lead. Meta was showing over 50 form fills, so the gap sat somewhere between the form and their team. I asked whether they were seeing those leads, and they weren't. The leads were reaching their CRM as contacts but never landing in the pipeline their team works from. I pointed them to Leads Center in Business Manager, where they found every one, and they had their manager call them all right away. The tracking specialist I work with escalated it as a top priority and got the lead import fixed.

Q4. You inherit a profitable Meta account. What do you do in your first two weeks, and what do you leave alone on purpose?

The first week is reading, not changing. I sit through the webinar the way a parent would, walk the funnel from ad to purchase, and check that every event fires once and that purchase values match real sales. Then I read results by window, evergreen webinar weeks against launch weeks, because an open cart can make a weak campaign look strong. By the end of week two you'd have a written audit and a testing plan.

What I leave alone is anything that's working. Adding a new ad or changing the bid strategy counts as a significant edit and sends an ad set back into learning, and big budget jumps can too, so winners get tested beside, not inside. I also leave prospecting alone even when retargeting looks cheaper. A mattress brand I run asked me to switch prospecting off for retargeting. Their retargeting pool was still small and prospecting was driving the sales, so I kept it running and will shift budget once traffic builds.

Q5. Tell us about an ads workflow you automated yourself: the problem it solved, what you personally built, and how you checked the output. Where don't you trust AI to make the final call? Also list your normal working hours in Pacific Time.

After a dental implant client rejected a round of ad designs as too generic, I built a review step for every creative. Designer notes go into Claude to write the design prompts, I refine them in ChatGPT, and each finished ad goes back through Claude to check it for consistency before a client sees it. The first run caught missing commas in prices and warranty wording that didn't match, which I sent back to the designer. Nothing goes out until I've read it myself.

I don't let AI decide what a client wants. An AI review of old threads once flagged that a client might object to more than 60 callouts across the approved creatives. Instead of pulling them, I checked with the person who owned that relationship, and they stayed. For your ads, I'd never let AI settle copy about a child's needs. AI drafts reach for "Is your child struggling with...", and Meta's personal attributes policy bars ads that imply knowledge of a person's or their family's health.

My normal hours are 8am to 4pm Pacific, Monday through Friday.
