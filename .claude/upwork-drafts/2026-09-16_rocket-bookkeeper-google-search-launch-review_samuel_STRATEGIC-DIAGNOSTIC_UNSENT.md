The biggest risk in an AI-built bookkeeping campaign usually isn't the headlines. It's that "Shopify bookkeeping" and "Amazon seller accounting" pull a lot of DIY searchers looking for software or a free template, and a campaign optimizing to raw form fills will happily buy those leads. So before touching copy, I'd confirm what a qualified lead means for you (monthly revenue floor, catch-up vs ongoing, number of channels) and make sure the campaign is built to find that person.

How I'd review the AI-written campaign
1. Search intent first. I'd split keywords by what the searcher wants: hiring a bookkeeper, fixing messy books (catch-up), or looking for software. Catch-up searches tend to carry the most urgency and deserve their own ad group, budget, and landing section.
2. Negatives before launch. Software names (QuickBooks, Xero, A2X login), "free," "template," "course," "jobs," "salary," and "how to" terms go in on day one, not after the first week of spend.
3. Match types. Mostly phrase and exact at launch, so search terms can be read before broad is ever considered.
4. Copy. The AI drafts will be generic ("Expert Bookkeeping Services"). I'd rewrite each ad group around what that seller actually cares about: Amazon settlement reconciliation, Shopify payout matching, sales tax by state, books ready before tax season. All within Google's 30/90 character limits, with pinning only where it protects the message.
5. Settings. Search Partners and Display off, auto-apply recommendations and automatically created assets off, location set to presence (not interest) in the US, and a bidding plan that starts manual or Maximize Clicks with a cap until real conversion data exists.
6. Landing pages. Each ad group should land on a page that repeats its promise. A Shopify searcher hitting a general "we do bookkeeping" page is the most common leak I see.

How I check conversion tracking and prevent duplicates
Our dedicated tracking specialist runs this with me. We check the Google Ads conversion list against GA4 imports and the Google tag / GTM setup, because the most common duplicate is the same form counted twice: once as a native Ads conversion and once as an imported GA4 event. Then we submit real test leads for each path (form, booked appointment, call) and confirm each fires exactly once in Tag Assistant and in the account. Booked appointments from embedded schedulers (Calendly, etc.) often need their own event since they sit in an iframe. Calls get a forwarding number with a minimum call length so 10-second hangups don't count. Only the actions that represent a real lead are set as Primary. Anything we can't fully fix gets documented with the reason.
