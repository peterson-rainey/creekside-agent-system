## PROPOSAL (paste-ready)

Do you know what one order leaves you after product cost, shipping, fees and returns? On a wellness brand that number, not ROAS, decides which campaigns are worth scaling, and it is the first thing I would want before touching the account.

I ask because established accounts almost never come to me broken at the campaign level. They come to me with a measurement problem. The most common one on a brand that already has a Pixel and a Conversions API running is purchases counted twice, once browser-side and once server-side, with no deduplication key. Ads Manager then reports a cost per purchase that looks fine while the bank account disagrees, and every scaling decision after that is made on the wrong number. That is a two-day check, and I would do it before a dollar moves.

Two Meta accounts of mine, for shape. A replacement parts store: a little over $120,000 in spend, about 4,260 purchases at roughly $29 each, around 5.5x. A consumer camera brand: about $58,000, 623 purchases at $94 each, where cost per purchase started near $120, fell to about $70 in months two and three, then climbed back above $115 once the warm pool was exhausted. I built and sold my own e-commerce business before this, so I read that second pattern as a supply problem in the audience, not a creative failure.

With creative already in hand, I would consolidate rather than spread: one campaign, broad, three or four hooks against your strongest one or two SKUs, retargeting switched on only once there is a pool worth retargeting. Each new ad gets about two to three times your target cost per purchase to prove itself, and winners get budget in roughly 20% steps while cost per purchase holds under break-even.

One thing worth saying plainly on the cross-platform piece: Meta cannot see an Amazon purchase. The Pixel never fires there, and Amazon Attribution reports after the fact rather than feeding the optimizer, so any spend pushed at Amazon teaches Meta to find browsers. Amazon PPC itself is not my lane. What I can do is keep Meta pointed at Shopify where the event is measurable, and read Amazon lift separately instead of pretending one number covers both.

What range are you thinking for monthly ad spend relative to what you run now? Below roughly $5,000 a month there is not enough weekly purchase volume for Meta to learn, and the testing framework stops being testing. Setup and the audit are a one-time $1,500, and management is the greater of $1,500 a month or 20% of spend, on a 90-day minimum, so "start small and evaluate" works on budget but not on term.
