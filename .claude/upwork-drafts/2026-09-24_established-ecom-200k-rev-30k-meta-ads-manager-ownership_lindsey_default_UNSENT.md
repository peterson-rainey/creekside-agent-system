How many days a month does your cost per acquisition move far enough that someone asks about it? That number is worth knowing before anyone touches the account, because on most accounts at your spend level the majority of those days are noise, and the edits they trigger are what actually costs money.

**What the noise looks like when you count it**

On a replacement parts e-commerce account I ran on Meta, I have 237 days with purchases recorded. Cost per purchase across the whole run was $24.55. Day to day it ranged from $9.62 to $62.18. Thirty-two of those days sat more than 50 percent above the blended number, about one day in seven. Smoothing to a seven day rolling window left ten. None of them needed a change.

**Where the movement actually came from**

Same account. December averaged $506 a day and recorded 11.3 purchases a day at $33.59 each. April averaged $509 a day and recorded 25.6 purchases a day at $18.73. Nearly identical daily spend, more than double the purchases per dollar. Click through rate over those months fell, 1.86 percent to 1.51 percent, and CPM fell from $11.68 to $8.18, so cheaper impressions are part of that picture and I would not hand creative the whole credit. But the months that looked worse on engagement were the months that sold more.

**The three accounts, and what I will not overstate**

Three e-commerce accounts on Meta are mine personally: the replacement parts brand above, roughly $14K a month over nine months; a DTC furniture brand, $105K across the four months I had it and $40K in its biggest month; and a consumer camera brand at roughly $13K a month. One of the three sits at the $20K threshold you named. My largest Meta account runs near $100K a month, but it is aesthetics rather than e-commerce, so I am flagging it rather than counting it.
