Hi there,

The fastest ticket gain in your setup is probably sitting in the Ad Grant, and it has nothing to do with Meta or TikTok.

**Why Grant accounts underspend**
Many Grant accounts leave a big share of the $10K unused. Sometimes it's the $2 bid cap, sometimes it's search volume. Maximize Conversions lifts the cap, but only if Google can see real ticket purchases. AudienceView checkout often sits on a separate domain, and if the click isn't carried through that hop, Google sees clicks and no sales, and the Grant stays limited. That handoff is the first thing we'd check, with "purchase" as the conversion rather than a page view.

Two other Grant rules shape the plan. It's Search only, so Display and YouTube have to come from your paid budget. And the account has to average a 5% click-through rate or it risks suspension after two months below it. Show-name and season searches carry that number, so broader "things to do this weekend" terms need to be added carefully.

**How we'd structure the shows**
Not a separate campaign per show. A season-level structure with each show as its own ad group or ad set, so budget can move to whatever is selling slow in the weeks before curtain. The signal for that is AudienceView sales by performance, not clicks, which is why tracking comes first.

- Grant: show-name and genre searches, plus the season itself
- Meta: your buyer list uploaded as an audience and lookalikes of it, retargeting of people who viewed a show page and didn't buy, and purchase events sent server-side so the pixel isn't the only signal
- TikTok and Spotify: tested on the shows with a younger or music-led audience, not spread across everything

We'd also split single-ticket buyers from subscribers and lapsed package holders, and shift creative and bids as each show gets inside three weeks.

**What we'd want to know**
Where the unsold seats sit matters more than the total. Are they spread evenly, or stacked in a handful of shows? And roughly what's your paid budget outside the Grant: closer to $2K a month, $5K, or $10K and up?
