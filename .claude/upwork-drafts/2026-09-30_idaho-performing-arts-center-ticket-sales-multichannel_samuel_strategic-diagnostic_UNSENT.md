Hi there,

The fastest ticket gain in your setup is probably sitting in the Ad Grant, and it has nothing to do with Meta or TikTok.

**Why the Grant usually underspends**
Most arts orgs use a fraction of the $10K because of the $2 bid cap. The way past it is Maximize Conversions, which Google lets Grant accounts bid above $2 on. But that only works if a real conversion is feeding it, and with AudienceView the purchase usually happens on a separate checkout domain. If the click ID doesn't survive that hop, Google sees clicks and zero sales, and the Grant stays capped. Fixing that one handoff is what turns the Grant from awareness money into ticket money.

Two other things worth knowing up front. The Grant is Search only, so Display and YouTube have to come from your paid budget. And Grant accounts need to hold a 5% click-through rate each month or they get paused, which matters a lot when you're launching 10 to 20 new show campaigns a quarter.

**How I'd run 10 to 20 shows a quarter**
Not 20 separate campaigns. A season-level structure with each show as its own ad group or ad set, so budget can move to whatever is selling slow two to three weeks out. The signal for that is AudienceView sales by performance, not clicks, which is why the tracking comes first.

- Grant: show-name and genre searches, "things to do in [city] this weekend", and the season itself
- Meta: past buyers and lookalikes of them, which is where live-event audiences tend to live, plus retargeting of people who viewed a show page and didn't buy
- TikTok and Spotify: tested on the shows with a younger or music-led audience, not spread across everything

**What I'd want to know**
Where the 4,000 unsold seats sit matters more than the total. Are they spread evenly, or stacked in a handful of shows? And roughly what's your paid budget outside the Grant: closer to $2K a month, $5K, or $10K and up?
