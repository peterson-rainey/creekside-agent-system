When your first users open the app, who is already there for them to connect with? On a social app, that says more about whether an install was worth buying than the cost per install does.

On the examples you asked for, Apple Search Ads isn't a channel I run. Our team hasn't run Google App Campaigns or TikTok either, and we haven't set up an attribution tool like AppsFlyer or Adjust. If hands-on Apple Search Ads experience is the bar for this first phase, I don't clear it.

My lane is Meta, and our team's app work is one account, a restaurant rewards app. Like any local deals app, its value depended on how many restaurants had signed up nearby. It ran in two markets on a few thousand dollars a month. When we took it over, the campaigns were optimizing for traffic instead of installs and sending people to a web page instead of the app store. Restructuring the campaigns and testing creative by market and gender brought cost per install from $7.36 to $3.90 on the best campaign, and about $5 blended across 2,662 installs.

A social app has the same dependency, on people instead of restaurants. Search ads reach people who are already looking for an app like yours. Paid social reaches people who aren't looking yet and can put them in the same city or community on purpose, which helps the app feel alive to whoever installs next. That's why I'd run Meta alongside whoever handles Apple Search Ads from launch, not after.

Where are you launching first, and is it one market or everywhere at once?
