When a Google Ads account is already generating results, the useful audit starts at the conversion column and works backward. Search, Performance Max and remarketing will all claim credit for the same customer. PMax picks up branded searches and remarketing reaches people who were already coming back, so a blended return can look profitable while very little of it is new demand. Brand also costs far less to convert than non-brand, whichever channel gets the credit. In one ecommerce account our team runs, branded search converts at about a sixth of the cost of non-brand, so how much brand sits inside a blended number changes what that number means.

That's why the order matters. The first pass is what counts as a conversion (anything counted twice, or a click counted as a customer), how much of the return is brand, and whether PMax is reaching people Search and remarketing would have missed. Bidding and budget allocation come after, because Smart Bidding chases whatever value it's given. If that value is inflated, more budget buys more of the wrong thing, which is usually where growing spend and growing profit split apart.

An example of what an audit turns up: a GLP-1 telehealth company brought us a Google Ads account with six issues, among them duplicate conversion tracking inflating reported results, targeting limited to brand keywords, and a landing page sending people to account creation instead of a lead form. We rebuilt it with deduplicated tracking, category and treatment keywords beyond brand, PMax and remarketing, and a 128-keyword negative list. Tracked ROAS came in at 8.81x after deduplication. The honest caveat is that with conversions double counted beforehand, there was no clean before number to compare against, which is exactly why tracking gets checked first.

For the ongoing part, a monthly review works well next to an in-house team. A Google Ads specialist on our team goes through the account before each call, your team brings what changed, and we push back on anything that isn't earning its spend. I stay involved on strategy.
