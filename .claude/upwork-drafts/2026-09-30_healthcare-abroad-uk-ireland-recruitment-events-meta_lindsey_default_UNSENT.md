Four cities times two professions is eight events, and with three weeks to go, eight separate campaigns will each be too thin for Meta to learn from before the doors open. How the account is structured in the first week matters more than any single ad.

**Structure for a short runway**

I would run one Nursing campaign and one Allied Health campaign, with each city as its own ad set and radius, and the event-specific copy and Eventbrite link in the ads. That keeps the learning pooled by profession while still letting me move budget toward Manchester or Dublin if one fills slower. Retargeting your 19k followers and recent video viewers runs on its own at a small share of spend, because that is where the cheapest registrations usually come from in the last ten days before an event. I would also check at setup whether Meta classes these as employment ads, since that changes how tightly each city and audience can be targeted.

**Tracking past the registration**

Eventbrite can take a Meta pixel, but the purchase fires inside Eventbrite's checkout, so a free registration and a real attendee look the same to Meta. I would set up the pixel with UTMs per city and profession, then upload the check-in list after each event so we can see cost per attendee, not just cost per sign-up. Those are the first two steps of the Ad to Placement journey, and they are the data that tells you which cities are worth a second round.

**Counting leads where they actually land**

On a dental account I run, the client told me leads had dropped for the week. I could see over 50 people had filled out forms and pointed it to where the leads were sitting in Business Manager. The same thing happens with events: registrations, attendance and candidate records live in three different places, and reporting has to reconcile them or the numbers you make budget decisions on will be wrong. Reporting comes every two weeks plus a live dashboard, and I will tell you which creative to film next based on cost per attendee by angle (lifestyle and pay, registration process, what the move actually involves).
