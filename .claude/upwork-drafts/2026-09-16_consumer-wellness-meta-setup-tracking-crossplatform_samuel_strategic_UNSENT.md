## PROPOSAL (paste-ready)

Meta cannot see an Amazon purchase. The Pixel never fires there, and Amazon Attribution reports after the fact rather than feeding the optimizer in real time, so on a brand selling across both, Meta quietly learns to find browsers and the campaigns that actually drove Amazon demand look like the losers. Before any campaign gets built, the call worth making is which storefront is the system of record for paid social. Everything downstream, budget, structure, what counts as a win, follows from that one decision.

The second thing I would check is your event setup, and on an established account it is almost always the same fault. A Pixel and a Conversions API both firing purchases with no deduplication key, so one sale gets counted twice. Cost per purchase in Ads Manager looks healthy, the bank account disagrees, and every scaling decision after that sits on an inflated number. That is a two day check and it happens before a dollar moves.

From there I would consolidate rather than spread. One campaign, broad, three or four hooks against your strongest one or two SKUs, retargeting switched on only once there is a pool worth retargeting. New ads get roughly two to three times your target cost per purchase to prove themselves. Winners get budget in twenty percent steps, and only while cost per purchase holds under break even.

Six years doing this, mostly ecommerce and lead gen. Two Meta accounts for shape: a replacement parts store at about $123,000 in spend, 4,260 purchases near $29 each, around 5.5x. A consumer camera brand at roughly $58,000, 623 purchases at $94, where cost per purchase started near $120, fell to about $70, then climbed back over $115 once the warm pool ran dry. No wellness brand in that list, so I am not going to pretend otherwise.

One tradeoff on starting small. Below roughly $5,000 a month there is not enough weekly purchase volume for Meta to learn, and a testing framework without volume is just guessing slower. Setup and the audit run $1,500 one time, management is the greater of $1,500 a month or 20% of spend on a 90 day minimum.

What range were you picturing for monthly spend, and roughly what share of your sales lands on Amazon today?
