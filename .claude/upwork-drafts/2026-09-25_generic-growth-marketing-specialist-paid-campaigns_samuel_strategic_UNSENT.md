Most accounts that underperform are not losing on bids and they are not losing on budget. They are losing on the conversion event each campaign is optimizing toward, and the account-level report is built to hide which way it is set.

Here is what that looks like with real numbers. One account on our book ran two campaigns side by side last August, same audience pool, same launch day, one set to Engagement and one set to Leads. Engagement spent $1,845.88 and bought 49,760 impressions, nearly twice the reach of the other campaign. It recorded no enquiries at all. Leads spent $1,955.17 on roughly half the impressions and produced 124 of them at $15.77 each. The account rollup reported $30.65 per lead across both, which described neither campaign. Reading the dashboard alone, you would conclude leads cost twice what they actually cost, and you would keep funding the half that was only buying traffic.

One limit I will state rather than let you find it: an engagement objective would not record lead events anyway, and that account had no call tracking, so I cannot prove it drove zero phone calls. That is the point. The measurement gap and the performance gap are usually the same gap.

The second place a blended number lies is geography. A survival gear store on our book ran the US and the UK side by side on Meta for six weeks. Blended, the account read 2.32x. Split by country it was 3.79x in the US and 0.59x in the UK, and the UK was the cheaper country to reach, roughly $27 CPMs against $41. Cheaper to reach turned out to have nothing to do with cheaper to sell in. Nobody looking at 2.32x goes hunting for that.

**What I would do before recommending anything**

Pull the last ninety days and split every campaign three ways: by objective, by geography, and by new customer against returning. Then hand you two numbers most accounts do not have. What a genuinely new customer costs at today's spend, and what that cost does when spend goes up. If the curve stays flat there is real headroom and we press. If it bends, the fix sits upstream in the offer or the creative volume, and adding budget makes the report worse instead of better.

**That the fix lands**

A bankruptcy firm on Google went from 117 leads to 229 while cost per acquisition fell from $86 to $50.29, on campaign restructuring by market. A custom home builder moved from 15 leads a month to 60, with cost per lead going from $454 to $239. A landscaping company ran 28 months at a $50.35 average acquisition cost and 298 percent return and opened new markets during it. That last account is no longer with us, so past tense on it.
