**1. How have you used a CRM (ideally Zoho) to drive segmented email campaigns? Describe a sequence you built and its performance.**

My email work has been in Klaviyo for ecommerce brands, not Zoho, and I don't have a sequence performance figure to give you. What I can show is how I set sequences up, because most of the work is deciding who should stop getting emails.

A welcome series should stop the moment someone buys. A cart reminder should check before every send that the person hasn't ordered or started checkout, otherwise someone halfway through checkout hears from two sequences at once. Whenever a new flow goes into an account, I check it against the exclusions already on the others.

On the CRM side, for a dental practice where I run the Facebook ads, leads go into the practice's GoHighLevel pipeline, and a tracking specialist I work with handles the workflows there. I kept the ad budget steady until leads and those workflows were confirmed in the pipeline, and only raised it after.

For Sunshine Pages and the Institute, the Zoho CRM fields would do the sorting. A provider who claims a listing moves from claim reminders into onboarding, an upgrade takes them out of upgrade offers, seekers and providers never share a sequence, and anyone the founder has just met is held out of automated asks for a set number of days.

**2. Describe a marketing campaign you built for a local business or community-focused brand. What channels did you use and what were the measurable results?**

Two, both on Meta.

Last month, for a local dental practice, I launched an engagement campaign and a leads campaign on the same day in the same account. The engagement campaign got nearly twice the impressions and didn't record a single enquiry. The leads campaign brought in 124 leads at $15.77 each. That's why I judge local campaigns on the action that matters, like a claimed listing or a booked class, rather than reach.

Earlier this year I ran lead ads for a dental implant group whose leads went straight to a call center. Over 90 days it brought in 2,558 leads at $25.31 each on about $64,700 in spend. Campaigns were split by town, Roseville and El Dorado Hills, and by language, and in its last 30 days Spanish-language leads came in at $12.99 against $22.79 in English. The forms carried qualifying questions. Cost per lead ran higher, and it was worth it for lead quality. When the call center couldn't keep up, spend was cut back to what they could handle, which is the same way I'd pace provider outreach around the founder's visits.
