## PROPOSAL

On Meta, the campaign objective usually decides results more than the targeting does, because Meta finds whoever is most likely to take the action you pick, and engagement and traffic campaigns can post great reach and still produce no sales or leads.

Last month a local dental practice our team runs had both side by side, same account, same launch day, similar budgets. The Engagement campaign spent $1,845, got nearly twice the impressions, and recorded not one enquiry. The Leads campaign spent $1,955 and brought in 124 at $15.77 each. Averaged across the account, that reads $30.65 per lead, a number that describes neither campaign.

So setup starts with measurement before any ads go live. The Pixel and the Conversions API both track the action that actually matters to you, whether that's a purchase, a booked call or a form fill, and if leads close later by phone or in a CRM, which ones became customers gets sent back to Meta so it learns from buyers instead of browsers.

Structure stays simple. Fewer campaigns and ad sets, so each one gets enough conversions for Meta's delivery to stabilize. Targeting on Meta is mostly broad now, so the creative does most of the targeting work. We test two to seven ads at a time against one clear goal, keep the winners, and batch changes together, since big edits push an ad set back into learning.

Improving over time mostly means three things: new creative on a steady rhythm before the current ads wear out, budget shifting toward whatever produces customers at the best cost, and reports every two weeks plus a live dashboard you can check anytime. Our book runs from small local accounts like the one above up to a Meta account spending around $99K a month.

Setup is $1,500 one-time. Management is 20% of ad spend with a $1,500 monthly minimum. We work on a 90-day minimum, then month-to-month, because the first month mostly proves the tracking works before there's enough data to say where budget should go.
