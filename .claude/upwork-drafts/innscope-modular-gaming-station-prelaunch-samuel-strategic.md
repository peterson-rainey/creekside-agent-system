## PROPOSAL

Prototype 2 plus a waitlist is the stage where most hardware brands quietly break their own ad account, and it happens through the conversion event. A waitlist signup is a shallow event. Point Meta at it and the algorithm goes and finds the people who are cheapest to talk into a free email submit, which is a very different population from handheld gamers who will put real money down on a modular station. You end up with a big list, a beautiful CPL, and a launch that converts at almost nothing. The fix is deciding up front which event actually predicts a buyer, a refundable deposit or a config-picker completion or a stated pre-order intent step, and treating the raw email as a number you watch rather than the one you bid on.

Related, from our own Meta book: across two consumer-product accounts, everything run on awareness, engagement and traffic objectives spent about $2,850 and recorded zero conversions between them. Those are exactly the objectives that feel right during "validation." They are the ones to stay off.

On proof, straight. The closest shape we have is a consumer camera brand we ran on Meta from February to June, $58,298 spent, 623 purchases at $93.58, where retargeting came in near $71 against roughly $93 on cold traffic. Physical consumer product, social-native buyer, real purchase tracking. Our consumer electronics account sits on Google, not Meta. There is no gaming hardware and no crowdfunding launch in the book, so what you would be buying is method and discipline rather than a handheld case study, and that is worth knowing before you pay for a plan.

Two things I would want before quoting the consultation properly. Target retail price and expected gross margin per unit, since that is what sets your maximum allowable CPL and CAC and there is no honest roadmap without it. And roughly what monthly test budget you are picturing relative to what validation has cost you so far.
