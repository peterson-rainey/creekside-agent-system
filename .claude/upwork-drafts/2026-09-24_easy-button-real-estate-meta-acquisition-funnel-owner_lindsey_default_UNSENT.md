## PROPOSAL
Do you know your show rate broken out by ad, not just overall? That cut separates a booking problem from a qualification problem, and the two have opposite fixes.

The reason I ask is Meta optimizes toward whichever event you hand it. Give it "call booked" and it finds people who book calls, not the same population as people who show up with capital and sign. The angle that books cheapest is often the one the sales floor likes least, and it still wins in Ads Manager unless later stage changes come back to the ad account.

I ran Meta for a full arch dental implant practice, cases at $36,000 to $38,000, everything sold on a consult. 2,558 conversions at $25.31 each on about $65,000 of spend over three months. The number that mattered was none of those. At one point I cut daily budget by more than half because the front desk could not work the volume already there. The ads had not changed.

The other one was a reverse mortgage lender. Meta leads came in around $173. The number the business ran on was cost per funded customer, and that target sat near $2,300. Two different stages of one funnel, and the ad account could only ever see the first. That is why I push pipeline stages back out of the CRM. Otherwise the campaign that wins on the cheap number is the one that gets scaled.

Straight answer on fit: I have not run paid acquisition for a real estate investing program. That mortgage account is the closest thing.

One thing that would shape a budget recommendation: is monthly Meta spend today closer to low five figures, or still four?
