A Spanish speaker learning English and an English speaker learning Spanish buy for different reasons, need different ads, and likely cost different amounts to reach, so the first few pairs you pick tend to set your CAC, your messaging and your channel split.

## Screening answers (QC PASS WITH FIXES, all 6 fixes applied; Blush campaign-level data re-pulled live 2026-09-30)

1. The closest I have is a consumer camera brand with one hero product under $200, sold direct. The camera was already on sale when I started, so what I took from zero was its Meta spend, not the product. Budget went from about $4,800 in month one to about $20,000 in May, roughly $58,000 and about 620 sales over four and a half months. The largest share of sales from February through April came from purchase-optimized campaigns on broad targeting. Over the same weeks, campaigns optimized for add-to-cart or checkout paid more than double per sale. It wasn't a controlled test, but it's why I optimize for the sale from day one. Retargeting brought in about one sale in seven. May spend ran about $8,000 above April and sales rose by fewer than 10, so I raise budget in steps and read each step before the next.

2. I haven't run a subscription on top of hardware, so this is how I'd set it up rather than a result I can point to. I start with what each unit leaves after landed cost, shipping, payment fees and returns, then what the $15 plan nets each month. Until you have real retention, I'd assume it's low and set the first CAC ceiling so the device alone covers most of it. For example, if a unit leaves $40 and the plan nets $12 a month, a $50 CAC is nearly covered by the device and the plan clears the rest in month one. A $100 CAC needs five months of subscription, and only pays back on buyers who stay that long. Then I'd loosen or tighten that ceiling as month-two and month-three retention comes in by cohort. Two things keep the math honest: activation or the first subscription charge sent back to Meta as a server event, so it has a signal beyond the checkout, and first-week setup emails that get people using the speaker, because one sitting in a box never starts paying back.

3. I haven't launched a brand-new category, but the camera brand faced a version of it: a separate device against the camera already in everyone's pocket. Its selfie campaign was one of the cheaper ones in March and April, about $54 a sale, so I'd start the speaker with angles built around one moment of use rather than a feature list. I'd run three or four "why a separate device" angles as their own campaigns, say family screen time, a daily habit that doesn't mean picking up a phone, and speaking practice out loud, and judge each on cost per sale, not views. The explaining happens after the click: a short demo of a real conversation on the landing page, a waitlist email that answers "why not an app" before launch, and a retargeting ad aimed at that exact objection for people who visited and didn't buy.
