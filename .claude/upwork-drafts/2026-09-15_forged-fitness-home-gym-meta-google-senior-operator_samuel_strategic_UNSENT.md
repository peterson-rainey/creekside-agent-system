Revenue with no source attached usually leaks at one of two handoffs. Either the GoHighLevel form or calendar drops the click ID and UTMs when it loads inside your site, or the deal closes weeks later on a Shopify order with no ad session behind it, so it lands unattributed unless the GHL contact carries its source onto the order. That gets fixed before any budget moves, since every later decision depends on knowing which channel actually produces held calls.

On the agency point, we're a small team, not a solo freelancer. A Meta specialist on our team would run your account, the same person running a local cosmetic dentistry and med spa account spending around $95,000 a month on Meta right now. Our conversion tracking specialist owns Pixel, Conversions API, GTM, GA4 and offline imports, and I stay on strategy. We work in GoHighLevel, including our own instance. There's no home gym installer in our book, so I won't claim one.

1. The cleanest before and after is on Google, for an asphalt paving and striping contractor in Virginia. The first two months ran about $3,400 for 7 leads, roughly $480 each. The account runs on phrase match service keywords, a long negative list, and tracking on both calls and forms. From there we replaced the launch Search campaign with one built around construction services, split a second city into its own Search and Performance Max campaigns, and gave striping its own campaign and budget. November through March came to 153 leads on $26,500, roughly $173 each, and March alone brought 62 on $9,100. It hasn't been a straight line. At about $10,500 a month in July and August, cost per lead ran $250 to $300, and since April Performance Max has cost about $490 a lead against $205 on Search.

2. Two Meta campaigns. Prospecting, broad inside the radius with no interest stacking, at roughly 85% of spend, with new concepts added there as new ads so they fight for the same budget. A warm campaign for site visitors, video viewers and leads who never booked, at about 15%. Held calls and closed revenue go back through the Conversions API, and those are what we make decisions on. The event Meta optimizes toward is booked calls from GHL, sent server-side, since it needs a signal that fires more often than held calls do, and it moves to held calls once they can carry it.

Google will likely start at a small fraction of the Meta budget. Search volume for design and install terms inside that radius caps spend, so the number should come from keyword data, not a fixed split. It also optimizes on booked calls, with the click ID stored in GHL so held calls and closed deals import back as offline conversions. Bidding stays on Maximize Conversions until there are enough of those, since Target CPA on a handful of conversions a month tends to choke delivery.

One pushback: three concepts a week against about 20 held calls a month means most concepts never earn a readable held-call number. We'd judge concepts on cost per booked call and hook rate, and read held calls monthly at the angle level.

3. Brand first. It's cheap, it stops competitors picking off people Meta already warmed up, and it shows how many searches Meta is driving. Second, core service intent: home gym design, installation and builder terms, garage gym builds, near-me and city variants, phrase and exact. That's the done-for-you buyer and should hold calls at the best rate. Third, premium equipment intent from people shopping commercial-grade brands or a local equipment store, since plenty of large projects start as an equipment search. It's the riskiest, so it runs with heavy negatives (used, cheap, Amazon, single pieces) and gets cut first if held-call cost climbs. All three use presence-only location targeting, because Google's default also serves people who are merely interested in the area.

Happy to start with the paid audit and Google plan, with the attribution gap as its first section.
