## PROPOSAL

A sample box request from a remodeler with a job starting next month looks identical to one from a homeowner browsing ideas, and Google and Meta will chase whichever is cheaper to get, which is often the homeowner. So the first job is teaching both platforms to tell them apart, or every sample shipped trains them toward the wrong buyer. Meta also has no job-title targeting, so contractor creative has to do the filtering. Install footage tends to hold an installer's attention and lose everyone else.

1. We haven't run a building materials account, and neither example here tracks through to revenue on our side. Closest: a referral software company selling to home service contractors, starting around $2K a month per platform, with Meta building awareness for a category contractors didn't know existed and Google catching the search demand. Inbound leads and ARR both doubled inside six months. And a paving contractor on Google, still running, where Search leads cost $131 against $289 from Performance Max, and the first 90 days ran $298 per lead against $212 for the year. Case study attached.

2. That software launch. Nobody was searching for the product yet, so Meta had to create demand before Google had anything to capture.

3. Remodelers and design-build firms. They buy for a job that already exists, so a sample can become a quote in weeks. Designers would be my second test, but their samples usually turn into orders on a longer cycle than 60 days can show.

4. Google Search on category terms with heavy DIY negatives, Meta retargeting plus contractor prospecting on install video, and outbound by your US sales support to the same segment, with that list uploaded to Meta so emails and ads reach the same people. Our team doesn't run cold outbound.

5. About $8K: $5K Google, $3K Meta. That buys a read on which segment and offer produce qualified samples, not sales volume. $3K is too thin for Meta to optimize on samples, so it starts on leads.

6. Click IDs and UTMs go into hidden form fields and onto the CRM record. Sales moves each lead through qualified, sample shipped, quoted and won with a value, and each stage goes back to Google as an offline conversion and to Meta through the Conversions API. Google only accepts uploads within 90 days of the click, so a long quote cycle needs an earlier stage as the bidding target.

7. Audit, segment and offer pick, tracking built, both platforms live by week two, a creative brief, a prioritized website change list, weekly readouts, and a day-30 call on what to scale or cut. You'd work directly with the specialist running the accounts.

8. Onboarding is $1,500 per platform, covering audit and buildout. Management is the greater of $1,500 or 20% of spend per platform, so $3,000 up front and $3,000 a month at that budget, on a 90-day minimum, since a new pixel is still learning at day 30. Spend, creative production, web development and outbound aren't included.
