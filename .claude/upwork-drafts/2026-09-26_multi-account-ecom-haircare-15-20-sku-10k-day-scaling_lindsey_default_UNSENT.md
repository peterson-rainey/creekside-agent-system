## PROPOSAL

Do you know whether first-time buyers shop a range like that by hair color or by hair problem? Blonde and brunette are one split, dry, volume and frizz are another, and a frizzy blonde sits in both. I ask because that decides the structure: the need people name first becomes the concept, and the catalog finds the exact bottle.

**On the $10K a day requirement**

If $10,000 a day on one account is the line, I don't clear it. The largest account I run spends a little over $3,000 a day, and it's a cosmetic dental practice. The biggest store I ran on Meta, a furniture brand, peaked at about $40,000 a month. I also built and sold my own e-commerce business.

**How I'd structure it**

I'd group the 15-20 products into four or five needs (color, repair, volume, frizz), each with its own concepts but not its own campaign, because splitting products apart divides the data Meta learns from. A replacement-parts store I took over had 232 live ads across 11 campaigns. Six campaigns were off within three weeks, and from March it ran on seven. Cost per conversion went from $34.72 the month before I started to $19.89 in April, with CPM flat around $8, then $22.91 in May. Creative and targeting changed too, so I don't credit structure alone.

**How I'd test and scale it**

New concepts get a fixed budget each in one testing campaign. Whatever holds your target cost per purchase over a set number of sales moves into one Advantage+ sales campaign under the same post ID, so it keeps its comments and likes. I raise budget on winners in steps, keep new concepts coming beside them, and judge each step on new-customer cost per purchase, because returning buyers flatter a big account.

**What I'd need from you**

Creative for each need, filmed on real hair (brassy blonde, faded brunette, frizz on a humid day), since that runs out before budget does. Server-side purchase tracking that lines up with your store's orders. Margin by product, so each need gets its own cost-per-purchase ceiling. A landing page per need, or a quiz that sends people to the right bottle. Email I'd own myself: a reorder reminder timed to when a bottle runs out, since repeat orders are what let a first purchase cost more.
