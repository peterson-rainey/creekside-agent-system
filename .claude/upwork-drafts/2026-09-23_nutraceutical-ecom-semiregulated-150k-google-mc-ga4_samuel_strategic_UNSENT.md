## PROPOSAL

Before anything else I would want your account split brand from non-brand, because on a mature account that one cut usually rewrites the whole performance picture.

An ecommerce account we ran on Google is a clean example. Branded Search spent $25,496 over ten months and returned 28.7x. Unbranded Search spent $27,183 over a near identical window and returned 5.3x. Almost the same budget behind each, and the blended Search figure landed at 16.5x, which flattered the account and said nothing about whether new customers could be bought profitably. CPC was $0.35 on brand against $1.26 on non-brand. After the split, 5.3x was the only number worth optimizing, and every test pointed at it.

The second thing worth checking is whether the campaign type is doing the work it appears to be doing. In that same account, between December and March, Shopping returned 5.9x on $4,336 and Performance Max returned 5.4x on $6,247. They were not live on identical days inside that window, so it is a rough read rather than a clean test, but nothing in it pointed to the choice between the two as the lever. What I would want tested properly on your account is the feed and which products are eligible to serve at all, since on a restricted catalog that is usually where the ceiling sits.

On the compliance side, the pattern with health products is that Merchant Center tends to flag at the category level, so rewriting a product title rarely clears anything, and Search disapprovals usually trace back to the destination page rather than the ad, an ingredient claim or a testimonial one click away. So we read the exact policy named, check the page and the feed before touching the ad, since any edit restarts review, and only then appeal. Google allows three appeals per ad, and when the first one fails we escalate on the support thread to their specialist team rather than burning the other two. We won a Health in Personalized Advertising appeal that way. Also worth knowing: since August, Google weighs an account's policy history when deciding whether to limit impressions across Search and YouTube, so a long disapproval record costs reach, not just time.

Two things to be straight about. On budget, the largest single Google account we have run spent $533,597 over twelve months and peaked at $65,682 in one month. Across both platforms our book has run about $3.8M in the last twelve months. So $150K a month on a single account sits above anything we have handled on Google, and you should hear that from me now rather than in month two. On the niche, our closest live results are a naturopathic clinic: $22,592 and 561 conversions over the last twelve months, around $40 each. That is a small local account, not a scaled store. We signed a supplement brand last month that has only just begun serving, so there is nothing to show there yet, and we have done eligibility diagnostic work for a research peptide brand whose Search and Shopping were both blocked.

Day to day the account would sit with the Google specialist on our team who runs our ecommerce accounts, and I would stay on strategy and the testing plan.
