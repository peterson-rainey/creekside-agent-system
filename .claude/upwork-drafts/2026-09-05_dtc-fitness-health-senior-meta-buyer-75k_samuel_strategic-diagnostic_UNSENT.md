Some of your ads are not failing. They are never getting judged.

At $75k a month in one CBO with one cost cap, the ad set is the unit Meta optimizes, and inside it delivery is allocated by predicted conversion rate before your creatives have data. Three or four ads clear the cap early, absorb most of the budget, and the remaining twenty sit at impression counts too low for the auction to form an opinion. The cost cap compounds it: any ad whose predicted cost sits above the cap gets bid down, so unproven creative is suppressed for being unproven. You are not missing efficiency because the account is too simple. You are missing it because testing and scaling are running on the same surface under the same constraint.

The fix is to split the surface, not to add complexity.

Scaling stays where it is. One CBO, cost cap, only creative that has already earned a place in it.

Testing moves to ABO ad sets, three to five ads each, highest volume bidding with no cap, budget sized so every ad clears a minimum impression volume inside 72 hours. Uncapped bidding on a small budget is what lets a creative prove or disprove itself. Graduation into the CBO runs on a fixed rule, not a feeling.

Under both, an impression floor rule that pulls or isolates any ad that has not accumulated enough delivery in 72 hours, so you stop paying rent on ads nobody will ever read.

Week 1 audit, in order:

1. Delivery concentration. Spend share by ad inside every ad set, plus a count of ads that never crossed statistical relevance. This sizes the wasted budget in dollars.
2. Cost cap forensics. Where the cap is binding versus where it sits idle, by ad set and by hour, and what it has cost you in lost auction volume.
3. Persona and angle overlap. One persona and one angle per ad set is clean, but at this budget the ad sets are likely bidding against each other.
4. Signal quality. CAPI event match quality, deduplication, and whether the event the algorithm optimizes toward is the one that actually makes money.
5. Frequency and fatigue curves by angle, so every scaling decision carries a ceiling with it.

You get findings with dollar figures attached, not a slide deck.
