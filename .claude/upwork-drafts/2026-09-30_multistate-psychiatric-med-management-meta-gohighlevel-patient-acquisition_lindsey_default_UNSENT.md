When a lead books an intake but never shows, where does that person sit in HighLevel right now, and does anything follow up with them?

I ask because the gap between "booked" and "completed" is usually where appointment-based practices lose the most money, and it's invisible if Ads Manager is the scoreboard.

**What I've seen in practice**

On a dental practice I run Meta for, the client told me leads had dropped to almost nothing. Over 50 people had filled out forms. They were sitting in the CRM as contacts but had never been added to the pipeline, so nobody was calling them. Once we found them, the office manager worked the list, and the sync got fixed as a top priority. That account ran at $30.65 per lead last month, but the lesson was that a lead only counts once it lands somewhere a person will act on it.

**Where psychiatry is different**

Meta limits what health-related data sources can send back, and it rejects ad copy that implies something about the reader's mental health. So the real cost per completed appointment has to live in HighLevel pipeline stages, with only neutral, non-clinical signals going back to Meta. Nurture gets built around reminders and rescuing missed intakes, not just the first text.

To be upfront, I haven't run a psychiatric practice yet. My healthcare work is dental, where the booked-to-showed problem looks very similar.
