1. A dental implant group in Northern California. Leads came through an Instant Form with qualifying questions and went straight to their call center. The offer was a consultation for full mouth reconstruction, where a case runs into the upper $30Ks, so a lead that never booked was expensive no matter what it cost. Over my last 90 days on the account, spend was $64,746.78 for 2,558 leads, $25.31 per lead. The last 30 days of that were $14,597.29 for 678 leads, $21.53 each.

What I did on it:
- I checked the practice's CRM for who actually booked a consultation, and kept one campaign running even though its leads cost more, because they were booking. Judged on cost per lead alone, it would have been the first thing cut.
- When the call center fell behind, I pulled spend back to what they could handle. A lead nobody calls quickly is wasted, however cheap it was.
- I broke out reporting by location and language, since the gap was too wide to average. In the last 30 days, Spanish leads ran $12.99 each against $22.79 for English.

2. Before launch: confirm the Pixel and Conversions API both fire on the lead event and share an event ID, so each lead counts once. Set up the form (Instant Form or site form with UTMs in hidden fields) and make sure leads land where your team will actually see them, then test it end to end with a real submission. Build the campaign with few ad sets and enough budget in each to exit learning, since Meta needs roughly 50 results a week per ad set. Load your creative with consistent naming so results trace back to each video.

After launch: daily checks the first week on delivery, disapprovals, and whether leads are reaching your inbox or CRM. I avoid big edits in that window because adding ads or changing the bid strategy restarts learning. After that, weekly optimisation, a creative test each round, and a written update every two weeks. If your direction and the numbers disagree, I'll tell you and show you why.

3. First I'd check whether the leads are bad or just expensive. If they're booking, a high cost per lead can still be the right trade, so I'd ask your team before touching anything. I'd also check Meta's lead count against what actually arrived, since broken tracking can make cost per lead look worse than it is.

Then I'd find where the cost is coming from, one step at a time. High CPMs point to the audience or competition. Low click-through points to the opening seconds of the video. Plenty of clicks but few submissions points to the form or landing page. I'd also look at which ads and placements are taking the spend, because it's often one ad eating the budget.

At day 7 most ad sets are still learning, so I'd change the one thing the numbers point to, not everything at once, and give it time to read.
