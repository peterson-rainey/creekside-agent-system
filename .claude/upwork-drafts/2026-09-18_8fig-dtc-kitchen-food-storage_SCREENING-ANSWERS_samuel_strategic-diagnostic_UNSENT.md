What the account does answer is the problem you actually have, which is a Google account sitting downstream of paid social: over one five month stretch its branded Search returned 27.4x on about $7,500 while non-brand Search ran 4.6x on about $18,000 and non-brand Shopping 4.3x on about $9,800.

**2. Deciding how much to spend on branded search**

Brand is a defense budget, not a growth budget, so it gets sized by what it prevents rather than what it returns. Two inputs. First, exact-match impression share lost to rank and budget on your own name, and who is actually showing there. If nobody is bidding on you, brand spend buys clicks you would have had free, and the reported ROAS on those clicks will be enormous and meaningless. If a competitor or a marketplace reseller is sitting above you, brand spend is cheap insurance.

Second, a holdout, because the first input cannot tell you what you would have kept for free. Turn brand off in a matched set of states for two or three weeks and read total Shopify revenue in those states against the rest. Whatever revenue does not come back organically is the real size of the brand budget.

For a brand where paid social drives the demand, I would also stop treating it as a fixed number. Brand search volume is a meter on your Meta output, so the budget tracks that volume rather than a monthly figure I picked. The rule I hold to is that brand never sits inside a campaign where it can subsidize something else, and it never enters the account's blended number without being labeled.

**3. Bidding strategies by lane**

Brand: Target ROAS set deliberately high, exact and phrase only, with a tight negative list. Intent already exists, so the job is to pay the floor and stay above competitors, not to buy volume. Brand also gets excluded from Performance Max, and I check the PMax search terms report to confirm the exclusion is holding rather than assume it.

Non-brand Search: Maximize conversion value with no target for two to three weeks to gather real data, then Target ROAS at a level the data supports, moved in steps rather than jumps. Worth knowing for planning: a new or reactivated bid strategy, a settings change or adding and removing keywords all put a campaign back into learning, so those changes get batched, not trickled in one at a time.

Shopping: Target ROAS, with the catalog split into separate campaigns by custom label rather than one campaign for everything, because the target is set per campaign. For a hero product in bundles that mostly means separating the bundles that carry margin from the singles and from clearance, so a cheap single cannot absorb budget the bundles should get.

Performance Max: Target ROAS with brand excluded, and new customer acquisition bidding turned on so it values a first purchase above a repeat one. Same reasoning as using a new customer conversion action, which is that on a brand your size an all purchases target quietly teaches the bidder to buy people you already own.

**4. How we charge, and what I would propose here**

Percentage of ad spend, on a monthly retainer. Never hourly, because hourly pays us for effort and the account does not care how long anything took. Not performance based either, since the platform reports the performance it is being paid on, which is a bad arrangement for both of us.

The structure is tiered and marginal, so the rate applies bracket by bracket rather than to the whole amount: 20% up to $30,000 a month, 15% from $30,000 to $60,000, 10% above that, with a cap so the fee stops growing on a large account.
