## Draft

FIGHTER

The most common blind spot in lead magnet funnels is that Meta gets told to find people who download the freebie, because that is the only event the pixel sees. The algorithm gets very good at finding freebie collectors. Cost per lead looks healthy while cost per booked call quietly climbs, and it usually shows up as a thin calendar a month later.

So before building anything new, our team would check what the account actually learns from. If a booked call and a new client can be sent back through CAPI from your calendar or CRM, prospecting can be judged on cost per booked call and eventually CAC, with the lead magnet treated as a way to build a warm pool rather than the finish line. The booking tool has to carry the click ID and the contact details from opt-in through to that event, or Meta can't credit the booking to the ad that earned it. And when high-ticket packages are sold on payment plans, CAC and ROAS only mean something measured against cash collected, not the headline package price.

The second check is whether campaigns with different jobs share one scoreboard. A top-of-funnel story ad and a booking campaign aren't doing the same work, and blending them into one cost per lead makes the whole account read worse than the part that's actually producing. Each stage needs its own goal and its own number, or budget decisions get made on an average that describes nothing.

On creative, testimonial and proof content usually earns its keep in front of people who already opted in or watched a good chunk of the VSL, where the real question is "does this work for someone like me." Watch depth is the easiest way to split that warm pool, so someone who watched three quarters of it sees a different ad than someone who left a quarter of the way in. Cold traffic is where hooks get tested, and your existing organic videos are the cheapest place to start: same body, different first three seconds, one variable at a time, before anyone films something new.

Straight answer on proof, since you asked for it specifically. We don't have a personal training or online fitness coaching account in our book, and we don't have a documented cost per booked call or CAC from a coaching account to show you. What follows is our team's work, not a solo freelancer's portfolio.

Closest coaching example: our team currently runs Meta for a coaching certification company. A weekly webinar with a registration campaign running continuously, instant form lead campaigns alongside it, and prospecting split by angle so budget can't pile onto whichever ad spends fastest. Ads are switched on and off around each webinar date.

Strongest numbers come from a funnel built the same way, ad to lead to a phone conversation: our team ran Meta for a two-location full-arch dental implant practice where every lead went to a call center. $64,700 in spend brought in 2,558 leads at about $25 each over 78 active days. Two decisions there: qualifying questions went onto the forms even though they raised cost per lead, and spend was pulled back to match what the call center could actually get to.

When someone books a call today, does that booking reach Meta as its own event, or does the account only ever see the opt-in?
