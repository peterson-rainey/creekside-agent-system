ATM ads are a strange Google problem because most people searching "ATM" want cash, not a machine. "ATM near me," bank names, "ATM repair," and "ATM technician jobs" can quietly eat a big share of a B2B budget, and those clicks still fill out forms. So the first thing I'd pull is your search terms report for the last 90 days, sorted by spend, to see how much is going to consumer intent before touching bids or copy.

Two other things I'd check early.

Hosting and buying are different buyers. A store owner asking about free placement and an operator buying several machines search differently, close differently, and are worth very different amounts to you. If both sit in one campaign under one bid strategy, Google drifts toward whichever lead is cheaper, which is usually the lower-value one.

Tracking should count qualified locations, not form fills. If the account bids on every submission, it learns to find more of whoever submits. Sending qualified leads back from your CRM gives Smart Bidding something worth chasing.

Consulting, management, or both

For management we don't bill hourly, so there's no rate to quote: it's the greater of $1,500 a month or 20% of Google spend, plus a one-time $1,500 onboarding that includes the audit, so you'd never pay for both. Minimum term is 90 days. A Google Ads specialist on our team runs the account day to day and I set the strategy.
