Your patient cost estimator is the best signal you have and the most sensitive page on your site. Someone entering their insurance plan is telling you they're close to booking, and that's exactly the kind of page a standard Meta pixel shouldn't be reading. Meta also restricts health-related signals for businesses it classifies as health and wellness, so the event you'd most want to optimize toward may not be usable as-is.

The way around it is to keep the pixel off the estimator's inputs and send two clean conversions server-side, "completed estimate" and "booked visit," with no plan, condition or health details attached. Meta then learns from bookings instead of clicks.

**How the four offers fit together**

1:1 nutrition therapy gets most of the budget, since it's where everything else points. Every Academy and corporate touchpoint should end in the same step: check whether your insurance covers visits. That gives you one number to judge all four offers by, cost per estimate that turns into a booking, instead of four separate scorecards.

Telehealth also means targeting only the states where your dietitians are licensed and the plans you're in-network with. Ads outside that footprint produce estimates that come back "not covered," and those are the most expensive leads you can buy.

**The physician referral piece**

Meta has no way to target by job title or employer, so reaching PCPs and HR decision-makers there is mostly guesswork. My read is that referrals are won less by ads and more by making referring easy: a simple referral path for the office, and a short note back to the physician after the first visit. Ads can support that once it exists. LinkedIn is a channel our team has not run for clients, so I'd treat it as a capped test, not a promise.

**What backs this**

Our largest Meta account is a healthcare practice spending around $99K a month. For a two-location dental group, our team ran Meta lead campaigns to 2,558 leads at $25.31 each. For a smaller dental practice, a Meta lead campaign on under $2,000 in August came in at $15.77 per lead. On Google, a naturopathic practice is running around $40 per conversion over the last year.

Where we fit is the strategy, Meta, the tracking, and the testimonial ads: hooks, captions and test variations, with the cut done in your approved tools. If you want one person who films, edits, posts and buys media, we're the wrong shape, and it's better you know that now.
