## PROPOSAL

On a 12,000-SKU catalog, most products never collect enough clicks on their own to prove they're underperforming. Even a product converting at 2% goes 50 clicks without a sale about a third of the time. So pausing product by product mostly measures luck.

You should know where we stand. We haven't managed a catalog anywhere near 12,000 SKUs. Our closest is a spa parts store with about 1,300 products, where our team ran Meta catalog ads, and our current Google feeds are all under 200 items. If proven large-catalog results are the bar, we don't clear it.

Here's how we'd run it anyway. Products with enough clicks get judged individually, against a cutoff built from your own conversion rate. The long tail gets judged in groups, using custom labels for margin tier, price band and sell-through, split into campaigns so each group carries its own ROAS target. Products with no impressions get a Merchant Center check before we push budget at them, since a disapproved product can't serve at any bid. Titles and labels get rewritten in bulk through rules and a supplemental feed, top spenders first, not by hand across 12,000 listings.

One tradeoff on device and geo. Once campaigns run on target ROAS, Google doesn't apply those bid adjustments, apart from excluding a device entirely, and PMax takes none at all. Leaning into a region means its own campaign or a conversion value rule. Search terms get reviewed on a schedule across Search, Shopping and PMax, and clearly irrelevant queries become negatives. Our tracking specialist would also confirm GA4 and Google Ads aren't double-counting the same order.

On a Shopify store our team built from scratch, non-brand Shopping ran at 5.66x and PMax overall, not split by brand, at 5.64x over the same 69 days. On your account we'd separate brand on both sides before deciding how much of the catalog belongs in PMax.
