Pixel and GA4 will both count a discovery call form as a lead, but neither one knows which of those calls turned into a website project. For an agency selling custom builds, that's where budget leaks: the campaigns learn to find anyone who books a call, including people shopping for a template site.

So the audit starts there. Cost per booked call only exists if the calendar booking fires as its own conversion, separate from the form. On Google, booked calls and won projects can then be imported back against the original click, as long as they come in within 90 days of it. On Meta, the booked call goes in as its own event through the pixel and Conversions API, so Meta optimizes toward calls instead of form fills.

What I'd test first: a starting price in the ad and on the page, because in custom web design the cheapest lead is often the one who can't afford the project, and a price screens them out before they reach your calendar.

First dollar goes to Google Search. Someone searching for a custom website designer already has a project in mind. On Meta you're creating the need, which works better once the pixel has booked calls to learn from, so I'd start Meta with retargeting and add cold audiences with your portfolio work after that.

Two lead gen examples from our team: a personal injury law firm on Google Ads at about $10,500 a month, 619 conversions at about $91 each, with leads going to intake before becoming cases (case study attached). And a two-location dental implant practice on Meta, about $65,000 over three months, 2,558 leads at $25.31 each into a call center. We cut daily spend by more than half there because the call center couldn't keep up, not because leads got expensive. Both accounts have since ended. We haven't marketed a web design agency, so I can't show you one.

I've been in paid ads for six years. A senior account manager on our team runs builds, bids and testing day to day, and our dedicated tracking specialist handles GA4 and conversion setup, so when tracking breaks it gets fixed.

On terms: a new booked-call conversion is still learning at day 30, so a 30-day read would judge it mid-setup. We work on a 90-day minimum, then month to month. The fee is a percentage of spend rather than a flat retainer: 20% up to $30K a month, $1,500 minimum per platform, plus a one-time $1,500 onboarding per platform that covers the audit.

Where does starting monthly spend land, closer to $5K or closer to $20K? That decides whether Meta runs from month one or waits.
