## PROPOSAL

Google Ads for an 8-Figure DTC Brand

The trap in an account like yours is not structure, it is the bidding. Smart Bidding buys whatever converts cheapest, and on a Shopify catalog sitting downstream of an in-house Meta program many times the size of the Google account, the cheapest conversions are the ones already paid for. So the account gets more efficient on paper every time your in-house team scales, and the reported ROAS becomes a measure of Meta's month rather than Google's contribution. Most accounts at your size are quietly optimizing toward harvesting.

Where we stand, so you can weigh it. We have not run Google Ads for a kitchen or food storage brand, and our largest Google ecommerce account spends well under fifty thousand a month. Your Meta spend is larger than our entire Meta book, so I am not going to offer to teach your team anything about Meta.

Three things I would separate in the first month.

Brand and non-brand into their own campaigns, with a brand exclusion on Performance Max and the PMax search terms report checked weekly to see whether it holds. Until that split exists, no account number means anything. On an ecommerce account our team built from scratch, over the same five months, branded Search returned 27.4x on about $7,500 while non-brand Search ran 4.6x on about $18,000 and non-brand Shopping 4.3x on about $9,800. Same account, same period. A blended figure would have read near 9x and told you nothing about whether the cold spend was worth keeping.

Second, a new-customer conversion action instead of all purchases. At your price point, repeat buyers and brand searchers carry a much cheaper cost per order than a first purchase, so an all-purchases target teaches the bidder to chase the people you already own.

Third, a geo holdout. Hold Google non-brand out of a matched set of states for three or four weeks and read total Shopify revenue in those states against the rest, not platform-reported conversions. That is the only way to price Google honestly when Meta is creating the demand, and it is the number I would want before recommending you move budget in either direction.

On Demand Gen, our record is mixed and you should have it before deciding. Across eight accounts we have run about $42,000 through it for roughly 440 conversions, and the results that carried it were Discover, Gmail and remarketing on a finance account that has since churned. The one time we ran Demand Gen on an ecommerce account it spent a hundred dollars and converted nothing. Demand Gen also runs on YouTube and Discover feeds, which is close enough to your Meta placements that the first question is not whether it performs but whether it adds anything your team is not already reaching. I would answer that with the same geo holdout before scaling it.

On working closely, what that looks like in practice is that every change comes with the reason and the number that triggered it, and a monthly session where we go through the account together and you get to argue with the logic. Learning Google means learning why bids move, not seeing a report.

Is the efficiency target the account is held to measured on Google-reported ROAS, or on blended contribution?
