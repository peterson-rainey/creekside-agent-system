## Cover letter

CRASH. When a buyer scrubs a lead, does the reason come back to you tied to the state and the ad it came from?

I ask because a return rate on its own doesn't tell you which ad to cut. "Already has a lawyer" returns point at who you're reaching, "accident too old" returns point at a hook pulling in people from years back, and those need different fixes. Most of my lead gen work has lived in that gap between what the platform counts as a lead and what the person on the other end accepts, on Meta for dental and cosmetic practices and a reverse mortgage lender.

To be straight on your first requirement: I have not run MVA campaigns myself. The personal injury work on our team was an Arizona firm on Google and Meta, in English and Spanish, with Google taking most of the spend. The numbers are in my answers below. It was one state and the firm kept its own cases, so it doesn't speak to per-state buying or scrub.

Where my own background carries over is policy. Cosmetic and dental ads get reviewed hard for copy that suggests you know something about the viewer's body, and "were you hurt?" style accident hooks run into the same wall. I check hooks against that before anything launches, because a disabled account costs more than a weak ad. After 10+ years, the accounts that hold up are the ones where the ad angle and the first quiz screen make the same promise.

On the trial, two weeks is enough to wire up return reasons and read the first few states, but not for cost per qualified lead by state to settle, so I work on a 90-day minimum.

My answers to your three questions are below. There's also a short video on my profile that shows how I work.

## Screening answers

**Which MVA or personal-injury campaigns have you personally run? Platform, monthly spend, CPL, states, and how the leads were monetised.**

Personally, none in MVA or PI. My own Meta lead gen has been dental and cosmetic practices and a reverse mortgage lender. On our team: a personal injury firm in Arizona only, Google plus a smaller Meta budget, English and Spanish, April to September 2025. Total ad spend across channels was $96,985, averaging about $16K a month, for 102 cases at $951 per case. Google was $56.6K of that at about $91 per conversion. I don't have a separate Meta cost per lead for that account, so I won't quote one. Monetisation: the firm signed the cases itself and did not sell the leads, so this is cost per case for a firm keeping its own leads, not leads a buyer scrubbed, and not a direct read on your model. That account has since ended.

**A funnel is getting clicks but almost no completions. Name the first three things you check, in order.**

1. Clicks vs landing page views, and whether the completion event actually fires. A big gap means load speed, redirects or accidental taps, and "almost no completions" is sometimes a pixel problem, not a funnel problem.
2. Placement, device and state breakdown. Audience Network and some video placements can send clicks with no intent, and one state with a problem can look like a national funnel problem.
3. Step-by-step drop-off in the quiz, starting with whether the first screen delivers what the ad promised. Leaving at question one points to a message mismatch. Leaving at the contact step means the ask came too early or felt like a sales call.

**Which three qualifying questions would you never remove from an MVA form, and why do buyers care about each?**

1. Do you already have an attorney? Represented claimants are usually unsellable, and it's the fastest return reason.
2. Did you get medical treatment, and when? No treatment usually means little case value, and a long gap before treatment is easy for the other side to challenge.
3. When did the accident happen? Filing deadlines differ by state and buyers want recent accidents, so an old date makes the lead unworkable no matter how good the rest is.

Fault is the one I'd fight to keep next, but how much it matters depends on whether the state follows comparative or contributory negligence, so I'd set that with each buyer instead of hard-cutting it on the form.
