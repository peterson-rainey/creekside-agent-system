A sleep apnea clinic in Montreal has a Google Ads leak most reviews never look at: Google decides who sees your ad by the language their Google account is set to, not the language they typed. Plenty of francophones run Google in English and the reverse, so a campaign set to French only quietly skips a share of French searches, while an English campaign with any broad match in it will happily serve English ads against "apnée du sommeil" queries. The fix is targeting both languages in every campaign and letting the keywords do the split, one campaign per language, each landing on a page in that language.

The second thing to check is what the account is optimizing toward. For a clinic that only sees patients on site, the conversion should be a phone call, a booked consultation, or a directions click from the location asset, not a page view or a catch-all "all conversions" bucket. If the bidding is pointed at the wrong event, every other setting is being tuned against noise. Same with location settings: the default includes people interested in Montreal, not just people in it, and for an in-store clinic that is pure waste.

On creative, the Collège des médecins rules keep testimonials and superlatives out of physician ads, which is fine, because the ads that work for a diagnosis-and-treatment clinic name the symptom, the test, and what happens at the visit. Most sleep clinic ads talk about CPAP before the person knows they need one.
