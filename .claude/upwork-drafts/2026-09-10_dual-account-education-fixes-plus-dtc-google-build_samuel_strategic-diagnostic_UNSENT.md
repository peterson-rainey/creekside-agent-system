Six diagnosed problems in one account is not really six problems. At least three of them are Smart Bidding inputs, and the order you fix them in decides whether you can read anything afterward.

The tracking template goes first, alone, and nothing else moves for a couple of weeks. Renaming gclid to a custom parameter does not just misattribute a session, it can break the click identifier the conversion is stitched back to, which means the bidding model has been learning from a partially blind dataset. Every CPA in that account is currently an artifact of that. If you reweight the conversion actions in the same week, you have changed the goal and the measurement at once and you will never know which move did what. Worse, you will be judging the new weighting against baseline numbers the template was corrupting.

On the two conversion actions, reweighting is usually the wrong instrument. Equal weighting is not really the bug; counting is. As long as both actions count as one conversion, a tCPA strategy is being asked to hit an average of two different products. The fix is assigning actual values and moving to value-based bidding, so budget chases the open day signup because it is worth more, not because you told the algorithm to prefer it. The honest tradeoff: if open day signups are thin, a value strategy on sparse data drifts badly, and you may be better off isolating the two into separate campaigns with separate goals and accepting the loss of pooled signal. Which way that goes depends on the monthly conversion count, and I would want to see it before recommending either.

The geo layers are the one I would be least dramatic about. Four overlapping layers in one campaign mostly produce a union that is not far off the widest layer, so stripping the postal codes usually changes reporting more than it changes delivery. The setting that actually moves money is Presence versus Presence or Interest. That one silently buys traffic outside the country entirely, and it is the reason exclusion lists get bolted on and then drift apart between campaigns.

Some numbers from our own accounts, because they cut against a tidy story. Across our active Google accounts over the past twelve months, Performance Max ran $559,678 across 52 campaigns at $37.64 per conversion, and Search ran $550,473 across 103 campaigns at $77.57. Blended, that reads as an easy argument for Performance Max. It is not one. In a naturopathic account, same account, Search came in at $36.65 and Performance Max at $50.25. In an asphalt account over an identical twelve month window, Search was $190.38 and Performance Max $272.77. Same direction twice, opposite the book average. The blend is being carried by a handful of accounts with different goals, and quoting it at you as a recommendation would be exactly the kind of polished number that does not survive being checked.

For the from scratch build, the closest thing I can show you is a consumer electronics ecommerce account our team started on Google last November. Search has run $53,239 for 1,769 conversions at $30.09, Shopping $22,947 for 251.7 at $91.18. Performance Max ran $6,632 for 67.8 at $97.78 and we switched it off in July because Shopping was doing the same job cheaper.
