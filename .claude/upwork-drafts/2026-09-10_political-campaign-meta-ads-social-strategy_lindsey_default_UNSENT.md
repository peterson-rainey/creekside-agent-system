Before anything else: what is the spend supposed to produce between now and election day, a signup you can follow up with, a donation, or name recognition? Those are three different builds, and only the first gives Meta something it can reliably optimize toward.

The reason I ask is an account our team runs where the blended cost per lead looks alarming and describes nothing real. One registration campaign produces nearly all the signups at a steady cost, while about ten thousand dollars sits in top of funnel campaigns that have recorded zero conversions for months. Average those and you get a number true of neither. On a campaign with a fixed end date that money is gone in a way you cannot get back, so separating persuasion budget from capture budget is the first thing I would do here.

Two things specific to this category. Political and social issue ads need advertiser authorization and ID verification before a single ad can run, a "Paid for by" disclaimer on every placement, and seven years in the public Ad Library where your opponent can read them. Authorization gates launch, so if it is not done it belongs on the calendar today, not after the creative is built. It is not restricted the way housing, employment and credit are, though. Lookalikes stay available and geo and interest layering survives, so targeting is stronger than most people expect.

Straight on the gaps: I have no political campaign in my portfolio and no fundraising results to show you. What I have is lead capture at volume. A dental group I ran took 2,558 form submissions at $25.31 each, and we pulled spend back to match what the call center could handle, not because cost rose. Worth asking on your side: once signups arrive, who is actually calling them?

Paid Meta and email is what I run. Organic posting is not.

Are you closer to a few thousand a month in ad spend, or well past that?
