Most of what looks like custom audience saturation in December is two problems arriving at once, and they need opposite fixes. One is the auction. Across the Meta accounts our team ran last year, the cost per thousand impressions ran close to 40% higher in November and December than in October on similar spend, then fell back by January. Nothing in those accounts broke. The other is the same few thousand supporters seeing the same ads again and again. Shrinking audiences fixes the second and makes the first worse, because a smaller audience in an expensive auction only gets more expensive.

A straight answer on your requirement: we haven't run Meta for a charity or a fundraising drive. What we do have is the part of this brief that decides Christmas, which is Q4 Meta at volume, roughly $200,000 a month (USD) across our team's accounts last Q4. The seasonal numbers above come from those accounts. Our Meta specialist would run this hands-on, our tracking specialist handles Pixel and Conversions API, and I stay on strategy.

How the audit would separate market cost from saturation:

1. Frequency, click-through and donation rate by audience, week by week. If frequency climbs while click-through and donations fall on the same audience, that audience is saturated. If costs rise across every audience at steady frequency, it's the market, and the answer is budget timing, not targeting.

2. Overlap between custom audiences. Donor lists, site visitors, video viewers and email subscribers often hold many of the same people, so several ad sets end up bidding against each other for one person.

3. Recent donor exclusions. Someone who gave last week and still sees "please donate" is wasted spend and a worse experience for a supporter. Excluding recent donors, then bringing them back later with a thank-you or a second ask, is usually the quickest saving in the account.

4. Creative before audience. On a warm list, fatigue is often the ad, not the people. Fresh creative can revive a list that looks exhausted on paper.

How I'd structure the Christmas build-up:

- Build new supporter audiences now, while costs are lower, using your donor list as the seed for lookalikes or as a signal for broad targeting. That gives December a bigger warm pool to work with instead of the same one.
- Pass the donation amount through as the conversion value, not just a count of donations, so the account can be optimised on money raised. If you offer regular giving, track a monthly gift separately from a one-off gift, since they're worth very different amounts.
- Hold the heaviest warm-audience spend for the stretch from late November to Christmas, when urgency peaks.

Reporting is a live dashboard you can check any day, plus a written read every two weeks on cost per donation, average gift and which audiences are tiring. Management runs a 90-day minimum, which lines up with a start in October running through the end of December. Your account, audiences, data and creative stay yours throughout.
