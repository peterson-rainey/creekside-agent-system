The first thing worth checking on a landscaping account is not the campaign. It is whether Local Services Ads are running alongside it. On the outdoor contractor account our team runs in Virginia, Local Services has returned leads at $18.07 while search on the same account runs at $192.80. That is six leads on $108 of spend, so it is a small sample and I would not build a forecast on it. But it prices on a different mechanism than the search auction, it sits above the paid results, and for a local home services business it is usually the cheapest lead in the account. In the contractor accounts we get handed, it is more often switched off than running.

Then the search side. Twelve months on that account: $79,094 spent, 373 leads, a blended $212 cost per lead, at $17.37 a click on the core service terms. Outdoor project work is expensive traffic, and a new account does not get many chances to be wrong at that price. That account's first two months ran $3,683 for 8 leads, about $460 each. Month three was $199. That early stretch is mostly search term mining and negative keywords, which is unglamorous and is where the first month of budget is either saved or wasted. A new advertiser should plan for that ramp rather than conclude in week three that Google does not work.

The second thing worth knowing before you spend anything. That account ran Search and Performance Max side by side in one metro, Charlottesville, over the same five months, on nearly identical budgets. Search spent $5,752 and returned 44 leads at $131 each. Performance Max spent $6,090 and returned 21 leads at $289 each. Performance Max clicks were five times cheaper, $2.71 against $14.53, and its leads cost more than twice as much. Google will push a new account toward Performance Max because it launches fast and fills the impression graph quickly. On an account with no conversion history to aim it at, that is a bet I would not make with your first sixty days.

Third is tracking, and it quietly decides everything else. Smart bidding is obedient rather than intelligent. It buys more of whatever you tell it a win is. If the account counts every form fill, every phone tap and every contact page view, Google will go and find the cheapest available person who does those things, and the dashboard will look healthier than the calendar. Landscaping work closes on a call or a site visit, so the phone call and the qualified quote request need to be the money events from day one, with closed jobs fed back in later.

How our team would run it. Week one is tracking and structure, not ads. Conversion actions defined and tested end to end, call tracking live, Local Services submitted for screening in parallel because it takes time to clear, search separated by service line so maintenance is not bidding against install and hardscape work, and geography drawn against the drive radius you actually want to serve. Then launch narrow on exact and phrase, read the search terms daily for the first month, and expand only where there is evidence. Performance Max comes later, if at all.

Landscaping also swings harder by season than most of what we run, so budgets get set against your booking calendar rather than spread flat across twelve months. Spring and fall carry the install and cleanup demand and that is where the money should sit.

On the pages, we would rather change one thing at a time, aimed at the search terms already converting, than hand you a redesign list. Usually it is the quote form and what the page says about service area and timeline.

You would not need to run this day to day. Search terms, negatives and budget shifts happen on our side, and you get the numbers rather than a request to log in and approve things. The account sits with a dedicated Google buyer rather than being handed off after signing, and you get the search terms and the spend breakdown in writing whether or not the month was flattering. For scale, we run about $67,000 a month across our active Google accounts, six years on the platform.

Two things I would want before quoting properly.

What monthly budget range are you weighing, and what can a booked job cost you before the math stops working?

What is the service mix and how far will you travel? Maintenance contracts, one off installs and hardscape carry very different lead values, and that changes the build more than the budget does.
