For a local music school, a qualified lead is usually a parent booking a trial lesson within a short drive, and Google Ads can't tell that apart from someone searching for guitar repair, teaching jobs, or free online lessons unless you tell it. If every form fill counts the same, bidding chases whatever converts cheapest, which is rarely a family that enrolls.

Before changing anything, I'd check three things in your current account. First, the search terms from the last 90 days, and how much spend went to instrument sales, rentals, repairs, jobs, sheet music, or "free" and "online" searches. Second, what counts as a conversion. If page views, form starts, or taps on the phone number are primary, the lead count looks better than enrollments do. Third, the location setting. Google's default also shows ads to people who are only interested in your area, and weekly lessons only work for families close by.

From there, Search campaigns split by instrument, since piano and guitar searches rarely cost the same and a shared budget lets one crowd out the other. Kids and adult lessons get separate ads if you teach both. The form asks instrument, student age and preferred days, so your front desk can tell a real inquiry from a price check, and calls from the ads count only past a minimum length. Budget should also lean into the months families usually sign up, like late summer and January.

The tradeoff is volume. A tighter radius and stricter negatives usually mean fewer leads at first, and each one costs more.

Our closest current work is local service on Google. For a paving contractor our team runs today, the main Search campaign has produced 227 leads at $187 each since last November, while Performance Max in the same account runs $285 per lead. The attached case study covers its best 12 days, when leads came in at $127 each. That's one account, but it's why I'd start a school like yours on Search rather than Performance Max.
