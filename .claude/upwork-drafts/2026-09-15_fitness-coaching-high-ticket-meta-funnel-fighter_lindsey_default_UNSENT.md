## PROPOSAL (paste-ready)

FIGHTER

When someone grabs one of your lead magnets, do you know how many of them book a call within 30 days, and is that booking being sent back to Meta? I ask because a campaign optimized for opt-ins gets very good at finding people who opt in. In high-ticket funnels the cheapest leads are often the ones who never book, and Ads Manager keeps calling that ad set the winner.

I haven't run a fitness or coaching account. The strongest case study I have is a dental implant group, and the part that carries over is the sale itself: a big-ticket decision that only happens after a conversation. Every Meta lead went to a call center to get booked in. Over about three months that account spent $64,747 and brought in 2,558 leads at $25.31 each. That's cost per lead, not cost per booked call.

What I personally did on it: split campaigns by location and ran Spanish-language leads as their own campaign, since they came in at a very different cost. Added qualifying questions to the lead forms so people were screened before they reached the call center, accepting a higher cost per lead for it. And when lead volume ran past what the call center could dial, I pulled spend back to what the team could actually work rather than keep pushing budget.

For scale, I currently run a single dental and med spa Meta account at just under $100K a month. After 10+ years in this, including building and selling my own ecommerce business, the accounts I've seen hold up when they scale are the ones where the ad, the lead magnet and the call all make the same promise. That's the first thing I'd check against your content and testimonials.

I recorded a short video on my profile that walks through how I run accounts like this.
