## Draft

Blended cost per conversion is the number most Google Ads reports lean on, and it is also the number that hides the most. On one ecommerce account we manage, branded and non-branded search run at roughly equal spend, about $25,500 on brand against about $27,200 on non-brand over ten months. Branded converts at $16.90 with a $0.35 click. Non-branded runs $104.25 with a $1.26 click. Averaged into one line, that account looks fine, and you cannot tell that the cheap half is people who already knew the business and were coming anyway. The expensive half is the only part buying new customers. That holds whether an account sells products or books appointments.

Google spend across the accounts we manage came to about $73,800 last month, over 13 active accounts.

Since an account is already in place, the first thirty days start with the review rather than new campaigns, in this order. Whether conversions are counted honestly, because a tag that fires on a page load or twice for the same person teaches the bidding to chase the wrong thing. Then brand pulled apart from non-brand, so the cost of a genuinely new customer becomes visible. Then the search terms that actually triggered the ads, checked against which leads turned out to be real, which is where the negatives come from rather than a pasted list. Then where and when the budget goes, usually the quietest leak. Landing pages are not something we build, though where the ad data points at the page rather than at the traffic, you will hear it. Day to day the account sits with a dedicated Google buyer on our team.

On fees, management is a percentage of ad spend and never hourly, so we are not paid to be slow or to make the account complicated. It is 20% of spend below $30,000 a month, then 15% on the part between $30,000 and $60,000, and 10% above that, with a $1,500 a month minimum per platform. Onboarding is $1,500 one time per platform, and the review above is included in it, not a separate purchase.

We can start as soon as access is sorted. What would sharpen this: the budget range you have in mind, which turns the structure above into a number, and which markets the ads need to run in. Analytics and Tag Manager access tells us what is genuinely tracked today.
