## ANSWER (paste below this line)

Most of my recent work has been exactly this: running Google and Meta on the same account at the same time rather than treating them as two separate jobs.

One example is a dental practice where our team ran a Leads campaign and an Engagement campaign side by side on Meta last month, same account, same audience, launched the same day. The Leads campaign spent about $1,950 against 124 tracked conversions, close to $16 each. The Engagement campaign spent nearly as much and didn't record a single enquiry. Blended together the two would read as one number around $30 a lead, which is what a naive account-level rollup would show you, and it would hide that half the spend bought nothing measurable. Splitting by campaign instead of by account is what catches that.

Beyond that one, we currently have several accounts running Google and Meta together across ecommerce and local-service businesses, so reconciling GA4, GTM, and the Pixel against two different definitions of a conversion is routine for us, not a new setup. That's usually the first week on an account like this, getting the tracking to agree with itself before touching bids.
