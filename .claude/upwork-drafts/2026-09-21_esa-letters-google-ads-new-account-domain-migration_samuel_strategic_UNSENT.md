## PROPOSAL (final, with Integrity attached, 350 words / 2,139 chars)

Importing into a new Google Ads account moves your ESA letter campaigns over, but not the ten years the account has behind it. Google's limited ad serving policy, updated in August, weighs account maturity and policy compliance history when deciding whose impressions to limit, and a new account starts with neither.

Advertiser verification counts too, so we'd start it before launch, and every ad would carry your brand with the new domain pinned to the front of the headline, Google's advice for new advertisers.

Google Ads Editor moves campaigns, keywords and ads between accounts, but not everything around them. Campaigns on portfolio bid strategies land on manual CPC, and no campaign brings its performance history. Conversion actions in the old account stay there, so Smart Bidding has nothing to optimize toward until our dedicated conversion tracking specialist rebuilds them and confirms they fire on the new domain.

Running both at once isn't clean either. Two accounts on the same searches means two ads for one business competing for one spot, which Google's unfair advantage policy prohibits, and once the old domain redirects, any old-account ad still running counts as a destination mismatch. So the switch works best as one planned day, with verification and tracking done beforehand, old campaigns pausing as the new ones go live, then the redirect.

Google puts Smart Bidding calibration at up to about 50 conversions or three conversion cycles, and says past conversion data can speed it up, which a new account lacks. We'd launch looser than the old targets and tighten as its own conversions build.

I've been in paid ads for six years, and our team runs about a dozen Google Ads accounts right now. The attached case study is one of them, a naturopathic clinic running Search, with PMax tested alongside it. The closest recent job was copying a dental practice's veneer campaigns, PMax included, into another practice's account in May.
