Are your catch-up bookkeeping leads worth more to you than your ongoing monthly leads? I ask because when I was running the ecommerce business I built and later sold, the month I finally went looking for a bookkeeper I was three quarters behind, my Shopify payouts never matched the bank, and I would have paid almost anything to get it fixed. The sellers searching "Shopify bookkeeping" in a normal month are often shopping for software or a free template. An AI-written campaign treats those two people the same, and a form-fill goal will happily buy the cheaper one.

That's the lens our team uses on a campaign like this before launch. Catch-up gets its own ad group, budget, and landing section. Software names, "free," "template," "jobs," and "how to" go in as negatives on day one. Match types stay phrase and exact until real search terms come in. Copy gets rewritten around what sellers actually lose sleep over: Amazon settlement reconciliation, payout matching, multi-state sales tax, books ready before tax season, all inside the 30/90 character limits. Search Partners, Display, and auto-applied recommendations get turned off.

On tracking, our dedicated tracking specialist pushes a real test lead through the form, the booking tool, and a call, and confirms each one fires once. The duplicate we find most often is a single form counted both as a native Ads conversion and as an imported GA4 event. Schedulers embedded in an iframe usually need their own event, and calls get a minimum duration so hangups don't count as leads.

The closest project in our book is professional-services lead gen for a law firm, where our team restructured their search campaigns and doubled leads from 117 to 229 while bringing cost per lead from $86 to $50.29.
