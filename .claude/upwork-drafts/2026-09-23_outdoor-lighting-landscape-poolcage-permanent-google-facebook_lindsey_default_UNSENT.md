## PROPOSAL

When the leads dry up, is it all three services at once, or does permanent lighting carry the fall and then landscape and pool cage go quiet?

I ask because those are two different problems. If everything drops together, it's usually the account: one campaign trying to sell three offers, and Facebook settling on whichever one is cheapest to get a form fill for. If it's seasonal, the fix is building demand for landscape and pool cage lighting before the slow months hit, so you're not starting cold every spring.

Lighting is also one of the few services where the ad can show the finished result in a few seconds. A short clip of a house or a screened pool going from dark to lit at dusk tends to do more than any offer line, and it tells Facebook exactly who stops to watch.

The part I watch most closely is what happens after the form. I ran Facebook lead forms for a dental implant practice, another high-ticket service, and brought in roughly 2,500 leads in under three months at about $25 each. Cheap leads weren't the win though. I checked the practice's CRM for who booked a consultation, and kept one campaign running even though its leads cost more, because they were booking. Here I'd want the same view: which leads turn into a design visit and a signed quote, not just a phone number.

After 10+ years of this, including building and selling my own e-commerce business, the accounts that stay consistent are the ones where each service gets its own campaign and its own creative.
