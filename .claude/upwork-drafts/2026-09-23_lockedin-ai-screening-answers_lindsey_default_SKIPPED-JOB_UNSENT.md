3. I would start from the position that the two will not reconcile to your billing data, and that most of the gap is structural rather than a tracking fault.

Google reports its primary conversion numbers on the date of the click that led to them rather than the date the conversion happened, because spend is counted on click date as well. A trial that starts today and converts next week is credited back to today. Meta counts separately on its own side, and its standard attribution credits a click for seven days at most. So both platforms can claim the same subscriber, and the two totals added together will usually exceed what your billing system shows.

That means the anchor has to be the billing side. In practice: one subscription record as the source of truth, a click identifier written against every signup at account creation, and cohorts read by signup date rather than by the platform's conversion date. Platform numbers stay useful for delivery decisions, and the billing data decides what gets kept, cut or scaled.

One constraint worth putting on the table early. Anything pushed back to Google as an offline conversion has to arrive within ninety days of the last click, and sixty three days for enhanced conversions for leads, so paid conversions landing after that never reach bidding at all and have to be judged in your own data instead.

Refunds and cancellations I would net off at the cohort level rather than trying to correct the platform number, since a cancellation landing well after signup will never map cleanly back to an ad.

On the mobile side I would want to confirm how your own reporting handles modeled versus observed conversions before assuming anything, rather than tell you now where it breaks.
