When a buyer searches for a specific watch in week three and the marketplace returns four listings, what happens to that user? That moment, not the cost per install, is what decides whether 200,000 downloads turns into a userbase.

Answering your three questions in order, and the first one honestly.

One. No app past 100,000 installs. The app work in our book is a single Meta account that drove 2,662 installs and moved cost per install from $7.36 to $3.90 through campaign restructuring and creative testing. That is a real result and it is under three percent of your bar, so if the 100,000 threshold is a filter rather than a preference, this is the point to stop reading. Day 7 retention is not a number we have reported on, because the book is lead generation and ecommerce rather than mobile.

Two. Pace them, and pace them against supply rather than against budget. A two sided marketplace with thin inventory has a demand ceiling set by listings, not by spend. Push installs hard and you buy a cohort whose first search returns nothing, and that cohort does not come back when inventory arrives four months later. You get one first session per user and it is the most expensive thing you own. The sequence that survives is sellers first, then buyers into categories that already have depth, category by category rather than nationally. Your store network is the reason this is even affordable, because a seller acquired at the counter with the item already in hand costs a fraction of a seller acquired on Meta, and sellers are the harder half of the two.

Three. The event map, before a dollar. Specifically which in app event you are calling a first transaction, whether it fires identically on iOS and Android, and what your SKAdNetwork conversion value schema currently encodes. Launches routinely discover in month two that the schema was left on the default, which means the only iOS signal available was install, which means the campaigns optimized to install no matter what the objective said. Alongside that, the organic funnel you already have: install to registration to first transaction, by store, for the last 30 days. Without those two things the only number anyone can honestly manage to is cost per install, which is the number you said you do not want to pay for.

Where the gaps are, stated plainly. Apple Search Ads and Google App Campaigns are not channels we run. Neither is TikTok. We have not stood up AppsFlyer, Adjust, Branch or Singular, and app store optimization is not a service line here. What we do run is Meta and Google at scale, roughly $250,000 a month in live spend across the client book, and the retail to app channel you described is closer to offline conversion tracking and creative testing than to mobile attribution, which is work that happens here every week.

The Latin America half of your store network sits outside where we buy media.
