Are you planning to send the ads to your booking page, or use a Facebook lead form and have your team call to book? A lead form gets cheap submissions fast, but Meta learns from whoever taps submit. The booking page usually costs more per lead, but if the campaign optimizes on bookings, it only learns from people who picked a time. Meta's conversion leads goal can steer a lead form toward the leads your CRM marks as qualified, but it needs at least 200 leads a month.

**Before anything else**

Our team hasn't run Meta for a roofing company, so there's no roofing cost per lead to give you. The closest is a gutter company near San Antonio our team ran on Meta until September, but its lead form results weren't tracked in a way I can quote a cost per lead from.

**What carries over**

Ten years in paid social and email, and I built and sold my own e-commerce brand. One dental implant account I worked on took 2,558 leads at $25.31 each in under three months. Those leads went to a call center, and when it couldn't handle the inflow, June's budget was revised down. After a hail event, your inspection crew's capacity can become that same ceiling.

**The first 30 days**

$5,000, about $165 a day, split between a lead form and your booking page, judged on booked inspections rather than cost per lead. Meta wants about 50 results in the week after a significant edit before an ad set leaves learning, so I'd keep your areas together, one ad set per path. Even then, month one reads as directional at this budget.

**Timing and fee**

The aim is first ads live about a week after access, as long as creative approval and the booking tracking test don't hold it up. My fee is 20% of ad spend with a $1,500 monthly minimum, a one-time $1,500 onboarding fee, and a 90-day minimum term, so the 30-day test is month one of three. At $5,000 the minimum applies.
