## Q2 variant A (band)

Here's how we'd run it, in the order that matters.

1. Access and ground truth. Read-only access to Google Ads, plus Analytics and Tag Manager if you use them, and a list of which leads from the last 90 days your sales team treated as real. Everything else gets checked against that list.
2. Conversion tracking. What each conversion action counts, which ones feed bidding, whether calls are recorded, and whether the platform's numbers match your lead list for the same window.
3. Search terms, keywords and match types. Which terms produced real leads versus junk, where broad match is drifting, and how negatives are structured.
4. Bidding and budget allocation. Bid strategy against the conversion volume each campaign actually gets, impression share lost to budget versus rank, and whether each campaign has enough daily budget for at least 10 clicks at your CPCs.
5. Geography, device and schedule. Location settings, clicks from outside your territory, and mobile versus desktop.
6. Structure, ads and landing pages. Whether ad groups, ads and pages line up, plus assets, policy warnings and auction insights.

The account is also scored against our 119-point Google Ads checklist as a backstop.

You'd get a written report that opens with what's working, then ranks every issue as critical, moderate or minor, lists three to five quick wins with time estimates, and lays out a 30/60/90-day plan for the structural and tracking work. We walk you through it on a call or in a recorded video.

Timing is about a week from access to delivery. A standalone audit typically runs $1,000 to $1,500 depending on how many active campaigns you have, and if you hand over management afterward, it folds into onboarding rather than being charged twice.
