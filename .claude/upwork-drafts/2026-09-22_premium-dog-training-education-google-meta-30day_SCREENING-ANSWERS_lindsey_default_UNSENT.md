## ANSWERS

**1. Please share one successful Google or Meta ad campaign you personally managed. What was the goal, approximate ad spend, what did you personally handle, and what measurable results did the campaign produce?**

I managed the Meta account for a dental implant practice with two offices. Its full-mouth cases averaged $36K to $38K and were sold in consultations, so the goal was leads the practice's call center could turn into booked consultations, mostly through Meta lead forms.

From April 21 to July 15 the account spent $64,747, about $23,000 a month on average, and produced 2,558 leads at $25.31 each. Over the last 30 days of that stretch, cost per lead was $21.53.

I ran the campaigns and the day-to-day optimization. I added qualifying questions to the lead forms so each lead arrived partly screened. I split results by office and by language, and the Spanish-language campaigns brought leads in at $12.99 against $22.79 for English over those final 30 days. For newer campaigns I checked Salesforce for who had booked a consultation, and kept one running at a higher cost per lead because its leads were booking. When the call center needed room, I brought spend down to match what it could handle.

**2. How do you approach difficult conversations with customers?**

Calmly, briefly, and never as an argument in public. Someone frustrated in an ad's comments is being read by every other prospect scrolling past, so the reply is written for both of them.

My order is simple. Acknowledge what they said without getting defensive. Answer it if the answer is already in your guidelines. If it touches pricing, their dog's specific situation, a refund or anything program-specific, I say so plainly and move it to a private message or to you, rather than improvise a promise you'd have to keep.

I hide or delete only what is actually spam, abuse or off-topic. A fair complaint stays up with a reply, because a deleted criticism tends to come back louder, and a calm answer under it does more for a premium brand than a clean-looking thread.

Anything I escalate comes to you with the comment, who posted it, what I already said, and what I'd suggest, so you can answer in a minute instead of reading the whole thread.

**3. How do you use metrics to inform your strategy?**

I start by making sure the numbers are real. Before changing anything, I check that the pixel and conversion events are firing and that each result is counted once, because a sudden drop in results is often broken tracking, not a bad ad.

Then I split before I judge. On a local dental practice's Meta account I run, an engagement campaign and a leads campaign ran side by side in August on similar budgets. The engagement campaign got nearly twice the impressions and not one recorded lead. The leads campaign brought in 124 at $15.77 each. Blended, the account read as $30.65 a lead, a number that described neither campaign.

After that, I rank metrics by how close they sit to revenue. For you that means enrollments or booked consultations first, then cost per lead or purchase, and only then click-through rate and CPM, which tell me why something moved rather than whether it worked. Budget follows the top of that list. When a test doesn't work, I say so and what I'd change, since a clear no is as useful as a win.
