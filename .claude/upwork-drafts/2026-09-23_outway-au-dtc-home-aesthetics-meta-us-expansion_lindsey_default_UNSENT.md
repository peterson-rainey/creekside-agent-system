## PROPOSAL

When the US campaigns go live, will you read them separately from the Australian ones, or will both sit inside one account average?

I ask because a survival gear store I run on Meta sent the same ads and offers into two countries over the same six weeks. One returned 3.79x. The other returned 0.59x. Blended, the account read 2.32x, which described neither. The weak one was the cheaper market to reach, $27 per thousand impressions against $41. Cheap attention and a market that buys are not the same thing, and an account average hides that for months.

So a US entry gets its own structure, creative and read from day one, not a budget line inside the account already running.

On the funnel, the first thing I look at is where the drop actually sits. When I took over a home and spa parts store, its history showed click-through rising while the share of clicks that converted fell about 24%, on flat spend. Same money, same ads, and not a traffic problem. That split tells you whether you are buying the wrong people or losing the right ones after the click, and I run it before anyone touches a page.

From my own accounts: that spa parts store came to me near $35 per conversion and was about $20 by April, with CPM flat around $8 throughout, which at least rules out a cheaper auction. A luxury furniture brand I ran spent about $105,000 on Meta over four months, the closest thing in my book to where you are taking Outway.

I built and sold my own e-commerce business, and 10+ years later I still read a Shopify account by what it sells.

Rate is a percentage of ad spend rather than hourly, with a $1,500 monthly minimum. Is the US launch closer to $5,000 a month in spend or closer to $20,000? I work 10am to 6pm Central weekdays from US Pacific time, which reaches the front edge of your Sydney morning.
