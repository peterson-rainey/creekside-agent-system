At $250k a month across Google and Facebook, the most expensive problem is usually invisible at the portfolio level. When spend consolidates into PMax and Advantage+, the blended cost per lead looks stable, so nobody goes looking. The gap only shows up when you isolate it.

We pulled that comparison recently on an asphalt and paving client, residential and commercial mix. Same 12-day window, same account: strict-match Search came in at $131 per lead, Performance Max at $268. Nothing was broken there. In that account, automation was absorbing budget that had a cheaper path sitting next to it, and the account-level average hid it. Whether the same thing is true of your book is an open question until someone looks. On a book your size, that spread compounds fast.

So the first thing our team would want is a per-campaign-type read across the accounts, not a rollup. Where automation is genuinely outperforming, we leave it alone and feed it. Where it is just cannibalizing existing intent, we pull budget back into controlled Search and let PMax work the incremental surface. That is unglamorous work, and it is where most of the recoverable money lives at this spend level.

Worth flagging the tradeoff honestly: restructuring at scale resets learning, and performance dips before it improves. On a client-facing book that is a reporting problem as much as a media one, so it needs sequencing rather than a big-bang rebuild.

On commercials, we price as a percentage of spend, never hourly. It is tiered and marginal (20% on the first $30k per platform per month, 15% to $60k, 10% above), with a $15,000 total monthly cap. At your volume the honest answer is the cap, but I would not quote a final number without knowing the shape of the book.
