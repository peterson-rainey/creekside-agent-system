**1.**
We'd build one season structure, not 20 separate campaigns. Each show gets its own ad group on Google and its own ad set on Meta, sitting under shared campaigns grouped by genre or audience (musicals, comedy, classical, family). That keeps the learning pooled so a new show isn't starting from zero, while every show still gets its own ads, landing page and targeting.

A template handles most of the setup: naming, UTMs, audiences and a creative checklist, so a new show can go live quickly once the assets are in. Budget follows sell-through. Every week we'd compare AudienceView sales by performance against where each show should be for its date, move money toward shows that are behind and still converting, and pull back on shows that are nearly sold out. That's what keeps spend from burning on shows that would sell anyway.

**3.**
We haven't managed a Google Ad Grant account. We run standard Google Ads accounts every day, and these are the Grant rules we'd work within from day one:

- CTR: the account has to average 5%, and two months below it risks suspension. We'd keep the Grant tight: show names, the venue name, the season and genre-plus-city searches, with poor performers paused fast. Broad "things to do" terms get added carefully and cut if they drag the average down.
- Bidding: the $2 cap applies unless you use Maximize Conversions, and that needs real purchases coming back from AudienceView. Getting that tracking right is the first job, and it's what lets the Grant compete for show searches.
- Structure rules: geo-targeting matched to where you serve, at least two ads per ad group, sitelinks, and no single-word or overly generic keywords.

Since the Grant is Search only, Display and YouTube would come from your paid budget.

**4.**
- Start with past buyers. Your AudienceView list uploaded to Meta and Google, split into single-ticket buyers, subscribers and lapsed package holders, each with its own message.
- Plan launches in phases. Before announcement: build audiences and retargeting pools. Announcement week: heavier spend to past buyers and lookalikes of them. Middle stretch: retargeting of show-page visitors who didn't buy. Final three weeks: "last seats" urgency, weighted toward whatever is selling slowest.
- Shift budget weekly by where each show stands against its sell-through target, not by clicks.
- Test creative in small batches: two or three angles per show (the performer, the experience, a review line), with the winner scaled and reused across similar shows.
- Track to the sale. Purchases from AudienceView sent back to Meta and Google, so each show reports cost per ticket against its average ticket price.
