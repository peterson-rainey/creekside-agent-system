## PROPOSAL (paste-ready)

Scaling a Meta campaign that works almost always costs some ROAS, and for a store the hard part is telling whether a drop means the campaign broke or the auction got more expensive. Cut on ROAS alone and you shut off ads that were fine, which controls spend but stalls growth.

That call gets harder from October on. A spa and hot tub parts store we ran on Meta spent about $15,500 a month through last year's Q4. ROAS went from 7.69x in October to 4.30x in December on flat spend, while CPMs rose about 45% and click-through rate rose from 1.59% to 1.86%. People were still clicking, the traffic just cost more. On your account our team would compare each ad to the others running that same week, not to its own September numbers, and only cut the ones still behind after enough spend to judge.

Scaling has its own cost curve. On a Shopify tech accessories brand we run on Google, non-brand spend grew from about $1,900 to $8,800 a month and ROAS came down from 11.56x to 4.81x. Whether that's worth it comes down to margin, so before raising any budget I'd set a break-even ROAS for your store and scale in steps until the next step falls below it.

On Meta I'd start with one broad prospecting campaign where creatives, offers and audience signals get tested, plus retargeting that shows people the products they looked at. Before any of it spends, test orders get checked in Events Manager against the real orders in your store, since purchase data is what Meta learns from. For a store, Google Shopping and branded search usually earn a place before TikTok, and TikTok isn't a channel we currently run for stores, so I'd rather say that up front.

A Meta specialist on our team builds and runs the campaigns, I stay on strategy, and you get regular updates on what's being tested and where budget is moving. We don't bill hourly. Management is the greater of $1,500 a month or 20% of ad spend, plus a one-time $1,500 onboarding covering the audit, tracking and build, with a 90-day minimum.

What margin do you make on a typical order, and is monthly ad spend closer to $5,000 or $20,000? The first sets the ROAS we scale against, and the second decides how quickly Meta gets enough purchases to learn from.
