## Draft

Validating a $4,600 record on $500 to $750 won't tell you your CAC, and it's worth building the test around that instead of against it. At that budget you're likely buying somewhere around 10 to 25 leads and a few calls. That can answer whether serious artists raise their hand at this price. It can't prove what a sale costs.

So the metric for the test is qualified booked calls, not cost per lead. That changes two decisions.

Geography. Five markets on $750 leaves each one with a handful of leads and nothing readable. The artist with $4,600 set aside for one record isn't concentrated in LA or Nashville the way studios are, so I'd run one US audience broad and let the qualification do the filtering. London means a second country and currency on a budget that can't support one yet.

Qualification. The ads and the form should name the investment level up front. It cuts volume and raises what each lead is worth, which is exactly the trade you want at a premium price. Before you finalise the edits, I'd make sure at least a couple of hooks say out loud who this is for and who it isn't, and that the form asks timeline and budget set aside before anyone reaches your calendar.

On your questions. Yes, high-ticket Meta lead gen is most of what our team runs. One dental account we managed sent Meta lead forms into a call team, $64,746 over about three months for 2,558 leads. No music or studio clients, and I won't dress that up. Everything gets built in accounts you own, with pixel and Conversions API tracked through to booked call and sale, and documented so you can see why.

On hours, we don't bill that way. There's a one-time $1,500 onboarding fee covering creative review, tracking and launch, then 20% of ad spend with a $1,500 monthly minimum. At $750 that minimum is double the media, which is why the next question matters.

If the test works, what monthly range would you scale into? A plan for $1,000 and one for $5,000 a month look very different.
