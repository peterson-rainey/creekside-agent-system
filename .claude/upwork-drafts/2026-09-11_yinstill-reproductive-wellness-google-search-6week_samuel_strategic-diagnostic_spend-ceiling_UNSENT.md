## Draft

Attended patients, not clicks.

The gap between those two is wider than it looks, because the booking click is the only event that ever reaches Google on its own. Everything after it happens inside your practice software, and Google cannot see any of it unless you send it back.

So the first thing worth checking is whether Jane's confirmation step can hold a tag at all. On a lot of clinic setups the booking flow sits on a separate domain, which breaks the session and files the booking as a referral rather than as an ad conversion. Even where the tag does fire, a completed booking still is not an attended patient, and the difference between those two is the number you said you would judge this on.

The mechanism that closes the gap is offline conversion import. The click ID gets stored with the booking, attended appointments are exported from the practice system on a weekly cadence, then uploaded back into Google so bidding learns from attendance instead of from button clicks. Our team runs exactly that on a naturopathic clinic account today, with a booked-appointment conversion imported from the client's CRM, sitting around $40 a conversion over the last twelve months. We also run Google, Local Services and Meta for dental practices.

What we do not have is a fertility or IVF clinic, an acupuncture account, a Jane integration or a Canadian client. The naturopathic account is the closest real analogue, and it is the same measurement problem rather than the same vertical.

One tradeoff worth raising before anything gets built. Four campaigns at your daily budget leaves each one with too little data to read on its own inside six weeks. Expect a readable account-level cost per attended patient and a directional read by area, not four separate verdicts. Concentrating Vancouver first buys a cleaner answer sooner.

On the three estimates. Audit, tracking and campaign build is a one-time $1,500. Management is 20% of ad spend with a $1,500 monthly minimum, so six weeks runs about $2,250, and ongoing support continues on those same terms. At $50 a day that minimum sits above your media, which is what makes the last question worth asking.

If the test works, what monthly range would you scale into? Any number we recommend is useless if it lands outside your range, so we would rather hear the range first.
