## PROPOSAL (paste-ready)

Before anything gets built, the thing worth settling is what the campaigns are pointed at, because driving traffic to the site and selling to high earners pull in opposite directions inside Meta.

Here is what that looks like in a real account. I ran Meta for a luxury furniture brand for about ten months, roughly $206,000 in spend. Two campaigns in there were set to a traffic objective. They bought clicks at 17 cents. The sales campaigns in the same account paid 95 cents a click, about five and a half times more. The traffic campaigns show no conversions recorded against them at all.

Nothing was broken. Meta did exactly what each campaign asked. A traffic campaign goes and finds the people most likely to tap a link, and on an upscale product those are rarely the people who were going to spend anything. If the brief is traffic, the traffic will look excellent and the revenue line will not move. So I would point the campaigns at the purchase event from day one and treat traffic as a diagnostic number rather than a goal.

The second thing I would watch is what launching fast usually costs. Meta needs volume through one campaign before its targeting settles, and on a high price point you do not get many conversions a week to feed it. In that same furniture account, nine of the sales campaigns never got past single digit conversions. They spent $19,703 and produced 45 conversions, around $438 each. The seventeen campaigns that reached double digits spent $145,539 for 721 conversions, around $202 each. Spend and volume travel together, so that gap is not proof on its own, but it matches what I see everywhere: launching wide splits the data so thin that nothing ever learns. I would rather start narrow, get one campaign past the point where Meta stops guessing, and expand from there. That is usually faster to revenue even though it looks slower in week one.

On reaching high earners specifically, Meta's income based targeting is limited in most markets now, so the qualifying has to happen in the offer and the creative rather than in the audience builder. Price visible early, the product doing the filtering.

For background, alongside the furniture brand I run Meta for a luxury mattress brand now, about $51,000 in spend and still live, and I ran a replacement parts store through roughly $118,000. I have been doing this for over ten years, and I built and sold my own ecommerce business before I worked with clients, which is why I watch margin as closely as ROAS.

Three things I would need before quoting anything:

What monthly ad budget do you have in mind, and is it closer to five figures a month or low four? The structure I would build is genuinely different at each.

What is the average order value, and what can you afford to pay to acquire one customer? A cost per purchase target with no customer value behind it is just a number someone liked.

Is the pixel or Conversions API firing on the actual purchase today, and has this account run ads before? An account with history is worth keeping. A fresh one is a different ninety days.
