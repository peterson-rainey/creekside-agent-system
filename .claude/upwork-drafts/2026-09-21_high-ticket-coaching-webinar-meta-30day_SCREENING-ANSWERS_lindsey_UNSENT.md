## ANSWERS (paste-ready; Q1 140 words, Q2 195, Q3 207)

**1. Describe a coaching or high-ticket lead-generation account you have managed and the outcomes you measured.**

I managed the Meta account for an implant dentistry practice with two offices. Its full-mouth cases were worth $36K to $38K on average and were sold in consultations. Most leads came in through Meta lead forms, and a call center on the practice's side phoned them to set appointments.

From April 21 to July 15 the account spent $64,747 and produced 2,558 leads, or $25.31 per lead. That was the headline number, not the only one I steered by. For newer campaigns, I looked up each lead in Salesforce to see who had scheduled a consultation. The weekly reports also broke results out by office and by language, and Spanish-language leads ran in their own campaigns at a different cost from English ones. And I put more qualifying questions into the lead forms, so each lead arrived already partly screened.

**2. How do you test new Meta ad messaging without disrupting established campaigns?**

I leave the ad sets your results depend on alone. Meta lists "adding a new ad to your ad set" as a significant edit, and a significant edit puts that ad set back into the learning phase. So new copy gets its own test campaign and budget.

Each test keeps your approved creative fixed and changes only the words: the opening line, the headline or the call to action. Any difference in results then comes from the message, not the asset. I judge a test by how long its registrants keep watching the webinar, then by how many book, not by cost per registration.

A winning hook graduates to the main campaign as a new ad set of its own, never as an extra ad inside a working one. It starts its own learning while the proven ad sets keep theirs. Learning runs per ad set, and Meta's help page confirms that even on a shared Advantage+ campaign budget, a new ad set won't make the others reenter it. Budget on those campaigns rises in steps, since a big change to a campaign budget can push several ad sets into the learning phase at once.

**3. How do booked-call and sales data change your budget decisions when registrations are inexpensive but not all leads convert?**

Cheap registrations only count if the people behind them watch, book and show up. I follow every ad through those steps, as far as your tracking ties each person back to an ad: how far into the webinar its registrants get, how many of them book, how many turn up for the call, and what closes. Budget follows cost per booked call first, since calls come in soon enough to act on within the month, and sales confirm or reverse that read as deals close.

When an ad's registrants are cheap but drop off early and rarely book, it gets reworked first: different opening words, or a qualifying question on the registration page. If bookings still don't follow, it's paused. Where an ad set's registrants cost more but book, its budget goes up in steps.

On an implant practice's Meta account I ran, both leads from a newer campaign scheduled consultations in one week, so I kept it running even though its leads cost more, to see how those people converted. It cuts the other way too. One week I pulled spend down deliberately to match what their call center could handle, and on your funnel, the open slots on your closers' calendars set that same ceiling.
