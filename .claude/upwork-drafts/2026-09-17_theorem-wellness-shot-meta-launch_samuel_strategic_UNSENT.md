## Proposal (paste-ready)

A few hundred creators posting with their own codes means a few hundred possible Partnership Ads, and adding a new ad to a live ad set is the kind of edit that sends it back into Meta's learning phase. Drop creator videos into the launch ad sets as they arrive and those ad sets never settle, so creator content should run in scheduled test rounds, with only proven videos moving into the ad sets doing the selling.

If launch is mid-November, 1,000 orders by January 1 is about 140 a week from every channel, and ads won't get credit for all of them. An ad set needs about 50 purchases a week to leave learning, so the account can support only a couple of purchase ad sets.

I've been in paid ads for six years. To be clear on the gaps: we haven't launched a beverage or supplement, launched a consumable from an empty pixel, or run Partnership Ads with results to show.

1. Attached are redacted screenshots from a consumer camera brand our team ran on Meta, with about $58K spend and 623 purchases, February to June. It's a $129 product and wasn't a cold launch.

2. First 30 days: pixel and Conversions API through Shopify, domain verification, and test orders matched to purchase events before real spend. With no purchase data yet, the October waitlist campaign optimizes for signups and builds launch audiences. Cold campaigns exclude the waitlist, beta group, Instagram engagers and buyers, so cost per new customer stays honest.

3. No Partnership Ads story to tell. A new ad built from a winning video starts at zero likes and comments, so we'd keep scaling the original post. The permission question is whether each creator grants allowlisting, since the ad code alone covers only what they've already published. I'd confirm your Trybe agreements include it before launch.

You'd get a small team rather than one person: a Meta specialist runs Ads Manager daily, a tracking specialist builds the pixel setup, and I lead strategy and your weekly check-in. Our fee is $3,000 a month for Meta spend up to $15,000, rising with spend after that. The two-week audit falls under our one-time $1,500 onboarding fee, and our 90-day minimum fits inside your October to January phase.

How many of the 1,000 orders do you expect creator codes to bring in on their own?
