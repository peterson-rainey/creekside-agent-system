## PROPOSAL

The search terms report in your Google Ads account is the most useful keyword research you'll get for your content, because every query in it has already been paid for, and with conversion tracking in place it shows which ones led to a sale. In most accounts, how-to searches like "how to stop my puppy biting" get clicks and rarely buy a premium program, and your own report will show whether that holds for you. Where it does, they leak budget as ads and are worth writing as articles.

So the paid side and your content can feed each other. Our team would sort recent search terms into three groups: commercial terms that convert (protect and scale), informational terms that get clicks without converting (add as negatives and pass to you as content ideas), and terms with real volume in Keyword Planner that nobody is bidding on yet (test on paid before you spend a month writing for them). You'd get that as a short list with volume and cost per click, not a 400-row export.

On Meta, someone who has read three of your training posts is far warmer than a cold lookalike, and once that audience is big enough to deliver, retargeting them is usually efficient reach.

Before any of that, week one is tracking. If the pixel, Conversions API or Google conversion action has drifted, every other read is wrong, and "delivery dropped" often turns out to be a broken event rather than the ads.
