## Draft

Is Rescented counting a new seller and a new buyer as the same conversion right now? On a two-sided marketplace that one setting decides whether the ad accounts learn anything useful. If every channel optimizes toward installs or signups, the reports look fine while the ads bring in people who browse and never buy or list.

When I ran my own e-commerce brand, I learned the hard way that the cheapest click is rarely the cheapest customer. So on a new account I start with tracking before budgets: which events actually fire, whether first purchase and first listing reach Meta, Google and Reddit as separate conversions, and how Apple Search Ads installs connect to anything after the install.

With 30,000+ users and real organic traction, I'd also want to know early how much of what paid gets credit for would have happened anyway. A simple holdout on the biggest channel answers that faster than any attribution report.

Then the work looks like this:
1. Fix the foundation. Buyer and seller events split, one source of truth for CAC and ROAS.
2. Move budget toward the channels producing first purchases and first listings at a CAC your take rate supports.
3. Test on a schedule. Creative, copy, audiences and landing pages, each with a pass/fail line, reported weekly with what won and what changes next.

On testing, splitting beats blending. On one furniture brand, prospecting was broken out by product line: one lane ran at 2.36x, another at 1.36x, and a chair lane sat at 0.40x and got cut after 11 days. One blended campaign would have hidden the losers.

I work alongside a small team, including a dedicated tracking specialist for pixel, server-side and app event setup. On the app side, our team restructured Meta campaigns for Birthday Club, a consumer app, and cut cost per install from $7.36 to $3.90 across 2,662 installs. For ReferPro, we ran Meta for awareness and Google for demand capture after their seed round and doubled inbound leads and ARR within 6 months.

Roughly where is monthly paid spend today compared to where you want it in six months, and which channel gets the biggest share right now?
