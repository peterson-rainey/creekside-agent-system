Most Google Ads accounts run in-house by a software company are not underperforming because of bids or keywords. They are underperforming because the account is optimizing toward the wrong conversion event, and every setting below that is faithfully executing the mistake.

Here is what it usually looks like. A demo request, a trial start, and a generic contact form each get counted as one conversion in the Google Ads column. Smart Bidding then does exactly what it was told and goes and finds the cheapest version of that event. In software, the cheapest version is a free trial from someone who will never pay, or a form fill from a student, a job seeker, or a competitor doing research. Cost per conversion drops month over month and pipeline does not move. Coaching your team on keyword structure inside that account will not fix it. It will make the account more efficient at buying the wrong thing.

The Analytics side compounds it. GA4 and Google Ads count conversions on different attribution models and different lookback windows, so most in-house teams end up holding two numbers that disagree with no rule for which one governs a decision. Campaigns then get judged on whichever dashboard happened to be open.

So the first block of work is diagnostic, not instructional. Four things, in this order:

1. Conversion actions. Which events are set to primary, which are secondary, whether any of them are tied to actual revenue or seat value, and whether duplicates are inflating the count.
2. The search terms report, read against your ICP rather than against CTR. This is where you find out what Google actually thinks you sell, and it is usually the fastest single fix in a young software account.
3. Campaign type and structure. Whether Performance Max or broad match is quietly absorbing branded searches you would have won for free, which is the most common reason a new account looks like it is working in month one and stops working in month three.
4. GA4 to Google Ads reconciliation, ending in one written rule for which number your team steers by.

You get that back as a written findings document, then we walk it through live so your team hears the reasoning and not just the conclusion. That structure matters more than it sounds: the point of coaching is that the second and third fix get made by your people without us. If it goes to longer term oversight from there, the same document becomes the baseline we measure against.

On our side, our team manages just under $3.84M in ad spend across Google and Meta over the last twelve months, roughly $1.2M of that on Google specifically, across ecommerce, healthcare, financial services, and B2B. We also run acquisition for our own software product, which is the reason the trial quality problem above is not theoretical for us. I have six years in paid search.
