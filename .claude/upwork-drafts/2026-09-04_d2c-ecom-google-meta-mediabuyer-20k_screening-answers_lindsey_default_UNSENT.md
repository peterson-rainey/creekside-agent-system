## Answers (paste-ready)

**1. Kindly share samples of your past work to be considered for this position**

The most useful version of this is a screen share where I pull the accounts up live and you ask about anything on the screen, including the months that went sideways. I would rather do that than send a polished file, and I do not circulate current clients' performance numbers, which is the same protection your client would get from me.

What I can put in writing are accounts that have since wound down.

Luxury furniture DTC on Meta. Scaled from about $17K a month to just over $40K at peak across four months, holding purchase volume while CPM climbed from the mid thirties to the mid eighties.

Spa parts retailer on Meta. 4,426 purchases at $28.93 each over nine months. High repeat, low consideration, very different economics from the furniture account.

Consumer camera brand on Meta. 623 purchases at $93.58 over five months, where retargeting ran about $71 a purchase against roughly $102 on cold prospecting.

On the Google side, our team's e-commerce spend runs closer to $8K a month than $20K. I said that in my proposal and I will keep saying it rather than let you find out later.

**2. How do you use metrics to inform your strategy?**

Two categories, and keeping them separate is most of the job.

Leading metrics tell me what is about to happen. CPM and frequency are the early warning. On that furniture account, CPM moving from the mid thirties to the mid eighties was visible well before cost per purchase moved, and it was the signal to widen the prospecting pool and push creative volume rather than touch bids. By the time CPA confirms a problem, you have already paid for it.

Lagging metrics tell me what to fund. The cleanest example I have is the camera brand. Campaigns optimized to a shallow event, add to cart and initiate checkout, were producing purchases at about $311 each. Campaigns optimized directly to purchase, same account, same months, overlapping audiences, were at about $61. Meta learns from whichever event you hand it, and the shallow ones taught it to find people who browse. Budget moved to purchase optimization and the shallow campaigns came off.

The number I trust least is front-end ROAS, because it is reported by the platform spending the money and it knows nothing about returns or refunds. I want cost per acquired customer after that, and I want the retarget versus prospect split visible at all times, because a healthy blended CPA can hide cold traffic failing while retargeting quietly carries the account.

On a new account, the first sixty days is mostly about making those numbers trustworthy before trying to make them better.
