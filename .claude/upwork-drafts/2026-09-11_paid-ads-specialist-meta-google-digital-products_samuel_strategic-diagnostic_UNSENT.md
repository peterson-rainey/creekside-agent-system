## PROPOSAL

Past advertising data for a digital products business is usually more useful than it looks, but only once it's split apart. Branded Google searches and Meta retargeting tend to claim the same buyers, so a blended ROAS from old campaigns can make prospecting look healthier than it really is. Before launching anything new, I'd separate brand, retargeting and cold traffic in your history, because that's what tells us where new budget should go.

A live example from an ecommerce account we built on Google: branded Search has returned 28.7x on about $25K, while everything non-branded sits at 5.4x on $57K. Both are profitable, but they're completely different decisions, and a blended number hides that.

1. Both platforms: we run eight accounts on Google and Meta at the same time right now. On Meta, a replacement parts ecom brand spending around $13K a month went from 11 campaigns to 7, with retargeting moved off shallow signals like page views onto purchases. Monthly orders went from 349 in December to 665 in April on lower spend, and ROAS from 3.2x to 8.1x. Some of that was Q4 costs easing, though CPM was flat from January to April while the gains kept coming.

2. Honest gap: we haven't run a pure digital products account. Our ecom work is physical goods.

3-4. First 30 days: tracking audit (Pixel, CAPI, GA4, GTM, and deduplication so both platforms aren't counting one sale), then brand vs non-brand Search, prospecting vs retargeting on Meta, and two or three creative angles from your team's assets tested head to head.

5. Spend pacing, tracking health and anything off-pattern get checked daily. Structural changes wait for enough data, since editing every day keeps campaigns stuck in learning.

6. Not me personally. Lindsey on our team runs Meta day to day, and a dedicated Google specialist on our team handles Search and Shopping.

7-8. A percentage of ad spend per platform, 20% to start and stepping down as spend grows, with a monthly minimum per platform and a one-time onboarding fee that covers the audit. Tracking, testing plans, reporting and recommendations are all included. One change on term: we work on a 90-day minimum, because at day 30 new campaigns are still learning and can't tell you yet whether they're working.
