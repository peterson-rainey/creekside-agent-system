## PROPOSAL

Mold remediation and water damage restoration look like one lead-gen business in Google Ads, but they buy very different phone calls. Water damage usually rides an insurance claim, so price rarely comes up and the caller hires whoever answers first. Mold is often excluded or capped by the policy, which makes it closer to a self-pay decision, and those searches mix real remediation jobs with renters, test-kit shoppers and people looking up how to clean it themselves. If both count as a lead, Smart Bidding leans toward whichever call is cheapest to get, and the dashboard looks better than the job board.

So I'd run them as separate campaigns. Water damage is speed: call-focused ads, a schedule that matches when someone can actually answer, and a tight service radius. Mold is qualification: heavier negatives on test kits, DIY and renter searches, and copy that screens for owners with a remediation-sized problem.

Tracking to revenue: every call runs through tracking numbers, recorded, with a minimum call length so hangups never count. Forms capture the Google click ID, stored against the lead in your CRM. Your team marks each lead qualified or not, then inspection booked, estimate sent, and job won with its value, and each stage imports back into Google as an offline conversion. One tradeoff: at launch there won't be enough won jobs for Google to bid on, so bidding starts on qualified leads while reporting runs on qualified CPL, CAC, close rate and revenue by campaign. Google also only accepts imports within 90 days of the click, so insurance jobs get imported at the won stage with the estimate value rather than waiting on the carrier to pay.

There is no restoration, mold or water damage account on our book. Our home-services Google work includes lawn and landscaping, pest control and construction. The closest active account is a Virginia commercial paving and striping contractor our team runs: its main Search campaign has spent about $40,600 since last November for 213 leads at $191 each. In one market last winter and spring, on similar spend, Search delivered leads at $131 while Performance Max came in at $289, even with PMax clicks five times cheaper. The second is a Tennessee custom home builder, where splitting one service line into its own campaign with its own budget and negatives took leads from 15 to 60 and cost per lead from $454 to $239. Neither account reports a qualified CPL, since both stop at the lead, which is the gap the setup above closes. The builder write-up is attached.
