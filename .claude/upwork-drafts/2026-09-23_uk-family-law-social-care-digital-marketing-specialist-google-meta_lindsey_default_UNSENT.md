## Draft

When a referral comes in today, is it more often a parent contacting you directly, or a solicitor, social worker or local authority sending someone your way? I ask because those are two different funnels. Meta is good at finding parents in a local area and weak at reaching professionals, because job-title data on Facebook is self-reported and patchy. A social worker is far easier to reach while searching for a service than while scrolling, so professional referrals are mostly a search job, and parents are where Meta earns its budget.

To be upfront: I run Meta, not Google Ads, and I haven't run accounts in family law, social care or the UK. Google Ads would sit with a specialist I work with, the same setup I have on three accounts I run now, and I work with a tracking specialist on GA4, Tag Manager and conversion setup. Local SEO and Google Business Profile aren't something I'd take on.

The closest thing I've run to your situation is a US dental implant practice, where every enquiry was valuable and a poor one wasted the call team's time. Over 90 days that was $64,747 in spend, 2,558 leads and $25.31 per lead. I added qualifying questions to the lead forms and accepted a higher cost per lead to get them. I checked the practice's CRM for who booked a consultation, and kept one campaign running even though its leads cost more, because they were booking. Spend was cut when the call centre needed room, and UTMs passed through hidden form fields into their CRM so each ad was tied to an outcome. I don't have a clean lead-to-consultation rate from that account to quote you. On a smaller local account last month, an engagement campaign got nearly twice the impressions of a leads campaign on similar spend and not one recorded enquiry, while the leads campaign brought in 124 at $15.77 each. That's why I'd judge this on referrals, not reach.

First 30 days: audit what's running, map how an enquiry becomes a referral, set up tracking so every enquiry carries its source into your records, and agree with your team what, if anything, goes back to the ad platforms, since a parent's details shouldn't leave your systems without a clear basis. Days 31 to 60: add qualifying questions based on which enquiries proved irrelevant, cut what's sending them, and test ad copy that describes the service rather than the person reading it. Days 61 to 90: move budget to what produces referrals and set the cost per genuine referral to scale against.

I won't forecast a cost per lead before seeing your enquiry data; the first 30 days set that baseline. You'd get a written report every two weeks plus a live dashboard you can check any day, covering spend, cost per enquiry, how many were genuine, and what I'd change next.

My fee is 20% of monthly Meta ad spend with a $1,500 USD monthly minimum, plus a one-time $1,500 onboarding fee covering the audit and setup. That includes management, ad copy, creative direction, landing page and form recommendations, and reporting. The Google side is priced separately on the same basis.

I'm on US Pacific time and work 10am to 6pm Central (8am to 4pm Pacific), Monday through Friday, which overlaps your working day from 4pm UK.
