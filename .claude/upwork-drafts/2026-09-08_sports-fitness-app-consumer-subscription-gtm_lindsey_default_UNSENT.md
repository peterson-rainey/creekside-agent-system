Are you planning to optimize the first campaigns toward paid subscriptions, or toward installs and trial starts? That one choice usually decides whether month one produces customers or a spreadsheet full of downloads.

A new app has almost no purchase history for Meta to learn from, so optimizing straight to paid subscription at launch volume tends to leave ad sets stuck in learning. Optimizing to installs fixes the volume problem and creates a worse one, because the algorithm gets very good at finding people who will download a free thing and never pay. The fitness apps I see struggling are usually not failing on creative. They are failing because the event they trained on was not the event that makes money.

After 10 years of this, and building and selling my own e-commerce brand before that, the discipline I hold to is keeping the acquisition campaigns clean. On the meal prep brand I run in Portland, the new customer campaigns exclude every previous purchaser, so nobody gets to call a returning buyer an acquisition. That account has done 844 purchases since January at about $12 each. I run a second meal prep brand where the same structure settles at $56. Almost five times the cost, same platform, same operator. Recurring consumer products live or die on knowing which of those you are, and finding out fast rather than after six months of spend.

For a movement assessment app, the assessment is the thing I would build the first month around. It is a reason to open the app that has nothing to do with motivation.

Two things worth saying plainly. My lane is Meta and Instagram plus lifecycle email, so TikTok and Google would need to sit with someone else. And my subscription experience is recurring consumer commerce, not a mobile app.
