When a homeowner books an estimate near the end of a 14-day pilot, how far out does that appointment usually land on your calendar? I ask because held appointments trail leads, sometimes by more than a week, so a short test can end before most of its own appointments have actually happened.

That lag decides how a pilot gets judged. Leads and bookings show up in real time, while cancellations and no-shows show up late, so the early read always flatters the campaign. On lead-gen accounts I have run, the fix is agreeing up front which appointments count toward the test, for example booked inside the window and held within a set number of days after it.

On qualifying, I ran Meta for a dental implant practice where lead forms fed a call team. We added qualifying questions to the form on purpose and accepted a higher cost per lead, because the real limit was how many conversations the team could handle in a day. Spend was throttled to match that capacity rather than pushed to whatever Meta would deliver. Over roughly 78 active days that account spent $64,747 and brought in 2,558 leads.
