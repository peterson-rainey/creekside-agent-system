On a lead-focused Google Ads account, lead quality and return on ad spend improve most when Google is told which leads turned into revenue. By default it sees the form fill or the call and nothing after, so Smart Bidding optimizes toward whatever is counted as a conversion, which is often the easiest lead to get rather than the best one. That's how a dashboard improves every month while sales says lead quality is slipping.

**Before launch**

1. Decide what counts as a conversion. Only real enquiries get set as primary. Page views, button clicks and duplicate form fires stay out, since the bidder learns from whatever is primary.
2. Build the feedback loop. If the sale closes on a call or after a quote, we pass qualified and closed leads back into Google, with a value on each where we can. A CRM makes this easier, but a simple sheet of which leads became customers works too. Google won't import an offline conversion uploaded more than 90 days after the click (63 days for enhanced conversions for leads), so on a longer sales cycle we'd import an earlier stage your team trusts.
3. Give branded searches their own campaign. Most people searching your name were already coming, and blended in they make new business look cheaper than it is.

**After launch**

We launch with a focused campaign per service rather than one catch-all, read the search terms every few days for the first month, and build negatives from what actually came in. Bid strategy changes wait until there's conversion data behind them, since each one puts the strategy back into learning. You'd get a written breakdown of spend, search terms and cost per qualified lead every two weeks, plus a live dashboard. A senior Google Ads specialist on our team runs the account day to day.
