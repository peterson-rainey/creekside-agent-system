## Q1. Have you migrated/rebuilt Meta and Google campaigns from another advertising platform into native Meta Ads Manager and Google Ads?

Not off a named third-party ad platform, no. I would rather say that plainly than stretch a story to fit your post.

What we do repeatedly is the harder half of it: inherit an account somebody else built, work out why it is shaped the way it is, and rebuild it natively without throwing away what was already working.

The closest example is Join Piper, a GLP-1 telehealth brand on Google. The account looked healthy on the surface and was broken underneath. The audit turned up duplicate conversion tracking inflating every reported number, targeting locked to brand keywords only, no in-market or custom audiences, no sitelinks, callouts or structured snippets, and a landing page pointing at account creation instead of the actual conversion. We fixed the conversion tracking first so there was a real baseline, then rebuilt Search around high intent non-brand terms, built the negative list, and layered remarketing back on.

Fixing tracking before structure is the part that transfers to your project. Rebuilding on top of inflated data does not give you a wrong answer, it gives you a confident wrong answer, which is worse because you scale it.

Your project is that same problem with one extra wrinkle. It is not only whether the tracking is right, it is whose account and whose dataset it has been living in, which is why I led the proposal with that question rather than with campaign structure.

On how the work splits: our search and paid social side runs the build. What sits with me is the diagnosis, the account architecture, the keyword and tracking spec, and sign off before anything goes live.

## Q2. One e-commerce account where you built or rebuilt Meta and Google campaigns, approximate monthly spend, and what you did.

Myriad Traders. Outdoor and survival gear, direct to consumer. We built both sides from scratch this July rather than inheriting them.

Google runs about $9,000 a month and carries the volume. August was $8,970 in spend against 269 conversions and $22,806 in tracked revenue, so roughly $33 per conversion at 2.5x. Search plus Shopping.

The number I would actually point at is July to August. Spend went up about 10 percent and conversions went up 91 percent. That was not more budget working harder, it was a reallocation. Two UK campaigns were burning around $2,500 a month and returning about $1,050 of it. We paused one and cut the other to a holding budget, then pushed the money into the Shopping and category segmented Search campaigns already carrying the account. Those two went from 2.16x and 1.46x in July to 2.81x and 2.47x in August, on more spend. Holding efficiency while adding budget is the harder half.

Meta is the newer half and I would rather give you the narrow window than dress it up: a short flight of about $4,600 that returned 148 purchases at roughly $31 each. Eight days of data is not a track record and I am not going to present it as one.

My part on that account: the keyword and search intent research, the campaign architecture on both platforms, the conversion tracking spec, and QA before either side went live. The team executed the builds.

If you want a larger ecommerce reference, Aura Displays is a Shopify brand we run on Google across 49 countries, $82,928 in spend against $1,043,575 in tracked revenue over the last ten months. Worth knowing that return there ran in the thirties early and has come down into the 7x to 9x range as we pushed budget into colder non-brand inventory. That is the honest shape of scaling past brand demand, and it is what your account will do too once it stops living on warm audiences.
