## DRAFT

Quick question before anything else: is your purchase event currently firing through the third-party platform's own pixel, or through your native Meta pixel? That single answer decides what this project actually is. If the events belong to their pixel, this is not a migration, it is a cold start, and the rebuilt campaigns will re-enter learning from zero no matter how faithfully the structure gets copied across. Anyone quoting you a launch date without checking that first is guessing.

The reason to lead with it is that the structure is rarely what breaks these rebuilds. Tracking is. After 10+ years on Meta, and having built and sold my own e-commerce brand before that, the pattern is consistent: the account gets recreated beautifully and then underperforms for six weeks because the signal history didn't come with it.

The second thing worth knowing before you spec the build: on the DTC accounts I audit, the consideration tier is usually the weakest tier. On one supplement-adjacent parts account I worked in, the Add-to-Cart, View Content and Time-on-Site retargeting campaigns all priced at or above plain broad targeting, while the catalog feed and customer-list campaigns came in roughly 20-30% cheaper than everything else in the account. So I would not port a separate Add-to-Cart campaign over just because it exists today. Build the catalog and buyer-list layers first, prove the purchase campaign, then decide whether consideration earns a line item.

Our team currently runs a supplement brand's account, so the compliance side of this category is familiar, and the Google half of the rebuild sits with our search side, who do the keyword and intent grouping from scratch rather than exporting the old platform's term list.

Fixed price for the full build across both platforms is $3,000, and realistically it is two to three weeks once access is granted.
