When a winner starts sliding in November, how will you know whether the ad is wearing out or the auction around it just got more expensive?

I ask because the two look identical in Ads Manager, and your Q4 ramp lands right where that happens. The largest Meta account I run is a high-ticket dental and aesthetics practice spending just under $100K a month across 130 campaigns. Last year its CPM went from $35.81 in October to $67.59 in December on nearly identical spend, then fell back to $35.01 in January. Nothing was broken in November.

To be straight about the gap: that account is my ceiling. I have not personally run $1M a month, and I haven't used Triple Whale or Northbeam day to day.

The closest answer I have to "what did you change": a replacement parts store I inherited ran $34.72 per conversion in January. By April it was $19.89, and $22.91 in May, with CPM flat around $8 throughout. I cut 11 campaigns to 7 and launched one new sales campaign, but targeting and creative changed in the same audit, so I can't honestly give structure the credit on its own.

My edge is consolidation and telling fatigue apart from the auction. Cost caps go on scaling budgets, testing stays on open bidding, and retargeting gets its own budget so it can't quietly eat prospecting.

The 7 days after a winner fatigues:
Days 1-2: ad or market? Compare its CPM to the account, CTR decay and frequency by age band. Step budget down 20%, don't kill it.
Days 2-4: new hooks on the same angle, plus static and carousel versions, into testing.
Days 4-6: whatever holds CPA moves into scaling under a cost cap near target, taking spend off the original.
Day 7: session with your strategists on what wore out, hook or offer, and brief the next round.

On the trial month: I work on a 90-day minimum instead, because a single month in October or November gets judged against Q4 CPMs.
