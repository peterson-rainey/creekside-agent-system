## 2. How do you use metrics to inform your strategy?

**Counts:** 177 words / 1,022 chars · 0 em dashes

Mostly to find the place where the account average is lying to me.

A live example from August. One of my practices ran two campaigns side by side, same account, same audience pool, same launch day, different objective. The engagement campaign spent $1,845.88 and bought 49,760 impressions, nearly twice the reach of the other one. It recorded no enquiries at all. The leads campaign spent $1,955.17 and brought in 124 at $15.77. Rolled up to account level that reads as $30.65 per lead, which is a number that describes neither campaign and would have had me optimizing the wrong half.

So the first thing I check is whether a metric is measuring anything. In health accounts that matters more than usual, because Meta can restrict what a pixel in that category is allowed to report, and an account can look like it is underperforming when it is really just under-reporting.

After that it is simple. I want the cost of the step that actually becomes revenue, split far enough down that an average cannot hide inside it.
