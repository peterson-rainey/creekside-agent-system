## PROPOSAL

Do you know how many of last month's leads actually booked, and how many of those showed up? I ask because on Facebook and Instagram that gap is usually where the budget disappears. Meta will happily deliver cheap leads when the form is easy, and plenty of those people have forgotten they filled it out by the time someone calls.

The clearest example I have is a dental implant practice where we ran Meta lead forms straight into their call center. Over 90 days that was 2,558 leads at $25.31 each. Two things made it work. We added qualifying questions to the forms on purpose, which raised cost per lead but meant the people coming through were ready to talk about treatment. And we split campaigns by location and by language, because those leads behaved very differently and blending them hid which ones deserved the call team's time.

What surprised them was where the ceiling turned out to be. It wasn't ad cost, it was how many leads the call center could work while they were still warm. We pulled spend back to match that capacity, because a lead nobody reaches while it's fresh is just an expensive name on a list.

Building and selling my own e-commerce business taught me the same thing from the other side: a number in Ads Manager only matters once you can tie it to who actually paid you. After 10+ years, the accounts that grow steadily are the ones where booking status flows back into Meta, so it learns to find people who book, not people who hit submit.

There's a short video on my profile that walks through how I set this up.
