Most accounts in this situation are not broken at the keyword level. They are reporting one number that averages two very different things together, and the average looks fine while the half you actually feel stays hidden.

A live example from one of our accounts, last 90 days. The branded search campaign returns conversions at $20.71 each. The unbranded search campaign in that same account, same tracking, same owner, returns them at $134.82. Blended, that account looks healthy. But the cheap half is people who already knew the business and were coming anyway, and the expensive half is the only part buying new customers. When reporting shows one figure, you cannot tell which half your money is in, and neither can Google's bidding, which is why it keeps buying more of the wrong one.

So the audit starts there, not with a keyword dump. Four things, in order:

1. Whether conversions are counted honestly. A conversion that fires on every ring, on a page load, or twice for the same person teaches the algorithm to chase the wrong behavior. This is the most common single cause of the pattern you are describing, and it is usually visible within the first hour in an account.

2. Branded versus non branded, pulled apart. Only then can you see what new business actually costs you.

3. The terms that actually triggered your ads, checked against the calls that turned out to be real. Not the keyword list, the search terms. Negatives come out of that comparison rather than out of a generic list someone pasted in.

4. Where and when the budget goes. Geography and hour of day are usually the quietest leak in an account like this.

That comes back as a written findings document, with what to fix and in what order. If it needs a rebuild rather than a tune up, it says so plainly instead of selling you twelve months of optimization on a broken foundation.

Two answers would sharpen this before we talk. What is the rough monthly ad spend and where are you advertising. And when a lead is low quality, is it the wrong service, outside your service area, or the right service at a price they were never going to pay. Those are three different fixes and the fourth item above only matters for one of them.
