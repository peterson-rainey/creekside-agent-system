Ecommerce brands running paid media on Google and Meta usually get one blended ROAS per account, and it is the least useful number in there. On a Shopify store we run right now, branded search sits above 20x while cold non-brand Search and Shopping sit around 4x. Blend them and you get a healthy looking 8x that says nothing about whether the next dollar of spend is worth it.

That split is where the real management call lives. The branded number is demand the brand already earned. The non-brand number is what the ads are creating. We built that account from zero last November and opened the non-brand lane at around 11x on a little under $2K a month. Cold spend has since scaled to close to $9K a month at peak, and ROAS settled around 4x, some months under. Still worth it at their order value, but the call was made on the non-brand line, not the blend.

Meta has its own version of the same trap, and it shows up in Q4. On a replacement-parts store we ran through last holiday season, spend stayed flat, click-through rate went up, and ROAS still fell from 7.7x in October to 4.3x in December. CPMs rose about 45% over the same stretch. Most of that drop was auction pressure, and the gap between the two is the account drifting on its own. Judging creative on ROAS in November kills your winners, which is why the Q4 read has to come off baselines set before October. From September through May that account ran about $124K into roughly 4,300 purchases at $29 each, and its best cost per purchase came that April.

On email, we build and run Klaviyo flows in house for our ecommerce clients. No revenue figure to hand you there. What that work tends to surface is tracking. One recent flow build found the store sending zero add-to-cart events, which meant the abandoned cart flow would have been firing on nobody.

Six years doing this, and our team runs Google and Meta concurrently for a handful of stores today. Not everything works. A UK expansion for a survival gear store sat at 0.6x for five weeks this summer and did not survive into August.
