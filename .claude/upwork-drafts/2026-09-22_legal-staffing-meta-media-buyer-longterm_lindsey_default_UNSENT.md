## PROPOSAL (paste-ready)

Do you know what size of firm actually signs with you? A solo attorney answering intake calls between hearings and a 30-lawyer firm with an office manager look the same to Meta. They book calls for different reasons and won't necessarily hire at the same rate. Treated as one audience, cost per booked call can look healthy while most calls come from the firms least likely to hire.

The closest proof I have isn't staffing or B2B. It's a high-ticket lead-gen account I ran, where the leads that looked expensive were the ones that made money. It was a dental implant practice with cases in the upper $30Ks, and Meta lead forms fed a call center. The form asked qualifying questions up front, which raised cost per lead on purpose. I checked the practice's CRM for who booked a consultation, and kept one campaign running even though its leads cost more, because they were booking. When the call center fell behind, spend came down to match it.

The same idea fits your funnel. One question on the form or booking page (how many attorneys, and whether they're hiring now) splits results by firm type. Tie that to which calls turn into placements, and the report stops saying which ad got cheaper leads and starts saying which angle works on two-to-five attorney firms versus solos. That's also what lets a creative brief explain why an ad worked.

I've run Meta ads myself for 10+ years. Pixel and Conversions API setup get built with a tracking specialist I work with, and I own the rest: structure, audiences, budgets, creative direction and daily optimization. I'm on US Pacific time and work 8am to 4pm Pacific, Monday through Friday, so a weekly call in your hours is easy.

I can walk you through real Ads Manager numbers from an account I run when we talk. There's a short video on my profile that shows how I work.
