Roofing Leads

An HOA board pricing a reroof and a homeowner with a ceiling stain both show up as a phone call or an estimate request, and unless the account is told otherwise, Google counts them as the same conversion. Bidding then learns from whichever lead is cheapest to buy, usually the small residential repair, while property manager searches get outbid. So residential and commercial should run as separate campaigns with their own budgets and conversion actions, with commercial allowed a higher cost per lead because the job is worth several times more. Calls should count only past a minimum length, so solar reps, job seekers and wrong numbers never teach bidding what a lead looks like.

1. Not roofing specifically, so that belongs first. Our closest work is high-ticket construction on Google: a commercial paving and lot striping contractor selling to property managers and general contractors, which our team runs today, and a Tennessee custom home builder where splitting one campaign into separate service lines took cost per lead from $454 to $239 and monthly leads from 15 to 60. That case study is attached.

2. Last month our active Google accounts ran from under $1,000 to about $17,000 a month each, around $74,000 combined across 13 accounts. The paving contractor was about $10,500 of that.

3. Ad calls through Google forwarding numbers and site calls through a tracking number shown to ad visitors, both with a minimum duration. Forms fire on a confirmed submission, not a thank you page view. For booked appointments and closed jobs, the click ID is captured on the form and stored on the lead in your CRM, then booked estimates and closed jobs are uploaded back into Google Ads with their value. That depends on your CRM holding the ID, so it gets checked in week one. Our dedicated tracking specialist owns setup and verification.

4. What counts as a conversion, before any keyword. Weak accounts are more often measuring wrong than buying wrong: page views counted as leads, duplicate tags, every call counted regardless of length. Then search terms for roofing jobs, supply stores, DIY, solar and insurance claim questions. Then whether location targeting reaches people in your area or anyone merely interested in it. Then how much Performance Max and search partners are absorbing.

5. From that paving account, current figures: the main Search campaign has produced 214 leads at $191 each since last November, turning 9.2% of clicks into leads. Performance Max in the same account runs $284 per lead at 1.1%. Side by side in one city over the same five months on similar spend, Search ran $131 per lead at 11.1% against $289 and 0.9% for Performance Max.

6. Access to Google Ads, GA4, Tag Manager, call tracking and CRM, plus whether Local Services Ads already runs alongside Search. The last 90 days of leads marked good or bad, so junk is defined by your standards. Your service area by city, and a rough job value for a repair, a full reroof and a commercial project, since that sets what each lead type can cost.

Leak repair searches jump with the first real storm of the season, so the budget needs room to scale then, and any recommendation only helps if it sits inside what you can actually spend. What monthly range are you working with, relative to $5,000, and is more of your revenue coming from residential or commercial right now?
