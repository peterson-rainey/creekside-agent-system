Hiring ads for a Rock Falls plant run into a rule most setups fight instead of use. Once Meta flags a campaign as an employment ad, it removes age, gender and ZIP code targeting and forces at least a 15 mile radius. For a tank manufacturer that radius works in your favor, because it naturally covers Sterling, Dixon and Morrison, which is where a lot of welders and fabricators already commute from. The move is to stop trying to narrow the audience and let the application form do the filtering.

That form is where I would put most of the effort. A Meta instant form with three or four real screening questions (can you work second shift, welding or forklift experience, can you pass a drug screen, how far are you from the plant) lowers raw applicant volume but raises the share who show up to the interview. Cost per applicant looks worse on paper while cost per hire improves, so the monthly report should show both, plus which ads produce people the plant actually calls back.

On platforms, I would start with Facebook and Instagram only. TikTok can reach younger hourly workers, but splitting a local budget across two platforms early leaves neither with enough data to optimize. Prove a cost per qualified applicant on Meta first, then test TikTok with a set amount if hiring needs grow.

To be straight on fit, our team hasn't run a dedicated recruitment campaign. What we run constantly is local Meta lead gen through instant forms for businesses that need qualified people calling back, and the employment category is that same build with a stricter policy layer on top.

Management is 20% of ad spend with a $1,500 monthly minimum, plus a one-time $1,500 onboarding fee that covers account setup and the form build. Before I recommend a spend number, what monthly range does your client have in mind? Any figure I give only helps if it fits what they can actually put in, and the range decides whether this starts at $3,000 a month on Meta or needs a different plan.
