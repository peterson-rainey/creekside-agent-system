## PROPOSAL (v2 FINAL, 496 words / 2,804 chars, QC PASS WITH FIXES applied)

Three finished videos and one offer usually give Meta more structure than it can use in week one. An ad set gets out of learning after about 50 results in the week following its last significant edit, so a starting budget split across three videos and several audiences tends to leave all of them unreadable. I would run the three inside one ad set against one broad audience and split them out only when volume supports reading them apart.

The lead quality lever sits earlier than targeting. It is which event Meta optimizes toward, and the options change depending on where the free experience lives. If the opt-in is an Instant Form, Meta's conversion leads goal is available, so you send lead stage changes back from your CRM and delivery learns to chase the registrants who progress. That goal works with Instant Forms only. If the opt-in sits on your own page, that lever is gone, and quality has to come from the event you choose, the friction you leave in the form, and offline conversions uploaded against the click.

Either way the campaign gets judged on your follow-up records, not on cost per lead in Ads Manager. Standard click attribution runs 7 days at most, so someone who opts in, goes through the free experience, and buys three weeks later never shows up next to the ad that found them. Results get grouped by the week people opted in, not the week they bought.

The campaign closest to yours: our team ran Meta lead generation for a dental implant practice selling full mouth reconstructions in the upper $30 thousands, where the goal was booked consultations rather than form fills. Over 90 days it produced 2,558 leads on $64,747, just over $25 each. The instructive part is what we did to that number on purpose. We added qualifying questions to the form and accepted a higher cost per lead for them, kept one campaign live even though its leads cost more because those leads were booking consultations, and cut spend when the practice's call center ran out of capacity to work what was coming in. Our team also runs Meta for a coaching certification company whose funnel is a free registration into an email sequence, which is the closer shape to yours.

Before launching I would want: where the opt-in lives and what the last 30 days of traffic did there; whether the pixel and Conversions API are both firing, and which event you would call the finish line; what happens in the first hour and the first week after someone opts in; what your follow-up records tell you about which leads turn into buyers; whether the three videos are one promise cut three ways or three separate angles; and who you sell to and where.

What does the ad budget look like here, closer to $5K a month or $30K? That answers whether Meta can optimize on anything past the opt-in or just keep score.
