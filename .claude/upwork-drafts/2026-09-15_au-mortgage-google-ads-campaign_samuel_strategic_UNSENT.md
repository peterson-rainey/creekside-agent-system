An Australian mortgage business can have its Google Ads campaign built perfectly and still not serve a single ad, because Google won't run financial services ads in Australia until the verification side is sorted. It goes one of two ways. Hold your own credit licence and you verify in your own name, with details that have to match the ASIC register exactly. Write loans as a credit representative under an aggregator and the approval runs through the licensed business behind you instead. Which one applies to you decides what the first week looks like.

After that, the real decision is which searches to pay for. The broad home loan terms are where the banks and the big broker brands spend heavily, and a single business usually loses money trying to outbid them there. The better start is narrower: refinance and first home buyer searches tied to the areas you actually serve, plus your brand name, in a Search campaign with tight negatives on things like broker job seekers and rate-only browsing. Performance Max can come later, once there's a record of which enquiries went on to settle, because without that signal it tends to chase cheap, low-quality enquiries.

Tracking should be built the same way from day one. Count the enquiry, then send back which ones qualified and settled, so Google bids toward loans instead of clicks.

Our team ran Google for a US reverse mortgage lender until last month. Search alone was about US$126K over just under eleven months at roughly $97 a lead. To be straight, all of our mortgage work has been in the US, not Australia, which is exactly why the licensing details are the first thing worth confirming.
