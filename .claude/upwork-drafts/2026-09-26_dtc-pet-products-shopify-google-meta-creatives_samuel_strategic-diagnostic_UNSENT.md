## PROPOSAL

If much of your range is something people buy again, like food, treats or chews, Meta's numbers are easy to over-read. A customer who reorders every month and taps an ad on the way gets counted as an ad sale, so retargeting and broad campaigns can look stronger than the work that finds new customers. Before scaling anything, we'd split first-time orders from repeat orders in Shopify and judge spend on the first.

**What I'd check first**

Tracking. If the store uses the Shopify Google & YouTube app, add to cart and checkout started are set as conversions by default next to purchase, so Google can bid as if a cart were an order. Our tracking specialist would confirm each order reaches Google and Meta once, with its value.

Brand vs non-brand on Google. Performance Max serves on your brand name unless brand exclusions are set, and brand searches return far more than anything else, which flatters the account average.

**Creatives**

Every ad gets tagged by product, format (static, video, carousel), angle (the pet using it, an owner talking, a close-up of the product, the problem it solves) and opening hook. That lets us read results by idea instead of by ad, so you hear "owner-talking videos are beating studio shots on cold audiences" rather than "ad 14 did well."

An ad is wearing out when frequency climbs and click-through falls while CPM holds. Every two weeks you get a short creative brief: what's winning on which audience, what's fading, and what to make next, down to the product and the shot. Google uses the same work, since Performance Max needs images and video per asset group and its asset reports feed the same brief.

**Accounts**

A consumer camera brand on Meta. Spend went from about $4,800 in February to about $20,000 in May. The early campaigns were optimized for add to cart and checkout, so we moved spend to purchase-optimized broad campaigns, ran each creative angle as its own test and split out retargeting. Cost per conversion was $46 in February and held between $41 and $53 through April at two to three times the daily spend. In May, at four times the spend, it rose to about $92 as CPMs went from $12 to $32. Audiences and offers changed along the way too, so no single change owns those numbers. The May jump is the real price of scaling fast, and why we raise spend in steps.

A replacement parts store we took over on Meta at about $15,600 a month, with 11 campaigns spending and 232 live ads. We consolidated to 7 and built one new sales campaign. Cost per conversion went from $34.72 in the inherited month to $19.89 by April with CPM flat around $8, and sat at $22.91 in May. Targeting and creative changed in the same stretch, so no single change owns that.

A store selling laptop screen extenders, built on Google from zero. Non-branded spend grew about four times, to roughly $7,800 a month, and non-branded return went from 11.6x to about 4.2x while branded Search ran near 27x. It was still profitable at their order value, and it's the reason brand gets split out before anything is judged.

We haven't run a pet brand yet.
