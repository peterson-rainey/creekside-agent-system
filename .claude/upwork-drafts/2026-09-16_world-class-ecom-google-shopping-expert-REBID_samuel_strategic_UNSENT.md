## PROPOSAL

On Google Shopping, your product listings do the job keywords do in Search. There's no keyword list, so Google reads each product's title, description and category to decide which searches it shows up for. That's why a weak conversion rate on Shopping is worth reading as a listing problem before it gets treated as a website problem: the product may be showing up for people who wanted something slightly different.

So the first read goes product by product, not campaign by campaign. Which products take most of the spend, which searches they're really matching, and which ones get plenty of clicks but rarely sell. Before trusting any ROAS figure, we'd split out searches for your store's name. Those clicks are cheap, so they make the whole account look healthier than it is with new shoppers. We'd also check Merchant Center for disapproved products and confirm each order is only counted once.

On a survival gear store we run now, the UK campaigns selling multi-packs spent about $2,500 in their first month and brought back roughly $1,070 in sales. We shut them off in August and moved that budget to the US catalog. Non-brand ROAS went from 1.5x in July to 2.6x in August, about half of that from the UK cut and the rest from the US campaigns improving. September is running around 2.4x.

The bigger structural call is Performance Max versus standard Shopping. PMax reaches further across Google, but it hands Google more of the decisions about which products and searches get the money. On a Shopify store our team built from scratch, non-brand Shopping came in at 5.66x and PMax at 5.64x, on the same 69 days with almost identical spend. With returns that close, I'd let control break the tie.

What range are you spending on Shopping each month? What I'd suggest at $5,000 a month looks very different at $50,000. And roughly how many products are in the catalog, and how many of them actually sell in a typical month?
