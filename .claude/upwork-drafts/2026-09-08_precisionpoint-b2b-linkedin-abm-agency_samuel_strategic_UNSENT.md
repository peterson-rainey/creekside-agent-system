The active LinkedIn campaign you have running is the right place to start, but the conversion tracking
and the target account list need a hard look before anything gets classified as keep or rebuild. A
rebuild judged against broken measurement just relocates the same problem into a new campaign structure.

Worth being straight with you up front, because it affects how you weigh this. LinkedIn Campaign Manager
is not a platform our team has run paid campaigns on. What we bring is deep hands-on Google Ads and Meta
Ads work, which your post lists as a requirement for where these accounts are heading, plus the
audit-and-rebuild judgment you are describing: deciding what is actually broken, why, and what the fix
should be, rather than executing changes someone else already diagnosed.

Named-account targeting gets a similar honest answer. The mechanism carries over from Meta, which has no
employer or job-title input at all, so reaching a named account list there is list-building and exclusion
work rather than native targeting. How that list was built, and whether it has been kept current, is
usually where these campaigns leak long before the ad copy gets blamed for it.

On the audit itself, the useful output is not the keep, change, or rebuild list. It is the reasoning
behind each call. Whether a delivery problem is a bidding problem or an audience problem. Whether budget
allocation matches where September intent actually sits. Where a test would teach you something new
instead of confirming a hunch. We ran that same diagnosis on a bankruptcy firm's Google account everyone
assumed just needed more budget. The real issue was campaign structure mixing intent types across markets.
Restructuring took conversions from 117 to 229 and cut cost per conversion 42 percent, from $86.09 to
$50.29, on spend that barely moved.

The one-hour paid audit works at my listed rate, and it is a fair way to see that reasoning before
anything ongoing gets decided. Past that, ongoing management runs on a percentage of ad spend rather than
hourly, which keeps the incentive on what the campaigns return instead of hours logged.

Curious how the target account list was built, whether it came out of your ABM tooling or out of your
clients' closed-won patterns. That changes how tight the targeting can realistically get.
