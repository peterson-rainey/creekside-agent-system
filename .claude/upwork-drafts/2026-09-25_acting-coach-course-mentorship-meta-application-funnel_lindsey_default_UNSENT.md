When a mentorship round fills, can you tell which ads your enrolled students came from, or only which ads brought in the most applications? The two lists rarely match, and Meta optimizes toward the second unless it's told otherwise.

**What I judge an application on**

On a high-ticket cosmetic dental account I run, the messaging ads produce plenty of conversations. Whether they work depends on how many become booked consults, and that only shows up when bookings get marked back in the CRM. When they aren't, Meta keeps learning from whoever was happiest to chat. A $5,000+ mentorship works the same way. I'd check that page visits and applications each fire once, feed your accept or decline back, and judge ads on accepted applicants. On the course, the $397 purchase has to match your checkout orders, and the better quality read is which buyers later apply for the mentorship.

**Running three-week windows**

A round that fills fast leaves little time to learn, so proven ads carry the budget in-window, audience tests run on the course and between rounds, and the winners go into the next launch.

**Reading your past ads first**

Building and selling my own e-commerce business taught me that the pain point getting the most clicks is often not the one people pay to fix. So I'd line up your tested ads by what they led to: applications, accepted students, course sales. Hook rate shows which openings stop the right actor, and the conversion step shows which promises they believed. New angles come from that gap, briefed for you to film.

**How I bill**

I don't bill hourly. Management is 20% of monthly ad spend with a $1,500 monthly minimum, and a one-time $1,500 onboarding covers the full review of past campaigns. You'd have a live dashboard, plus a written read every two weeks on what the spend produced and what I'd test next.
