**1. HCP campaign experience, stated straight**

No HCP registration campaign with a citable CPL, conversion rate or registration volume sits in our book. Saying that plainly before anything else, because reshaping patient-side work into HCP work would be easy and wrong. The patient-side work we do run is dental, aesthetics and naturopathic care. None of it is oncology, so there is no adjacency to lean on even loosely, and it does not transfer by assertion.

The one account where the audience is practicing clinicians rather than patients is an AI medical scribe product sold to US clinicians. Our team runs its Meta, and it is running now. That product sells software adoption rather than event or portal registration, so the funnel shape is not yours, and we are not going to hold it up as HCP registration evidence.

Conversions are not recorded in our reporting warehouse for that account. There is no CPL, no conversion rate and no lead count available to quote, and we are not going to reverse engineer one. That is a measurement gap on our side rather than a verdict on the account in either direction.

**2. Reaching oncology consultants without a specialty input**

Agreed on the premise, so the useful question is what replaces the checkbox. The standard route to an HCP audience is a licensed contact file derived from NPI records through a healthcare data vendor, uploaded as a customer list. The trap is match asymmetry. Those files carry professional email addresses. LinkedIn matches professional addresses well, because that is what people register with there. Meta matches the address on a personal account, so the identical file matches far worse. That match rate is testable before any spend, and it should set the budget split between the two platforms. Deciding the split first and discovering the match rate after is the usual order and it is backwards.

On Meta there is no job title input, so the audience is inferred rather than selected. Three things do the filtering. The list match narrows who is reachable, creative built to be deliberately unappealing to patients and caregivers narrows who responds, and the registration form verifies who actually came through. Creative that reads like patient education will pull patients, and on a universe this small that noise gets expensive quickly.

LinkedIn is the honest gap. We have not bought a single impression on it, so what follows is product reasoning rather than a playbook. Its published inputs are title, specialty, seniority, skills and group membership, which on paper is the closest any platform gets to selecting a specialty outright. Whether those profiles are current for working clinicians is the part worth testing, because doctors are not the demographic that keeps a profile fresh.

Google is the other half of this. A consultant working through a treatment decision types the question, and that is the one place the audience declares itself instead of being inferred.

**3. Quality over volume on a narrow specialist audience**

Quality needs one agreed definition before spend moves, and here it is a practicing clinician whose role actually touches cancer care. An NPI typed into a field proves nothing on its own, because those numbers are published and anyone can retype one. It has to be cross-matched on name and state, or handed to a credentialing vendor.

The operational problem is lag. Verification lands after registration and is usually part manual, so the signal arrives days late. On an audience this narrow, daily conversion volume is also too thin for a platform to learn on verified status even when you do pass it back. Optimizing delivery on verified status sounds correct and does not hold up at this scale.

So delivery optimizes on registration, and budget allocates on verified share. That share gets reviewed per ad set and per creative on a fixed cadence, and the verified figure travels next to the raw one in every report. When the two diverge, the cheaper channel is usually the wrong one to fund.

A verified or qualified stage can be pushed back to the platforms as an offline conversion import. That mechanism is platform-side and indifferent to whatever system holds your records. Worth standing up as volume accumulates, not worth optimizing against on day one.

One more constraint to plan around. Practicing US oncologists number in the low tens of thousands, so frequency climbs fast and creative fatigues faster. Scaling this is a creative problem before it is a budget problem, which means new angles on a schedule rather than more spend behind a winner.
