Most funnels that sell through booked calls break in the same spot, and it is rarely the creative. Meta optimizes toward a form fill or a registration while you actually get paid on a held call with an experienced investor, so every unqualified signup you let through is a training signal telling the algorithm to go find more people exactly like that. It compounds quietly. Cost per lead looks fine, show rates sag, and the sales team starts saying the leads got worse.

The fix is to make the conversion Meta chases the same thing your team counts. That means qualifying questions on the front end even though they raise cost per lead, and the CRM outcome pushed back as the optimization event rather than the raw registration. Once that loop is closed, creative and funnel tests start to mean something, because you are reading them against qualified calls instead of clicks.

The closest analogs in our book are a coaching certification business and a high ticket implant practice. The first runs off a recurring weekly workshop, and cost per application there came down from $256 to $123 while monthly applications went from 41 to 90, on a 5 percent budget increase, so the gain came out of the account rather than the spend. The second fed a call center, and our team added qualifying questions, accepted a higher cost per lead, and cut spend when the call center needed room. Closer to your world, we also ran a Texas land seller with a separate campaign per parcel, which is where the spread shows up: same offer, same creative, $15 a lead in one county and $52 in another.

One thing to be straight about. Building pages and funnels is not what we sell, and there is no before and after conversion rate in our book to point at. What I would own is the ad to page intent match, the tracking, and the briefs, alongside a tracking specialist on our team.
