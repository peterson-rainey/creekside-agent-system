## ANSWERS (paste each answer below into its box, or as one numbered message with the bold labels removed)

**1. Please tell us about your lead generation experience.**

On Meta, an implant dentistry practice our team ran from April to mid-July: about $65,000 across 78 days of spend brought in around 2,560 leads, roughly $25 a lead. Partway through, the call center couldn't handle the lead inflow, so spend was cut back and general dentistry campaigns were paused, with implant consultations as the priority.

On Google Ads, a naturopathic clinic, still running: about 550 conversions at around $41 each over the last twelve months, on roughly $22,600 of spend.

Both are cost figures, not booked or closed revenue.

I've been in paid ads for six years and helped manage more than $20 million in ad spend across Google and Meta, including lead generation for local and service businesses such as dental and other healthcare practices, home services and legal.

**2. Do you typically handle content direction, for example copy or direction on creative?**

Yes. Our team writes the ad copy and briefs the creative, with the team's creative director handling the visual side. A brief gives the ad's job in the funnel, who it's for, the headline and supporting text, the visual direction, and sizes for feed and stories. Direction comes from me, and the team writes from there.

On a new account I'd start with a small batch of clearly different ideas rather than variations of one, because a small budget can only read big differences.

**3. Do you provide any services for post-click experiences, for example optimizing landing pages and things of that nature?**

Yes, with one limit: we have no before-and-after conversion rate from a client page, so I won't quote a lift. A landing page specialist on the team builds pages to match the ads, and a tracking specialist makes sure the right events fire.

On the post-click side I'd start with what usually decides lead quality: whether the offer on the page matches the ad, what the form asks (a question about the job can cost some leads and screen out poor fits), and how fast someone calls back. The test for a page would be cost per qualified lead, which needs your team's feedback on which leads were qualified.

**4. What is your hourly rate? Also will you be doing the work directly or do you have people who work for you?**

We don't bill hourly. Our fee is a percentage of monthly ad spend, so it moves with the budget you set: 20% of the first $30,000 a month, 15% of the next $30,000 and 10% of anything over $60,000, with a $1,500 monthly minimum per platform. Onboarding is $1,500 once per platform, and the account audit is part of it.

The work is done by our team of specialists. A Meta specialist builds and runs the account day to day, a tracking specialist owns conversion tracking, a landing page specialist builds pages and the creative director handles the visual side. I stay on strategy and direction.

**5. Outside of lead generation, what other type of marketing do you have experience in, as we have multiple roles open?**

Ecommerce. Our team has run Google Shopping, Performance Max and Meta catalog ads for online stores, including Shopify stores, where the goal is revenue and return on ad spend rather than leads.

We've also run Local Services Ads for one client. Around the ads we've done conversion tracking in GA4 and Google Tag Manager, and Google Business Profile optimization for local businesses. Beyond that, SEO isn't something we offer.

We've drafted Klaviyo email flows (welcome, abandoned cart, abandoned checkout) for an ecommerce brand, awaiting the brand's sign-off, so I have no email results to share yet.
