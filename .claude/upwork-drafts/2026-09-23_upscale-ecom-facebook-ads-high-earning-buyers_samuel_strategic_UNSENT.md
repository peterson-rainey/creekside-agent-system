Before any creative gets written, the setting worth arguing about is what the campaigns optimize toward. On an upscale catalog that one choice moves cost per customer more than the ad copy does, and it is the thing that quietly decides whether you get browsers or buyers.

Our team ran Meta for a luxury furniture ecommerce brand, and that account happened to have both kinds of campaigns live in the same window. Time matched across the identical date range, the traffic objective campaigns bought clicks at $0.17 against $0.84 on the purchase objective campaigns, at a $4.97 CPM against $27.28. Click through rate was almost the same on both, 2.90 percent versus 3.24 percent. So on a traffic report, the cheap campaigns looked like the winners by a factor of five. Not one conversion was recorded against them. Two caveats you should have before you weigh that. Some of the zero is attribution rather than a true zero, since a campaign not optimized to a purchase event does not get credited the same way. And the two sides were nowhere near the same size, $2,035.97 of traffic spend over 131 active days against $75,719.48 of purchase spend over 196, so treat this as a rate comparison and not a matched budget test. What it does show is that the cheaper clicks were never bid toward buyers, and the cost of a visit told you nothing about the cost of a customer.

The same pattern repeated inside the purchase campaigns, which is the part I would want you to see. The cheapest clicks in that account sat alongside the most expensive results. One campaign bought clicks at $0.40 and spent $6,033.58 to record 6 conversions, or $1,005.60 each. The product feed catalog campaign paid $1.15 a click, nearly three times more per visit, and returned 119 conversions on $14,644.11, or $123.06 each. The retargeting campaign landed at $200.00 across 85. I am giving you the conversion counts because the cheap click campaign is thin at 6 and you should weigh it accordingly, but the direction holds across every line in the account with enough volume to read.

One more, because it bears on the audience you are after. The deepest discount campaign in that account was also its single largest line of spend at $23,843.26, and its worst result at $567.70 per conversion, against $123.06 on the catalog. I would not present that as proof that discounting caused it, since that campaign also ran across the July 4th period against a different audience and different creative, and any of those could carry the difference. It is the first thing I would want tested, because on an upscale brand a discount changes who answers the ad, not just how many.

That is also the honest answer on reaching higher earning buyers. Meta has stripped back direct income and affluence targeting, so anyone promising to simply target that audience is worth a hard question about what selector they actually intend to use. What actually does the screening is the price point on the page, the creative, and the catalog feed, and then letting the purchase signal compound until the algorithm finds the people who buy at your prices rather than the people who click at any price.

What the first thirty days would look like:

Purchase objective from day one, with the pixel and Conversions API checked before spend runs, since a broken signal makes every later decision guesswork.

A product feed catalog campaign built off your site, which in the account above was the best performer of every campaign with enough volume to read.

Prospecting and retargeting split into separate campaigns so returning buyers are not flattering the numbers on cold audiences.

Three to five creative concepts tested against each other rather than variations of one idea, since most of the spread in an account comes from concept, not from headline edits.

Reporting runs every two weeks with a live dashboard you can open whenever you want, rather than a monthly summary arriving after the decisions have already been made.

Two questions before we talk specifics. What is your average order value and your rough gross margin on it, since that sets what a customer is allowed to cost before any of this is worth doing? And is your monthly ad budget closer to five thousand, fifteen thousand, or above thirty thousand, since the structure I would build is genuinely different at each of those.

If you can share the site and the two or three products that carry the most revenue, our team can come back with a specific read on where the first campaigns should point.
