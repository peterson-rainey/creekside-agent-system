## PROPOSAL

Quick question before anything else: are your campaigns split by product, or is one brand-level message carrying everything you sell? Doubt in supplements is product-specific. Someone doubting a sleep formula has a different objection from someone doubting a protein, and when one campaign carries both, Meta quietly shifts spend toward whichever product gets the cheapest clicks that week. So results swing when the account is really just rotating between products.

**What product lanes showed me**

I saw this clearly on a DTC furniture brand I ran on Meta. Same spring, similar budgets of roughly $5,000 to $6,000 each: a campaign built around one product returned 2.36x in Meta-reported revenue, a second product returned 1.36x, and a general brand campaign returned 0.08x. A third product lane ran at 0.40x and was cut inside two weeks. It wasn't a formal test, but the split made the weak spots obvious fast. Each lane then gets an angle built around the doubt buyers have about that product.

**Reorders and structure**

I haven't run a supplement account. The closest in my book are meal prep brands, where reorders carry the business the same way. What I learned there, and from building and selling my own e-commerce business, is that Meta should mostly be judged on first orders. Existing buyers get excluded from prospecting, and retargeting is judged against its own target. On the furniture account retargeting reported 5.4x, which would have hidden a weaker prospecting side if the two were blended. Reorders are mostly won through email flows timed to when a bottle runs out, far cheaper than paying Meta for the same customer twice.

**What you'd get**

A written audit with a conversion tracking check (pixel and Conversions API deduplication, and whether Meta's purchase count matches your store), the rebuilt structure set up in the account, creative and messaging direction per product, and optimization notes tied to the numbers.
