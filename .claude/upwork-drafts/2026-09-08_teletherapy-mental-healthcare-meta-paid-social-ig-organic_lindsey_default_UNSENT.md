Do you know what share of your leads come from places where you have a clinician licensed and actually open to new patients? On teletherapy accounts that is usually where the budget leaks, and it never surfaces in Ads Manager, because the campaign records the lead either way.

That gap between a lead and a kept appointment is most of what I work on in healthcare. A dental group I ran on Meta brought in 2,558 lead form submissions at about $25 each. What governed that account was never the cost of a lead. We pulled spend back to match what the call center could physically handle. The ceiling was booking capacity, not media. I run four healthcare accounts on Meta right now, and by the time someone asks me to look, the ad account is rarely the part that is broken.

One thing specific to this niche changes how creative gets written. Meta does not allow copy that implies you know something personal about the reader, and mental health is squarely inside that rule, so a line like "struggling with anxiety?" is the most common rejection here. The same promise written about the work instead of the person runs clean. That is not a compliance footnote to check afterward. It is the hook problem itself, which is why feedback on a video or a static has to come from someone who knows where the line sits before the editing starts.

Straight on the gaps: I have no mental health case study for you, and organic Instagram is not something I sell. Paid social and email are my side of it. Worth flagging now if the organic piece has to sit with the same person.
