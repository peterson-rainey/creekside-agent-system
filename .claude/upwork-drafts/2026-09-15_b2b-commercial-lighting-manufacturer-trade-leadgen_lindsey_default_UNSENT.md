## PROPOSAL (paste-ready)

When a fixture gets specified on a commercial job, does that choice usually hold through to the order, or does the electrical contractor swap in something cheaper at bid time? The answer decides who the ads need to win first, and the message for each is completely different.

I ask because the closest thing in my book is a software company selling referral automation to home service contractors, where I ran the Meta side. Contractors weren't searching for a product they didn't know existed, so Meta had to reach the owners first and search picked up the ones who went looking later. That account taught me trade buyers rarely convert on one ad. They need a few touches, then a reason to hand over an email.

For a lighting manufacturer, that reason is usually something they need on a job, like spec sheets, photometric files or lead times on a product line. I'd build audiences from your existing customer and contractor lists rather than Meta's job title targeting, which is self-reported and patchy, then follow up by email based on what each person downloaded, so a specifier and a buyer aren't getting the same sequence. I'd expect contractors to respond on Meta sooner than architects, who usually need a longer run of retargeting.

To be straight about fit, my work is Meta and email. SEO and search ads aren't mine, and I haven't run a lighting or architecture brand, so there's no case study in your space to attach. I've done this for over 10 years, and I built and sold my own e-commerce business before working with clients.

One thing to plan for here: lead costs for a trade audience on Meta take a few months of testing to settle, so it helps to decide early who keeps it running after the build.

What are you planning to spend on Meta each month, closer to a couple thousand or into five figures?

There's a short video on my profile that walks through how I run accounts like this.
