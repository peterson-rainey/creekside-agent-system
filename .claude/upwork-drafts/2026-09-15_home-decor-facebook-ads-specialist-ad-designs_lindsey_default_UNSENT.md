## PROPOSAL (paste-ready)

When you look back at which ad designs drove conversions, were those sales at full price or during a promotion? For something people buy on looks, that one detail can change which design seems to be the winner.

I ask because of a luxury furniture brand I ran on Meta until August, about $105,000 in spend over four months. In May, June and July the biggest campaign every month was a sale, and in July the 50% off campaign alone took more than half of that month's spend. An ad that sells at 50% off mostly tells you people like 50% off. So I read creative in two groups, full price and promo, and a design only counts as a winner once it holds up at full price on enough spend to trust the read.

For the designs themselves, I make the static ads myself in Canva and write the copy. With decor I'd take a few of your best sellers and show each one three ways using photos you already have, styled in a room, a clean product shot, and a close-up of the material or finish. My guess is the room shot works better on strangers and the close-up on people who've already browsed, but that's what the test is for.

Meta also gets more expensive through November and December as every retailer piles in, so I'd want this testing done in October and the holiday budget going behind designs that already proved themselves.

I've run Meta and email for e-commerce brands for over 10 years, and I built and sold my own e-commerce business before doing this for clients. I haven't run a pure home decor brand. The closest in my book are that furniture brand and a luxury mattress brand I run on Meta now.

What are you spending on Meta each month right now, closer to a few thousand or into five figures? How I'd split testing and holiday budget depends on that range.

There's a short video on my profile that walks through how I run accounts like this.
