Lead quality and tracking accuracy are usually the same problem wearing two names, and on a challenge funnel that is almost always the case. A week inside the account tells you which version of it you have, and all of it is checkable before anyone changes a budget.

The first version is a shallow optimization event. If the campaign optimizes to demo signup or free account registration instead of the challenge purchase, Meta goes and finds the cheapest available person who will do the shallow thing. Lead quality collapses while the dashboard improves.

The second is signal loss between the browser and the checkout. If event IDs are not matched cleanly across the pixel and the server side, or the Conversions API is not deduplicating against the browser event, Meta is optimizing on a fraction of real purchase volume. From inside the campaign view that looks identical to bad targeting, which is why it usually gets treated as one.

The third is reversal, and this one is specific to how challenges work. Failed attempts, resets and refund guarantees mean a purchase event that fired today can be worth nothing a week later, and a reset bought by an existing trader gets counted as a fresh acquisition. Most accounts feed neither fact back to Meta, so the algorithm is being trained toward revenue that did not survive the month.

The fourth is attribution overlap with affiliates. This space runs heavily on affiliate and promo code traffic, and when those are not separated from paid, Meta takes credit for purchases it did not cause and gets rewarded for finding more of the wrong audience.

Week one is the audit, in that order: event and Conversions API integrity first, then deduplication and how reversals are handled, then structure and learning phase status, then audience overlap and creative fatigue. You get the findings in writing whether or not they flatter whoever built the account.
