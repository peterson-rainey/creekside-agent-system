Beating a mature Google Ads account on the same budget almost never comes from smarter bidding. It comes from spend that is already leaking before the auction starts, and that leak is invisible in a standard week over week report.

Three things worth checking before anything touches the account.

First, what the conversion action is actually counting. Accounts that have been running for years are often still optimizing toward a form submit or a call goal configured long ago and never revisited. If the target is stale, "beating" the current campaigns can mean efficiently buying more of the wrong outcome. That one is worth answering before anything else, so it is the first question we would ask you.

Second, search terms and match type drift. Broad and phrase match campaigns accumulate irrelevant query matches over time. That spend shows up as cost in the top line and never as an obvious problem, because the top line has no idea the clicks were off target.

Third, if Performance Max is running alongside Search, whether PMax is intercepting branded and near branded traffic that was already going to convert. This is the one most accounts get backwards, because PMax looks cheap on the surface. On one of our accounts, a Search campaign and a Performance Max campaign ran over the same four months at nearly the same spend, within two percent of each other. PMax bought clicks at $3.00 against Search at $14.53, roughly five times cheaper. On cost per lead it went the other way: $268.19 on PMax against $131.22 on Search. Cheap traffic, expensive outcomes. Reallocating between those two is exactly the kind of move that improves results on the same budget or less.

None of that surfaces in a week over week comparison. It surfaces in the search terms report, asset group breakdowns, and a conversion action audit, which is why the first couple of weeks under our team would be diagnosis rather than a rebuild. Changing a mature account fast is how you lose the baseline that tells you whether you improved anything.

One note on the weekly change log you are asking for, because we think it is the right thing to ask for. If several changes go in during the same week, the report can tell you the number moved but not which change moved it. Our team sequences changes so each week's report can attribute cause. That is slower in week two and considerably faster by week eight, because you stop re paying for the same lesson.

It is from an ecommerce account, so the metrics on it will not line up with yours. The structure is the part worth looking at: the week set against the prior week, then a kill, keep and scale section giving the reasoning behind each call, and a footer stating plainly what is not being measured yet and why.

On structure, we work on a percentage of ad spend rather than hourly, so what we earn tracks what the account does rather than hours logged. You are managed by an assigned specialist who runs the account and writes the weekly update, not a rotating queue.

Which brings up the one thing that shapes everything above. Is current monthly spend closer to the five to ten thousand range, or well above that? The answer changes how much room there is to reallocate before adding a dollar, and on most accounts at that size the first real gains come from redirecting waste rather than spending more.
