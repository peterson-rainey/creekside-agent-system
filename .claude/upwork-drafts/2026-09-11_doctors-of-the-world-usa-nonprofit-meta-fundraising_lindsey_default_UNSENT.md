Is your donation form hosted on your own website, or does it hand off to a platform like Donorbox, Classy or Givebutter? I ask because that one detail decides whether the Pixel ever sees a completed gift. When the checkout lives on another domain or inside an embedded frame, Meta records the click and never the donation, and a conversion campaign quietly starts learning from page views instead of donors.

Having built and sold my own e-commerce business, I check that hand-off before anything else, because an off-site checkout is the most common reason conversion data goes quiet. Every audience and every budget decision downstream depends on it.

The second thing I look at is overlap. People who watched most of a video and people who visited the site are often the same people, and when both audiences run side by side on one day they end up bidding against each other. Excluding site visitors from the video audience keeps each ad set clean and makes budget shifts actually readable.

Timing matters too. Giving Tuesday lands the day after Cyber Monday, one of the most expensive weeks of the year to buy attention on Meta. On a replacement parts store I ran, CPMs rose about 40% from October to November on flat spend, while click-through rate kept climbing. The ads were getting better and the auction was getting pricier. Judging that week on cost per donation alone can cut your strongest creative right when you need it most.

For lookalikes, your existing donor file is usually a stronger seed than the Pixel will be by November, since a few weeks of new gifts may not reach the 100-person minimum Meta needs for a source audience.
