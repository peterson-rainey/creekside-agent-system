IV therapy and mobile IV therapy usually pull impulse, same-day intent, someone feels rough and wants a drip within the hour, while medical weight loss and peptide therapy searches come from people comparing programs for a week or two before booking a consult. Lab testing sits between those two. If all five services share one campaign and one broad keyword list, the algorithm chases whichever converts cheapest, almost always the same-day IV bookings, and the weight loss and peptide side never collects enough volume to optimize. Splitting structure by service line is usually the first fix here.

One more thing worth raising early: if the weight loss or peptide programs involve prescription medications like semaglutide or sermorelin, Google's healthcare and medicines policy requires LegitScript or NABP certification before those campaigns can run in Search at all, separate from anything about keywords or budget. Is that already in place, or are these programs structured as non-prescription and supplement-based, which sidesteps the requirement entirely? Weight-loss content can also get classified under Google's health-conditions policy for personalized ads, which would rule out remarketing or uploaded contact lists for that service line specifically, so we'd lean on in-market and demographic targeting there instead.

Our team runs Google Ads for a naturopathic wellness clinic in Sacramento on a modest budget, a bit over $22,000 spent across the past year, landing around $40 per patient inquiry. We also manage a dental aesthetics practice in Irvine that's spent close to $99,000 on Google since April, generating over 600 tracked conversions. Neither is IV therapy specifically, and I'd rather say that upfront than stretch it, but the shape matches: local, appointment-based treatment services where qualified bookings matter more than clicks.

**Tracking, before anything else**
We'd confirm whether a lead is currently a form submit, a phone call, or a booked appointment on your calendar, and keep the conversion event itself generic, something like "consult booked," rather than condition-specific, since Google doesn't sign HIPAA business associate agreements. Cost per lead only means something once it's tied to the appointment, not the click.

**Local vs. service area**
Mobile IV is a service-area business needing radius or drive-time targeting matched to where your techs travel, while the boutique services need tighter geo around the physical location. Mixing the two into one campaign wastes spend outside the real area for one side or the other.

**What this costs**
Our model is a percentage of monthly ad spend, covering day-to-day optimization, negative keyword upkeep, ad copy testing, and the reporting below, plus a one-time $1,500 onboarding fee for account build, conversion tracking, and campaign structure by service line. The percentage itself depends on spend level.

**Reporting**
Every two weeks: total spend, leads generated, cost per lead, appointments booked, and what we're changing going into the next stretch, plus a live dashboard rather than waiting a month between updates.
