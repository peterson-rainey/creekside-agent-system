## SCREENING ANSWERS

**Experience with managing high budget accounts**

The largest account on our books runs around $100K a month on Meta and has held that level for twelve straight months, peaking at $114,587 in March and still running now. On Google specifically, the largest single account we have run spent $533,597 across twelve months and peaked at $65,682 in a month; that engagement has since ended. Across both platforms the book has placed about $3.8M in the last twelve months, currently around $249K a month.

So the honest read: we are comfortable operating at roughly $100K a month on one account, and $150K a month on a single Google account would be the largest we have run. You should weigh that rather than hear it for the first time in month two.

**Overall account performance**

The most useful account to show you is an ecommerce brand we ran on Google, because the shape of it is what actually happens when you scale non-brand rather than what a case study tends to say.

Non-branded campaigns, by quarter: 9.2x ROAS on $5,562 in the first quarter, then 5.7x on $11,860, then 4.6x on $19,791, then 3.8x on $9,353. Across the full engagement, non-brand ran $46,567 and 451 conversions at 5.3x. Branded search over the same period returned 28.7x, and the blended account number came out at 12.6x.

Two things worth taking from that. First, the early number is the one that flatters you: 9.2x came from the smallest quarter, and returns compressed as non-brand spend tripled. Some of that is the ordinary cost of reaching further into cold demand, though seasonality and creative fatigue sit in there too, and I would want the account's own data before telling you which. Second, the 12.6x blended figure is the one that looks best in a report and tells you least, because brand is carrying it.

On the health side, a naturopathic practice has run on Google continuously through the last twelve months at $22,592 and 561 conversions, around $40 each, and is still running.

You would get that same split in reporting from us: brand separated from non-brand, and non-brand tracked as it scales rather than averaged into a headline.
