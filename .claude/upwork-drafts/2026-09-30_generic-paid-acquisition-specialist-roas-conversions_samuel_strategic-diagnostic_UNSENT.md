One Meta account our team runs for a jewelry brand read 2.32x ROAS overall across a six-week window. Split by country, the US was returning 3.79x and the UK 0.59x. That kind of split is the first thing I'd look for in your account.

**Blended numbers hide the answer**

Account-level reporting showed nothing wrong on that jewelry account. One segment was carrying the blended number.

Lead gen has the same pattern. A local dental account's blended cost per lead sat near $30. $1,846 of that month's spend had gone to an engagement-objective campaign, which isn't built to produce leads, while the leads campaign beside it produced 124 at $15.77 each. The average made the whole account look mediocre when the part aimed at leads was doing well.

So before touching bids, creative, or audiences, the first pass is:

1. Is conversion tracking counting the right event, once, with value attached where it applies?
2. Where does the blended number split (campaign, objective, geo, placement, new vs returning), and which segments are carrying the rest?

That tells you where the next dollar should go before any new test is launched.

**How I'd run it after that**

- Weeks 1-2: tracking audit, segment breakdown, and a short written list of what to cut, scale, and test, ranked by expected impact
- Setup: new or rebuilt campaigns follow that split, with objective, geo, and new vs returning kept separate, and conversion events and naming locked before launch
- Ongoing: creative and audience tests, each with a read date set before launch
- Every two weeks: a written recommendation on what changed, why, and what's next, plus a live dashboard you can check anytime

**Two quick questions**

Which platforms are you running, and is the goal online sales or leads? And roughly where does monthly spend sit: under $5K, $5K to $20K, or above $20K?
