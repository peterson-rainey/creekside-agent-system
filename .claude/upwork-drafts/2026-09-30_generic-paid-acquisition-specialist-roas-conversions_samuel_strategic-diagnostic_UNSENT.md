The post doesn't name a platform, an offer, or a spend level, so I'll skip the generic pitch and show you the one thing I'd check first in any account, because it's where most "improve ROAS" projects are actually won or lost.

**The blended number is usually hiding the answer**

Account-level ROAS is an average, and averages bury the split that matters. On a Meta account our team runs for a jewelry brand, blended ROAS over a six-week window read 2.32x. Split by country, the US was returning 3.79x and the UK 0.59x. Nothing was "broken" at the account level. One segment was quietly funding another.

Same pattern on the lead-gen side. A local healthcare account showed a blended cost per lead around $30. Split by campaign, one campaign had spent $1,846 on an engagement objective with zero recorded conversions, while the leads campaign next to it produced 124 leads at $15.77 each. Same account, same month, same audience. The average made the whole thing look mediocre when half of it was working well.

So before touching bids, creative, or audiences, the first pass is always:

1. Is conversion tracking counting the right event, once, with value attached where it applies?
2. Where does the blended number split (campaign, objective, geo, placement, new vs returning)?
3. Which segments are carrying the account, and which are riding on them?

That usually tells you where the next dollar should go before any new test is launched.

**How I'd run it after that**

- Weeks 1-2: tracking audit, segment breakdown, and a short written list of what to cut, what to scale, and what to test, ranked by expected impact
- Ongoing: structured creative and audience tests with a defined read date, so results come from decisions and not from waiting
- Every two weeks: a clear written recommendation on what changed, why, and what's next, plus a live dashboard you can check anytime

**A few questions so I'm not guessing**

- Which platforms are you running today, and is this ecommerce (ROAS) or lead gen (cost per qualified lead)?
- What's your monthly ad spend roughly in? Under $5K, $5K to $20K, or above $20K?

Which of those numbers do you trust least right now: the conversions the platform reports, or what you see in your own sales data?
