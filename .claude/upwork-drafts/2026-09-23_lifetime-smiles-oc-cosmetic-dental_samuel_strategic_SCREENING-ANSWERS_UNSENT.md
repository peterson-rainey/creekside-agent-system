### 1. A healthcare or local-service client you helped grow, with real numbers

The closest match is a cosmetic dentistry practice our team runs on Google. Over the last ninety days it spent $52,741 and produced 252 patient inquiries, a blended $209 each. The spread inside that account is the useful part: Search came in at $135, Performance Max at $219, and Demand Gen, which serves YouTube and Discover, at $324. A second dental practice sits at $205 across $39,753 in the same window.

Outside dentistry, our team ran a hair restoration clinic to 690 patient inquiries at $134 on Google Ads, against procedures worth $8,000 to $15,000. A Sacramento practice case study is attached showing $14 to $40 per inquiry on a much smaller budget.

Worth being straight about one thing: every number above is cost per inquiry, not cost per booked appointment. We do not publish a booked-appointment cost or a show rate, because getting either honestly requires the booking system to report back to the ad platform. Most practices are not wired that way. Yours is not either, which is the next answer.

### 2. One specific thing you would change about our Instagram or website right away

Make the booking measurable, before anything creative.

Your Schedule Appointment button sends patients to NexHealth on a separate domain. Your Google Ads tag sits on your own site and not on that page, so the completed booking never reports back. Right now Google and Meta can see someone clicking toward a booking, but not the booking itself, so that number does not exist anywhere yet.

Two related things I found while looking. Your main site has the PixelYourSite plugin installed with no pixel configured, so there is no Meta signal at all and Instagram or Facebook would launch blind. Meanwhile your sleep center site does run a pixel, and it carries the same Google Ads tag as the main practice. Unless the conversion actions are split, appliance cases and smile makeovers are landing in one pool, which has bidding optimising across two very different case values.

This is days of work, not weeks, and it makes every number after it worth reading.

### 3. Your recommended starting monthly ad budget for a practice like ours

$6,000 to $8,000 a month to start, weighted heavily toward Google Search.

The reasoning is the cost spread above. On the comparable cosmetic dentistry account Search produces an inquiry at $135. Put $4,000 to $5,000 there and you are looking at roughly thirty inquiries a month, which is enough volume for Smart Bidding to learn on instead of guess. The remaining $2,000 to $3,000 goes to Meta, where the before-and-after library your team already shoots is the strongest asset you have.

I would hold YouTube and Shorts at the start. On that same account the YouTube and Discover side runs $324 per inquiry, around two and a half times Search. That inventory works better once there is conversion data to aim it at, and you will not have that until the booking tracking is fixed.

Expect it to scale toward $12,000 a month by month three or four if the early numbers hold. For reference, the comparable practice runs closer to $17,500 a month on Google alone.

### 4. Your approach for the first 30 days

Week one is measurement. Get a conversion firing on the completed NexHealth booking, get a Meta pixel on the main site, and split the conversion actions so sleep cases and cosmetic cases stop sharing a pool. Little else is worth doing until that is true.

Weeks two and three are the audit and the market work. A full read of both sites and both ad accounts, then competitor research across Santa Ana and Newport Beach, since you cover both. For cosmetic specifically I want to see who is bidding, roughly what they are paying, and where the gaps sit in what they all say.

Week four is the written plan and the first Search build. Search goes live first because it is the cheapest channel, the fastest to give feedback, and the one that produces the conversion data everything else depends on.

Meta follows once the pixel has some history behind it. Shorts and long-form get tested around week six.

That is Phase 1 as you scoped it, with the measurement work pulled to the front.
