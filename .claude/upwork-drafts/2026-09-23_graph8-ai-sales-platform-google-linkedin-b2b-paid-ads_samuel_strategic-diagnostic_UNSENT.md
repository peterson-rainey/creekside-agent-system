## PROPOSAL (paste-ready)

A free trial with no card on it and an enterprise demo cannot share one conversion action. The trial will always be cheaper to buy, so a campaign bidding on the blend drifts toward self-serve signups and away from the deals that actually pay for the program.

The clearest version of this we have measured was brand against non-brand search on an ecommerce account, running nearly identical money over the same ten months, $25,496 against $27,183. Branded came in at $16.90 per conversion on $0.35 clicks. Non-brand cost $104.25 on $1.26 clicks. Blended, that account reads as one tidy business. Set a target off the blend and the algorithm hits it the easy way, by buying more of the people already typing the brand name. A free-tier signup sitting in the same conversion action as a demo request does the same thing to a B2B account, and the fix is the same: separate campaigns, separate targets, each judged on its own math.

Revenue makes it harder still. Google will not import an offline conversion uploaded more than 90 days after the last click, or 63 days with enhanced conversions for leads, so on deals that run past a quarter the closed-won number never makes it back into the account. Revenue stays the scoreboard and bidding learns from the earliest stage your sales team will vouch for. Imported conversions also report back to the day of the click rather than the day they happened, which is why the newest month always looks worse than it will end up, and why that is usually the month someone proposes cutting a campaign that is working.

The useful part is that the stage definitions and the intent signals already live in your own platform, so the measurement build is mostly a matter of getting them back out to Google cleanly, with click IDs and UTMs stored on every form fill so a qualified stage has something to match back to. The same discipline decides when a test is actually readable. A free signup will hand you a winning ad or audience on raw volume long before there are enough qualified-stage conversions underneath it to mean anything, and calling that early costs more than calling nothing.

On the work itself, our team runs an AI medical scribe sold to clinicians, live on Meta for over a year and $116,000 in the last twelve months, with testing split by clinical specialty and by message angle alongside a larger general campaign.

The attached case study covers a B2B software company that split the same way, Meta building awareness for a category its buyers did not know existed and Google capturing the search demand once it did. It reports inbound leads and ARR both doubling over six months, though that window also contains a seed round, so the credit there is shared. The split is the part worth a conversation, because the category you sell into is one people do not yet search for by name.

The gap is LinkedIn. Our team buys Google and Meta and has not run paid LinkedIn, so there is no track record there worth asking you to take on faith. I would not ask you to fund it on faith either. What I would want to see first is what LinkedIn has actually returned for graph8 so far, measured at the meeting stage rather than at the form fill, and whether that clears the bar Google is already setting. We would also come to this as a team rather than a single hire, with a dedicated conversion tracking specialist on the measurement build.

What does graph8 count as the qualified stage today, and does the free trial currently fire the same conversion action as a demo request?
