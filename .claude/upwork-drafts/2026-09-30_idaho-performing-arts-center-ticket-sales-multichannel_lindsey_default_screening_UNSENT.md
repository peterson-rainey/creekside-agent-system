I wouldn't build 10 to 20 separate campaigns. Each show would get too few sales for Meta to learn from, and the budget gets spread thin. I'd run one always-on season campaign aimed at past buyers and lookalikes of them, then add a short push for each show in the two to three weeks before its date. Every show uses the same structure, naming and audience set, so setup is mostly swapping in creative, dates and the ticket link. Shows with similar crowds (family, classical, comedy) share audiences, so each one isn't starting from zero.

3.
I haven't managed a Google Ad Grant myself, and I don't want to overstate it. The Grant rules I work around are that the account has to hold a 5% click-through rate, conversion tracking has to be in place, and it's limited to Search. Grant money can't be spent on Meta, so I'd treat the Grant as its own channel and keep Meta focused on the purchase.

4.
Start with the tracking, so sales from each show report back with their value. Then, a few weeks out from each launch, build warm audiences (past buyers, email list, video viewers) so the push has somewhere to go on day one. Use short clips from rehearsals or past shows, tested against poster-style ads. Move budget toward the shows that are selling slower as their dates get close, and use email to past buyers alongside the ads.
