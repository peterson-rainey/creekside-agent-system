## COVER LETTER

Native will quote you click prices that make Meta look expensive. That is the trap, and it is the specific thing that breaks a margin floor.

A time-matched cut from a paving account our team runs, same market, near-identical spend, 2025-11-29 through 2026-04-17:

Search: $5,752 spend, 43.8 leads, $131.22 per lead, $14.53 CPC
Performance Max: $6,090 spend, 21.0 leads, $289.38 per lead, $2.71 CPC

The cheap-click channel produced clicks 5.4x cheaper and leads 2.2x more expensive. Anyone grading on traffic cost kills the wrong campaign. On native that gap is wider, because quality varies per widget and per publisher, not per campaign.

Before you read further, the thing you screened for: I have not personally run spend on any of your five platforms. Our book is Google and Meta. You asked for two of five in the last 12 months and I would be starting at zero on all five. If that closes it, it closes it, and I would rather you know now than three paragraphs in.

What I would bring is the part that does not change across platforms. We manage roughly $1.2M a year on Google alone and have run over $20M across 200+ accounts, all of it lead gen where the lead has to resell or the account dies. I have spent six years buying to a cost-per-outcome floor, blocking placements that convert on paper and not in the CRM, and rebuilding attribution when the platform number and the client number disagree.

On tracking specifically: I would not trust native platform pixels for attribution. Postback to a tracker, pass a click ID through the form, and reconcile to your own lead record. Every lead ties back to platform, creative, widget and publisher, or the optimization is guesswork. Your dev handles the server-side leg, I specify the parameters and own the reconciliation.

On roofing, the short version of how I would open it:

Native is interruption traffic, not intent traffic. Nobody on NewsBreak is searching for a roof. So the unit of work is the angle, not the keyword. Storm events, roof age, and insurance eligibility are the three triggers that make a homeowner stop mid-article, and they are targetable by ZIP against housing stock age and recent weather claims.

Your quiz funnel is likely the wrong first page here. It works on Meta and Google because those users arrive with intent or social context. Native readers arrive mid-article with none. I would put an advertorial bridge in front of the quiz, earn the click-through, then hand off to the form you already know converts. Test that split before touching bids.

First 30 days is blocklist discipline. Site and widget level exclusions daily, not weekly. On most native accounts a minority of publishers eat the majority of the waste, and the account does not find its floor until they are gone.

Budget allocation across platforms comes last, not first. Two platforms to signal, then move money toward whichever holds CPL at volume.

Happy to walk through the tracking spec or the roofing angle map in more detail if it is useful.

## SCREENING QUESTION 2
General strategy for roofing across platforms?

1. Economics first. Back the target cost per lead out of your resale price and the 30% floor, per vertical and per ZIP tier. That number sets bids, not platform benchmarks.

2. Angle before creative. Roofing on native sells on storm damage, roof age, and insurance eligibility. Build three angle families, four headline variants each, and let thumbnails ride a fixed control until the angle winner is clear. Native thumbnails swing performance harder than copy, so isolate one variable at a time.

3. Landing page. Advertorial bridge into your existing quiz, not the quiz cold. Measure bridge click-through and quiz completion separately so a bad bridge does not read as a bad angle.

4. Launch order. NewsBreak and Taboola first, broad geo, aggressive daily blocking, no bid optimization for the first week while the tracker fills.

5. Scale rule. Hold a platform flat until it proves CPL stability at 2x the test budget. Native decays as you scale into worse inventory, so the scale test is the real test.

6. Measurement. Tracker is the source of truth. Platform reporting is directional only.
