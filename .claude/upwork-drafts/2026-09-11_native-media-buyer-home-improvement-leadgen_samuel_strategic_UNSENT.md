## FINAL PROPOSAL (single block, paste as-is)

Native will quote you click prices that make Meta look expensive. That is the trap, and it is the specific thing that breaks a margin floor.

A time-matched cut from a paving account our team runs, same market, near-identical spend, 2025-11-29 through 2026-04-17:

Search: $5,752 spend, 43.8 leads, $131.22 per lead, $14.53 CPC
Performance Max: $6,090 spend, 21.0 leads, $289.38 per lead, $2.71 CPC

The cheap-click channel produced clicks 5.4x cheaper and leads 2.2x more expensive. Anyone grading on traffic cost kills the wrong campaign. On native that gap is wider, because quality varies per widget and per publisher, not per campaign.

Spend on your five platforms: zero.

No NewsBreak, Taboola, Outbrain, MSN or RevContent, and no hands-on time in Voluum, RedTrack or Everflow. You asked for two of five in the last 12 months and I would be starting at zero on all five. If that closes it, it closes it, and I would rather you know now than three paragraphs in.

Home improvement, and what does carry over:

The paving and asphalt account above is ours, active, and lead gen. Over 12 months it has run $79,433 at 373.6 leads, a $212.60 blended cost per lead, with a best stretch of 35.5 leads in 12 days at $119.54 after cutting cost per lead 31% off the prior matched window. We have also run lawn and landscaping, gutter and construction trades accounts on the same model, all selling to homeowners. The paving account is the one still live today. All of it is Google and Meta. None of it is native.

Across the book we manage roughly $1.2M a year on Google alone and have run over $20M across 200+ accounts, all of it lead gen where the lead has to resell or the account dies. I have spent six years buying to a cost-per-outcome floor, blocking placements that convert on paper and not in the CRM, and rebuilding attribution when the platform number and the client number disagree.

How I would open roofing:

Native is interruption traffic, not intent traffic. Nobody on NewsBreak is searching for a roof, so the unit of work is the angle, not the keyword.

1. Economics first. Back the target cost per lead out of your resale price and the 30% floor, per vertical and per ZIP tier. That number sets bids, not platform benchmarks.

2. Angle before creative. Storm damage, roof age and insurance eligibility are the three triggers that stop a homeowner mid-article, and they are targetable by ZIP against housing stock age and recent weather claims. Build three angle families, four headlines each, and hold thumbnails on a fixed control until the angle winner is clear. Native thumbnails swing performance harder than copy, so isolate one variable at a time.

3. Landing page. I would not assume your quiz carries over. It works on Meta and Google because those users arrive with intent or social context. Native readers arrive mid-article with none. Put an advertorial bridge in front of the quiz, earn the click-through, then hand off to the form you already know converts. Measure bridge click-through and quiz completion separately so a bad bridge does not read as a bad angle.

4. Launch order. NewsBreak and Taboola first, broad geo, aggressive daily blocking, no bid optimization for the first week while the tracker fills.

5. Blocklist discipline. Site and widget exclusions daily, not weekly. On most native accounts a minority of publishers eat the majority of the waste, and the account does not find its floor until they are gone.

6. Tracking. I would not trust native platform pixels for attribution. Postback to a tracker, pass a click ID through the form, reconcile to your own lead record. Every lead ties back to platform, creative, widget and publisher, or the optimization is guesswork. Your dev handles the server-side leg, I specify the parameters and own the reconciliation.

7. Scale rule. Hold a platform flat until it proves CPL stability at 2x the test budget. Native decays as you scale into worse inventory, so the scale test is the real test. Budget allocation across platforms comes last, not first.
