When a wellness brand sells on both Shopify and Amazon, the hardest problem is almost never the campaign build. It is that roughly half the revenue the ads produce happens somewhere the pixel cannot see. Amazon never fires your event, and Amazon Attribution reports after the fact instead of feeding Meta's optimizer in real time. So Meta learns from the Shopify half only, and quietly concludes the buyers it cannot see are not worth finding.

That single fact changes what "properly structured and tracked" has to mean here, and it is checkable in an afternoon.

Three things I would verify before touching a budget, because one of them is usually the whole story:

What event the account actually optimizes to. Purchase gets swapped for add to cart or view content when the volume column looks thin. Meta then buys the cheapest possible version of a person who does that shallow thing. The dashboard improves and the deposits do not.

Whether the ad sets ever exit learning. Meta needs roughly 50 conversions per ad set per week. Budget split across six or eight ad sets on a modest conversion count means none of them ever learn, so all of them are permanently guessing. Consolidation feels like giving up control right until cost per purchase drops.

Whether prospecting and retargeting are bidding against each other. If warm buyers were never excluded from the cold campaigns, the account pays a premium to reach people you already own. Invisible in the campaign view, obvious in audience overlap.

You get those findings in writing, whether or not the diagnosis flatters whoever built the account. Then structure, launch on your existing creative, and a testing framework that judges an angle on the signal it controls rather than on a blended return it does not.

Two accounts, both ecommerce, both mine to describe honestly.

A US replacement-parts ecommerce brand on Meta. Retargeting had fragmented into six thin campaigns chasing shallow events, so we consolidated into one conversions campaign and cut creative on click-through rate rather than on return. Over the following quarter cost per purchase went from $34.72 to $19.89 and return went 5.62x to 8.57x, with cost per thousand impressions flat across the window at $8.10 and $8.18. The flat auction cost is the part that matters, because it means the gain was structure and creative, not cheaper media.

A Shopify consumer-products brand on Google, which is the cross-platform half of your question. We built it, then opened a non-brand lane deliberately. Lifetime non-brand Search runs about 5.4x and non-brand Shopping about 5.6x. Branded search runs far higher and I would not quote you the blended number, because a blend that includes brand terms tells you how well your brand is doing, not how well the ads are working. Separating those two is the same discipline your Meta, Amazon and Google numbers will need before any of them can be compared.
