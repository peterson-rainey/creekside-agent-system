**2. How do you use metrics to inform your strategy?**

Metrics decide two separate things: what gets scaled, and what gets believed. The believing part has to come first.

The reverse mortgage account is the clean example. Performance Max there reported roughly $33 per conversion while Search reported about $97, same account, same window. Read straight off the dashboard, that says move the budget into PMax. Worth slowing down there. Those two campaign types rarely count the same conversion actions, and the likeliest read on a gap that size is PMax taking credit for the cheapest and weakest of them rather than genuinely acquiring three times cheaper. Either way the number is not comparable until someone has checked. So the first move on any account is opening the conversion actions and working out what each number actually counts, before treating cost per conversion as comparable across campaign types. Most bad budget decisions in paid search trace back to skipping that.

After that the loop is fairly plain. Search terms get reviewed weekly early on, and most of the first month's gains come out of the negative list rather than out of bid changes. Campaign types get judged on cost per qualified outcome, not blended cost per conversion. Where a CRM exists, qualified and closed stages get pushed back to the platform so bidding trains on pipeline instead of form volume. Google's offline import window closes at ninety days, so that has to be built early rather than backfilled later.

The two things metrics cannot settle are creative and offer. Those get tested deliberately, not inferred from a report.

On reporting itself, ours runs every two weeks alongside a live dashboard, so the numbers stay visible between check-ins rather than only at them.
