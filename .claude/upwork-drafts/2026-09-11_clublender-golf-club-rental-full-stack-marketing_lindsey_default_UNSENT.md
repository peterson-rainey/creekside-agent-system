Do you know what share of your bookings come from golfers who have rented from you before? That one number decides where the budget and the effort should go first, more than any campaign setting will.

The reason I ask is that a rental like yours gets booked after the trip is already planned, so the person Meta needs to find is someone who has a flight and a tee time but has not yet decided against hauling their own bag. That is a narrow moment and it is hard to target cold. Usually the cheapest bookings sit with people who already know you: past renters with another trip coming, and site visitors who checked a date and left.

That warm pool is smaller than it looks, though. On a consumer brand I ran on Meta, I compared retargeting against cold prospecting over the same three weeks. Retargeting came in at $87.61 per purchase and prospecting at $102.14. Cheaper, but not by the margin most people expect, and because the pool was small it never absorbed much more budget. So retargeting does not replace prospecting. It just stops you overpaying for people who were already coming back.

That is why I would look at email alongside the ads rather than after them. I build Klaviyo flows hands on, and the details are what make them work. On a luxury mattress brand, the existing abandoned cart flow was set to exclude anyone who had started checkout. A new cart flow built without that same exclusion would have emailed the same shopper twice alongside the checkout flow. For you, the flows that matter most are repeat-trip and seasonal win-back, timed to when your golfers actually travel.

That account has since finished, so I am happy to walk you through the full numbers on a call. There is also a short video on my profile that walks through how I run accounts like this.

What is your monthly Meta spend roughly, and is it closer to a test budget or a meaningful share of revenue?
