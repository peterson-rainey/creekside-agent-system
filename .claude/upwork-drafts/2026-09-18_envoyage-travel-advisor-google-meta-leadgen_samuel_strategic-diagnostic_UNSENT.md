Most paid media programs where a person closes the lead share one blind spot. Google and Meta only ever learn who filled out the form. They never learn which inquiries turned into a consultation with an advisor, so they keep finding more people who fill out forms.

With 55 employee advisors and an independent network, I'd expect that to show up for you in a specific way. An inquiry routed to an employee advisor and one routed to an independent agency probably count the same in both platforms today, even though the employee team is the priority. If they count the same, the campaigns can't tell them apart either.

Three things I'd look at first:

1. What each platform is optimizing toward. If both are bidding on the raw inquiry, the fix is feeding a later stage back from your CRM, like a consultation booked with an employee advisor. On Google, imported conversions have to come in within 90 days of the click (63 for enhanced conversions for leads). A trip can easily book later than that, so the consultation is the signal to train on, not the booking.

2. How Meta leads are captured. Meta's conversion leads goal, which optimizes toward leads that reach a later CRM stage, only works with Instant Forms, needs roughly 200 leads a month, and needs the stage recorded within 28 days. At your spend that's reachable, but it changes the form versus landing page decision, and it's worth making on purpose.

3. What the monthly report will undercount. Google reports a conversion on the date of the click, not the date the inquiry came in. A report pulled in the first five business days will show late-month clicks light, and they fill in afterward. Worth agreeing up front how that's shown so the monthly review doesn't chase a dip that isn't real.

On the handoff model itself: we ran Google and Meta for a reverse mortgage lender where every lead went to a loan officer to close, at about $50,000 a month on Google alone. We also ran a personal injury firm where leads went to intake before becoming cases. That case study is attached: 619 conversions on $56.6K in spend, about $950 per case on total spend. Neither is a current client. We haven't run a travel advisor or agency account, so I can't show you a travel example.

Our dedicated conversion tracking specialist handles GA4 and GTM, so when tracking breaks it gets fixed, not just noted in the report. Day to day, a senior account manager on our team runs the builds, pacing, search terms and testing, with me on strategy and in the monthly review with your US and Global Digital teams. Anything off between reviews gets flagged when we see it, not saved for the report.

When an inquiry comes in today, does your CRM record whether it went to an employee advisor or an independent agency, and whether it became a consultation?
