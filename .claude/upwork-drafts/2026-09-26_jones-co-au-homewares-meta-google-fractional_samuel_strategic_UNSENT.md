## PROPOSAL

Selling through stockists as well as Shopify changes how the Google side should be read. Performance Max and Shopping will bid on searches for your own product names, and some of those shoppers were already heading to a retailer that carries you, so the account reports sales the ads didn't really create. Splitting brand and product-name terms out, and reading non-brand return on its own, shows where spend is actually buying new customers.

**On the Claude layer**

The first finding I'd challenge is attribution. Meta and Google will both claim the same Shopify order, so platform revenue added together runs higher than what Shopify actually banked. We judge total spend against Shopify revenue and new-customer orders, then use each platform's own numbers to decide where the money goes inside it. Klaviyo matters too, since a repeat customer who clicks a Meta ad after an email isn't new demand.

**The account you asked about**

A US replacement parts store we took over on Meta at about US$15,600 a month. We inherited 11 campaigns spending, 232 live ads, and targeting set to every language. We retired the small overlapping campaigns, consolidated to 7, and built one new sales campaign. Cost per conversion went from $34.72 in the inherited month to $19.89 by April with CPM flat around $8, and sat at $22.91 in May. Targeting and creative changed in the same stretch, so no single change owns that result. On Google, a store selling laptop screen extenders ran non-branded Shopping at 5.5x over about ten months at roughly US$8,000 a month. We haven't run an Australian store yet.
