When a broad campaign with no interest selection underperforms, is it the targeting that failed, or the objective sitting underneath it? Most audits answer that backwards, and it changes what you fix first.

The reason to ask is a replacement parts brand I ran on Meta for about nine months. Two broad campaigns, no interest targeting on either, running side by side. One returned 4.99x and earned about $65,000 of the budget. The other returned 3.37x on roughly $6,000 before it was retired. Same audience definition, same catalog, same creative pool. What separated them was the objective and the conversion event underneath it.

So the honest answer to when broad works is that broad is a signal decision, not a targeting decision. It performs when the account is feeding Meta a purchase event with enough weekly volume to learn from, and it quietly wastes budget when it is layered onto a shallower event. That is where most of the accounts I audit are losing money, and it is not something creative can fix.

That same account had the overlap problem too. Six small campaigns were dividing one warm pool between them, each too thin to get out of learning. All six came down inside three weeks and the spend consolidated into one. Splitting an audience across separate campaigns costs more than it appears to, because every split restarts learning.

Ecommerce is most of my ten years, including a brand I built and sold myself, so an audit here would start with events, structure and settings before it touches creative.
