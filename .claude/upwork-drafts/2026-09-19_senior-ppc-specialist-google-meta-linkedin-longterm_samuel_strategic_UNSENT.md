## PROPOSAL

With Google Ads, Meta and LinkedIn all sharing one budget, the platform that looks worst is often just the one with the least money behind it. Meta needs roughly 50 conversions a week per ad set to get out of its learning phase, and when spend is split three ways the smallest slice rarely gets there. Its cost per result stays high and jumpy, and it gets cut before it ever had a fair read.

So before launching anything new, we'd work out what each platform can realistically buy at its budget. Where one can't reach enough real conversions, there are two honest options: concentrate the money until it can, or optimize to an earlier step like a form submit and accept a weaker signal. Which one fits depends on how long your sales cycle is.

The other trap is judging on the wrong number. On a paving contractor account our team runs, Performance Max bought clicks at $3.00 against $14.53 on Search at near-identical spend, yet Search produced leads at $131 versus $268. A report that stopped at cost per click would have moved budget the wrong way. On Meta, we ran lead forms into a dental group's call center that produced 2,558 leads at $25.31 each over 90 days. A lead that cheap only pays off if someone calls it back fast, which is why we'd report on what happens after the form, not just what it cost.

To be straight about LinkedIn: our team hasn't run LinkedIn Ads. We run Google and Meta every day, with a dedicated tracking specialist handling conversion setup, so if LinkedIn is a large share of the spend, that's worth weighing. This would also be run by our team rather than one person, so the account never depends on a single person's availability.

Roughly what's the monthly spend across all three today, closer to $5K or $50K?
