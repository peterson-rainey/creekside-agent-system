When a Facebook lead for a water heater comes in at two in the afternoon, who calls it back, and how fast?

I ask because the cheap part of Meta lead gen is the lead itself. On a dental group I run, Meta forms brought in 2,558 leads on $64,747 between late April and mid July, about $25 each. Volume stopped being the problem after month one. Callback speed did not. Someone who filled out a form about a slow drain calls the next company if nobody reaches them that hour.

A $25 Meta form and a $190 Google search call are also not the same buyer, and when both land in one leads column the cheap one wins the budget while the phone stays quiet. Our team runs Google for a Virginia paving contractor at $205 a lead across the year. Inside that, one market split $131 on Search against $289 on Performance Max on almost the same spend, with Performance Max clicks a fifth the price.

Your six questions, gaps first.

Water mitigation is a real gap and dressing it up would not help either of us.

Cost per lead: most lead-form campaigns on my local accounts land between $16 and $37, some above $50. The paving account sits at $205 across the year on Google. Connecticut plumbing will have its own range.

Meta and Instagram are mine, 10+ years of them. Google Search, Local Services Ads and the measurement build run through a Google specialist and a tracking specialist on our team.

Tracking: instant forms and site events through the Conversions API, then booked and sold stages sent back from your CRM so Meta optimizes toward jobs instead of form fills.
