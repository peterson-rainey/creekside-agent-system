Hi there,

When Google and LinkedIn run side by side, they can easily claim the same lead. Someone sees a LinkedIn ad on Tuesday, searches your company name on Thursday and fills in the form. Google gets the conversion, and LinkedIn may count the same person too. Neither number on its own tells you what the channel is worth, which is how a channel that is doing its job can end up with its budget cut.

One thing up front: our team's paid work is Google and Meta, not LinkedIn, so I won't claim a track record there. We'd own Google and the measurement across both channels, and judge LinkedIn on the same qualified-lead standard so budget decisions between the two rest on real numbers. If your LinkedIn setup needs a ground-up rebuild, that part isn't ours to claim.

**What I'd look at first**

1. Conversion setup on both accounts. What each one counts, whether the two are measuring the same event, and whether anything is counted twice inside Google (a Google Ads form conversion plus the same form imported from GA4 is a common one).
2. Branded search in its own campaign with its own target, so branded demand stops flattering Google's average. We'd also watch branded search volume against LinkedIn flight dates. That's a signal, not proof, of what LinkedIn is creating.
3. Search terms and structure on Google, so budget can follow the campaigns producing qualified leads.
4. What LinkedIn has returned so far, measured at the qualified stage rather than the form fill.

**Getting lead quality back into Google Ads**

Qualified leads and closed deals go back into Google Ads with a value from your CRM or wherever deals are tracked, so bidding learns which leads to buy more of. Google won't import a conversion uploaded more than 90 days after the click (63 days with enhanced conversions for leads), so on a longer sales cycle we'd send an earlier stage your team trusts, like a booked meeting. If qualified leads only come in at a handful a month, bidding can't learn from them reliably, so we'd optimize to that earlier stage and use qualified leads to judge it. Our tracking specialist builds this and tests every form path before any bids change. A Google Ads specialist on our team runs your Google account day to day, and you'd have a live dashboard plus a written breakdown every two weeks by channel and campaign.

**Testing new channels**

Each test gets its own budget, one success measure at the qualified stage, and a read date set before it starts. A new channel often looks good on raw lead volume before there are enough qualified leads underneath to mean anything, so we don't call a winner early. Meta is one candidate I'd weigh, most likely retargeting site visitors, once I know who you sell to and how much traffic there is to work with.

**Relevant work**

A B2B software company where Meta built awareness for a category its buyers didn't know existed yet, and Google captured the search demand once it did. Over six months inbound leads and ARR both doubled, though those months followed a seed round and ad spend roughly doubled at month three, so the credit is shared.

On Google alone, for a consumer bankruptcy law firm in Orange County, we restructured the account into market-specific campaigns. Compared with the prior period, leads went from 117 to 229 and cost per lead from $86 to $50.29. I've attached both case studies.

**A few questions**

1. Roughly what's the combined monthly spend across Google and LinkedIn today: under $5K, $5K to $15K, or above that?
2. What does your team count as a qualified lead, and is that stage recorded anywhere the ad platforms could read it?
3. What has LinkedIn produced so far at that stage, compared with Google?

With those I can tell you where I'd start.
