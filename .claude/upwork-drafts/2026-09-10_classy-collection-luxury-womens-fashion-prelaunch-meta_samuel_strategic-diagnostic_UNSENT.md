## PROPOSAL

Four campaign types are named in the brief and two of them are where pre-launch brands lose their first sixty days. I pulled our own Meta book before writing this. Across every account we run, campaigns set to awareness objectives have spent $15,284 over 56 campaigns and recorded zero conversions. Traffic objectives, 82 campaigns and $32,515, recorded 70. Sales objectives over the same book spent $586,299 and recorded 6,323 purchases at roughly $93 each. That is not an argument that awareness is worthless. It is an argument that a brand with no pixel history cannot afford to spend its first weeks teaching Meta what a click looks like instead of what a buyer looks like, because on launch day the algorithm has learned exactly what you paid it to learn.

The second thing worth saying before anyone quotes you a retainer. Retargeting cannot carry a launch that has not happened. For the first three or four weeks there is no pool to retarget, so the real budget question is not how to split cold and warm, it is what fraction of cold spend exists purely to build the pool and how fast you can get it big enough to matter. When we did have a warm pool to work with on a social-native DTC brand, warm came in at $87.61 against $102.14 on cold, time matched. Useful, real, and not the miracle multiple people expect.

The third is the audience-testing plan, which for luxury fashion usually starts with interest stacks. In that same account, same quarter, fashion and lifestyle interest targeting ran $336.08 per purchase. Broad purchase targeting in the same account ran $62.06. Meta's own model beat the interest list by more than five to one. I would still test interests for a luxury buyer, but I would not let them own the majority of the budget on the assumption that they should.

On proof, straight, because you should know what you are buying. We do not have a luxury womens fashion account with citable results. The one apparel brand in the book has no reportable ad account and another apparel account ran $16.7K without a recorded purchase. What we do have that is the same shape is the consumer brand above, $58,298 spent, 623 purchases at $93.58, and a high-AOV DTC brand where our team spent $105,010 over four months for 343 purchases at $306 with CPM running near $51. That second one matters more for you than the first, because it is the honest picture of what considered-purchase economics look like at a real price point rather than an impulse one. Also worth stating plainly, we hold no platform certifications.

Two things I would need before putting numbers on a plan. Your target price point and gross margin per unit, since that is what sets maximum allowable CAC and there is no honest budget recommendation without it. And roughly what monthly budget you are picturing relative to what you have set aside for the launch overall.

Our team handles pixel, event setup, UTMs and GA4 in house, and reporting is against the conversion event we agree on up front rather than against a ROAS number that means nothing in week one.
