## Answers

**1. Describe a marketing campaign you built for a local business or community-focused brand. What channels did you use and what were the measurable results?**

Two local clients our team works with now.

A dental practice on Meta. Last month our team ran two campaigns side by side in the same account, one set to engagement and one to leads, launched the same day. The engagement campaign got nearly twice the impressions and more clicks, but no recorded leads. The leads campaign produced 124 at $15.77 each. It's a good example of why we judge every campaign on a real action rather than reach.

A naturopathic clinic in Sacramento on Google Search. Over the last twelve months it has brought in about 568 conversions at around $40 each, with the account restructured around specific, local searches for the services the clinic offers.

Neither is a directory or a community organization, so there's no directory result I can point you to. For Sunshine Pages the real actions would be listing claims and upgrades, and for the Institute, bookings and space rentals.

**2. How have you used a CRM (ideally Zoho) to drive segmented email campaigns? Describe a sequence you built and its performance.**

Our email work so far has been for ecommerce brands in Klaviyo, not Zoho. A recent build for one brand was a four-email welcome series plus abandoned checkout and abandoned cart sequences, set up as drafts awaiting the brand's sign-off, so there's no performance data from it. Here's how it's put together instead.

Emails two through four of the welcome series stop for anyone who buys. The cart sequence checks before each send that the person hasn't ordered or started checkout, because without that check someone who reaches checkout gets both sequences at once.

On the CRM side, a dental practice we run ads for sends leads from both ad platforms into its GoHighLevel pipeline, where a lead can be traced from its ad to a booked consultation.

For you, the same logic would run off your Zoho CRM fields. A provider who claims a listing leaves the claim reminders and starts onboarding. An upgrade removes them from upgrade offers. Seekers and providers never share a sequence, and anyone the founder has just met in person is held out of automated asks for a set window.

**3. How would you market two related brands (a wellness institute and its directory product) without blurring or diluting either?**

By organizing around the person rather than the brand, and keeping the directory visibly independent.

Both brands reach the same two people. A practitioner might claim a listing, upgrade it, or rent space at the Institute. A seeker might search the directory, book a practitioner, or take a class. Ad audiences and email segments get built around those two, and each person hears from one brand at a time based on what they did last. Someone who just claimed a listing gets Sunshine Pages onboarding that week, not an Institute rental offer.

The part I'd protect most is the directory's neutrality. A practitioner deciding whether to list may wonder whether a directory owned by a place that also offers practitioner services will send clients to its own rooms first. So Sunshine Pages recruiting should stand on its own: the free listing, who searches the directory, and what an upgrade adds. The Institute shows up as one listing among many, and cross-promotion runs mostly from the Institute toward the directory.

Reporting stays separate too. Claims and upgrades for Sunshine Pages, bookings and rentals for the Institute, each with its own cost, never blended into one number.

**4. Share your experience balancing marketing automation with a personal, human-first customer experience.**

Automation decides when something sends and who gets it. People handle anything someone replies to.

The clearest lesson I have is about capacity. A dental implant practice we ran Meta ads for sent its leads to a call center, and when the callers couldn't keep up, budgets were cut back to what they could handle. A lead nobody calls back in time isn't a cheap lead.

For you, the founder's visits are that capacity. I'd pace provider outreach to the in-person conversations that fit in a week, and log each visit in Zoho CRM so that person is held out of automated emails for a set window afterward. Replies go to a person, and nothing automated answers on the founder's behalf.

One Zoho setting shapes this. The scheduled sync between Zoho CRM and Zoho Campaigns runs daily at most, so a visit logged after that day's sync isn't visible to Campaigns until the next run. Immediate sync updates in five to ten minutes but can't filter which records it syncs. Which one you pick decides how long that hold-out window needs to be.
