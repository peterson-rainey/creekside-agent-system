Selling flat-pack furniture on the big UK marketplaces and your own site changes what a good ROAS target looks like. Some shoppers your site ads reach would have bought the same wardrobe from your marketplace listings anyway, so a site ROAS figure can look healthy while mostly moving sales between channels. The target that holds up is set per product group on margin after delivery and returns, because a bulky order can cost more to ship than a small one earns, and it gets checked against total business revenue, not only what Google or Meta reports.

Two e-commerce accounts our team has run (figures in USD):

A replacement parts store on Meta, spending $12,000 to $15,000 a month. In January, the month before we took it over, cost per conversion was $34.72 at 5.6x Meta-reported ROAS. By April it was $19.89 at 8.6x, and May held at $22.91 and 7.0x. CPM stayed flat around $8 throughout, so traffic did not get cheaper.

A multi-screen monitor brand on Google, built from launch and run from November 2025 until it paused in August 2026. Non-brand Search returned 5.31x at a $104 CPA on about $27,000, and non-brand Shopping 5.47x at a $97 CPA on about $18,000. Branded search ran at 28.7x and was reported separately. Blended in, Search would have read about 16x and said nothing about new customers.

Closest to your category, we also ran Meta for a premium furniture brand, about $105,000 over four months. CPM nearly doubled from May to July, a stretch with a big sitewide sale, and that is the pressure to plan for on furniture: creative volume has to keep pace with budget.

How our team would audit your setup:

1. Tracking. Match purchases and revenue in Google Ads, Meta and GA4 against real orders, check for double counting, and confirm whether VAT sits in the value (that alone overstates ROAS by 20%).
2. Split brand from non-brand and new customers from returning, so ROAS reflects acquisition.
3. Merchant Center feed: dimensions, materials and assembly in titles, disapprovals, and whether marketplace ads for your own products compete with your Shopping ads.
4. Margin by product group, to set a break-even ROAS for each not one account-wide number.
5. A short list of fixes ranked by profit impact, before any budget goes up.
