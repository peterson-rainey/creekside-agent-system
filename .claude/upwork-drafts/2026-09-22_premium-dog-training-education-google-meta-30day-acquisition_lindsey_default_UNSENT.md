## Proposal

When someone asks "how much is the program?" under one of your ads, does that comment land on one post, or on one of several copies of the same ad?

Ads built fresh inside each ad set usually get their own post, so comments and questions split across copies you only see one at a time, and the moderation work multiplies. Building from one existing post keeps everything in one thread, so an answer you've approved stays visible to the next person reading.

The same kind of split hides in the numbers. On a local dental practice I run, an engagement campaign and a leads campaign ran side by side last month on similar budgets. The engagement one got nearly twice the impressions and not one recorded lead. The leads campaign brought in 124 at $15.77 each. Blended, the account read as $30.65 a lead, which hides both stories. That's the first thing I'd check in your existing campaigns, along with where the daily spend goes: which ones are buying attention and which are buying students. Before trusting those numbers, I'd confirm your pixel and conversion events still fire, since a campaign that suddenly stops converting is often broken tracking, not the ad.

On a high-ticket dental implant account, I checked the practice's CRM for who booked a consultation, and kept one campaign running even though its leads cost more, because they were booking. For a premium training program I'd want the same view of what happens after the click, not just what the click cost.

I haven't run a dog-training account, so I'd work from your guidelines on comments and escalate anything program-specific. Google Ads and the keyword and search-intent research would sit with a Google specialist I work with, the same setup I have on three stores.

After 10+ years in this, including building and selling my own e-commerce business, the accounts that go well are the ones where someone says plainly what the data shows, including when a test didn't work.
