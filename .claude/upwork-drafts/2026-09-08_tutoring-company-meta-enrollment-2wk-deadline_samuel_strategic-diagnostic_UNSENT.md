## PROPOSAL (4,259 characters, under the 5,000 limit)

The decision that will settle how many students you get is not the targeting or the creative. It is which event
you tell Meta to optimize for, and it has to be right on day one, because changing it restarts learning and
there is no second learning period inside a window this size.

Meta wants roughly 50 optimization events per ad set per week before that ad set leaves learning. If the event
you pick is a paid enrollment, most tutoring programs do not enroll 50 students in a week, let alone per ad set,
so the ad set never stabilizes and spends the whole run in the expensive, volatile part of delivery. If the
event is a trial class booked, a seat held, or an info session registered, 50 a week is reachable, the ad set
settles around day seven, and the back half runs on something Meta has actually learned. Enrollment becomes the
number you report on, not the one you bid on.

That changes how the two weeks get shaped. Week one should not be judged on cost per enrollment. It should be
judged on how large a warm pool it builds, because a fresh pixel has no retargeting audience on day one, and the
last 72 hours before a start date is where a deadline actually converts. In that window you are closing people
who already raised a hand. Throttle week one for efficiency and there is nothing left to close with.

Two weeks is also not enough volume to pick a winner out of twelve variants of one idea, because at that scale a
losing variant and an unlucky one look identical. So the split should be by angle, each with its own budget, so
spend cannot collapse onto the fastest spender before a slower and better angle gets a read. For a class with a
fixed start date I would run three. The report card angle, aimed at the parent who has just seen a grade drop.
The cohort angle, carrying the real start date and the real number of seats left. And the instructor angle,
where who is teaching and what they have done is the entire pitch. The date belongs in the ad itself, not only
on the landing page.

On example ads, I am not going to put a client's live creative into a proposal, so those three angles are the
honest version of that answer. What we hand over per concept is a brief with the headline, the supporting copy,
the visual direction, and both the square and vertical specs.

Our team runs Meta today for a children's education and life coaching company whose entire funnel is
registrations for a dated, recurring event, with campaigns scheduled to switch on and off around each date. That
is the same shape as a class that starts on a fixed day. That account is also the clearest warning I can hand
you. The top of funnel storytelling campaigns and the campaign actually producing registrations sit side by side
in it, and the account level cost per registration reads far worse than the campaign producing them. On a
fourteen day sprint, reading the blended number is how you kill the campaign that is working, on day six.
Separate campaigns and separate conversion actions per intent, from the start.

Straight with you on the case study you asked for. There is no tutoring account in our book. The closest is the
education one above, and it sells a certification to adults rather than classes to families, so I would rather
name that than stretch it. The nearest match on shape is a dental account our team ran on Meta lead forms
feeding a phone room, 2,558 leads on $64,747 between April and July this year, about $25 a lead. Qualifying
questions were added to the form on purpose and cost per lead went up, because the real constraint was how many
calls the room could make in a day, not how many leads Meta could deliver. A tutoring launch has that same
ceiling in whoever rings the parent back. That account has since ended.

Three things before a plan means anything.

Are the classes in person in one area, or online and open to the whole country? That is the biggest single fork
in the media plan.

What is the budget for these two weeks relative to a normal month for you? The number on its own tells me less
than whether it is one month's spend compressed, or three.

And who calls a parent back after the form comes in, how fast, and how many can they get through in a day? That
ceiling decides how hard we are allowed to push.
