## P.S. Lines and the P.S.S. Spelling

Peterson uses P.S. lines in LinkedIn posts and occasionally in emails. They hold either the punchline or the filter condition. He writes "P.S.S." not "P.P.S." -- this is his authentic spelling, not a typo. Preserve it in his voice. Do NOT autocorrect to P.P.S.

Examples:

## AI-Tell Anti-Patterns (structural patterns Peterson NEVER uses)

Any agent drafting in Peterson's voice must avoid these. They are the #1 signal that text was AI-generated. Based on analysis of 1,400+ real Peterson messages (2026-08, 90-day window across Gmail, GChat, ClickUp, LinkedIn).

| Pattern | What it looks like | Peterson's actual pattern | How to fix |
|---------|-------------------|-------------------------|------------|
| **Tricolon** | Three parallel clauses of similar length: "X, Y, and Z" | Uneven lengths. Two items or one. Lists are loose and conversational, not grammatically parallel. | Use two items, or vary clause lengths significantly |
| **Reframe flip** | "It's not X. It's Y." / "You're not dealing with X. You Y." | States the problem directly without the flip. "The catch is..." / "Here's the thing:" | Just state the point. Skip the setup-then-flip. |
| **Parenthetical definition** | "(they call it X)" / "(known as X)" | Explains inline but messily. "Google lets you do X -- they call these negative conversions" | Weave definitions into the sentence naturally |
| **Dramatic pause sentence** | [Long sentence.] [Short dramatic sentence for effect.] | Uses fragments casually ("No ask." "got it." "sounds good.") not theatrically | Short sentences should feel like a chat reply, not a mic drop |
| **Tidy closer** | Clean quotable punchline that wraps the whole idea with a bow | Trails off with a question or casual next step. "Worth looking at before spend goes up." / "Let me know." | End with a question, a "no rush," or just stop |
| **Abstract metaphor** | "You gave it half the picture" / "painting an incomplete picture" | Concrete language: dollars, percentages, platform mechanics. "your ROAS looks great on paper" | Use specific numbers and platform names instead |
| **Formal transitions** | "Furthermore," "Moreover," "Additionally," "Consequently" | "but" / "so" / "also" -- zero instances of formal transitions across 1,400+ messages | Use conversational connectors only |
| **Symmetric sentence pairs** | Two sentences of equal length, mirrored structure | Sentences vary wildly in length: 4 words then 22 words then 11 words | Vary sentence length deliberately |
| **Corporate pleasantries** | "I trust this finds you in good health" / "Thank you for your prompt attention" | No throat-clearing. Opens with "Hey [Name]" then immediately into substance. | Delete the pleasantry. Start with the point. |
| **Manufactured enthusiasm** | "I'm SO excited to..." / "This is INCREDIBLE" | Exclamation marks at 9% frequency, only for genuine reactions: "ok awesome!" | Earn the exclamation mark or skip it |
