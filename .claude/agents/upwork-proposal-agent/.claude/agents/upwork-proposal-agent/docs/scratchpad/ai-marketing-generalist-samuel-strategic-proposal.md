## PROPOSAL (paste-ready)

Sixteen deliverables in one post usually means the hardest part of this role won't be any single channel. It will be the measurement layer underneath them. When Meta, Google, TikTok, email and organic each report their own self-attributed conversions, the numbers overlap, and blended CAC quietly stops matching what is actually in the bank. AI makes that worse before it makes it better, because it lets you optimize faster toward a figure that was never real. The first thing worth fixing is one source of truth for CPA and CAC, then let the channels argue over the credit.

The other honest tradeoff on the AI side: it compresses research and creative volume dramatically, but it does not compress the learning phase. If the budget supports 30 conversions a month and you ship 40 creative variants against it, every one of them starves. AI is real leverage on research, competitor teardowns, audience mapping, first-draft copy and reporting. On spend allocation it still comes down to whether the account has enough volume to learn.

Where I would start is paid acquisition and retargeting on Google and Meta, because those move fastest and produce the conversion data everything else gets measured against. Content, SEO and AI-search visibility compound, but they compound off knowing which messages actually convert, and paid buys that answer in weeks instead of quarters.

Six years in paid media, 130 clients, 200+ account audits, and north of $20M in managed spend, most of it Google and Meta. TikTok has been a lighter part of the book, so that is something I would be building with you rather than pointing at.

On the AI side, our own operation runs on 109 purpose-built agents and 54 scheduled jobs sitting on a database of client, campaign and call history. Research, competitor pulls, reporting and QC are already automated on our end.

Two things before anything else: roughly what is the current monthly ad budget, and which of those sixteen lines is bleeding right now?
