## PROPOSAL (263 words)

Do you know how many months the average $33 member stays before they cancel? That one
number decides almost everything about how the ad account gets built, and most membership
businesses I talk to are still tracking signups instead.

The reason it matters is that with a free tier sitting in front of a paid membership, Meta
optimizes toward whichever event fires most often. That is almost always the free signup.
The algorithm gets very good at finding people who will take a free thing, your cost per
signup looks excellent, and paid conversion quietly falls off. I have watched that happen
in accounts where the client was convinced the creative was the problem.

I run Meta for a meal prep brand with the same underlying shape: low ticket, recurring, and
the real money lands in month three rather than month one. What moved it there was
separating new customer acquisition from everyone already in the retention loop, so the
account stopped paying to re-acquire people who were coming back on their own. The same
principle applies to a membership with a free tier in front and coaching on the back end.
Retreat and coaching buyers are too sparse for Meta to learn on directly, so they get built
as an audience rather than a campaign objective.

After 10 years of this, and having built and sold my own e-commerce business, the accounts
that work are the ones where the optimization event matches where the money actually is.
