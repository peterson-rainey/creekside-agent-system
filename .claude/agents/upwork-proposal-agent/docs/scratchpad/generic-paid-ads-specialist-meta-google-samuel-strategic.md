Paid ads and lead generation get treated as one job, and that is usually where the conversion numbers stop making sense. Google Ads and Meta Ads are not two versions of the same channel. Google captures demand that already exists, so a lead there sits close to the decision. Meta creates demand, so a lead there is earlier and needs different follow up and a longer measurement window. When both get optimized against the same form fill and reported side by side in the same table, Meta almost always looks cheaper per lead and worse per dollar of revenue, and the budget slowly drifts toward the wrong platform.

The fix is boring. Pick one downstream event that actually means money to you, get it back into both platforms, and let each one optimize toward that instead of toward raw form fills. That single change usually moves conversion rate more than any targeting or creative work does.

Which raises the question worth answering before anyone touches the account. What does a good lead look like on your end, and roughly what is one worth to you? That decides whether the priority is more volume or better filtering, and those are opposite builds. It also tells us whether Meta belongs in the mix right now or whether the budget should sit on Google until tracking is clean.

Six years doing this, mostly lead gen for service businesses and ecommerce brands across both platforms. Our team runs the day to day account work and reports weekly, since monthly is too slow to catch an ad set going sideways.
