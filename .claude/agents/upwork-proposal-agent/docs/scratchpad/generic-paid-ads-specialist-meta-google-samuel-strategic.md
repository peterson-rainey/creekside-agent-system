If Meta's cost per lead looks better than Google's while your best customers keep coming from Google, the problem usually isn't targeting. It's that both platforms are being optimized toward a form fill, and a form fill is not what you sell.

Meta will optimize toward whatever event you feed it. Feed it raw form fills and it does exactly that, cheaply and in volume, from the people most willing to fill out a form. That's why Meta almost always looks cheaper per lead when you're running native lead forms, and it's exactly where the revenue per dollar math falls apart.

The fix is boring. Pick the downstream event that actually means money to you, a qualified call, a booked appointment, a closed deal, and push it back into both platforms so each one chases that instead. This won't move your landing page conversion rate. It changes who the algorithm goes after, and that usually does more for lead quality and cost than another round of targeting or creative tests.

The tradeoff is volume. That deeper event has to fire often enough for the platform to learn from it, so on thin conversion counts you either stay on the shallower event and filter manually, or run both until the deeper one earns its weekly numbers. Worth knowing which situation you're in before anyone promises a rebuild.

Six years doing this, mostly lead gen for service businesses and ecommerce brands across Google and Meta. Our team runs the day to day account work and reports weekly, since monthly is too slow to catch an ad set going sideways.

Whether you need more volume or better filtering usually comes down to what one lead is worth to you, and that's the first thing I'd want to know. What are you selling, and where are the ads running?
