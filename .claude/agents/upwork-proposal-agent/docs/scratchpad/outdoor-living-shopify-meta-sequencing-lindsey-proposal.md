## Proposal body

Do you know how much your add-to-cart to purchase rate moves ad by ad, rather than at the campaign level? That number decides the sequencing question. When it holds steady, the cart event is a fair proxy and you can let it keep building. When it swings, Meta is buying the cheapest carts instead of the cheapest customers, and a fast creative pipeline makes that worse, because every new hook gets scored on an event that is not predicting revenue.

Asking because I ran Meta for a hot tub and spa parts brand sitting in almost the same spot. Over nine months that account did $131,596 in spend and 4,526 purchases at a $29 cost per purchase and 5.5x blended, on a $161 average order. What it taught me is that shallow-event campaigns flatter you at small budgets. We had separate campaigns optimized to add to cart, view content, time on site and site visits, and every one posted a better ROAS than the broad purchase campaign. None of them ever carried real budget. The purchase campaign did, about half the spend and half the orders, at a lower number that held.

At a $69 to $89 order value the CPA ceiling is thin enough that blended ROAS will hide the answer. After 10 years of this, and my own e-commerce brand before that, the accounts I trust keep existing customers in a separate campaign. On that account the customer list ran separately at 5.4x, so the broad prospecting number was never propped up by repeat buyers.
