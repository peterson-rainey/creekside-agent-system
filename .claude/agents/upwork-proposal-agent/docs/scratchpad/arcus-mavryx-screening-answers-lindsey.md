Q1. How would you approach building a LinkedIn campaign for a niche industry?

Start with the size of the real universe, not the targeting options. For a niche compliance buyer the addressable list is usually a few thousand companies, and that single number sets the structure. I would build one broad audience rather than five narrow ones, because slicing a small list into segments starves each of them and the algorithm never exits learning. Exclusions matter more than inclusions here: current customers, competitors, and anyone already on the waitlist come out on day one so budget is not spent re-reaching people who already converted.

From there the plan is fewer audiences and more creative angles against the same people. Frequency is the constraint, so the testing calendar is built around message rotation, not audience expansion. I would run the waitlist offer against two or three distinct problem framings before touching budget levels, because at this list size the offer moves results more than the bidding does.

Measurement gets locked before launch. UTMs, conversion definitions, and what counts as a qualified lead all get agreed in writing first, since a small-volume campaign cannot survive four weeks of ambiguous attribution.

Q2. Best and weakest relevant paid-media campaign.

BEST: Fusion Dental. Platform Meta. Industry cosmetic and implant dentistry, high ticket. Roughly $20,000 per month. Over a 90 day window the account produced 2,558 leads at a $25.31 cost per lead, delivered through Meta lead forms routed into a call centre. It worked because the offer was matched to a genuinely high case value, the lead form was short enough to keep volume up, and the call centre closed the gap between a cheap form fill and a booked consultation. The lesson that transfers here is that the handoff after the click decided the outcome more than the targeting did.

WEAKEST: ReferPro. Platform Meta. Industry B2B SaaS, referral software. Around $2,000 per month. This is the closest match to your offer and it is also the one I would not hold up as a win. The budget was too small for a considered B2B purchase, and the account was never instrumented well enough to report a defensible CAC, which means I can tell you what it cost but not what it was worth. That is precisely why I now insist on the tracking and qualified-lead definition being signed off before launch rather than reconstructed afterwards.

Q3. Can you independently operate both LinkedIn Ads and Meta Ads?

Meta, yes, independently and end to end. LinkedIn, no, and I would rather say that plainly than discover it on a call.

What I personally handle on Meta: account and campaign structure, audience architecture including cold, retargeting, site visitor, and CRM-matched audiences, budget and bidding, creative and copy briefs, offer testing, pixel and conversion setup, UTM discipline, and weekly reporting against qualified outcomes rather than platform CPL. I also handle email, which is the part most paid-media buyers hand off and which matters here because a waitlist is worth very little until something nurtures it.

Given your post rules out a Meta-only buyer for the combined role, the honest fit is me owning the Meta, retargeting, and lifecycle layer alongside a dedicated LinkedIn buyer, not me covering both.

Q4. Time to results on a campaign you built or optimised.

On the Fusion Dental account the first two weeks produced volume at a cost that looked fine on the surface, and the real problem only showed up once lead quality was traced past the form. The change that mattered was moving spend behind the creative angles that produced booked consultations rather than the ones that produced the cheapest form fills, and tightening the form so the call centre was not chasing unqualified contacts. Across the 90 day window that produced 2,558 leads at $25.31 each.

The pattern I would expect on a waitlist build is similar but slower. Week one and two tell you almost nothing except whether tracking is clean. Weeks three to six are where offer and creative separate, and that is the window where most accounts get reallocated too early on numbers that were never stable.
