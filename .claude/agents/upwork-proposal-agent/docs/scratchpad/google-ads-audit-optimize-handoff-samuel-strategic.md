Wasted spend in a Google Ads account is usually a symptom, and the cause is normally sitting in the conversion column. If the account is optimizing toward form fills, click to call events, or a page view marked primary, Smart Bidding is doing exactly what it was told. It gets better every week at buying the cheapest version of an event that does not turn into revenue. You can strip out half the search terms and the account will still spend toward the wrong target, just more efficiently.

Across a couple hundred account audits, that has been the most common reason a decent looking account underperforms. So my first pass is not the structure. It is which conversion actions are primary, whether they double count each other, and whether anything downstream of the lead flows back in. If closed revenue never returns to Google as an offline conversion, no CPA or ROAS number in the account is describing the business, and an audit built on them inherits the error.

Sequencing matters here. Fixing measurement first and letting it run two or three weeks before restructuring feels slow, but restructuring first resets learning against the old targets and costs a month of unreadable data.

The handoff is where these projects usually leak. A recommendations list decays within a quarter because whoever inherits it knows what changed but not why a threshold was set where it was. I would rather hand over the decision rules than the change log.

Closest in shape: a bankruptcy firm in Orange County, restructured by market, which went from $86 a lead to $50.29 and from 117 leads to 229. And a health brand running duplicate conversion tracking on effectively brand only traffic, at 10x ROAS in the first thirty days after the rebuild. Both attached.

Two things would sharpen this: what is counted as a conversion today, and what one of those leads is worth once it closes. Also, is the handoff going to someone internal or to a hire?

Happy to jump on a call and walk through what I would look at first.
