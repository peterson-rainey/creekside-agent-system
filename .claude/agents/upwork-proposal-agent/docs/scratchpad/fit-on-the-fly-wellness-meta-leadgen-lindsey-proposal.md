# Fit on the Fly - Meta lead gen for a free six-week wellness program
Profile: Lindsey | Style: lindsey_default | Drafted 2026-08-30 | STATUS: UNSENT

## PROPOSAL BODY

Before anything else, what does a registration need to cost for the six week program to pay for itself downstream? That one number decides most of the first 30 days, and it is the number founders running a free offer almost never set, because free makes every signup feel like a win.

It matters here because of budget math. At roughly $25 a day, Meta needs about 50 conversions per ad set per week to exit the learning phase and optimize honestly. That means one ad set, one audience, and a registration cost under about $7. So a structured creative testing program and a $750 test month cannot both fit inside the same 30 days. I would use month one to get one broad audience and two or three angles to a stable, believable cost per registration, then test properly in month two against a baseline that actually exists.

On lead quality, an account I worked on was a dental implant practice running Meta lead forms. Cheap leads were never the problem, working them was. We deliberately added qualifying questions to the form, accepted a higher cost per lead in exchange, and held daily spend to what the call team could reach the same day. It finished at 2,558 leads at $25.31 across 90 days with the sales side able to keep up. The same principle applies to a free program. The metric that matters is what percentage of registrants are still opening email three, not what the signup cost.

Health and wellness copy is its own problem. I run a cosmetic and aesthetics account spending just under $100K a month on Meta, so I write inside the personal attributes rules every day. Ads aimed at women over 40 around weight get rejected for implying you know something about the reader's body, and that is a copy fix, not an appeal.
