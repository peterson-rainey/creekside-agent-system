## Draft (330 words, no links, no em dashes, signs "Samuel")

A prop firm's Meta account usually dies for a reason that has nothing to do with the creative. It's the destination. The financial-product reviewers key on the claim ladder they find after the click, the payout screenshots, the 95% split, the leaderboard proof, far more than on the hook itself. So the real account-structure question isn't how many backups you keep warm. It's which domain and which page each angle is allowed to touch, and whether the community and education angles can live on infrastructure that never shares a page with the funded-payout angle. Firms that separate those two survive review cycles that kill everyone else.

The second thing usually broken in this category is what gets fed to the optimizer. Most firms fire a purchase event on the evaluation. That's the cheap event, not the valuable one. The money event is eval passed, funded, paid out, and it lands two to six weeks later, well past the learning window. Since you own the stack, you can score a signup at purchase time, which is exactly what a Spike Score is good for, and send a modeled value back through CAPI instead of a flat eval price. Meta then buys traders who resemble the ones who get funded, not the ones who buy an eval and vanish. That one change tends to move blended CAC further than any creative round will.

On Google the honest read is that non-brand search here is thin and the competitor terms are a policy trap. The durable spend is brand defense plus a demand layer fed off your Meta audiences, not broad search.

Where I'll be straight with you: we've run regulated financial acquisition on Meta and Google, including scaling a reverse mortgage lender from $10K to $80K a month across both platforms. We have not run a crypto prop firm. If prop-firm history is the hard filter, I'd rather say that now than three weeks in.
