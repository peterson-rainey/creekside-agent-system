## 1. First two weeks on an underperforming Search account

Nothing structural changes in the first two weeks. I diagnose, write it up, and we agree the plan before I touch the account, because a restructure built on a miscounted conversion is worse than leaving it alone.

Order, and the order matters:

**Conversion tracking, before anything else.** What is firing, what is deduplicated, which actions are set as primary versus secondary, and whether the thing being optimised toward is the money event or a step before it. Then I reconcile Google's conversion count against your own record of submitted applications for the same window. If those two numbers disagree, every other finding is provisional until they don't.

**Policy status, per ad and per keyword.** In your category this is the first place I expect to find the problem. I am looking for outright disapprovals, and more importantly for "eligible (limited)" status, which keeps ads serving and quietly throttles them, so nothing looks broken from the dashboard. I have watched an account run for weeks in that state while everyone involved assumed it was a bidding issue.

**Location and language settings.** For a UK travel authorisation product this is the highest-value ten minutes in the audit. The location option defaults to "presence or interest", and half the planet is arguably interested in the UK, so that default can quietly spend your budget on people who will never apply. Language targeting is a second common misread: it filters on the user's Google interface language, not on what language your ad is written in. Multi-country and multi-language accounts get both of these wrong constantly.

**Search terms.** My rule for wasted spend is a term with zero conversions and $20 or more over a rolling 30 days; anything running zero-conversion past 30 days is persistent and gets escalated rather than trimmed. In your vertical I expect a specific cluster: "official", "gov uk", "free", "check status", "refund", plus every other country's ETA and visa equivalents bleeding in.

**Structure and match types.** Whether countries and languages are sharing ad groups, whether broad match is running without a real negative list behind it, and whether budget can be attributed to a market at all.

**Bidding.** Which strategy, how much conversion data it actually has, and whether it was changed recently enough to still be in learning.

**Then assets and landing page.** Extension coverage, and the page itself read against the policy, not just against conversion rate.

What you get is a written audit with the findings in priority order and the reasoning attached, not a task list. Week three we agree what gets prescribed. Week four onward is execution.

## 2. One account turned around

A paving and lot striping contractor in Virginia. Everything ran through a single catch-all Search campaign: one bucket, every service, every location. Its last full month spent about $2,459 and produced four conversions, roughly $615 each.

The first thing we tried did not work, and it is worth saying so. We split it by category and that version spent $720 for one conversion in under two weeks. It went off.

What worked was rebuilding around how the business actually sells, which meant separating the service line from geography instead of treating them as one segment. The service line campaign went live at the start of November and has run since on $38,471 with 198 conversions, about $194 each. The separate geographic campaign ran for five months at $131 per conversion before we folded it back.

At account level, Search went from $3,118 and five conversions across September and October, to $18,606 and 83 conversions across June, July and August. Cost per conversion down roughly 64 percent while monthly spend went up about six times, which is the direction that matters, because holding CPA flat while scaling is a different result than cutting CPA by shrinking.

The reason I picked this one for you: it never had much conversion volume. One to six a month at the start. That is the same constraint you are describing.

## 3. Bidding at limited conversion volume

The threshold I work to is roughly 15 to 30 conversions per campaign per 30 days. Below that, automated bidding does not have enough signal and spends its first two months learning noise instead of learning your customer.

What I avoid at low volume:

**tCPA and tROAS as an opening move.** They need the data they do not yet have. Setting a target on a campaign with six conversions a month produces confident-looking bids built on almost nothing, and the account gets worse before anyone can explain why.

**Maximise conversion value in particular.** Value-based bidding on thin data is the fastest way to hand the budget to whichever conversion happened to be recorded at a high value.

**Performance Max as the primary campaign early on.** It pools your budget and hides the search terms, which is exactly the visibility I need in the first months of a rebuild. I do run it, but after Search is stable and there is data to feed it, not as the diagnostic phase.

**Restructuring every few weeks.** Every reset puts bidding back into learning. At low volume you can spend a quarter permanently in learning if you keep changing things.

What I do instead: tight exact and phrase match under manual or maximise-clicks control while the data accumulates, portfolio bid strategies to pool volume across campaigns that would each be too thin alone, and where it fits, optimising toward a reliable earlier step in the funnel while still reporting on the completed application as the number that matters. Then switch to automated bidding once the volume justifies it, and say in advance what that threshold is so it is a decision rather than a mood.

## 4. Regulated and policy-restricted verticals

Yes, and it is most of what we run.

We managed reverse mortgage and HECM Search for a US lender, about $131,000 and 1,358 leads over twelve months at roughly $97 each. Reverse mortgage is a category Google restricts and verifies. That engagement has ended, so I am describing it in the past tense.

Alongside that, healthcare. A naturopathic clinic we still run has done $22,985 and 600 conversions over the last twelve months at $38.31. What makes it relevant to you is what happened in March, when the account was hit with a policy disapproval. That month came in at $37.65 per conversion, in line with the rest of the year. The account did not crater during a policy event. I would rather show you that than a rescue story.

On handling approvals, three things I have learned the hard way:

**Certification attaches to the advertiser, not the agency.** It does not transfer from me to you. No appeal will ever clear a disapproval that is really a missing certification, so the first thing I check on an account like yours is whether the business itself is certified and verified for the category. Getting that answer wrong costs months.

**The landing page is usually the cause, not the ad text.** When ads get disapproved, most people rewrite the copy. The destination is where the violation usually lives. For a third-party government-document service, the page needs to be unmistakable about not being the government and about the applicant's option to apply direct.

**The appeal path is two-tier and it is sanctioned process.** Self-service appeal through Policy Manager first, 24 to 48 hour turnaround. If it comes back failed or partially successful, you reply on the support thread and escalate to Google's specialist team. I have filed and won one at the escalated tier, on a health advertising policy, where Google reviewed and approved ads that had been flagged. I would not lead with appeals though. They are the slow lever, and they are what you use after certification and the page are right, not instead.

One more, from a small mental health account: ads that named a diagnostic category would not run at all, ads that opened with a phone number did, and the account carried a "limited" warning while still serving. That is the pattern I described above and the reason I check status per ad rather than trusting the account to look healthy.

The honest boundary: I have not run a visa, ETA or immigration account, and we hold no Google certification in that category ourselves. What transfers is the diagnostic order and the appeal process, not credentials in your vertical.
