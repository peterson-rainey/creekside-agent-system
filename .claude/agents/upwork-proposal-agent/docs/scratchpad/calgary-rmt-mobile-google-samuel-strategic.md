Noterro is where this build usually breaks, and it has nothing to do with the campaign. On the standard setup your booking portal sits on its own noterro.com subdomain, so the moment someone leaves your site to book, GA4 opens a fresh session and credits the completed booking to noterro.com as referral traffic rather than google / cpc. The key event still fires, GA4 looks healthy, and the conversion column in Google Ads stays near empty. Cross-domain measurement across both hosts and a referral exclusion on noterro.com have to be in place before any spend runs, otherwise months one through three get spent tuning against data that never made it back.

That same handoff is most of what I would look at on the landing page. For $170 to $300 house calls the drop tends to happen between the ad promise and the first Noterro screen, so what matters is whether service area, price and therapist credentials are settled before the portal loads.

One flag on targeting since you left it open. Income brackets are available in Canada, so that is buildable, but anyone Google has not classified falls into Unknown, and Google's own guidance is that cutting Unknown removes a substantial share of reach. Stack top-tier income onto a handful of affluent postal codes plus a discount negative list, on a new account in one city, and you can starve the campaign before it learns anything. At launch I would let the postal codes carry the qualifying, since Calgary geography already correlates with income, then run income as observation only. Once volume exists the data tells you what bid adjustment to set instead of guessing.
