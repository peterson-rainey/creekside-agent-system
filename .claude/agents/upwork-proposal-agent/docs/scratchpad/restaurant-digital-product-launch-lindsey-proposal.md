What is the price point on the product, and is that landing page meant to sell on the first visit or feed an email list that sells later? Both sit on the brief as parallel line items, and in practice they compete for the same click. A page that asks for an email and a purchase with equal weight usually gets neither.

The reason I ask is who you are selling to. I run Meta for two meal prep brands right now, so the food operator is my client rather than my audience, and what I have learned working with them is that they are unreachable during the hours they actually work. Attention shows up late at night and on Sundays, in short bursts, on a phone. That is not a browsing audience. If the product is under about $50, a direct checkout can work cold. Above that, for someone who has been on their feet twelve hours, the email capture has to win and the sale happens in the sequence behind it. That one decision changes the headline, the CTA, and what the tracking should even be watching.

I built and sold my own e-commerce business before this, so I have been on the operator side of a launch where the page looked finished and the offer order was wrong.

Straight with you on fit. My portfolio is paid acquisition and email, not organic account setup, bios, or content pillars, and I have not built a TikTok presence from zero. I would rather say that now than have it surface in week three. What I can own is the part that decides whether the launch earns anything: the offer sequencing, the conversion logic on the page, and the email flow behind that capture form.

Attached a few results below from the food side of my work.

There is a short video on my profile that covers how I handle launches like this.
