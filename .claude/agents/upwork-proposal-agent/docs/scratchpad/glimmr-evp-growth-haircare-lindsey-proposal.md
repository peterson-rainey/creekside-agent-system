## 1. A brand I personally managed, and the growth achieved

Master Spa Parts, a US replacement parts e-commerce brand on Shopify. I ran Meta for them for about ten months as the sole operator.

December 2025: $15,682 in spend, 349 purchases, 3.22x blended ROAS.
April 2026: $13,224 in spend, 665 purchases, 8.07x blended ROAS.

So purchases roughly doubled on about 16% less spend. Across the full engagement it was $131,597 in spend and 4,526 purchases.

Two things I want to be accurate about. December is the low point of the whole tenure, and Q4 CPMs unwinding is part of why the January-to-April line looks as steep as it does. The work that did the rest was cutting creative back to the handful of angles actually earning instead of keeping a wide rotation alive, and consolidating a fragmented campaign structure so budget stopped fighting itself. The account also churned in May 2026, so this is past tense and I would rather say that than let you assume it is live.

Before agency work I built and sold my own e-commerce business, which is where I learned to read this from the owner's side rather than the media buyer's.

## 2. Meta creative strategy and media buying, and my biggest monthly budget

Ten years, and it is the channel I am deepest in.

Biggest single month: **$114,587 in March 2026** on one account. That account has run just under $100K a month for thirteen consecutive months, about $1.19 million over the trailing year, across roughly 130 active campaigns. It is live right now and I am the operator on it.

The caveat you should have: that account is a cosmetic dental and aesthetics practice, not DTC e-commerce. It is lead generation, not purchases. The scale is real and the operational load is real, but the economics are a different shape from what you are running, and I am not going to pretend a patient booking is a Shopify order.

On creative, I brief video and static rather than produce it. Hooks get rewritten and retested against performance rather than taste, the same source footage gets trimmed to different end points to find where attention actually drops, and multiple versioned cuts of one asset run at the same time with spend deciding which survives. The discipline I care most about is running new-customer campaigns with all past purchasers excluded, because a blended number will happily hide the fact that you are buying back people who were going to reorder anyway.

## 3. TikTok Shop

I do run TikTok, and I want to frame the scale accurately rather than let the word "yes" do more work than it should. It sits secondary to Meta in every account I have had it in, usually somewhere between $2,000 and $8,000 a month. So I know the channel from operating it, not from reading about it, and I also know I am not the person who has scaled it as a primary acquisition engine.

The distinction that matters for your post, since you named ads and Shop separately: what I run is TikTok ads pointing at the brand's own store. TikTok Shop, meaning the in-app marketplace with the affiliate program and seeding operation behind it, is a different discipline and not one I have operated.

My read on what actually moves Shop GMV, offered as assessment rather than record: it behaves like a creator marketplace with an ad layer bolted on, not like an ad account with content attached. Affiliate and creator volume is the engine, so the commission rate has to genuinely compete for creator attention against every other SKU in the category, and sample seeding has to run continuously rather than as a launch stunt. Many small creators posting steadily tends to beat a few large ones. Review count and rating gate how much organic traffic the algorithm gives a listing at all, which makes early review velocity a growth lever rather than a hygiene task. And Shop Ads amplify a listing already converting organically. They are poor at manufacturing demand for one that is not.

If TikTok Shop is a primary channel for Glimmr rather than a secondary one, that is a gap you should weigh, and I would want a Shop specialist working alongside me rather than under me.

## 4. Klaviyo and email

Hands on and current. I personally handle email for three accounts right now, all e-commerce or aesthetics.

A concrete example: Tiami, a luxury mattress brand on Shopify. Two things that came out of the build are more representative than any headline number. Their Klaviyo catalog endpoint returns empty, so product blocks cannot be built off the catalog at all and have to be assembled from event properties instead. And their Added to Cart event carries a URL pointing at the raw myshopify domain rather than the live storefront, so any CTA built on that event variable quietly sends buyers to the wrong domain. Both get found by opening the event payload rather than trusting the template preview. I also had to mirror the filters on their existing abandoned cart flow so a new flow would not double-send against the checkout flow.

On Nightlark, another e-commerce account, the catch was that onsite tracking was returning zero Added to Cart events. The abandoned cart flow would have been firing on nobody.

The limit: these builds are recent and I do not have a clean attributed email revenue percentage I would stand behind. If you want one, I would rather earn it in your account than quote someone else's.
