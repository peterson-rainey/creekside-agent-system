**1. Describe your recent experience with similar projects**

Closest match is a cosmetic and aesthetics practice our team runs on both platforms right now. Meta spend there has held between $90K and $115K a month for twelve straight months, and the Google side has run since late April and produced about 550 conversions. Beyond that one account, August ran 21 Meta accounts and 16 Google accounts across the book, so multi account work is the normal shape of a week rather than a stretch. On the product side we ran a DTC brand selling to women where click through held above four percent.

**2. Please list any certifications related to this project**

Nothing formal to list. No Google or Meta certification badges here, and that is worth saying straight rather than padding the answer.

What sits behind the work instead is volume and policy fluency. Roughly $268K moved across Google and Meta in August alone, and the aesthetics side carries rules that matter more day to day than a badge does. Google's personalized ads health policy blocks remarketing audiences for cosmetic procedure and body modification services, so a remarketing campaign sitting at $5 a day with zero impressions is a policy problem and not a bidding one. That kind of thing decides whether an aesthetics account works.

**3. How do you approach difficult conversations with customers?**

Bring the number before they ask for it. Most hard conversations in this work are about a month that underperformed, and the damage comes from the client finding it first in a dashboard. So the call opens with what happened, what caused it, and what changes this week.

The other half is refusing to soften a diagnosis that involves them. If lead quality is the problem and the front desk is not calling leads back inside an hour, that has to get said plainly, because paid traffic cannot fix a follow up gap.

**4. How do you decide what tone of voice to write in?**

From the buyer's state at the moment they see the ad, not from the brand deck. Someone considering a treatment on their own face is anxious and private, so what works is calm and specific, and pressure reads as a red flag. A skincare product buyer is comparing, so the tone is evidence led and concrete.

The bigger rule is matching. Ad tone, landing page and consult call all have to sound like the same business, or lead quality drops even when cost per lead looks fine. Most of the actual language gets pulled from reviews and consult recordings rather than written fresh.

**5. How do you use metrics to inform your strategy?**

Start by picking the event that matches where the money is made. For aesthetics that is booked and attended consults rather than form fills, and because consult to treatment lag is real, a first month judged on cost per lead usually misleads. For product accounts the first job is separating brand from non brand so Google is not credited for demand Meta paid to create.

Then compare against the market and not just last month. Blended Meta CPM across our accounts ran about $22 last September and about $47 by July at similar spend. Without that baseline a client sees rising costs and asks what broke, when often nothing did.
