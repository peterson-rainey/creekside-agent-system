Three storefronts and margin tier targets usually pull a Shopping account in two opposite directions, and the resolution matters more than the bidding does. Splitting AU, NZ and US into their own campaigns looks like the clean structure. In practice the secondary markets end up starved. I have run geo-split Shopping and PMax campaigns on two different ecommerce accounts, one carved out to 49 countries outside the US, one to the UK, and both underperformed the home market campaign badly enough that neither survived. Not because the demand was absent, but because a separate campaign means a separate pool of conversion data, and a smart bidding strategy that never accumulates enough of it never gets good. With NZ tracking already unreliable, that is the failure mode I would be most worried about inheriting.

The margin tier work is where account structure earns its keep. On a Shopify ecommerce account this year the all products Shopping campaign ran 5.52x on about $18,000. Carving the best sellers into their own campaign with its own target took that subset to 8.30x. Same feed, same account, same period, better allocation. Margin tiers are that mechanic with a different attribute, and it is usually worth more than any bid adjustment available inside a single campaign.

Two honest gaps. TikTok I can run, but I cannot show you results for it, so if that is a proof requirement rather than a scope item I am not your best option. Channable specifically I have not used, though the product set logic underneath it is the same whatever the tool.

Curious what your margin tiers look like in the feed right now. Are they already a custom label, or something that would need building before any of it can be bid on?
