## PROPOSAL
Your retargeting number is the one I would push back on. A 7.03 ROAS on $77 is roughly three purchases, and at $1,800 a month your remarketing pool is probably a few thousand people at most. That ROAS is not evidence that retargeting works. It is evidence that a small warm audience converted demand your cold ads already built. Fund it heavily and it collapses inside two weeks, because you end up buying the same people repeatedly. Rebuild it, but cap it near 15 to 20 percent of spend and let prospecting refill the pool.

The bigger problem is your kill rule. At a $180 CPA, spending $68 to $98 on a test buys you half a purchase at most. No verdict on earnings is possible there, which is exactly why the tests told you nothing. At your budget you cannot judge creative on purchases at all. You judge on the signals that arrive early, hook rate, cost per outbound click, cost per landing page view, cost per add to cart, and you let only the survivors run long enough to price a purchase. That means fewer concepts, longer runs, and a strict order of elimination.

On the 41 percent initiate checkout rate, be careful. Fifteen purchases implies roughly 36 checkouts started, so you are reasoning from about 20 people. Your 92 percent landing page view rate says the page loads and the offer reads. I would check shipping cost reveal, currency, and payment options before rebuilding anything, and confirm initiate checkout is not firing on cart open.

Closest work to yours was a spa parts ecommerce account on Meta, about 5.5 ROAS across roughly $131,000 and 4,500 purchases. The useful detail is that its worst ROAS month came at one of its highest spend months, and its best came later at lower spend after a restructure.

I would start where you suggested, a paid audit and a written plan, around $1,000, no retainer attached. Fair warning, at $1,500 to $2,500 a month my management minimum would eat a large share of your spend, so I would rather get the structure right and earn more budget first.

## SCREENING ANSWERS (received 2026-08-29)

### Q1 — First hypothesis
Fifteen purchases in ninety days is about one a week, and Meta wants roughly fifty conversions per ad set per week before it stops guessing. So my first hypothesis is that no ad set in your account has ever finished learning, which means nothing has actually been optimized yet. Every symptom you listed is downstream of that one fact. The single ad carrying 59 percent of spend is not a fragility problem, it is the algorithm correctly concentrating on the only thing it has enough signal to trust. The starved tests, the reset from the twenty day gap, the retargeting that looked incredible on $77, all of it is what an under-powered account looks like. Underneath that there is a second issue I would want to check before touching anything. At a $199 AOV and a $140 CPA, after cost of goods, shipping and payment fees, your contribution margin on a first order is probably close to zero. If that is true, the account is being asked to clear a bar that first purchase economics may not support at any efficiency, and the answer is repeat rate or AOV rather than a better campaign structure. So: under-powered for purchase optimization, and possibly being measured against the wrong bar.

### Q2 — Creative test thresholds
$150 and 4 days minimum for the first cut, $400 and 10 days for the second, $600 before I will let anyone call a creative on purchases. Here is the arithmetic behind those. At your $1.39 CPC, $150 buys about 108 clicks and roughly 99 landing page views, which is enough to rank hook rate and cost per landing page view with confidence and to see add to cart directionally. $400 gets you near 265 landing page views and somewhere around 13 to 21 add to carts, which is enough to rank on cost per add to cart. $600 at a $140 CPA is four purchases, which is the absolute floor for saying anything about earnings. Four days rather than three because Meta delivery is unstable for the first 48 to 72 hours and you will misread it. If your sales skew to weekends, and for sunglasses they usually do, a test does not count until it has covered one. The tradeoff is honest: at $1,500 to $2,500 a month this buys you about three new concepts a month, not five. Your $68 to $98 kills were not too aggressive, they were too early to be measuring purchases at all.

### Q3 — The 41 percent
I think it is roughly normal and it is not your constraint. Fifteen purchases at 41 percent implies about 37 checkouts started, so the entire gap you are worried about is around 22 people, which is not enough to call anything. For most DTC stores initiate checkout to purchase lands somewhere between 30 and 50 percent, so 41 is unremarkable. Your 92 percent landing page view rate tells me the page loads and the offer reads, which rules out the boring explanations. If I were going to look at all, I would check three things in this order: whether shipping cost appears for the first time at checkout, whether currency displays correctly for traffic outside Australia, and whether you offer Afterpay or similar, because at a $199 frame that matters more here than most places. I would also confirm initiate checkout is not firing on cart open, which would make the number meaningless. But I would not rebuild the site over this. The purchase count is too low for the rate to be telling you anything yet.

At that spend most accounts cannot generate purchase data fast enough to get there quickly, and I would rather say that now than hand you a number that does not survive scrutiny. What transfers is the diagnosis and the order of operations, not the timeline. If that is too slow for where you are, that is a fair reason to pick someone else.
