A defined niche usually gets spent on the targeting. On Meta that is the one place it stops paying off, because narrow interest stacking starves delivery of the volume it needs to find your readers. The niche belongs in the creative. A headline that makes exactly one kind of person laugh filters harder than any interest list, and it lets you run broad while the system sorts on who engages. If your instinct from a decade ago was to segment tightly first, that is the biggest thing that flipped.

The harder question is what counts as a conversion. Paid pageviews almost never pay for themselves against display revenue, so a campaign optimized for clicks or landing page views buys one visit at a time and stays underwater. Treat the spend as subscriber acquisition instead. Email signup, Page follow, push opt in, something that lets you reach that reader again for free. The site earns on the repeat visits, not the bought one.

Two things to handle before launch. If the satire touches politics or social issues, and most of it does, Meta will want advertiser authorization with ID verification and a paid for by disclaimer, and those ads sit in the public Ad Library for years. Start it early, it is slow. Second, ad review reads a satirical headline literally, so copy that is fine as a post gets pulled as a false claim. The fix is usually putting the setup in the ad and leaving the punchline on the page.

Do not save remarketing for later. The pixel has to fire from day one or there is no pool in month three. And on Google, Display is usually cheap traffic that inflates numbers without building anything, so Demand Gen is the better first test.

No satirical publisher in our book, so I will not pretend otherwise. Closest shape our team has run was a free consumer app on Meta where the conversion was a signup, not a sale. The audit at takeover found it optimizing for traffic instead of the signup event, the same trap above. Fixing the objective and working through creative testing took cost per signup from $7.36 to $3.90, a 47 percent cut, across 2,662 signups.

Roughly what monthly range are you thinking on spend, and how does the site earn once someone is reading. Display revenue, subscriptions and merch point at different conversion events, and the wrong one quietly burns a launch budget.
