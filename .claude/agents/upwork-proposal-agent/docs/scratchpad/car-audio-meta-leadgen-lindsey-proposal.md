Are your leads coming in through instant forms inside Facebook, or are you sending people to a landing page first? I ask because that single choice usually decides whether "leads, not just likes" holds up once those leads reach your funnel.

One account I ran on that exact structure was a dental group using Meta lead forms feeding a call center. Over about eleven weeks it brought in 2,558 leads at $25.31 each on roughly $64,700 in spend. Volume was never the hard part. What made it work was tightening the form so it filtered out the merely curious, and making sure someone called back in minutes instead of hours. Cheap leads that sit for a day cost you the same as no leads, and that is where most of the lead gen accounts I audit are quietly losing money.

On creative, after ten years of this the pattern I keep landing on for install and service businesses is that polish loses to proof. What pulls is real work in real vehicles, before and after, close-ups of the actual product in the door panel or the trunk, cut into carousels so someone can swipe through a job start to finish. When I was running my own e-commerce brand, clean studio images lost to messy honest ones more often than not, and it took me longer than it should have to accept that.

Most of my last few years have been local appointment-based businesses where the win is a booked job rather than a click, which is the same problem you are describing.
