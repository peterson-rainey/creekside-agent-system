Most accounts spending north of $200K a month on Meta find that a large share of their CPM movement was never theirs to control. Worth establishing which share is which before anything gets changed.

Across the Meta accounts our team runs, spend has sat between $198K and $266K a month for eleven straight months, so we watch the same auction from about two dozen angles at once. Blended CPM ran $22.39 in September, climbed to $33.90 by December, then came back down to $22.12 in March. On the single largest account in that group, a high ticket dental and aesthetics practice holding spend near $100K a month with no structural changes, CPM went $29.39 in September, $59.52 in November, $67.59 in December, then $28.95 by March. Nothing was broken in November. The auction was just expensive.

So the first thing worth doing is separating your CPM curve from the market's. If your rise matches the market, the diagnosis is a different one than if you rose while the market held.

The other thing worth saying plainly: CPM is one of the easiest metrics to cut and one of the easiest to cut wrongly. Opening Audience Network, widening to broad, or pushing budget into Reels and Stories will drop CPM quickly, and it will often drop conversion rate faster. Cheap impressions are usually cheap for a reason. The version that holds up is mostly creative side, since quality ranking prices you differently, plus placement mix judged on cost per outcome instead of cost per thousand.

One thing to be straight about. The $200K plus is across our Meta book, not one account. The largest single account we run is a bit over $115K in a month. If the requirement is one account at that level, that is a fair reason to pass.
