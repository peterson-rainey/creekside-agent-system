When you compare what the platforms report against your backend sales for NZ, which direction does the gap run? Over-reporting and under-reporting are two different problems and they push you toward opposite decisions on the same campaign. It matters more here than on a single storefront, because target ROAS bids against whatever conversion value you send it. A wrong number in a dashboard is a bad report. The same number inside a bid strategy is a bad account.

SPLITTING THE STOREFRONTS. Giving AU, NZ and US their own campaigns looks like the clean structure and it is the first thing I would push back on. I have run geo-split Shopping and PMax on two ecommerce accounts, one carved to 49 countries outside the US, one to the UK. The 49-country campaign returned 0.65x. The UK split ran 0.64x on Shopping and 0.19x on Search while the main campaign held 2.59x. Neither survived. Demand was not the problem: a separate campaign is a separate pool of conversion data, and a bid strategy that never accumulates enough never gets good. With NZ tracking already unreliable, that is the failure mode I expect to inherit.

MERCHANT CENTER AND MARGIN TIERS. Yes, directly, though not through Channable. Bidding by margin means the feed has to carry margin, so the work starts with a custom label in Merchant Center, not in the campaign. I have run that carve-out: on a Shopify account, all-products Shopping ran 5.52x on $17,883, and pulling the best sellers into their own campaign with their own target put that subset at 8.30x. Same feed, same account, same period. I also run category-segmented PMax as feed-only, 4.56x, the cleanest way to see what a feed does before creative takes credit for it. The tradeoff: every tier splits the data further, and smart bidding on a thin campaign is worse than a blended target on a fat one. Three tiers is the ceiling.

ACCOUNTS. These sit in Creekside's Business Manager, so what follows is the book I work across, not a private client list. None of it spans all three platforms, so I will split rather than blur. Google: the Shopify brand above, 5.52x non-branded Shopping and 5.35x non-branded Search. Meta: a US replacement parts retailer, about $131,000 over nine months to May, roughly 4,500 orders at 5.53x blended on a $161 average order value, where catalog campaigns were the strongest structure at 6.96x. No single ecommerce account I run reaches $500,000 a year on its own. Mine sit between $100,000 and $200,000 per platform.

BACKEND DATA. Platform reporting is not wrong at random, it is wrong in a direction. That replacement parts account ran campaigns optimized to website visits, add to cart and view content, and every one reported a better return than the broad purchase campaign carrying half the budget and orders. None ever held real budget. Allocating on platform ROAS moves money toward whatever is best at claiming credit. For NZ: if the data is unreliable, target ROAS there is bidding against noise and I would not run one until reconciliation exists. Send me orders by SKU or margin tier, by storefront, by date, with an order ID or UTM at checkout, and I can push corrected values back as offline conversions, which fixes the bidding rather than the reporting.

FIRST TWO WEEKS. Diagnosis and access, no campaign changes. Access first, because that part becomes unrecoverable. Conversion history, audiences, Merchant Center and feed rules often sit in assets the outgoing agency owns, and I want those in your name before they finish. Week two, narrow changes: feed fixes, then the budget-limited campaigns you flagged. What I would not do is restructure. That resets bid strategy learning and costs you the baseline you just built.

CREATIVE. An existing team, not one I would assemble. A creatives director and a creative handle static, motion and video, and I write the briefs and copy. On this category the constraint is the shot, not the volume. Most wall art accounts run the artwork flat off the product page. That works in Shopping, where the buyer expects a catalog image, and fails on paid social, where a flat JPEG says nothing about scale or texture.

GAPS. TikTok I can run and have no results to show you on it, so if that is a proof requirement rather than a scope item, I am not your fit. Channable I have not used. My ecommerce book is US and UK, not AU or NZ.

TERMS. I price management as a percentage of spend on marginal bands, per platform, not hourly. Tracked hours are fine for a defined handover and audit phase and I will run Time Doctor for that. Once an account is stable hours are the wrong instrument, the incentive runs against you. I have not quoted a figure because you have not said what you are spending. Roughly where does spend sit across the three storefronts, and are your margin tiers already a custom label in the feed, or something that needs building before any of it can be bid on?
