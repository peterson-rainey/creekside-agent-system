## PROPOSAL

Women's fashion is one of the categories where a single site-wide conversion rate tells you the least. The same Shopify store converts very differently depending on which ad sent the person. A collection ad drops someone onto forty items and a price they were not prepared for. A single-product ad drops them on the exact dress in the exact colorway they just watched. Both get averaged into one number, and that average is usually what brands try to fix.

So before touching the site, the thing worth pulling is conversion rate broken out ad by ad, or at least by landing product. If the spread is wide, the problem sits upstream of the page and it is an ad and traffic problem. If every ad converts at roughly the same rate, then it really is the site, and that is the more expensive fix. Worth knowing which one you have before anyone starts redesigning.

The second thing specific to fashion is variant integrity. Ads show a colorway, and catalog links land on the default variant more often than people think, so the shopper has to go find the thing again. That is a feed problem wearing a conversion problem's clothes, and it usually pays better than anything you would test on a button.

Fair warning on scope. What I do is Meta management, and the on-site half I treat as diagnosis from the data rather than redesign. There are no published conversion-rate-lift figures on my side to show you. If rebuilding the store is the main thing you are hiring for, someone else is the better fit.

Where I am useful: an ecommerce Meta account we ran fell from 6.45 return on ad spend in October to 3.22 in December on flat spend near $15,000. Click through was rising the whole time. CPMs were up 45 percent. The number said the store had broken. Nothing had. By April it ran 8.07 at almost exactly October's CPM.

What is your return rate after exchanges, and is Meta spend closer to five thousand a month or fifty?
