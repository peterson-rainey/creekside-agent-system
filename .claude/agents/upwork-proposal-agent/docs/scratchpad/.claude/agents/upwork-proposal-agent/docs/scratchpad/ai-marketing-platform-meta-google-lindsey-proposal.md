## PROPOSAL (287 words)

Do you know what share of your signups are still active thirty days later? That number
decides more about how these accounts get built than the targeting does, and with an AI
product it is usually the thing nobody is watching closely enough.

The reason I ask is that AI tools pull in an enormous amount of curiosity traffic. People
who collect new tools will click anything, start a free trial, and never come back. If Meta
is optimizing toward the signup event, it gets very good at finding exactly those people.
Cost per signup looks excellent, the account looks healthy on the surface, and revenue does
not move. I have watched that pattern play out more than once.

I run Meta right now for a CRM software company, and the structure that finally worked there
was pulling demo requests away from general lead capture so each campaign optimized on its
own event. The demo side stayed more expensive per action and was worth considerably more.
Before that I ran the Meta side of a referral software company that had just closed a seed
round. Meta generated awareness for a category their buyers did not know existed, and search
captured the demand it created. That is also the honest answer for how the two platforms
divide on a product like yours. My side of this is Meta and email, and the search side sits
with our team.

After 10 years of doing this, and having built and sold my own e-commerce business, the
accounts that scale are the ones where the optimization event matches where the money
actually is. Everything else is downstream of that.
