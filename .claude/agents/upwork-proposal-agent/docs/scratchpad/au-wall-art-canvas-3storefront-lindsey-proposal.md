When you compare what Meta reports against your backend sales for NZ, which direction does the gap run? Over-reporting and under-reporting are two different problems, and they push you toward opposite decisions on the same campaign.

I ask because the accounts I have worked from backend data on were almost always over-reporting, and the overstatement was never spread evenly. It concentrated in retargeting and in anything optimized to a shallow event. I ran Meta for a US replacement parts retailer into this year, roughly $131,000 spent across about 4,500 orders at 5.53x blended. That account had separate campaigns optimized to website visits, add to cart, time on site and view content, and every one of them showed a better return than the broad purchase campaign that carried half the budget and about half the orders. None of them ever held real budget. The flattering numbers were sitting where the money was not, and that only becomes visible when you rank campaigns by orders in your own sales data rather than by ROAS in Ads Manager. Put margin tiers on top of that across three storefronts and the same distortion will be hiding which tier is genuinely funding the account. When I was running my own ecommerce brand I spent a year making budget calls off platform reporting and learned that the expensive way.
