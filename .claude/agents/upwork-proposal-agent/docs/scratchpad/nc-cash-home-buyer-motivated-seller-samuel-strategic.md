## PROPOSAL (paste-ready)

Housing Special Ad Category takes away almost every lever a media buyer normally pulls on a seller campaign. No lookalikes, no exclusions, a locked age band, a minimum radius on the geo. So with 90 days of data and a creative library that already has known winners, targeting is not where your next gain lives. What is left is the event you optimize on and the friction in the form.

Which raises the first question: is Meta still optimizing on the form submit, or are you sending contract signed back in as an offline conversion? You know what a lead is worth and your lead team turns feedback around fast, so that loop is buildable. It matters because lowering CPL and lowering cost per contract usually pull in opposite directions. Instant Forms will find you the cheapest person willing to tap through, and under SAC you cannot exclude the people who already came through last month, so duplicates and tire kickers read as a CPL win on the dashboard and as nothing in the pipeline. One real qualifying question and a higher intent form tends to raise CPL and raise contracts at the same time. That tradeoff needs your buy in before anyone touches the account, not after.

On disapprovals, most of what gets killed in a restricted category starts on the page behind the ad, not in the hook. Moving the compliance pass to before publish instead of after rejection is what made rejections rare for us.

Closest work I can show you: a reverse mortgage lender on Meta under a restricted Special Ad Category, the credit and financial one, not housing, twelve months at roughly $8K to $22K a month. And a dental group running Instant Forms into a call center that graded leads daily, 2,558 leads at $25.31 blended, including a stretch where spend went from $9K to $32.5K and CPL went $18.74 to $29.85 before settling back at $20.06.

Straight with you: I have not run Housing SAC and I have no seller side account. If that is the hard filter, better you hear it now than three weeks in.

What is a contract worth to you, and do you know your cost per contract today or only your cost per lead?
