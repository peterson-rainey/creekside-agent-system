## PROPOSAL (paste-ready)

Housing Special Ad Category takes away almost every lever a media buyer normally pulls on a seller campaign, so with 90 days of data and a creative library that already has known winners, targeting is not where your next gain lives. No lookalikes, no exclusions, a locked age band, a minimum radius on the geo. What is left is the event you optimize on and the friction in the form, and on an $8-10K account that is usually where the money is sitting.

So the question worth asking first: is Meta still optimizing on the form submit, or are you sending contract signed back in as an offline conversion? You already know what a lead is worth and your lead team turns feedback around fast, so that loop is buildable. It matters because lowering CPL and lowering cost per contract often pull in opposite directions. Instant Forms will find you the cheapest person willing to tap through, and under SAC you cannot exclude the people who already came through last month, so duplicates and tire kickers read as a CPL win on the dashboard and as nothing in the pipeline. One real qualifying question and a higher intent form usually raises CPL and raises contracts at the same time. That tradeoff needs your buy in before anyone touches the account, not after.

On disapprovals, most of what gets killed in a restricted category starts on the page behind the ad, not in the hook. Once the compliance pass ran before publish instead of after rejection, rejections got rare.

Closest work I can put in front of you: a reverse mortgage lender on Meta under a restricted Special Ad Category, the credit and financial one, not housing, twelve months at roughly $8K to $22K a month. And a dental group running Instant Forms into a call center that graded leads daily, 2,558 leads at $25.31 blended, including a stretch where spend went from $9K to $32.5K and CPL went $18.74 to $29.85 before settling back at $20.06.

Straight with you: I have not run Housing SAC, and I have no seller side account. If that is the hard filter, better you hear it now than three weeks in.

What is a contract worth to you, and do you know your cost per contract today or only your cost per lead?
