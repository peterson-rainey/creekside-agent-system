Mixed beauty and aesthetics rosters usually break at the same place, and it is not creative. Google's personalized ads policy treats cosmetic procedure and body modification as health, which blocks remarketing and customer list audiences on the aesthetics accounts while the skincare and product brands sitting next to them retarget freely. Same buyer, same playbook, and one account quietly has no retargeting layer at all. So when an aesthetics account shows a remarketing campaign running at low spend with almost no impressions, that is a policy outcome and not a bidding one, and it changes what the top of funnel has to carry.

The other thing that shows up on beauty accounts specifically is testing from supplied assets. If the visuals are fixed and only copy and hooks rotate, Meta learns very little, because it separates concepts far better than it separates variations of one. Worth agreeing early on how much angle freedom a brief gives, otherwise a month of testing returns noise and the report has to say so.

Closest match on our side is a cosmetic and aesthetics practice we run on both platforms right now. Meta spend there has held between $90K and $115K a month for twelve straight months, and the Google side has run since late April and produced about 550 conversions. Multi account is the normal shape of a week rather than a stretch, August ran 21 Meta accounts and 16 Google accounts. Where I would be straight with you is the product side. We have run a DTC brand selling to women where click through held above four percent, but a shelf of skincare and body care brands is not what our results are built on, and if that is most of your roster you should weigh that against someone whose is.

Curious how the roster splits between clinics and product brands, and whether accounts live in your Business Manager or the client's. That answer shapes reporting more than anything else does.
