Most recovery centres running Meta hit the same wall, and it is rarely the creative. The pixel fires on a booking, so the algorithm goes and finds the cheapest possible booking, which is usually someone who redeems an intro offer once and never comes back. Cost per acquisition looks excellent in Ads Manager and the bank account does not move. When the real money sits in memberships and repeat visits, the fix is to send the membership conversion back to Meta as its own event with a value attached, then optimize toward that instead. It changes who the algorithm goes looking for.

The second thing worth testing early is which service is the cheapest front door. Massage, infrared sauna, cryo, red light and general recovery attract different people with different urgency, and the service that converts cheapest into a first visit is often not the one that converts best into a membership. Running them as separate entry offers rather than one campaign for the centre answers that in a few weeks instead of a year.

Worth flagging honestly: Meta reports the booking, not the membership. Until that downstream value is fed back in, any ROAS number the platform shows you for this business model is a guess. Fixable, but it is setup work, not a reporting toggle.

The closest work in our book is appointment based health rather than recovery specifically. Our team ran Meta lead campaigns for a US dental implant practice feeding a call centre, 2,558 booked inquiries at $25.31 each on roughly $65,000 of spend across about three months, plus a currently active practice sitting near $30 per booked inquiry. No cryotherapy or sauna client yet, so I would not pretend that vertical is familiar ground.
