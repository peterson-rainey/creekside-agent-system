## PROPOSAL

Quick question before anything else: when a reader clicks your Meta ad and buys on Amazon, what actually fires back to the pixel?

Nothing does. That is the structural problem in book advertising and it is why most author campaigns quietly stall. Amazon does not pass purchase data back to Meta. Attribution links give you delayed, aggregated reporting after the fact, but they do not feed the optimizer in real time. So Meta has no purchase signal to learn from and falls back on finding people who click. On a title earning a few dollars a copy, that gap eats the margin before a single hook gets a fair test.

The reason I ask is that I hit the same wall on every account where the transaction happens somewhere I do not own. When I was running my own e-commerce brand, the campaigns that worked were the ones where I could see the purchase event and let Meta learn from it. The ones that burned money optimized toward a proxy and looked fine in Ads Manager while revenue said otherwise. After 10+ years the pattern has not changed: feed the algorithm a shallow event and it finds shallow buyers.

Where that leaves your project. The Meta build I can do properly: interest stacking across the historical fiction and thriller overlap rather than treating them as one audience, 2 to 3 ad sets testing genuinely different hooks, and an attribution setup that tells you which hook sold copies instead of which one got clicks. Amazon PPC is not my lane, and you would be better served by a specialist there than by me learning on your budget.

One thing worth knowing before you choose anyone: the setup is not the hard part. The measurement is. Build that wrong and the playbook you hand yourself will point you in the wrong direction for months.
