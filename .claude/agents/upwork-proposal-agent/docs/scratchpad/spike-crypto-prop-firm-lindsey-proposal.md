## PROPOSAL (289 words / 1,742 chars — under the 5,000 limit)

Are your Meta campaigns optimizing on the first evaluation purchase, or on the traders who come back and
buy a second one after busting the first? That single setting decides which buyer Meta goes out and finds
for you.

It matters more here than in most categories, because an evaluation is priced low enough to be an impulse
buy. Optimize on that event and the algorithm will happily fill the funnel with people who try once, fail,
and never return. The account looks efficient in Ads Manager while the revenue never compounds. I had a
parts brand on Meta with the same structure, everything pointed at a shallow front-end event, and the fix
was moving optimization down the ladder to the event that actually carried margin. Because you own your
stack end to end, you can feed a signal like that back server-side at a granularity most advertisers never
get access to.

One thing worth knowing up front: prop firms are not where my reps are. Ten-plus years for me is beauty,
ecommerce, restaurants, events, and service brands, plus an ecommerce business I built and sold myself.
The account I'm in most days runs about $99K a month on Meta, so the spend level and the creative testing
cadence you're describing are familiar territory even though the category isn't. Better you hear that from
me now than a month into it.

The first thing I'd ask to see is your CPM trend across the last twelve months laid against spend. In this
category that curve usually explains more about a bad quarter than the creative does.

Attached a few results below that show what this looks like in practice.

There's a short video on my profile that covers how I work an account.
