## 1. Fee for a 90-day DTC launch

Management is 20% of monthly ad spend with a $1,500 monthly minimum, plus a one-time $1,500 setup fee. All figures USD, ad spend billed by you directly to the platform.

The setup fee covers the pre-launch work, which is the part that has no spend to price against: business manager and ad account structure, pixel and Conversions API installation and event QA, catalog if you are running one, audience and exclusion architecture, campaign and ad set build, naming and UTM conventions, and a reporting view before anything goes live.

Worked examples for the 90 days:
At $5,000/month spend: $1,500 setup plus $1,500/month, so $6,000 total.
At $10,000/month spend: $1,500 setup plus $2,000/month, so $7,500 total.
At $20,000/month spend: $1,500 setup plus $4,000/month, so $13,500 total.

Included: everything in question 4 below, on Meta.

Excluded: ad spend, creative production and photo or video shoots, brand strategy and messaging (yours), landing page or Shopify development, email and SMS platform fees, and influencer or affiliate fees. A second platform adds its own $1,500 setup and the same percentage on that platform's spend.

## 3. Platforms and initial budget split

For a brand nobody is searching for yet, Meta is where I would start. Facebook and Instagram are the only place you can create demand at volume for a premium consumer product, and it is the fastest way to find out which of the strategist's angles actually moves people.

Alongside it I would keep a small search budget running, handled by our team, covering brand terms and the two or three highest-intent category terms. Not to scale, just so the demand Meta creates does not get intercepted by a competitor bidding on your name at launch.

I would hold TikTok until Meta has told us which creative angle wins. Launching on both at once means paying twice to learn the same lesson.

How I would set the split: check actual AU search volume for the category (if it is thin, search stays a safety net rather than a channel), look at who is already advertising in the Meta ad library in your category, and work backwards from average order value, margin and how long you can wait for payback. Practically that usually lands near 80% Meta and 20% search for the first 30 days, then gets revisited once there is real purchase data rather than an assumption.

## 4. What I would handle

Setup: account and business manager structure, campaign and ad set architecture, audiences, exclusions, and the creative testing framework the strategist's concepts get loaded into.

Tracking: pixel and Conversions API, event mapping and deduplication, UTM conventions, and verification that platform-reported purchases reconcile with Shopify before launch rather than after. This is the step most launches skip and then spend a month arguing about.

Launch: staged, starting on the event the account can actually learn from given zero conversion history, and moving to purchase optimization once volume supports it.

Budget pacing: daily monitoring against the monthly plan, with adjustments made deliberately rather than reactively. Meta punishes constant budget changes during learning.

Monitoring and optimization: daily checks on spend, delivery, frequency and cost per result, weekly structural decisions on what scales, what gets rebuilt and what gets shut off.

Testing: one variable at a time, angle before format before placement, with enough spend per test to reach a real read instead of calling a winner on 12 conversions.

Reporting: a weekly written summary of what happened, what changed and what is next, plus a live dashboard you and the client can open any time. Monthly, a fuller read on cost per new customer and payback.

## 5. Working under a lead strategist

That structure suits me. You own brand, message, concept and creative direction. I own the account and the numbers.

In practice: you hand me concepts and assets, I decide how they get tested, which audiences see them and how budget moves between them, then I bring back what the data says about your angles rather than my opinion of them. When something is not working, I tell you whether it is a creative problem, an audience problem or a tracking problem, because those need three different people to fix and only one of them is you.

The one thing I would ask for is enough creative variety at launch to actually test the positioning, meaning distinct angles rather than several versions of the same idea. Three real angles beats nine crops.

I will also flag when platform mechanics push against a brand decision, for example if premium positioning wants a longer story than the placement rewards. Your call, always. I just want the tradeoff visible before spend runs against it.
