Add-to-cart optimization usually gets judged on the wrong number. Cost per add-to-cart looks fine and often improves, because delivery is doing exactly what it was asked to do and finding people who add to cart. Whether those people buy was never part of the objective. So the series worth watching is the add-to-cart to purchase rate on its own trend line. If carts climb while that rate slides, the account is getting cheaper and worse at the same time, and every extra week on that objective trains the pixel further into the wrong audience.

Worth checking one thing before you switch, though. Most accounts that feel stuck on add-to-cart are not short on purchases. They are short on purchases per ad set. At $69 to $89, aggregate volume is often already past the threshold, but it is split across enough ad sets that none of them individually clear it. That is a structure problem, not a volume problem, and consolidating usually beats waiting.

Ran into the same thing on a parts brand doing around $160 average order value. The account was spread across fourteen campaigns. Cutting to seven took ROAS from 4.87 to 8.07 over the next two months, with CPM and frequency both flat. Same budget, same creative, fewer places for the signal to fragment.

On the angle that is working, the call to make before scaling is fatigue versus saturation, because a fast creative pipeline makes the wrong fix very cheap to reach for. Frequency holding while click-through decays is fatigue, and more hooks on that angle will work. CPM and frequency rising together is saturation, and more hooks will not help you. Different problem, different sequencing.

Two things would sharpen this. Roughly what is the account spending monthly now, and how far is that from where you want it? And is the add-to-cart to purchase rate holding steady or drifting?
