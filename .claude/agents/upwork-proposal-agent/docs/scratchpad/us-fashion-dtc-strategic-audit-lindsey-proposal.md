## Proposal

When Meta looks like it's underperforming, do you know yet whether it's buying the wrong people, or buying the right people into a site that can't close them? Those two failures look identical in Ads Manager and they have opposite fixes, which is why most audits at this stage hand back a list of observations instead of an answer.

The reason I lead with that is what I've watched it cost. I ran a replacement-parts account on Meta for about ten months at roughly $15K a month, and the ads looked like the problem the entire time. Traffic was cheap, revenue was flat. What was actually happening is that the campaigns were optimizing on add-to-cart, so Meta had spent months getting genuinely excellent at finding people who browse. Moving it onto the purchase event changed who it went after. The site never changed at all.

The reverse is just as common. I took a luxury furniture brand on Shopify from about $17K to $40K a month over four months, and there the site really was part of the constraint, but not in a way a redesign would have fixed. It was a considered purchase, people came back three and four times before buying, and the account was being judged on last click. The creative that actually started those journeys kept getting paused for looking unprofitable.

I'll be straight with you on one thing, since you asked for specifics. Ten-plus years in, and after building and selling my own e-commerce brand, my DTC book is furniture, mattress, meal prep, beauty and replacement parts. I don't have a published fashion result to point at. What I do have is a repeatable way to find which layer is actually costing you money and to tell you plainly what to stop, which is usually the least comfortable part of the deliverable.
