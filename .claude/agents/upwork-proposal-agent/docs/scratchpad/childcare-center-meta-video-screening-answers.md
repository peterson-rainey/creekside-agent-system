## Q1. Describe the source(s) of your sales training experience?

Two sources, and neither one is a course.

The first is running our own sales process end to end. The discovery framework we use is one we built and
rewrote ourselves over a few hundred calls, and it is closer to diagnosis than pitching. Open with a broad
question, follow whatever they go long on, restate the problem back in cleaner language, and disqualify early
when it is not a fit. I have no sales certification to point to and would not claim one.

The second is the ad side, which I think matters more for what you are hiring for. Running lead gen for
appointment-based service businesses means sitting with whoever actually works the leads and asking which ones
closed and which ones wasted their morning. On one dental account the Meta forms fed a call center, and the
only useful feedback loop was what the callers told us about lead quality. That is where you learn what a
sales-ready lead actually looks like, and it is usually not what the form volume suggests.

## Q2. Specific example where you led the social video strategy for a sales campaign for a service business

The clearest one is our own, and I want to be straight that it was our campaign rather than a client's.

We built a five-video talking-head sequence on Meta selling marketing services to dental practices. I led the
creative strategy on it. We brought in an outside creative consultant, pulled the pain points from where
dentists actually complain to each other rather than from keyword tools, and locked one structure across all
five: name a specific pain in the first line, agitate the consequence, one line of positioning with no
explanation of how it works, then a dead simple form CTA. Each script ran 30 to 45 seconds.

The discipline was in what we cut. No case studies inside the ad, no explaining the mechanics, no education.
Each video carried exactly one objection, which is what gave us five distinct angles instead of five versions
of the same message.

That is the same build your Head of School sequence needs, and the same trap: the temptation to explain the
program instead of naming the one thing a parent is already worried about.
