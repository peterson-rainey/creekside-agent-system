When a call comes in and never turns into a booked job, does anything about that make it back into Google or Meta? In most HVAC accounts it does not, and that gap explains both symptoms. Smart Bidding buys more of whatever you count. If the conversion is "phone call" or "form submitted," it finds the cheapest version of that event: the price shopper, the out of area caller, the person asking what a capacitor costs. Quality drops and cost per lead climbs together, because you are bidding against every other contractor for the same undifferentiated click.

So the tracking work is not really a reporting project. It is the bidding fix. Once booked jobs and job values flow back to the platforms, the bidder can price a $9,000 install differently from a $180 diagnostic, and a lot of the keyword problems resolve on their own.

One tradeoff before I say anything confident about structure. Furnace repair and AC installs are not the same business. Repair is emergency intent, small ticket, closes on the phone. Installs are considered, multi week, high ticket, quote driven. Running both through one conversion action and one budget is how install money gets spent on repair clicks. Separate campaigns, separate targets, and the seasonal handoff planned rather than reacted to.

On PMax I would build it, but second. Without a value signal and a real exclusion list, PMax on a service area account mostly harvests your own brand terms and reports it as a win.

Straight with you: our trades work is landscaping, construction, pest and awnings, not HVAC specifically. Closest are a Nashville lawn and landscape account our team ran for 28 months at 298% ROI, and a Tennessee builder where monthly leads went from 15 to 60 and cost per lead came down from $454 to $239. The nearest structural match is a Florida awnings group, Google and Meta together across three service entities with territory precise targeting.
