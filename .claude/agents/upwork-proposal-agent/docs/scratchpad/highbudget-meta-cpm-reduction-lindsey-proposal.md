## Proposal (FINAL)

Is the CPM climbing evenly across the account, or is it concentrated in the few ad sets carrying most of the budget? That fork decides the whole diagnosis. Even inflation is usually auction pressure and seasonality, which you manage around. Concentrated inflation is almost always frequency and creative fatigue, where a handful of ad sets have burned through their best audience and Meta is charging a premium to keep serving them.

I ask because on large accounts CPM is a symptom, not the disease, and chasing it directly is the fastest way to make it worse. I have seen accounts pull CPM down by opening targeting and leaning on the cheap placements, then watch cost per purchase climb right behind it, because the savings came from people who were never going to convert.

The Meta accounts I operate have run between $138,000 and $224,000 a month over the past year, and the largest single one sits just under $100,000 a month on its own. That one is cosmetic and aesthetic dentistry, so the auction is crowded and expensive, and its CPM has swung from the high twenties to the high sixties inside a single year. Holding cost per booked patient steady through that swing is most of the actual job. Before agency work I built and sold my own e-commerce brand, which taught me to check the back end before touching the ad account.

In the first week I would want frequency, placement, and creative age broken out at the ad set level, plus what a customer is genuinely worth to you.
