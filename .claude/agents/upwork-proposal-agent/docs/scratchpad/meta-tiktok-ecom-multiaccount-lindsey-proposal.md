## PROPOSAL BODY

ECOM.

Across the accounts you run now, is new-customer revenue reported separately from returning-customer revenue, or is every account judged on blended ROAS out of Ads Manager? That split decides which creative gets briefed next month, and it is the number most multi-account setups do not actually have.

The reason I ask is what happened on a replacement-parts brand I ran on Shopify. Every shallow-event campaign we tested, add to cart, view content, time on site, posted a prettier ROAS than the broad purchase campaign, and not one of them could hold real budget. What moved the account was putting past purchasers in their own campaign so I could see what a stranger cost, then cutting the live creative count back to the angles actually earning and consolidating ad sets so the pixel had one place to learn. Over ten months that account did $130,153 in spend and 4,492 purchases at a $29 cost per purchase and 5.5x blended. It churned in May, so I am describing it in past tense.

The other pattern worth having before you hand someone several accounts: a consumer hardware brand I ran held 1.88x at $11,000 to $12,000 a month, went to $20,000, and fell to 1.22x within four weeks. Audience was never the ceiling there. Creative supply was. Since your design and video team is in-house, brief-to-asset turnaround is the real scaling constraint, and I would size it per account before promising anyone a spend curve.

Ten years on Meta and email for ecommerce brands, plus my own ecommerce business that I built and sold, which is where the offer and margin instincts come from. Today I run Meta for two live Shopify brands and one lead-gen account sitting just under $100K a month.

Straight with you on one of your bullets: TikTok is not on my record as a channel I have scaled for ecommerce. I would rather say that now than in month three. Meta, Shopify and Klaviyo are where I earn a seat.
