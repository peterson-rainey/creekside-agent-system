Is the document fee firing back into Meta and Google as a purchase event, or does it just land in Shopify and get reconciled by hand?

That question decides most of the build. Marketplace works because the inventory does the qualifying for you. Someone finds a specific parcel, in a specific county, at a specific price, and self selects. Paid traffic has none of that, so both platforms will happily buy form fills from people who never send a dollar. The document fee is the one event that cleanly separates a browser from a buyer, and it already exists in Shopify as a real transaction. Getting it back into both platforms as a conversion, with the parcel and the value attached, is the difference between an account that scales and one that just gets more expensive.

One honest tradeoff. Early on there will not be enough doc fee payments per week to feed Meta's optimizer, so anyone promising purchase optimization on day one is guessing. Start on lead volume, track the doc fee beside it from week one, and move campaigns onto that event once volume carries it.

Structurally, avoid one blended owner financed land campaign. It averages a $4,000 half acre against a 300 acre ranch and learns nothing about either. Campaign per parcel, named by county and acreage, with the property photos cut several ways, static, video and panorama. Financing changes the copy and the keywords too. Ads lead with down payment and monthly, not sale price, and the Google intent sits in owner financed land, no credit check and county level acreage searches rather than land for sale.

Our team ran exactly this for a Texas owner financed land seller, both platforms. On Meta, 48 parcel level campaigns, $26,341 for 1,136 leads at $23.19, best parcels between $14.86 and $17.92 and the worst above $50, which is the whole argument for splitting by property. On Google, $11,963 for 1,023 leads at $11.69, mostly county named search, with a remarketing layer at $6.87. Straight with you on scale, when Meta spend went up roughly 5x cost per lead drifted from about $17 to $24, so plan for that. Performance Max there reported leads at $2.57 and they were not the same lead, so I would add it late and gated.

To start I would put $5,000 a month on Google and $3,000 on Meta. Below that the parcel splits starve.

We charge a percentage of ad spend, tiered so the rate comes down as spend grows, with a monthly minimum per platform and a one time build fee per platform. Happy to put exact numbers to it once I know roughly what range you are considering.
