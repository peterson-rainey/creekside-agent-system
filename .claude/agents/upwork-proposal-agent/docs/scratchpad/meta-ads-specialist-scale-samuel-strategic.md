## PROPOSAL
Before optimizing anything that is already live, the number worth checking is what you cut ads on. If it is ROAS or cost per purchase, that one habit is usually what caps an account at its current size.

Both of those move for reasons that have nothing to do with your ads. An ecommerce account we ran through last Q4 is a clean example. Return on ad spend fell from 6.45 to 3.22 on flat spend, about $15,000 a month either way. Every instinct says the creative broke. It had not. Click through was climbing the entire time, 1.59 percent up to 1.86 percent, while CPMs rose roughly 45 percent because the holiday auction got expensive. The reported number was describing the auction, not the work. Anyone cutting on return that quarter would have killed the ads that were actually getting better.

We kept the angles whose click through held up through the expensive weeks and cut the ones that only looked good on cheap impressions. By April the account was at 8.07 on $13,000, cost per purchase down to $19.89 from $44.93 in December. Part of that was simply Q4 pricing normalizing, and I would rather say so than oversell it. The part that was not: October and April ran at almost identical CPMs, and April still did 8.07 against October's 6.45, at $19.89 a purchase against $27.50.

So the first thing worth looking at in your account is not the campaigns. It is whether your CPM trend and your return trend explain each other. That tells you whether you have a creative problem, an auction problem, or a measurement problem, and those get very different treatment. Optimizing the wrong one is how accounts stall at the same ceiling for a year.

Two things before I can say anything useful. What are you selling, and roughly where does monthly Meta spend sit today? Also, when you say scale, is that adding twenty percent or doubling it? Those fail in different ways.

Happy to look at the account and tell you which problem you have.
