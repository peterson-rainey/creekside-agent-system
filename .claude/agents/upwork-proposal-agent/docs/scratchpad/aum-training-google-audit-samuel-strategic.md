Most premium studios that write off Google Ads never actually had a Google Ads problem. They had a conversion definition problem. If the account was optimizing to form fills or phone clicks, the algorithm spent every month getting better at producing the cheapest possible inquiry, which in a high-ticket Boston market is the exact person who will never enroll. The account can look fine on paper and still return nothing, because nothing downstream of the consultation was ever fed back into it.

That is the first thing worth checking. If enrollments never flowed back into Google as offline conversions, then no CAC figure from that period is real, and neither is the conclusion that the channel failed. It means the channel was aimed at the wrong endpoint.

The second is where the spend actually sat. In this vertical, budget tends to pool on brand terms and on the "personal trainer" head query, which is crowded with certification seekers, job seekers, and big-box gym shoppers. Stripping that out often removes a majority of historical spend with no loss of qualified volume.

Worth naming the tradeoff up front: qualified non-brand search volume in a single metro is finite. For a premium studio in Boston, the real question a test answers is not whether leads can be generated. It is whether there is enough high-intent monthly volume to hit an enrollment target at a CAC your lifetime value can absorb. Sometimes the honest answer is that Search is a strong supplement rather than the primary acquisition channel. I would rather establish that in a quarter than defend a budget for a year.

I would also plan the read around a full quarter instead of a month. With a consult-to-enrollment lag, a four week window measures leads, not economics.

Closest work: a hair restoration clinic with $8K to $15K procedure values, consultation gated the same way yours is, at 690 inquiries and a $134 cost per inquiry across $92.5K in Google spend. And a single-metro practice running Search at $86 a lead with flat volume, rebuilt into geo and service specific campaigns, which finished at $50.29 across 229 conversions on $11.5K in spend. Both are attached.

What were you spending monthly, and across how long a stretch? That decides whether the old data is readable at all.
