Are your margin tiers actually in the product feed right now, or do they live in a spreadsheet off to the side? That one answer decides whether tier bidding is a two week job or a two month one, on Meta as much as on Shopping.

I ask because the same problem shows up in Meta catalogs and almost nobody checks it. I ran Meta for a US replacement parts retailer into this year, about $131,000 across roughly 4,500 orders at 5.53x. The catalog campaigns were the strongest structure in that account, 10.83x on the first build and 6.96x after we scaled it to four times the budget, both well ahead of the account average. What made them work was segmenting the catalog into product sets instead of running it as one pool. On another brand I split the feed by availability, so in stock products had their own campaign and their own target. Same mechanic your margin tiers need, different attribute.

After ten years of this the pattern I trust most is that structure beats targeting. When I was running my own ecommerce brand I made the opposite mistake for a year, one catalog and one target, letting the algorithm average my best and worst products together.

On the Google side, Shopping, the Merchant Center feed and PMax sit with the person who runs Google in our book, not with me. Worth knowing the same carve-out works there: on a Shopify account of ours, all products Shopping ran 5.52x, and pulling the best sellers into their own campaign took that subset to 8.30x. So you would get me on Meta and Instagram and our Google side on the feed, which is two people rather than the one you posted for. TikTok neither of us can show you results for.

Those numbers are ours to walk you through at any date range you pick. There is a short video on my profile that covers how I run accounts like this.
