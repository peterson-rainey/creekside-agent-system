## PROPOSAL (paste-ready)

Sending lead stage back through Conversions API only changes how Meta spends your money once the account clears a bar most seller campaigns never reach. The conversion leads goal, the one that actually optimizes on a CRM stage, only works with Instant Forms, needs at least 200 leads a month, and needs that stage to happen within 28 days of the lead. At the $3K end of your range, 200 leads means $15 seller leads, which is not a realistic number.

So the plan runs in two stages. Build CAPI on day one anyway, because it gives you cost per appointment and cost per contract by ad, not just cost per lead. Until volume clears the bar, optimize on the lead, with a form built to screen: one real question on timeline or property condition. That raises CPL and cuts the tire kickers. Once volume is there, flip the goal to appointment set, not contract signed, since contracts often land outside that 28-day window.

Housing SAC makes the form matter more. No lookalikes, locked age and gender, a minimum radius on the geo. The form and the optimization event are most of what is left to pull.

Straight answers on your list. I don't have wholesaler or flip campaigns on Meta to show you, so I can't give you a seller-side CPL or lead-to-contract rate. Our team runs a direct-to-seller account for a wholesaler right now, but on Google and too new for numbers. Closest Meta work: a reverse mortgage lender under the financial products Special Ad Category for about a year at roughly $8K to $22K a month, and a dental group running Instant Forms into a call center that graded every lead, 2,558 leads at $25.31 blended.

On CAPI, not me personally. The tracking specialist on our team builds it, not an outside developer.

On the trial, we work on a 90-day minimum. Under Housing SAC the first month mostly finds which seller angles get approved and deliver, so a 30-day read judges the setup more than the account.

Weekly check-ins during US business hours are standard for us, we're on Central time.
