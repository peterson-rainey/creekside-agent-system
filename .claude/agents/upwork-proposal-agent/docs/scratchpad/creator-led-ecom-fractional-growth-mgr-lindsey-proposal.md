## Proposal body

Do you know what your cost per acquired customer looks like with everyone who already follows the creator excluded?

That single cut decides whether a monthly campaign cadence compounds or just recycles. On a creator-led brand the warm audience converts on almost any ad, so Meta finds those people first and cheapest. Blended ROAS looks healthy, every launch posts a decent number, and the account quietly plateaus the month the warm pool runs dry. The campaign calendar hides it, because each new drop re-harvests the same list and reads as a fresh win.

I ask because I ran Meta for a parts and equipment brand in a similar spot. Over ten months that account did $130,153 in spend and 4,492 purchases at a $29 cost per purchase and 5.5x blended. What it taught me is that existing customers have to sit in their own campaign or you never learn what a stranger costs. Every shallow-event campaign we ran, add to cart, view content, time on site, posted a prettier ROAS than the broad purchase campaign. None of them could ever hold real budget.

The other pattern worth naming, since you want roughly one campaign a month: I ran a consumer hardware brand that held 1.88x at $11,000 to $12,000 a month, then pushed to $20,000 and watched it fall to 1.22x inside four weeks. Audience was never the ceiling. Creative supply was. That is the constraint I would size first here, because your brief has the creator content coordinated by an internal team, and campaign cadence is only as fast as the brief-to-asset loop.

Ten years of this, plus my own e-commerce brand that I built and sold, mostly beauty, fashion, food and events. This year I built Klaviyo welcome, abandoned checkout and abandoned cart flows for two DTC brands, so email is hands-on here, not advisory.

Straight with you on two of your bullets: TikTok Shop and Amazon are not on my record, and I would rather say so now than in month three. Your sequencing is right regardless. Shopify, Meta and email first.
