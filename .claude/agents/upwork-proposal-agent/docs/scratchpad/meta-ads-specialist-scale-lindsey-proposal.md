When you cut an ad right now, what number are you cutting on? If it is ROAS or cost per purchase, that one habit is usually what keeps an account from scaling.

The reason I ask is that both of those numbers move for reasons that have nothing to do with your ads. I ran Meta for a US replacement parts ecommerce brand through a fourth quarter where return dropped from 7.7x to 4.3x on flat spend. Every instinct says the creative broke. It had not. Click through rate was climbing the whole way, 1.59% up to 1.86%, while CPMs rose about 45% because the auction got expensive. The blended number was reporting the auction, not the work. We kept the angles whose click through held through the expensive stretch and cut the ones that only looked good on cheap impressions. Cost per conversion went from $44.93 in December to $19.89 by April, and CPM was flat across that recovery, so it was not just Q4 pricing normalizing.

Ten years on Meta, plus an ecommerce brand I built and sold, have mostly taught me that scaling is less about new strategies than about not killing what already works.

Separately, on scale itself, the largest account I personally run is just under $100K a month on Meta, about $1.18 million over the last twelve months.

One thing I would want before saying anything useful about strategy. Roughly where does your monthly Meta spend sit today, and does scaling mean doubling it or adding twenty percent? Those are very different problems.
