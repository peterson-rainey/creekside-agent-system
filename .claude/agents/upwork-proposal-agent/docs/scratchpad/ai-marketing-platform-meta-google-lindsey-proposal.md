## PROPOSAL (313 words)

Do you know what share of your signups are still active thirty days later? That number decides more about how these accounts get built than the targeting does, and with an AI product it is usually the thing nobody is watching closely enough.

The reason I ask is that AI tools pull in an enormous amount of curiosity traffic. People who collect new tools will click anything, start a free trial, and never come back. If Meta is optimizing toward the signup event, it gets very good at finding exactly those people. Cost per signup looks excellent and revenue does not move.

The closest thing I have run to your situation was the Meta side of a referral software company that had just closed a seed round. Meta generated awareness for a category their buyers did not know existed, and search picked up the demand it created. That is also the honest answer for how the two platforms divide on a product like yours. My side of this is Meta and email, and the search side sits with our team.

Outside software, the account I lean on most for this problem is a subscription brand I run now. The fix there was building new customer campaigns that excluded every previous buyer, so we stopped paying to re acquire people who were coming back on their own. On a product with a free tier in front of it, that same trap shows up as returning users counting as fresh signups.

After 10 years of doing this, and having built and sold my own e-commerce business, the accounts that scale are the ones where the optimization event matches where the money actually is.
