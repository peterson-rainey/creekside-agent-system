## Draft (paste-ready, 296 words)

The thing that usually decides a three-part Head of School sequence is not the scripts, it is whether your radius can feed it. Sequential video retargeting works by building audiences off view depth, so part one has to reach enough parents that the 25% and 75% viewer pools are large enough to serve parts two and three. At a single campus with a tight geo, those pools tend to run dry inside a couple of weeks, and the sequence quietly collapses into part one running at rising frequency against the same few thousand people.

There are two ways to handle that, and they are a real tradeoff. Either you keep the sequence and accept a wider radius than you would like, or you keep the radius tight and run the three videos as parallel messages to the same audience rather than as gated steps. The second one is usually the right call for one location. It gives up the narrative and buys you stable delivery.

Worth asking early: when a tour gets booked, does that event make it back into the ad account, or does it stop at the CRM? That single connection is where most single-location accounts leak. If Meta is only ever taught what a form fill looks like, it gets very good at finding parents who inquire and never walk in, and the cost per lead looks great while the tour calendar stays soft. Wiring the booked tour back as the optimization event changes what the algorithm hunts for.

We have run this pattern for appointment-driven local businesses, including a children's education franchise on Meta and Google, and the accounts that work are always the ones where the calendar, not the form, is the scoreboard.
