## Proposal (paste-ready)

Do you know your close rate on Meta leads separately from your Google leads, or are they both landing in one bucket? I ask because that one split decides almost everything about how these two accounts should be built, and most of the accounts I get handed have never separated them.

The pattern I run into when I audit lead gen accounts is that the blended cost per lead looks healthy while one platform is quietly carrying the other. I worked on an account where Meta was the cheap winner on every report and almost none of it was turning into booked business, because the campaigns were optimizing for whoever filled out a form the fastest. Moving optimization onto a deeper event further down the funnel cost us volume on paper and fixed the part that actually mattered. Search behaved nothing like that, because those people had already typed the problem into a search bar before they ever saw us.

I have been doing this for over ten years, and before agency work I built and sold my own e-commerce business, where I learned to care about what actually banked rather than what the dashboard claimed. Meta and paid social is my side of the house. Our team runs the Google side across service businesses, home services, legal, healthcare and B2B software, so each platform gets someone who works in it daily, not one generalist splitting attention.

On reporting, the number I chase is what happened after the lead, not how many arrived. That is the difference between a report you read and a report that changes a budget.
