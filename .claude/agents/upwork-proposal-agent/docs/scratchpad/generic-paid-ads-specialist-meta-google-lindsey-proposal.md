## Proposal (paste-ready)

Do you know your close rate on Meta leads separately from your Google leads, or are they both landing in one bucket? I ask because that one split decides almost everything about how these two accounts should be built, and most of the accounts I get handed have never separated them.

Google leads come from someone who already typed the problem into a search bar. Meta leads come from someone who was not looking for you at all and got interrupted. Those two people close at very different rates, and when you average them into a single cost per lead the number looks healthy while one platform quietly carries the other. I have seen accounts where Meta looked like the cheap winner on paper and was producing almost nothing that turned into revenue, and the fix was not better targeting. It was feeding the platform a deeper conversion event so it stopped optimizing for whoever fills out a form the fastest.

I have been doing this for over ten years, and before agency work I built and sold my own e-commerce business, where I learned to care about what actually banked rather than what the dashboard claimed. Meta and paid social is my side of the house. Our team runs the Google side across service businesses, home services, legal, healthcare and B2B software, so each platform gets someone who works in it daily, not one generalist splitting attention.

On reporting, I would want to track what happens after the lead, not just how many arrived. That is usually the difference between a report you read and a report that changes a budget.
