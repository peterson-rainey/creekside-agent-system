Before anything else, what does your call center do with a lead in the first five minutes, and what happens to the ones they cannot reach? That shapes the build more than any targeting decision.

I ask because the last account I ran at real volume was a high ticket dental group feeding a call center, and the thing that actually moved cost per qualified call was never the audience. It was the handoff. We did 2,558 leads at a $25.31 average CPL over about three months on $64K in spend, and partway through we deliberately cut ad spend because the call center could not keep up with the flow. Lead volume dropped, CPL went up, and it was the right trade. Most media buyers will not throttle a campaign that looks like it is winning. If your transfer team is the real constraint, that is the number I would manage to.

The other lever there was adding qualifying questions to the lead forms. CPL rose immediately, and that was the point. Cheap forms fill up fast with people who never answer the phone.

Straight answers to what you asked. Ten plus years, Meta and email. Verticals are dental and medical, aesthetics, e-commerce, real estate, restaurants and local services, plus the e-commerce brand I built and sold myself. Lead forms and landing page forms at volume, yes. Live transfers and pay per call platforms, no, I have not run those. Current spend is just under $100K a month on a single Meta account.

On MVA specifically I am not going to pretend. Legal has been a Google vertical in our shop, not a Meta one, and the law firms we tested on Facebook did not convert directly. That is exactly why a pay per lead operation with a call center in front of it is the right shape for Meta here, and why a single firm chasing signed cases straight off Facebook is not.
