## PROPOSAL

Feed-only PMax sitting next to branded Search is the most common reason a $45K account goes flat. PMax quietly absorbs brand-adjacent shopping queries, so blended ROAS gets propped up by traffic that was already yours, and the moment you add budget the incremental dollar lands in non-brand where the true ROAS is a fraction of what the account average implies. That is usually why an account sits under target at current spend and gets worse when you push it.

On a Shopify ecommerce account we run with that same three-way structure, brand Search sits near 28x while non-brand Search and non-brand Shopping both sit between 5.3x and 5.6x. Blended reads 12x. Nobody scaling against that 12x would ever hit it. First thing I would do in your account is force the separation, brand exclusions on PMax, a clean brand versus non-brand read, then a tROAS target per segment instead of one number across everything.

Second piece matters more if margin is the real goal. At a $259 AOV, tROAS optimizes gross revenue, so Google will happily buy your lowest-margin SKUs all day. Pushing margin-adjusted conversion value out of Shopify through GA4 into Google changes what the bidder is actually chasing. Same idea with new-customer value in PMax if repeat purchase is meaningful for you.

Where I would push back. If the account comes back clean, then at $45K a month on one product category you may simply be near the ceiling of harvestable cervical pillow and neck pain intent. In that case the lever is margin and demand creation, not bidding, and I would tell you that rather than sell you a restructure.

## ANSWERS

1. The trade is reach for predictability. Stripping assets keeps spend on the surfaces where purchase intent is highest, which is usually the right call for a catalog like yours, but you give up any real read on whether Display and YouTube would have been incremental. The caveat most people miss is that feed-only does not make PMax brand-safe. It still serves branded shopping queries, so without brand exclusions it harvests demand you already own and books it as PMax performance. Worth confirming final URL expansion and automatically created assets are both off, since either one quietly reintroduces surfaces you thought you stripped. On optimizing it, the feed is your only targeting lever once assets are gone, so I would segment with custom labels by contribution margin and sell-through, then split into separate campaigns so each margin band carries its own tROAS. Titles do the query matching, so cervical and physical therapist designed belong in the title itself, not just the description.

2. Usually it means the target is not actually binding. A target that binds makes the campaign underspend rather than spend in full and miss, so if you are spending every dollar and landing short, the bidder is optimizing toward a predicted value that does not match the value you are realizing. First thing I check is conversion value accuracy. A duplicate purchase tag, GA4 and gtag both firing, or a secondary action counted as a conversion will inflate predicted value enough that the algorithm believes it is on target while Shopify disagrees. I would reconcile Google reported revenue against Shopify revenue for the same window before touching a bid. After that, whether brand and non-brand sit under one target, since a blended number is trivially beaten by brand and unreachable for non-brand, and how far the target sits from trailing actual. If actual is 3x and target is 6x there is no path, and moving targets more than fifteen or twenty percent at a time just resets learning.

3. Arithmetic before the account. ROAS is roughly conversion rate times AOV divided by CPC, so if CR improved and ROAS stayed flat, CPC rose by about the same proportion and ate the gain. That is not an efficiency problem in the account, that is the price of traffic going up, and no amount of restructuring changes the cost of the auction. Which points the lever at the two terms the auction cannot inflate away, AOV and margin. At $259 a bundle or a second unit attach moves ROAS permanently in a way a bid adjustment never will. The account side version of that same lever is mix, finding intent that has not been bid up rather than paying more for the same head terms. Two things I would confirm before settling on that read: whether the CPC rise is market wide or self inflicted, which auction insights and impression share will tell you, and whether the conversion rate gain is concentrated in brand traffic. If CR only improved on brand, your non-brand economics did not actually change and the flat ROAS is telling you something else.

4. Yes. The closest comparison is a Shopify brand we run at about $500 average order value, a considered purchase where people research before they buy, so roughly double your price point, and the structure is close to yours. What changes for premium is that the research then return pattern makes last click punish non-brand unfairly. In that account non-brand costs about $60 to acquire against about $17 on brand, but non-brand order value is higher, around $545 versus $486. Judge non-brand on last click ROAS alone and you cut the campaigns feeding the brand demand you are harvesting later. Second difference is that discount led copy is a mistake for premium. It trains people to wait for a sale and works directly against margin, so credibility carries the ad instead. That is where the physical therapist design, review extensions and seller ratings earn their place in the feed and assets. Third, consideration lag is longer, so if your conversion window is short you are undercounting, and I would look at the lag distribution before judging any campaign.
