## Draft (paste-ready, 271 words)

Before the three videos, do you know how many people in your radius are actually in-market for a spot this term, and what your reach ceiling looks like at your current budget? I ask because a sequenced video build is the right idea and the most common way it quietly fails at a single location. A three-part sequence only works if part one reaches enough people that the 25% and 75% view audiences are big enough to serve parts two and three. In a one-campus radius, those pools starve fast, and the sequence collapses into one video running against a shrinking audience while frequency climbs.

I have run this exact shape for local businesses selling a booked appointment rather than a product, mostly in dental and aesthetics, where the whole job is getting a real person onto a calendar. The pattern is always the same. The account is optimized for the form, not the booked visit, so Meta learns to find people who fill things out and never show up. The fix is feeding the booking event back, not rewriting the ads.

For the Head of School videos, the strongest structure I have seen is one objection per part. Part one is the thing parents worry about at night. Part two is how the day actually runs. Part three is the tour itself and what happens when they walk in. That gives us three real audiences to build from instead of three versions of the same message.
