Splitting emergency, maintenance and installation into three ad groups is the right instinct, but it will quietly cost you if all three sit inside one campaign. Ad groups share a budget and a bid strategy, and a blocked-drain click converts far cheaper than a full installation enquiry. Smart Bidding notices that, chases the cheap conversion, and your installation keywords lose impression share while the emergency side eats the daily budget. Three campaigns rather than three ad groups is usually the fix.

The other half of that is conversion values. If a 2am call-out and an installation enquiry both land in GA4 as "lead", Google has no way to know which one you actually want more of. Assigning a value to each conversion action in GTM, even a rough one based on average job value per service, is the change that makes weekly optimisation mean anything. Without it you are reallocating budget on lead count and calling it ROI.

On evidence, a straight answer: no drainage account. The closest is a lawn and landscaping company where Google Ads produced just over 1,500 leads at a $50.35 average cost per lead across 28 months, which they tracked to roughly 298% ROI. For your installation side, a construction client went from 15 to 60 leads a month while cost per lead came down from $454 to $239, which is the same longer-cycle problem your install phrases have. There is also a pest control account worth mentioning, because the emergency-plus-recurring mix there is closer to your business than the lawn work is.

Drainage terms are not exotic and the buying behaviour is one I have worked in for six years. Emergency is proximity, call answer rate and dayparting. Installation is patience and tracking that survives a three-week gap between click and job.

One thing before anything else: roughly what share of revenue comes from installations versus call-outs? That ratio sets the budget split, and it is the number these accounts usually get wrong.
