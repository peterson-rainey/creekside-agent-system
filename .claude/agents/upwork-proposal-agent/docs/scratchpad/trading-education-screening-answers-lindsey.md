## 1. One business you took from little or no paid spend into real, profitable acquisition

Straight answer first: not one that gives you all four things you asked for. You were specific, so you should hear the shape of the gap from me rather than find it on a call.

The pattern is that the accounts with the full economics were already spending when I picked them up, and the account I did take from a standing start does not pass revenue back, so there is no MER to quote on it. Splitting those two apart is the honest version.

The standing start is a dental practice I still run. First month was $29.62 in September last year, building to roughly $4,000 to $5,000 a month now, about $42,000 through the account. Conversions only began recording in July. Cost per booked consult was $149.25 that month and $63.87 in August. Direction is right and the trend is only two months old, so I would not sell it to you as more than it is. No revenue is passed back into the platform on it, so I have no ROAS and no MER for you.

The full economics come from a spa parts account I ran on Meta until it wrapped. $129,606.97 spent, $721,277.57 in Meta attributed revenue, 4,481 purchases. That is $28.92 acquisition cost and 5.57x blended. Call that what it is: revenue Meta claimed over Meta spend. It is not MER. MER is total business revenue over total ad spend and I did not own the denominator on that account, so quoting one would be inventing a number.

What I changed there is the part worth your time. The account had a Website Visits campaign showing 10.29x on $3,341 and the broad purchase campaign carrying the volume, $66,917 of it, sitting at 5.02x. Every dashboard read said move budget to the winner. The winner was buying people already on their way to purchasing. Budget went the other direction, into the campaign with the worse headline number and the real incremental buyers.

## 2. A low-ticket or subscription funnel you have scaled, and how I thought about payback

Scaled is the wrong word for what I can show you, so take this as pattern rather than scale.

The subscription account our team runs is a meal delivery brand, taken over in January. The acquisition campaign has spent $8,939.50 and brought in 1,173 subscribers at $7.62 each. Monthly cost per subscriber went from $11.53 in January to $5.50 in July, then back to $11.59 in August. Across March to July, the clean reporting months, it returned 9.76x on $5,392 of spend. It runs at about $1,400 a month. That is a real continuity business and it is not your scale, and I would rather put both facts next to each other.

I have not run a $297 tripwire into a $297 monthly back end. Nearest shape is that meal subscription, where the first order also does not pay for the customer.

On payback, the thing I actually watch is not the blended number, it is which acquisition source produces which retention curve. On the meal account the first order never covered acquisition. What decided whether a cohort was worth buying was the reorder rate in weeks two through six, and that rate was different by angle. Ads selling convenience produced people who lapsed after the first box. Ads selling a specific outcome produced people who stayed. Same cost per first order, completely different businesses six weeks later.

That is the same question in your funnel. Two ads can both sell the $297 suite at the same cost and one fills the room with annual members while the other fills it with month-two cancellations. Blended together they are invisible. My working rule is that the front end sets how fast you can spend and the back end cohort curve sets how much you are allowed to pay, and you cannot set a target cost per acquisition until you can see the second one broken out by entry angle.

## 3. What I would want to know about your numbers before spending a dollar

Contribution margin on the $297 suite after processing, refunds, affiliate or JV payouts and support. Whatever survives is the actual acquisition budget for the front end.

Plan mix at the point of sale and a month by month survival curve for monthly, quarterly and annual held separately rather than blended. Blended retention on a three plan product hides the only thing that matters.

The same mix and curve broken out by which ad or angle acquired the person. If that does not exist yet, it is the first thing I would build, because every budget decision afterwards depends on it.

Take rate and lag from suite buyer to trading room subscriber. What share ever start, and how many days later. That decides whether we buy the suite or buy the room, and it changes the entire structure.

Refund and chargeback rate on both products and the window they sit in. In this category that number is usually the difference between a campaign that looks profitable and one that is.

An organic versus paid baseline. You have a real audience already, which means the first paid dollars will get credit for revenue that was arriving anyway. I would want a pre period baseline and a holdout, otherwise we will both misread month one.

How many months of negative contribution the business can carry. Running a front end at breakeven only works if the cash position allows the payback window you are underwriting.
