## PROPOSAL

Fashion is the category where blended ROAS lies the most. Markdown cycles plus a warm list mean the account number can look healthy for a year while cold acquisition never actually gets measured. So the first pull from your Meta account is not CAC by month. It is what share of spend and what share of revenue sat inside promo campaigns and retargeting. Until those are stripped out, the question you are actually asking, whether this is acquisition or creative or conversion, cannot be answered. Most audits skip that step and go straight to creative, because creative is the easiest thing to have opinions about.

A high ticket DTC brand we ran shows the shape of it. In its best month, $40,389 in spend returned around $168,000. A sitewide fifty percent off campaign carried 59 percent of the spend and 59 percent of the revenue. Retargeting took another 19 percent of spend and returned 35 percent of the revenue. Strip both and roughly $9,000 of genuinely cold spend is left, on an account everyone internally described as scaling.

The second thing specific to fashion is that the creative variable is usually the product, not the hook. On that same account, prospecting split by product lane ran from 2.36 down to 1.36, one lane was killed at 0.40 after eleven days, and the undifferentiated catch all campaign returned 0.08. Same brand, same buyer, same month.

Straight about the gap before you go further. No published fashion or apparel results on our side. One apparel account, ended in June, nothing worth showing you. No conversion rate before and after either, so the site half of this is diagnosis from your data, not a CRO track record. If in category proof is the bar, better to know now.

What we do have is the diagnostic work itself, run in house by our team. Another ecommerce Meta account we managed fell from 7.69 return to 4.30 across Q4 on flat spend near $15,000. Click through rose the entire time, 1.59 to 1.86 percent. CPMs were up 45 percent. Nothing had broken. The auction had. By April it ran 8.57 at almost exactly October's CPM. Reading that quarter wrong buys a brand a creative rebuild it never needed.
