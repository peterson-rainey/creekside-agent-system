## PROPOSAL (paste-ready)

Is the account optimising for Purchase right now, and in the ad copy, are the symptoms written as the reader's own ("your bloating, your 3am wake-ups") or in third person? At a $69 price point those two things decide more than the targeting does.

I ask about the copy first because Meta reads second-person symptom language as an assertion about someone's health, which falls under personal attributes. Sometimes that surfaces as a clean rejection you can appeal. More often it surfaces as an ad that stays live and quietly underdelivers, which looks like creative fatigue and gets treated as a creative problem. It is not. Rewriting the same hook as an observation about a group rather than a claim about the reader usually restores delivery without softening the message, and in this niche that is most of the gap between copy that reads well and copy that actually runs.

On the price point: a low ticket offer is the friendlier case for Meta, because the purchase event fires often enough to teach the algorithm quickly, but only if purchase is what you are optimising for. Brands sitting on a warm organic audience almost always hedge into a lead magnet first. That builds a list and starves the pixel at the same time.

The closest work I can point to is a low AOV e-commerce account on Meta where, across five months of purchase-optimised buying, cost per purchase came down from $44.93 to $19.89 on roughly flat spend. That client has since churned. Straight with you on the rest: I have not scaled a digital course specifically, and the coaching account in our book runs on a lead objective rather than a checkout, so it is a shape match and not a like for like. I built and sold my own e-commerce business before this, so the offer side is familiar ground.

What monthly spend are you working towards, relative to what you are running now?

Attached a few results below.
