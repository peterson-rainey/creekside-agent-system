Getting the Pixel and the Conversions API to agree on one event ID is the easy half. The harder problem on a plugin and sample library store is that even a perfectly deduplicated purchase usually reports the wrong number. Crossgrades, upgrade pricing, bundle discounts and multi currency checkout all tend to pass list price or pre discount value into the event, and everything downstream inherits that. Value based lookalikes get seeded off revenue that never existed, and the campaigns actually finding new producers look worse than the ones selling upgrades to people who already own three of your instruments.

That second part is what I would look at first. On most music software accounts a large share of revenue comes from existing owners buying the next thing. If those buyers are not excluded from prospecting, prospecting reads profitable without being incremental. Splitting reported revenue into first time buyers and returning owners is usually the most useful thing an audit establishes.

The other structural issue is event volume. Instrument purchases are infrequent and heavily considered, so if Purchase is carrying optimization, ad sets can sit in learning most of the month. Demo or free plugin registrations are often the better optimization event, with purchase value handled through value rules instead of the objective. Not automatic though, since it hands Meta a cheaper event to chase.

Worth planning around the calendar too. Plugin companies do a disproportionate share of the year in November, and auction costs climb into it. On a spa parts ecommerce account our team ran on Meta, spend held near $15K a month while blended ROAS went 6.45 in October, 4.38 in November, 3.22 in December, then recovered to 8.07 by April. $131.6K across the run at 5.53 blended. Better to know that shape before Q4.
