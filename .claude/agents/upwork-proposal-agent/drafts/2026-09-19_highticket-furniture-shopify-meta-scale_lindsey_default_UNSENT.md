# High-ticket furniture Shopify, Meta Ads Specialist, Lindsey (lindsey_default), UNSENT, 2026-09-19

When you check ROAS in Ads Manager, do you know how many of your orders come in more than a week after the first ad click? At $500 to $3,000, most buyers save the piece, measure the room, maybe order a swatch, and come back later. Meta's click window tops out at 7 days, so the prospecting that starts those purchases can look like a loser while retargeting takes the credit. Cut the wrong campaign and acquisition cost goes up, not down.

I ask because I ran Meta for a luxury furniture brand earlier this year. The account spent about $105,000 over four months, scaling from roughly $25K to $40K a month, and the sales campaigns brought in 334 conversions at about $274 each. Two things stood out. First, CPMs nearly doubled as spend went up, from around $42 to $79, so every budget step needed fresh creative behind it or efficiency slipped. Second, about a third of those conversions came from sale events. That is great for a month's revenue, but it tells you very little about what a cold customer will pay full price for. On a brand trying to lower acquisition cost, I'd split those two numbers apart before deciding what to scale.

Before that I built and sold my own e-commerce business, and I currently run Meta for a premium mattress brand, so long consideration cycles on high-ticket products are familiar ground. The pattern I keep seeing is that the angle in the ad (the material, the comfort, how it looks in a real room) moves results more than audience tweaks do, which is where working closely with your creative team pays off.
