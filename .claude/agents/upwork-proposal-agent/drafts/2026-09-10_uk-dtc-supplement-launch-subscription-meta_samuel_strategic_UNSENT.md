What makes or breaks a supplement launch happens before a single ad runs, and it is not the creative. It is which conversion event the account is allowed to optimise toward while it has no data at all.

At zero, Purchase is too thin to hold delivery, so opening on a wide test matrix backfires. Every ad set sits under the volume Meta needs and nothing exits learning. Consolidating hard, usually one Advantage+ shopping campaign with testing pushed down to creative level, concentrates the signal enough to read. Subscription then complicates the scoreboard, because a discounted first order is low by design, so day one ROAS looks like failure while the cohort underneath is fine. So agree the judgment metric before launch: payback period against a 7 and 30 day retention proxy. The 90 day number you want will not exist for 90 days, which is exactly the stretch where launches get killed.

Second thing for UK wellness. Supplements are not a Special Ad Category, so targeting and lookalikes stay intact. What bites is Meta's personal attributes and unproven claims surface, and in our experience that shows up more often as quiet throttling than clean rejection. Worse, because the best angle degrades around week three and reads as creative fatigue. Build a compliant twin of every claim alongside it.

Straight answer on the evidence you asked for. Our book has no supplement brand with published results and no subscription commerce client, so I will not dress one up. What we do have is a peptide and supplement ecommerce brand that signed last month, zero paid history behind five years of organic demand, now moving onto a separate compliant site. Google rather than Meta, still in build, nothing to show you yet.

The quantified work is off vertical. One consumer ecommerce account went from 349 purchases at 45 dollars in December to 665 at 20 dollars in April on roughly 16 percent less spend, by cutting creative rotation back to the few angles earning and consolidating a fragmented structure. Replacement parts, an optimisation arc not a launch, since ended. It does not close the gap you screened for.

Creative is ours, static and video, iterated against the test plan rather than handed over in batches. Klaviyo we run as a service line. Landing pages and CRO we build and test, though on a Shopify subscription build I would want to know who owns the theme before promising speed.

Day to day would be Lindsey. Ten years on Meta, owns our consumer ecommerce accounts, built and sold her own ecommerce business. Strategy and the testing roadmap sit with me.

Worth a short call. Roughly what media budget are you planning for the first 90 days, and what is a subscriber worth across the first year?


Samuel
