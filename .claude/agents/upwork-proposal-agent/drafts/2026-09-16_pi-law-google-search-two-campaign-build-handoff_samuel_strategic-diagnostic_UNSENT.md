## PROPOSAL (paste-ready)

For a personal injury firm, the hard part of an offline conversion import is the phone. A call placed straight from the ad never touches the website, so intake has no click ID to capture. When that caller signs, the case goes back to Google matched on the caller's number and call start time, which only works if the call came through a Google forwarding number. Put the firm's own number in the call asset and those calls can't be imported at all.

Our team ran Google Search and Local Service Ads for an Arizona PI firm. In the attached case study (April to September 2025), Google Ads shows 619 conversions on $56.6K, about $91 each. The case table counts 102 cases against about $97K of total ad spend across channels, roughly $950 per case. As more leads were getting marked spam, we tracked which ones became cases. Calls were the strongest driver, so the call conversion was tightened to calls over three minutes. That account has since ended.

A lot of PI junk can be blocked before launch. Location goes on Presence, not Google's default of Presence or interest, so people searching your city from elsewhere stay out. Exact and phrase only, Search partners and Display off. Day one negatives cover job seekers, law students, accident news like "car accident today" and people looking up their insurer's claims number. Call assets run only while intake answers, and the call conversion length comes from your real intake calls. Ad copy avoids outcome promises, with final bar-rules sign-off left to the firm.

Handing off at launch works, with one flag. The rest of the junk only surfaces in search terms once spend starts, and the import can't be confirmed until the first signed case uploads and matches its call or click. If you'd rather take over an account where both are already checked, our team can stay on through that stretch. A dedicated conversion tracking specialist on our team builds the tracking and import either way.

One question shapes the import: where does a signed case get logged today, and does that record keep the caller's number and call time?
