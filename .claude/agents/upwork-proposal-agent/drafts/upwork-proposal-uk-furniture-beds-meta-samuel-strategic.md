# Upwork Proposal - UK furniture & bed manufacturer, Meta Ads expert
Profile: Samuel Rainey | Style: strategic + diagnostic | Status: UNSENT
Date drafted: 2026-09-04

---

Most Meta accounts selling beds look unprofitable before they actually are. A bed is a two to six week decision, and Meta reports on a seven day click window by default. The purchases the ads genuinely caused land outside that window, in-platform ROAS reads low, the campaigns doing the real work get cut, and the account gets rebuilt around whatever converts same day. That is usually accessories and discounted lines, not beds.

So the first thing I would check is whether reported ROAS and actual revenue move together month to month. If they do not, everything downstream is guesswork.

**The UK angle I would build on**

Your real advantage is the fulfilment, not the pricing. Competitive pricing is a claim every furniture advertiser makes and none of them can prove it in a feed. Next-day delivery is falsifiable, specific, and it removes the exact reason people stall on a bed purchase. If the brands you compete against are quoting multi-week lead times, that gap is the entire ad, not a bullet point in it. Lead with the date the bed arrives.

**Campaigns I would start with**

Two, not six. One broad prospecting campaign optimised to Purchase with the budget concentrated, and one catalog campaign for retargeting and range browsing. Furniture is visual and multi-SKU, so the catalog earns its place here.

The trap is fragmenting to feel thorough. Meta needs roughly 50 purchases per ad set per week to leave the learning phase. Split a starting budget across five and all five sit in learning permanently, which is when a brand concludes Meta does not work for them.

**How I would structure the testing**

At the creative level inside the consolidated campaign, not by launching new audiences. Three or four distinct angles at a time, each a genuinely different argument rather than a recrop of the same hero image: delivery speed, price against a named alternative, the made-by-us story, the room shot. Angles get replaced, not tweaked.

**Audiences**

Broad first. Meta's targeting has beaten manual interest stacking in wide categories for a while, and furniture buyers do not cluster into tidy interests. From there: catalog retargeting on viewers and cart abandoners, movers and new-home behaviours, and lookalikes seeded from delivered purchasers rather than all purchasers.

**Creative**

Room-context stills over white-background product shots, with price and delivery date on the image itself, since most of the audience never reads the copy. In-home customer footage mid-funnel, carousel for range. Static beats video at lower spend: ten concepts cost less than one shoot.

**Metrics, in the order I would act on them**

CPM, hook rate, outbound CTR, landing page view to add-to-cart, add-to-cart to purchase. Those five tell you where the leak is. Above them sits the only number that decides anything: cost per delivered order against contribution margin, read blended rather than in-platform.

This is where your delivered-order ratio stops being an ops metric. Meta's Purchase event fires at checkout, not at delivery. If a meaningful share of orders cancel or get refused, the optimiser is being taught to find people who check out and do not receive, and it will scale toward exactly that cohort. Feeding delivered orders back as a conversion signal fixes the incentive. Worth saying plainly: I would be building that for you, not pointing at one I already have.

**Scaling or stopping**

Kill an ad once it has spent roughly three times your target cost per order with no purchase. Scale by 20 to 30 percent every three or four days, since larger jumps re-enter learning and cost you the week. Judge on cohorts, not days. Daily ROAS on a considered purchase is mostly noise.

**Where our results actually are**

Our team ran Meta for a luxury furniture DTC brand: about $105K in spend, 343 purchases, roughly $306 per purchase, scaling from around $25K a month to $40K. The more useful number: CPM went from about $42 at $25K a month to about $79 at $40K. Scaling a considered-purchase furniture brand on Meta is a fight against your own rising CPM, and a plan that does not account for that is not a plan.

We also currently run Meta for a premium mattress brand, so the bed decision cycle is familiar.

The honest gap: that work is US. Our furniture and bed experience is real, our UK experience is not, and I would rather you hear it from me now than find out in month two. The mechanics travel; the seasonal and competitive read would take a cycle to earn.

Two things before committing to any of this. What is Meta spending now, and roughly what range are you working within? And what is the average order value on beds? Those decide whether this is a wide test or a narrow one.

One note: we work on a percentage of ad spend rather than hourly, which tends to suit a scaling brief better than a fixed hours arrangement.

Worth a short call to look at the account as it stands.
