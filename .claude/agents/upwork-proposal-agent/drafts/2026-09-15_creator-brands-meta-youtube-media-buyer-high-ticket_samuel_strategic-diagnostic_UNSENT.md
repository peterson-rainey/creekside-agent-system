## PROPOSAL + SCREENING ANSWERS COMBINED (paste-ready, one cover letter)

Just under $100K a month on one Meta account our team runs (medical aesthetics lead gen, about $1.19M over the last 12 months), though I can't give you a verified return from it. The cleanest return I can show is ecom, not info: a replacement parts brand that returned about 5.5x on a little over $120,000 in spend. We also run Meta for a coaching certification sold through a weekly webinar.

On creator brands, the leak often sits between the creator's warm audience and cold traffic. Warm opt-ins book and show well, so blended cost per call looks healthy until budget pushes into cold audiences, where opt-ins stay cheap and show rate slips. If opt-ins are cheap but calls aren't getting booked, I first check whether the booking step works at all: for the same days, opt-ins, calendar page views and booked calls in the CRM, plus how fast each opt-in gets a follow-up. Then what Meta is optimizing on, since optimizing on the opt-in finds more cheap opt-ins, not always people who book. Then placements, and whether the ad promises something the call doesn't. Booked calls and shows go back to Meta, for measurement first at low volume, then optimization once there's enough of it.

I don't have a cost per booked call I'd stand behind. The closest is a dental implant group that ran Meta lead forms into a call center, about $25 per lead across 2,558 leads in 90 days, but I don't have the booked consult number from that account.

On finance and investing offers, income and return claims in the ad or on the page are the usual trigger, and Meta can re-review ads after they're live. An investing offer can also fall under Meta's financial products special ad category, which rules out lookalikes. I don't have a Meta restriction case I can document for you. The closest is a Google account where ads were flagged under a health policy, and after we took it to Google's support team, they reviewed and approved the ads. On Meta, the first check is where a restriction sits. It can hit the ad account, Page, business portfolio or a personal profile, and a restricted profile can disable ad accounts where it's the only user, so every asset needs a second admin.

The failed one was a YouTube test: two video views campaigns, audience vs keyword targeting, and no leads from either. The YouTube spend that has turned into leads for us ran on a conversion goal, and YouTube is still the thinner half of our record.

I should be clear that I'm not a solo buyer, or a 40-hour hire. I'm in Nashville, Tennessee, on Central time. A dedicated Meta buyer on our team runs the accounts day to day during US hours, our tracking specialist owns pixel, CAPI and UTMs, and I stay on strategy. We work on a percentage of spend rather than base pay, so weigh that before a call.

Do booked calls and shows make it back to Meta today, or stop in your CRM?
