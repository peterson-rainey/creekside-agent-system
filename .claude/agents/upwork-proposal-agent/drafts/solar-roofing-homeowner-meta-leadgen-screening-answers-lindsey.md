## 1. Describe your recent experience with similar projects

Most of my recent work is local lead generation on Meta, which is the same shape as what you're describing even when the vertical differs. The account I've spent the most time in over the past twelve months is an aesthetics and dental practice running just over $1.19 million in Meta spend in that window, still live today. Geography-bound service business, leads come in by form and call, a front desk closes them. The constraint there is identical to yours: the sales team feels lead quality long before the ad account shows it.

On home improvement specifically, I've run exterior home services on Meta, though at a much smaller budget than the account above, so I'd rather be straight about the scale than dress it up.

I have not run solar. If that's a hard requirement, you should weigh it.

Before agency work I built and sold my own DTC brand, which is where I learned to look at offer and margin first and targeting second.

## 2. How do you use metrics to inform your strategy?

Different metrics matter at different stages, and reading the wrong one early is how good campaigns get killed.

In the first two weeks there usually isn't enough conversion volume to judge anything honestly, so I read hook rate, CTR, and cost per landing page view. Those tell me whether the problem is the creative or the offer behind it, which are two different fixes that most people treat the same way.

Once there's volume, CPL becomes useful, but only segmented. Account-level numbers hide everything. The decisions get made at the angle and creative level, because it's almost always two or three ads carrying the account and a long tail quietly spending.

Frequency and CPM tell me when fatigue has set in, which is a signal to change the creative rather than the targeting.

The number I actually steer toward is cost per closed job, once your CRM data can flow back into Meta. Meta optimizes for whatever you feed it. Feed it form fills and it finds people who fill out forms.
