## THE DRAFT (343 words / 1,912 chars - inside her 350 multi-question ceiling and Upwork's 5,000)

Do you know what a fitting customer is worth to you over the twelve months after they buy? On a small budget that number matters more than any targeting or creative decision, because it tells you whether an acquisition cost is a win or the reason you switch everything off in week three.

The reason I ask is that early accounts rarely have enough purchase volume for Meta to learn on purchases, and the usual response is to optimise for something shallower and hope it correlates. I ran Meta for a US replacement-parts retailer for ten months, about $131,000 of spend across roughly 4,500 orders at around 5.5x blended. That account had four campaigns pointed at shallow events, website visits, add to cart, time on site and view content, and every one of them reported a better return than the broad purchase campaign. None of them ever carried real budget. The flattering numbers lived where the money wasn't, which is the trap a new account walks into when it judges an event by the return it reports rather than the volume it can produce.

So the first thing here would not be creative. It would be settling what a fitting is worth, picking the one event that fires often enough at your budget to be learnable, and getting that event back into Meta cleanly before any scaling decision rests on it. Your own golf content is the raw material for the testing after that, and I would rather cut angles out of footage you are already making than commission new ones.

Two things worth saying plainly. Our book has no golf account and no UK account, so I would be bringing method rather than a golf case study. And the closest thing I have to an early-stage start is taking a meal prep brand from zero into three new metros, which is the same cold-start problem at a different stage.

Attached a few relevant results below, and there is a short video on my profile covering how I work through accounts like this.
