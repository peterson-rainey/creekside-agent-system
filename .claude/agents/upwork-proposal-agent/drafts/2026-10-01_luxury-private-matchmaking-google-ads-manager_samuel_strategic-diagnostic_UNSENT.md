## PROPOSAL

The cheapest matchmaking leads on Google are usually the wrong ones, because gamers, free-app shoppers and aspiring matchmakers all type the same word your buyers do. Winning here usually means paying more per lead and less per membership.

"Matchmaking" is what gamers search when a lobby won't fill, it's in the title of several reality shows, and it's how people look for dating apps and matchmaker jobs. With loose match types and a thin negative list, a real share of the budget can go to people who were never going to pay for a private search. That tends to show up as a decent cost per lead and a sales team saying the leads are bad.

**Where we'd look first**

1. **Search terms, split brand from non-brand.** The search terms report shows most of what you actually paid for. Referred buyers also search your name, so blended cost per lead can look healthy while non-brand, where new members come from, wastes budget.

2. **What Google is optimizing toward.** If the form submit is the primary conversion, bidding treats a casual browser the same as a serious prospect. Qualified consultations sent back as the primary conversion, with the form demoted to secondary, let bidding chase the right person. If consultations are too few to learn from, start with the first qualified call and step up. Offline conversions have to be uploaded within 90 days of the click (63 for enhanced conversions for leads), so a membership that closes later gets judged in your CRM.

3. **How hard the landing page filters.** A few qualifying questions, or a stated starting investment on the page, usually raise cost per lead and cut the calls your team wastes. That tradeoff is worth making on purpose.

**How the work runs**

Our team manages the account day to day, and our conversion tracking specialist owns the tracking setup. You get a written update every two weeks plus a live dashboard, with cost per qualified lead next to cost per lead.

We haven't run a matchmaking account. The nearest parallel is a personal injury firm whose Google Ads were judged on cost per qualified case rather than cost per lead.

Is the account spending closer to $5K, $15K or $30K a month, and at what point does your team know a lead is a real fit: the form, the first call, or the consultation?
