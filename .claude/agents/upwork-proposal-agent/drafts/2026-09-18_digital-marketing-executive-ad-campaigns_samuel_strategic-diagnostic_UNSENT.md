## PROPOSAL

Reach and engagement are the two numbers an ad platform finds easiest to deliver. They're also the two that can climb while sales stay flat.

If you run a traffic or engagement objective, Meta and Google will find the cheapest clicks and likes they can. Those tend to come from people least likely to buy. The dashboard looks healthy, cost per engagement drops every month, and it's hard to tell what the spend actually earned.

So before launching or changing anything, I'd look at three things:

1. **What the account is optimizing for.** Every campaign optimizes toward one conversion event: a purchase, a form submit, a booked call, or just a click. If that event isn't the thing that makes you money, the "data-driven improvements" will improve the wrong number.

2. **Whether that event is being recorded correctly.** Duplicate firing, a pixel on the wrong page, or leads counted twice will quietly send the algorithm after bad traffic. That's usually faster to fix than any creative or targeting change, and everything after it depends on the fix.

3. **Where the money is going compared to where the results come from.** Most accounts have a small group of campaigns, audiences or search terms that produce most of the outcomes. Budget tends to spread evenly across everything else. Moving it is usually the first real improvement.

After that, the ongoing work is what you'd expect: a structured test plan for creative and audiences, budget moves backed by conversion data rather than engagement, and regular updates that show what changed, why, and what it did to cost per result.

**How we work:** our team manages Google Ads and Meta Ads accounts for service businesses, ecommerce brands and B2B companies. Our account managers run the day-to-day work independently, and a dedicated tracking specialist handles pixel, conversion and analytics setup. You won't have to manage the person managing your ads.

**On communication:** you'll get a short written update on a set schedule. It covers what we changed, what we're testing, and what the numbers did since the last one. When something needs a decision from you, it goes at the top, not buried in a report.

To point you in the right direction, one question:

Which platforms are you running ads on right now, and what should a campaign produce for your business: sales, leads, booked calls, or something else?
