# High-ticket furniture Shopify, Meta Ads Specialist, Lindsey (lindsey_default), POOLED 'our team', UNSENT, 2026-09-19

When you check ROAS in Ads Manager, do you know how many of your orders come in more than a week after the first ad click? At $500 to $3,000, most buyers save the piece, measure the room, maybe order a swatch, and come back later. Meta's click window tops out at 7 days, so the prospecting that starts those purchases can look like a loser while retargeting takes the credit. Cut the wrong campaign and acquisition cost goes up, not down.

I ask because I ran Meta for a luxury furniture brand earlier this year. The account spent about $105,000 over four months, scaling from roughly $25K to $40K a month, and the sales campaigns brought in 334 conversions at about $274 each. Two things stood out. First, CPMs nearly doubled as spend went up, from around $42 to $79, so every budget step needed fresh creative behind it or efficiency slipped. Second, about a third of those conversions came from sale events. That is great for a month's revenue, but it says little about what a cold customer will pay full price for. I'd split those two numbers apart before deciding what to scale.

Before this I built and sold my own e-commerce business. Today I work inside a small team whose accounts sit in one shared book, over $20M in ad spend managed between us, including a premium mattress brand where orders average around $1,300. So high-ticket buyers with long consideration cycles are familiar ground across the whole team, not just in my own accounts. The pattern we keep seeing is that the angle in the ad (the material, the comfort, how it looks in a real room) moves results more than audience tweaks do, which is why working closely with your creative team matters.
