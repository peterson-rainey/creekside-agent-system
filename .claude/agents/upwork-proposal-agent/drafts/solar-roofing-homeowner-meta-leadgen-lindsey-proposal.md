Do you know what your cost per closed job is, and not just your cost per lead? That gap is usually where homeowner lead gen quietly falls apart, because the two numbers move in opposite directions more often than people expect.

The reason I ask is that in home services accounts, the fastest way to cut CPL is to make the form easier to fill out. Instant forms, three fields, no friction. Volume goes up, cost per lead drops, the dashboard looks great, and then the sales team tells you half of those homeowners do not remember filling anything out. I have watched that exact pattern play out on trades accounts more than once. The accounts that hold up are the ones where closed jobs get fed back into Meta, so it optimizes toward people who actually book instead of people who tap.

That is also how I would answer your static versus video question, and it is less about format than about awareness. Video earns its keep cold, where you have to make someone notice a problem they were not thinking about that morning, roof age or a bill that keeps climbing. Static and copy do the heavier lifting warm and in retargeting, where the job is handling objections, price anxiety, and proof.

One honest tradeoff on the research side: research narrows what you test, it does not remove the need to test. Anyone promising a first build that skips learning is selling you certainty that Meta does not give.

Ten years in and a DTC brand I built and sold myself, and the thread through the accounts that work is always the feedback loop, not the launch.

Happy to walk through the home services numbers with you.
