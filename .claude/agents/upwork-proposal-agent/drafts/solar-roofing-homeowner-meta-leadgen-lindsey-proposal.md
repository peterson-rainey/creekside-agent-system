Do you know what your cost per closed job is, and not just your cost per lead? That gap is usually where homeowner lead gen quietly falls apart, because the two numbers move in opposite directions more often than people expect.

The reason I ask is that in home services accounts, the fastest way to cut CPL is to make the form easier to fill out. Instant forms, three fields, no friction. Volume goes up, cost per lead drops, the dashboard looks great, and then the sales team tells you half of those homeowners do not remember filling anything out. I watched that play out on an exterior home services account on Meta. The accounts that hold up are the ones where closed jobs get fed back into Meta, so it optimizes toward people who actually book instead of people who tap.

That is also how I would answer your static versus video question, and it is less about format than about awareness. Video earns its keep cold, where you have to make someone notice a problem they were not thinking about that morning, roof age or a bill that keeps climbing. Static and copy do the heavier lifting warm and in retargeting, where the job is objections, price anxiety, and proof.

On research, I start in the ad library with the ads your competitors have kept running the longest, since longevity is the only public signal of what is actually converting for them. Then I map their offers and guarantees against yours and build the first test around angle rather than audience, because Meta's delivery does most of the audience work now. Worth saying plainly though: research narrows what you test, it does not remove the need to test.

I have not run solar. Closest is exterior home services on Meta, alongside 10+ years in local lead gen and a DTC brand I built and sold myself.

Happy to walk through the home services numbers with you.
