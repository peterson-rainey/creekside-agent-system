## PROPOSAL (paste-ready)

Have you counted how many edits a campaign takes in an average week? Asking because on a brief built around
monitoring and adjusting, that number tends to predict performance better than anything sitting on the dashboard.

Every meaningful change puts an ad set back into learning. A weekly reporting rhythm quietly invites a weekly
change, so the account spends most of its life re-learning and never sits still long enough to give a clean read
on anything. The test that would have told you something useful gets cut on day four for looking soft, and the
account never moves past what it is already doing. Separating how often you report from how often you touch the
account is usually the first fix, and it costs nothing.

Ten years in, and I built and sold my own e-commerce brand before that, which is where I learned to sit through a
number I did not like. On the lead gen side I ran cold Meta lead forms into a call team for a dental implant
practice, 2,558 leads at $25.31 each over about three months. On the e-commerce side a spa parts store held around
5.5x blended across its last nine months with me. Budget range is the part that surprises people: single Meta
accounts on my desk run from roughly $3,000 a month up to just under $100,000, and one of those has held near six
figures a month for twelve months straight. Structure that works at one end breaks quietly at the other.

Two things worth settling before anyone hands you a plan: roughly what monthly range you are working with, and
which countries you are selling into.

There is a short video on my profile that covers how I work an account like this.
