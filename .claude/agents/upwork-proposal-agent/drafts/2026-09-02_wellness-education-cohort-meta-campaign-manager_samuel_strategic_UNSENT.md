## PROPOSAL (paste-ready)

Cohort based offers break Meta's optimization in a way evergreen offers do not, and the 25 percent figure is where it shows up. If ads only need to cover a quarter of each cohort, a campaign optimizing on enrollment might produce ten or fifteen events across an entire launch window. Meta wants roughly fifty per ad set per week before delivery stabilizes, so an enrollment optimized ad set sits in learning for the whole launch, and then the launch is over. Add five creative variations splitting that same volume and nothing separates.

The fix is to optimize on the densest reliable event upstream, usually registration or application, and let enrollment flow through the Conversions API as the measurement and value signal rather than the optimization target. Since the CAPI is already built, that is a structural change and not a rebuild.

The second one argues against how the workload is described, so worth flagging now. Each cohort launch cold starts if nothing has been running between launches. Audiences decay, recent signal thins, and the first week of every launch pays for it. Keeping one low budget prospecting campaign warm through the quiet months costs very little and means each launch opens against a warm pool instead of from scratch. Maintenance months carry more weight than they look like they do.

On testing, at that volume five variations running against each other in parallel will not separate cleanly. Better to test at the angle level, one distinct promise per ad set, and rotate creative inside whichever angle holds. Angle differences show up at a fraction of the sample size creative differences need.

Straight with you on the setup. This is a small team rather than a single freelancer, and the day to day would sit with one media buyer rather than getting passed around. Our team currently runs Meta for a children's life coaching certification, a cohort program sold off a recurring weekly webinar, where campaign activation and deactivation are scheduled around each webinar date. That is the closest structure to yours that we run.

**Q2. A campaign managed end to end, the offer, and what was tested or adjusted.**

The certification above. The offer is a paid certification that trains adults to coach children, and the entry point is a free weekly webinar rather than a direct sale, because the program is too considered a purchase to close on a cold click.

The structural decision was to split prospecting by narrative angle and give each angle its own campaign rather than stacking them as ad sets inside one. With this little conversion volume, a shared budget collapses onto whichever angle happens to spend fastest in the first days, and the other angles never get a fair read. Separate campaigns cost some efficiency and buy back the ability to actually learn something.

Angles tested against each other: a start a business with purpose framing, an educator framing aimed at teachers looking beyond the classroom, and a proof led framing. Adjusted over time by pausing angles as they fatigued and rotating fresh creative inside the ones still holding. The webinar registration campaign runs continuously underneath all of it so the account is never cold at the start of a cycle.

The honest tradeoff from that account: an evergreen webinar offer fatigues on a fairly predictable cycle, and it is the angle library that keeps it alive. New creative inside a tired angle does not do it.

**Q3. Ongoing optimization process.**

Daily, a short pass. Spend pacing, anything with zero or collapsed delivery, learning phase status per ad set, and Events Manager for event match quality and browser to server deduplication.

Weekly, a longer one. Frequency and CPM read together as the fatigue signal, performance judged at the angle level rather than the individual ad, and budget moved toward whatever is holding.

When something underperforms, the first job is deciding which of three things it is, because the fix is different for each and guessing wastes the budget. Delivery, meaning frequency climbing, audience saturating, or an ad set thrown back into learning by an edit. Tracking, meaning match quality dropped, events deduplicating wrong, or a parameter quietly stopped arriving on the server event. Creative, meaning click through and hook rate falling while CPM sits flat. Budget changes come after that call, not before it.

One specific thing worth checking on any Conversions API setup: fbc and fbp frequently get dropped on server events even when the browser side looks clean, so match quality reads healthy on PageView and poor on the event you actually optimize toward.

**Q4. Hours to build a campaign with five variations, and rate.**

Five to seven hours for the build. That covers campaign and ad set structure, audience build, verifying the event and CAPI payload actually fire the way the structure assumes before anything spends, uploading and checking five variants across placements, naming and tracking parameters, and a pre launch pass. Then one to two hours across the first seventy two hours after launch, which is when delivery and tracking problems surface if they are going to.

Maintenance only months run lighter, roughly two to three hours.
