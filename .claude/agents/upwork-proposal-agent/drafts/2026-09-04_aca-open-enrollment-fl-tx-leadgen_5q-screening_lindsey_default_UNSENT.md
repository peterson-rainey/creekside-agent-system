**1. Describe one U.S.-based lead-generation campaign you personally managed.**

A multi-location dental implant group in California. I ran Meta; our team ran their search. Leads came in
through Meta lead forms and went straight to a call center that qualified and booked them, so the handoff
looked a lot like yours does with your assistant.

From April 21 to July 15 this year the account spent $64,746.78 and produced 2,558 leads at $25.31 blended,
across eleven campaigns. Two of those were Spanish and nine were English, built separately rather than
translated, and the Spanish campaigns came in at $17.93 against $26.38 for English.

The most useful thing I took from it is not the cost per lead. Partway through, spend got pulled back, and the
reporting made it look like an ad problem. It was not. The call center could not work the volume, and contact
rate and set rate were not being reported next to cost per lead, so nobody could see that for weeks. That is
why I now insist lead-quality feedback is a tracked field from week one instead of a monthly conversation.
The engagement ended in July when they brought marketing in house.

**2. Have you worked with ACA, health insurance, Medicare, healthcare, or another regulated U.S. industry?**

Healthcare and legal, yes. Insurance, no.

Healthcare is most of my regulated work: the dental implant group above, a cosmetic dentistry practice where
cost per consultation went from $48.79 to $9.58, a naturopathic practice running $14 to $40 per patient
conversion, and a GLP-1 telehealth account, which is the closest thing in our book to a genuinely restricted
health category. On the legal side, a bankruptcy firm in Orange County where we doubled leads from 117 to 229
and cut cost per acquisition from $86 to $50.29.

What I have not done is ACA, health insurance or Medicare, and I hold no health certification on either
platform. I know the terrain that matters here exists, CMS marketing rules for agents and brokers, required
disclaimers on lead ads, how carefully subsidy language has to be phrased, and Meta's limits on targeting a
health audience. I have not personally carried an account through an ACA compliance review. If that is a hard
requirement rather than a preference, you should weigh it seriously against the rest of this.

**3. What is your experience managing both Meta Ads and Google Ads for lead generation?**

Meta is mine directly, ten-plus years of it. Google Ads sits with our search team, and you would be hiring a
paid acquisition function that covers both, with me owning Meta and the overall strategy. I would rather say
that plainly than present myself as a single generalist doing both hands-on.

Where that combination has mattered most is that the two platforms do different jobs and get judged wrongly
when you treat them as interchangeable. Search captures people already looking and tends to produce fewer,
more expensive, more ready leads. Meta creates the consideration and reaches people who are eligible but not
actively shopping, which for your Hispanic and Brazilian audience is the whole opportunity, because community
presence is an audience and creative advantage and there is no way to express it on a search results page.

For lead gen specifically, the accounts that worked best are the ones where both ran together and we let the
downstream qualification data decide the split, rather than fixing a percentage at the start.

**4. What cost per lead, and if available cost per qualified lead or customer, did you achieve?**

Cost per lead I can give you precisely. On the dental account, $25.31 blended across 2,558 leads, $26.38
English and $17.93 Spanish. On the bankruptcy firm, $50.29 per lead on search, down from $86.

Cost per qualified lead I cannot give you honestly, and I would rather flag that than hand you a number I
cannot stand behind. In nearly all of these engagements, qualification happened inside the client's own CRM
and that outcome never came back to the ad platform, so what we captured reliably was cost per lead.

That gap is the specific reason your setup interests me. You already have someone qualifying, tracking and
giving quality feedback. If that status gets written back as an offline conversion, cost per qualified lead
stops being something we reconstruct by hand at month end and becomes the number the campaigns actually
optimize toward. I would want that wired in the first few weeks, not at the end of the engagement.

**5. What would you charge for initial setup and ongoing monthly management?**

Setup is a one-time onboarding fee of $1,500 per platform, so $3,000 for Meta and Google together. That covers
account structure, tracking and conversion setup, audience and keyword research, and the campaign build.

Ongoing management is a percentage of ad spend rather than an hourly rate: 20% per platform, with a $1,500 per
platform monthly minimum. At the budget I would recommend for an initial Florida test, roughly $10,000 a month
split $6,000 Meta and $4,000 Google, that comes to $3,000 a month. The percentage steps down in tiers as spend
grows, so the fee does not scale linearly with your budget.

Ad spend stays paid by you directly to Meta and Google, separate from the management fee, as you specified.
