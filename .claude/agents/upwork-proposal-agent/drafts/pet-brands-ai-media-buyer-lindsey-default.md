AI MEDIA BUYER

Do you know what a subscriber costs you on the vet app, separately from what an install costs? Those two numbers pull in opposite directions more often than people expect, and which one you optimize toward decides how the entire account gets built.

The reason I ask is a restaurant rewards app I ran on Meta. 2,662 installs, cost per install down from $7.36 to $3.90, on a budget of $2,000 to $3,500 a month. Eight creatives through six rounds of testing, winners split by market and by gender. The biggest single move was killing retargeting, which was buying installs at $10.22 while cold traffic sat under $4. That account was judged on installs, so installs were the honest target, and I would not quote you a return figure off it. If your app monetizes on subscriptions, install cost becomes the vanity layer and the event you feed the algorithm has to sit deeper than the download.

Which runs into the arithmetic I would want settled before anything launches. An ad set needs roughly 50 optimization events a week to leave learning. Three brands across seven platforms is twenty one funded lanes. At most realistic budgets almost none of them clear that bar, so you end up with three brands worth of data that reads like signal and is not. I would rather run two platforms properly per brand and earn the rest.

Straight on your first question so you can filter fast. Meta is my platform. I have run app promotion, ecommerce, beauty and food accounts on it for 10+ years, and I built and sold my own ecommerce business before I did this for anyone else. The book I work out of runs $185K to $266K a month on Meta across 21 to 28 accounts, and runs Google Ads as well. Apple Search Ads, Play Store campaigns, TikTok, LinkedIn and Reddit are not on my record. Better you know that now than in week three.

The AI section is the part I would enjoy, and it is not prompts typed into a chat window. We run 109 purpose built agents against our own database, 54 of them on schedules, including a morning monitor that checks pacing, frequency and pixel health across every account before I open Ads Manager, plus anomaly alerts and audit agents that write structured daily summaries.

First 30 days, same order for each brand, staggered so they are not competing for attention: verify tracking and the event ladder end to end, rebuild account structure, stand up a fixed testing lane per brand with its own budget so tests are never starved by the scaling lane, then scale only what clears its own cost per subscriber.

Fee is a percentage of ad spend, never hourly. 20% up to $30K a month, 15% from $30K to $60K, 10% above that, $1,500 per platform minimum, capped at $15K. Onboarding is $1,500 per platform and covers the audit. Management runs on a 90 day minimum, because a fresh pixel read at day 30 is being read mid learning phase. Availability: [FILL: hours/week].

Roughly what are the three brands spending a month combined, and how is that split today? That decides the whole sequence above.

Rather than send redacted screenshots, I would put live Ads Manager on a screen share and let you drive any date range you want. There is a short video on my profile that covers how I separate testing from scaling.
