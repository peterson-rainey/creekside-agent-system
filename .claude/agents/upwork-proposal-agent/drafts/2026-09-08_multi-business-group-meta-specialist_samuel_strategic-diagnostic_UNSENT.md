## Draft (1,833 characters mechanically counted, under the 5,000 limit)

Running several businesses on Meta usually creates one specific blind spot, and it is not targeting. It is that each account gets judged on its own numbers, so the cheap-looking account keeps its budget.

A client of ours runs two Meta accounts under one owner, both on lead objectives. Since June, one spent $32,610 at CPMs between $132 and $220. The other spent $24,072 at CPMs around $30. On paper the second account is the efficient one. It produced 13 leads. The first produced 178. That is roughly $183 a lead against roughly $1,850, and about 40% of the combined budget was sitting in the account that looked cheaper.

That is the first thing I would want to check across your group. Not because your accounts are broken, but because improving efficiency and improving results pull in opposite directions when budget is allocated per business instead of to whichever account is actually converting that month.

Worth flagging on timing. Our Meta book ran a $24.45 CPM last October and $33.78 in November on flat spend. This year has been higher all summer, $46.90 in July and $37.99 in August. If your Q4 plan is built off last year's costs, the budget runs short before December.

Over the last twelve months our team has run 34 Meta accounts and about $2.6M in spend, including two clients who each run multiple accounts under one owner. Lindsey handles Meta here and would be the one in your accounts.

Two things worth knowing before I say anything useful about approach. Roughly what is the combined monthly spend across the group, and are these businesses selling to overlapping audiences or genuinely separate ones? Overlap changes how the accounts should be structured, and it is the difference between consolidating them and deliberately keeping them apart.

Happy to jump on a quick call if that is easier.
