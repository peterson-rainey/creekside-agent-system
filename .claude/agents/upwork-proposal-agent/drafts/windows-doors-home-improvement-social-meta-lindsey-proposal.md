Before anything else, two gaps you should have up front. I have not run a windows, doors or remodeling account, and I am a paid media buyer rather than a daily content producer. If the video and post every day is the center of this role, I am not your person.

Where I would be useful is the Meta Ads half, and there the first number I would want is your cost per booked in home estimate, not your cost per lead.

That distinction is most of the game in homeowner lead gen. Instant forms with three fields will drive cost per lead down on command. Volume rises, the dashboard looks healthy, and then the sales team reports that half of these homeowners do not remember filling anything out and will not pick up the phone. Windows and doors is a high ticket purchase that happens in the home and usually needs both spouses to agree, so the drop off between a form fill and a set appointment is steep.

The accounts that hold up are the ones where the set appointment gets fed back into Meta, so delivery optimizes toward people who actually book.

The closest thing in my book is reverse mortgage lead gen on Meta. Same shape: homeowners, long consideration, a sit down conversation before anything closes. April on that account ran 8,988.28 in spend at 109 conversions, an 82.46 cost per conversion. May ran 8,306.66 at 140 conversions, 59.33.

Two things I would need to size this properly. What you are spending on Meta monthly now, and whether that figure is closer to your floor or your ceiling.
