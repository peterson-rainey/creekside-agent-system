## PROPOSAL (paste-ready)

The first thing to settle for commercial lighting ads is what counts as a lead, because Google and Meta will both go looking for more of whatever you pick. An architect, an electrical contractor and a homeowner redoing a kitchen all fill in the same contact form, and the order behind the good inquiries often lands months later, where neither platform can see it.

Our team runs Google Ads for a paving contractor, and in one market it ran Search and Performance Max side by side for the same four months on almost identical spend. Performance Max clicks cost $3.00 against $14.53 on Search, and its leads cost $268 against $131. Cheap clicks and cheap leads turned out to be different things.

The build starts on Search, on exact and phrase match around fixture types and commercial project terms, with residential and retail searches blocked from day one. The form asks for role and project type, and only a trade answer counts as a conversion. When your team marks an inquiry as a real project, meaning a named job with a timeline, not the eventual order, it goes back to Google and Meta as the conversion that matters. A dedicated conversion tracking specialist on our team builds that.

Meta can learn from that signal once enough of it builds up. Until then the creative does most of the filtering: commercial installs, not styled rooms, give a homeowner little reason to stop.

SEO isn't something we sell, and none of our case studies is close enough to B2B lighting to attach. Paid search does show your SEO which commercial lighting terms bring in real projects, and pages for those terms are the ones worth building first.

With an end date on this, I'd call it done when the tracking is proven end to end: trade answers counting, and the first projects your team marks showing up in Google against the clicks that produced them. Bidding toward projects on either platform comes later, once there's a steady flow of them.

All of this depends on someone marking the real projects, so the first thing I'd want to know: when an inquiry comes in today, does it stay with your own sales team or go out to a rep or distributor?
