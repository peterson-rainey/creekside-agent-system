## PROPOSAL

For B2B SaaS lead generation on Google and Bing, the most useful thing ad creative can do is turn the wrong people away before they click. A headline that says who the product is built for, or what it costs, gives up some clicks. On software searches those are mostly freelancers comparing tools, people hunting for a free version, and companies too small to ever sign, each one a lead your sales team has to sort through.

Most B2B software keywords also don't have the volume to settle a test on a small wording change, so the tests worth running are big swings, like demo versus free trial, pricing shown or hidden, or problem-led versus outcome-led. Winners get picked on qualified leads, not click-through rate. The leads your sales team marks as qualified get sent back to both platforms, set up by our dedicated conversion tracking specialist. Reporting runs on the same measure, cost per qualified lead by campaign and platform.

Google usually carries the volume, so tests get decided there, and Microsoft picks up the winners through its Google import instead of splitting a thin test in two. Microsoft also has a B2B lever Google doesn't, bidding up searchers whose LinkedIn profile matches the companies, industries or job functions you sell to.

Image ads come from our creative director, and on B2B they usually earn their keep retargeting people who've seen your pricing or demo pages rather than cold prospecting.

The closest example is a B2B SaaS company selling referral software to home service contractors. In the six months after their seed round, inbound leads and ARR both doubled, and our part was Meta building awareness for a category contractors didn't know existed and Google capturing the search demand that followed.
