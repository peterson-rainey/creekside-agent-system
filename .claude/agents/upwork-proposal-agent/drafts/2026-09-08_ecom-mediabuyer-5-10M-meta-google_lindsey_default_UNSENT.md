ADS

Have you mapped where CPM starts climbing faster than volume in your best accounts? That inflection, not the ROAS number on the dashboard, is what tells you whether the next chunk of budget is actually buyable or whether you are just paying more to reach the same people.

I ask because scaling ecommerce on Meta almost always breaks at that point, and it usually gets blamed on bidding when it is really a creative and audience problem. I ran a luxury furniture DTC brand straight through that stretch. Monthly spend went from about $12K to just over $40K across four months. CPM more than doubled, from roughly $35 to $79, but purchase volume roughly tripled, from about 37 a month to 129, and cost per purchase held around $310 the whole way. That held because creative refreshed fast enough to keep the audience from fatiguing while budget grew, not because of anything clever on bids.

Ten plus years on paid social, almost all of it ecommerce, which is where I learned to distrust front-end numbers. On a spa and pool parts retailer we drove over 4,300 purchases at about $29 each on high repeat, low consideration buying. On a consumer camera brand, retargeting looked cheaper at $71 a purchase against $102 on cold, and the real risk there was over-funding retargeting until it started eating an audience that prospecting had not refilled yet.

Since you called out angles and creative strategists, that is usually where the leverage is. The winners rarely come from more targeting levers. They come from testing enough distinct concepts to find the two or three that carry cold traffic, then pouring budget into those. Meta is the platform I personally live in. The bit of Google sits with the team I work alongside.

What is the current monthly spend per brand, and where does CPA land against your target right now?
