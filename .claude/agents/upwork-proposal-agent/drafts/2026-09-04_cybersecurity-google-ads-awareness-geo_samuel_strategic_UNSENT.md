## PROPOSAL (paste-ready, everything between the markers)

<<<BEGIN PASTE>>>

Awareness campaigns in cybersecurity fail in a specific way, and it is worth naming before the target area gets locked. Reach and engagement are the two cheapest things Google will sell you, and in our own upper funnel book they turned out to be poor predictors of anything. Over the last twelve months, across eleven Demand Gen campaigns and roughly $45,000, CPM ranged from $4.06 to $233.98. The two biggest campaigns landed within a few hundred dollars of each other on spend, about $12,200 and $12,900. The cheaper one bought 977,000 impressions at a $12.51 CPM and returned 60 conversions. The dearer one bought 309,000 impressions at $41.82 and returned 152. A third held a $233.98 CPM for 235 days and returned zero. Cheap reach did not predict the outcome in either direction, which is the argument for not letting reach be the number this campaign gets graded on.

The geography is where I would start once the area arrives, and not with the keywords. Google's location settings include a mode that serves people who have merely shown interest in a target area rather than only people inside it. On upper funnel surfaces that is where a geo fenced budget quietly leaks, and it stays invisible unless reporting is split by matched location against location of interest.

Policy is the other gate. Security language collides with Google's unauthorized access rules more often than people expect, and anything reading as third party tech support sits behind a certification gate rather than a normal review. Those disapprovals are rarely won on appeal. They get cleared by the certification and by rewriting the landing page, which makes it a timeline item rather than a footnote.

Google's bidding also relearns every time budget stops and restarts, so staged releases buy control and give back efficiency. That is a fine trade made on purpose and an expensive surprise discovered in month two.

Straight answer on fit: there is no cybersecurity account in our book. The closest is B2B software, where the split was Meta for awareness and Google for capture, and a land client where one offer ran across separate county level campaigns and cost per lead spread far enough apart that geography was doing more work than the creative.

Is monthly spend closer to $5,000 or to $50,000, and what has to happen behind the awareness for this to have been worth doing?


Samuel
