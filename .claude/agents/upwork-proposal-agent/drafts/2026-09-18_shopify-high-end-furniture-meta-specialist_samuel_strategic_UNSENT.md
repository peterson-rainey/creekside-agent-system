At $500 to $3,000+ per order, the purchase event on your Meta campaigns probably doesn't fire often enough for every ad set to get out of learning. Meta wants roughly 50 results a week per ad set, and a high-end furniture store splitting budget across lots of audience tests rarely gets there, so ROAS swings week to week on a handful of orders.

That shapes how I'd scale. Fewer, broader ad sets that each see enough purchases to learn, with testing done at the creative level inside them instead of spinning up new ad sets. The other piece is attribution. Meta's longest click window is 7 days, and a $2,500 sofa is often a decision that takes weeks, so prospecting tends to look worse in Ads Manager than it really is while retargeting looks better. Before cutting anything cold, I'd check Shopify new-customer revenue against spend so we don't starve the audiences your site visitor and abandoned cart retargeting depends on.

On creative fatigue, frequency and CTR decay by ad are the early signals. For furniture, the angles worth testing first answer the objections a $2,000 buyer actually has: the piece in a real room, materials and scale up close, and delivery or returns reassurance. I can brief your creative team in those terms rather than asking for "more content."

The closest example in our book is a DTC luxury furniture brand on Meta. Our team ran it from April to August this year, about $105,000 in spend over four months, going from roughly $24K to $40K a month. The honest part: CPMs climbed from about $42 to $79 as spend grew, the sales campaigns averaged around $274 per conversion, and most of the peak month's budget went into a sitewide sale and retargeting. Scaling high-ticket furniture on Meta is mostly a fight against rising CPMs, and offers carry more weight than cold audiences alone.

Weekly updates would cover CPA, ROAS, AOV, CTR and conversion rate by campaign, plus what we're testing next and why.

Roughly where does monthly Meta spend sit today, under $10K, $10K to $30K, or above that?
