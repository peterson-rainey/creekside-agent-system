Do you know yet which first-week behavior inside Quantum Body predicts a trial turning into a paid subscriber? Logging a first intention, coming back on day three, turning on reminders? I ask because that answer decides what Meta should learn from long before there's enough paid volume to optimize on payment itself. Without it, the account ends up rewarding whoever downloads fastest.

I've watched that same trap on the ecom side. A replacement parts brand I ran Meta for had retargeting split across six thin campaigns, each learning from a shallow signal: site visits, add to cart, view content, time on site. We retired all six within three weeks and moved that spend into one campaign learning from purchases. Cost per purchase went from $34.72 in January to $19.89 in April while CPM stayed flat, then gave a little back to $22.91 in May. The lesson carries straight to an app: pick the deepest event Meta can learn from reliably, and pass everything past it back through the Conversions API as it happens.

That account also taught me not to judge creative on return during an expensive stretch. When CPMs rose about 40% in Q4, the concepts worth keeping were the ones whose click-through held, not the ones that only looked good on cheap impressions. With a creative team feeding assets, I'd want the next hook already in testing before a winner's click-through starts sliding.
