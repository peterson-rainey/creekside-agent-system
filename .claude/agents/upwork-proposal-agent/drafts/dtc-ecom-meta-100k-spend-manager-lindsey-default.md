## PROPOSAL (paste-ready)

Scaling aggressively during winning periods is where most six figure DTC accounts quietly break, and it is usually not the creative. What does your CPM do when you double daily budget on a proven ad set?

The reason I ask is what happened on a DTC furniture brand on our book. We pushed it to $40K a month in Meta spend, more than double its running average for the year, and CPM went from the mid $20s to the mid $80s on the way up. Volume held, but the cost of buying that volume more than tripled, and on a dashboard that looks identical to creative fatigue. So teams kill working ads and rebuild for nothing. Auction cost is the tax on scale, and if you are not reading CPM and frequency as a curve against spend, Advantage+ will spend into the expensive tail of your audience and you will misdiagnose it every time.

The other pattern I see in nearly every account I audit is a shallow optimization event. On an ecommerce parts account, monthly conversions went from about 525 to 665 while spend came down from $14.7K to $13.2K over the same stretch. Most of that work sat in the event ladder and dataset priority, not in new creative.

I built and sold my own ecommerce business before I did this for other people, and after 10+ years the accounts that scale cleanly all share one thing: the testing lane is funded separately from the scaling lane, so you are never choosing between learning and revenue.

Straight answer on your $100K bar so you can filter fast. Our agency book runs $185K to $266K a month across roughly two dozen Meta accounts. Our largest single account clears $100K a month, but it is healthcare, not DTC. Our largest ecommerce account peaked at $40K a month. If single account DTC spend is the hard line, that is a real gap on my side.

Answers to your list:

Structure and testing: consolidated CBO on broad for scaling, a separate ABO lane at fixed budget for testing so tests are never starved or overfed. New creative runs against a control and graduates on cost per purchase and hold rate rather than CTR, then moves into the scaling lane as its own ad set. Retargeting stays capped and windowed tight so it is not buying credit for purchases that were already coming.

Rate: percentage of ad spend, not hourly. 20% up to $30K/month, 15% from $30K to $60K, 10% above that, capped at $15K/month. At $100K a month in Meta spend that is $14,500. One time $1,500 onboarding covers the account audit and rebuild.

On screenshots: instead of posting redacted images, I would rather put live Ads Manager on a screen share and let you drive, pull any date range you want, and check the numbers above yourself. That is harder to fake than a static image. There is also a short video on my profile that walks through how I separate testing and scaling lanes.
