## Answers (paste-ready)

**1. Have you personally managed U.S.-based lead-generation campaigns? Describe one.**

Yes. The most relevant is a multi-location dental implant group in California. I ran the Meta side; our team
ran their search. Meta lead forms fed a call center that qualified and booked, so the handoff shape matches
yours. From April 21 to July 15 this year that account spent $64,746.78 and produced 2,558 leads at $25.31
blended, across eleven campaigns. That engagement ended in July when they took marketing in house.

**2. Regulated industries?**

Healthcare and legal, not insurance. Dental and medical are the bulk of it: the implant group above, a
cosmetic dentistry practice where cost per consult went from $48.79 to $9.58, a naturopathic practice running
$14 to $40 per patient conversion. On the legal side, a bankruptcy firm in Orange County where we doubled leads
from 117 to 229 and cut cost per acquisition from $86 to $50.29. We have also run a GLP-1 telehealth account,
which is the closest thing in our book to a restricted health category.

No ACA, no health insurance, no Medicare. I would rather say that plainly than stretch a dental result to
cover it.

**3. Approximate monthly ad spend?**

The dental account ran about $20,000 a month on Meta. Our Meta book overall sits in the low six figures a
month across clients, and the largest single account I manage runs near $99,000 a month.

**4. What cost per lead did you achieve?**

$25.31 blended on the dental account over that window. Split by language it was $26.38 English and $17.93
Spanish. For a different shape, the bankruptcy firm ran $50.29 per lead on search.

**5. Cost per qualified lead, appointment, or customer?**

This is the one I cannot give you cleanly, and I would rather flag it than produce a number I cannot stand
behind. Our reporting has consistently captured cost per lead, not cost per qualified lead or per booked
appointment, because in most of these engagements qualification happened inside the client's own CRM and the
outcome data never came back to the ad platform.

That gap is exactly why your setup interests me. You already have an assistant qualifying and tracking. If
that status gets written back as an offline conversion, cost per qualified lead stops being a report we
assemble by hand and becomes the number the campaigns optimize toward. I would want that wired in the first
few weeks, not at the end.

**6. Experience with both Meta and Google?**

Meta is mine directly, ten-plus years, alongside email. Google Ads sits with our search team, and the accounts
where I have the strongest results are ones where both ran together rather than one of us working alone. You
would be hiring a paid acquisition function that covers both, with me owning Meta and strategy. I say that
because the honest version matters more here than sounding like a single generalist.

**7. Meta, Google, or both, and why?**

Both, but not evenly and not on day one. Google first, at a smaller number, because ACA search demand is real
and already in market. The catch is that ACA search is one of the most aggressively bid categories in the
country during Open Enrollment, largely by lead aggregators buying the same clicks to resell, which is exactly
what you said you do not want to buy. So Google is where you capture intent, and it will not be cheap.

Meta is where your actual advantage is. Hispanic and Brazilian community presence is an audience and creative
advantage, not a keyword advantage, and there is no way to express it on a search results page. Meta lets you
run separate Spanish and English creative and forms against communities you already have standing in. On the
dental account, the Spanish campaigns ran at 68% of English cost per lead for exactly that reason.

Practically: start Google two to three weeks ahead to gather baseline intent data, bring Meta up alongside it,
then let measured cost per qualified lead move the split rather than deciding it now.

**8. How do you tell whether the problem is the ad, targeting, landing page, offer, or follow-up?**

By reading the funnel in order and finding the first place the number breaks, rather than guessing at the end.
Low click-through with healthy impressions is creative or audience. Healthy clicks with low form starts is the
page or the message match between ad and page. Form starts with low completions is the form itself, usually
too many fields or one that feels invasive too early. Volume that looks fine but converts badly downstream is
targeting or offer.

Follow-up is the one people misdiagnose most, and I have watched it happen. On the dental account, spend got
pulled because the call center could not work the volume, and for weeks that read as a cost per lead problem
in the ad reporting because contact rate and set rate were not sitting next to cost per lead in the same view.
Nothing was wrong with the ads. The fix is boring: contact rate, qualification rate and set rate reported
beside cost per lead from week one, so an ad problem and a capacity problem cannot be confused.

**9. Conversion tracking and attribution tools?**

Meta Pixel with the Conversions API server side, Google Tag Manager, GA4, UTM discipline that survives into
your tracker, and offline conversion imports pushing your assistant's qualification outcomes back to Meta and
Google. Call tracking where the phone matters, which for an ACA audience it will.

I want to be accurate about what that is: it is the method I would use, and I can wire it. It is not a claim
that we have a published tracking-build case study, because we do not.

**10. Multilingual campaigns in English, Spanish, or Portuguese?**

English and Spanish yes, with numbers. On the dental account I ran two Spanish campaigns against nine English
ones, $5,808.51 and 324 leads at $17.93 on the Spanish side versus $58,938.27 and 2,234 leads at $26.38 on the
English side. Spanish ran at about 68% of English cost per lead, and that was consistent across seven, thirty
and ninety day windows.

Portuguese is a straight no. There is no Portuguese campaign anywhere in our history on either platform. Your
scope names Portuguese strategy as a deliverable, so you should weigh that honestly against the rest of this.
If Portuguese matters as much as the other two, I am the wrong pick or you need someone alongside me for it.
If it is a smaller third segment, I would build English and Spanish properly and bring in a Brazilian
Portuguese copywriter under my structure rather than pretend the language is mine.

**11. U.S. health insurance or ACA advertising compliance experience?**

No, and this is the one I would push back on hardest if I were you.

What I have is adjacent: running health categories where accounts get restricted, ad copy gets disapproved,
and claims language has to be handled carefully, including a GLP-1 telehealth account. What I do not have is
ACA-specific compliance history, and we hold no health certification of any kind on either platform.

For your situation the specifics that would matter are CMS marketing rules for agents and brokers, the
disclaimers required on ACA lead ads, restrictions on how subsidy and cost-savings language is phrased, and
Meta's health-category limits on how narrowly you can target. I know that terrain exists and how to work
inside it. I have not personally carried an account through an ACA compliance review, and if that is a hard
requirement you should hire someone who has.

**12. Case studies, screenshots, or examples?**

The dental account above is the closest one and the numbers in these answers are the real figures, not
rounded. I cannot send screenshots of it, because the client took marketing in house in July and we no longer
have access to that ad account, and I will not send a screenshot I cannot currently stand behind.

What I can do: walk you through any of these accounts live on a call, or export the reporting for a comparable
active account with the client name removed.

**13. What would you charge?**

Setup is a one-time onboarding fee of $1,500 per platform, so $3,000 if we run both Meta and Google. That
covers account structure, tracking build, audience and keyword research, and campaign build.

Ongoing management is a percentage of ad spend rather than an hourly rate, 20% per platform with a $1,500 per
platform monthly minimum. At the test budget in my next answer that lands at $3,000 a month total. As spend
grows the percentage steps down in tiers, so the fee does not scale linearly with your budget. Ad spend is
paid by you directly to Meta and Google, as you specified.

**14. Recommended monthly ad spend for an initial Florida test?**

$10,000 a month, split roughly $6,000 Meta and $4,000 Google, for at least 90 days.

The reasoning: Florida is the largest ACA marketplace in the country and Open Enrollment is a compressed,
expensive window, so a small budget spread over three languages and two platforms produces four or five
underpowered campaigns that never leave the learning phase. $6,000 on Meta is enough to run English and
Spanish as genuinely separate builds with real creative volume behind each. $4,000 on Google is enough to hold
position on a narrow, high-intent keyword set without trying to outbid aggregators on the broad head terms.

I would not go below $5,000 a month total. Under that, the honest answer is that you would not learn anything
you could act on before enrollment closes.

**15. Comfortable working with your internal assistant?**

Yes, and I would want more from that relationship than most people would ask for. Your assistant is the only
person who will know why a lead was bad, and that is the single most valuable input into the campaigns.

Concretely: a shared disposition field with a fixed set of reasons rather than free text, so "wrong income
band" and "not looking to switch" and "never answered" stay separate. A weekly fifteen minute call during
Open Enrollment. Their qualification outcomes pushed back into Meta and Google as offline conversions so the
platforms optimize toward the leads that actually enroll. That last piece is what turns their feedback from a
conversation into an optimization signal.
