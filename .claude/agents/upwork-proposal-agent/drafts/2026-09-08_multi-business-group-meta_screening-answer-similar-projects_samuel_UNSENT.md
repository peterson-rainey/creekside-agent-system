## Draft answer

Right now our team runs 16 live Meta accounts across about ten verticals, and three of those situations are close to what you are describing.

The closest structural match is a client with two Meta accounts under one owner, both running lead campaigns, which we still run today. Since June one account took $32,610 to 178 leads and the other took $24,072 to 13. The second had far cheaper impressions, roughly a $30 CPM against $132 to $220, which is exactly why it kept getting funded. Getting the two judged against each other on cost per lead rather than on their own separate dashboards was most of the work there.

We also ran a dental group where one owner had two brands, an implant practice and an oral surgery referral practice, on a single contract with a single payer. Across the last 90 days they were live, Meta produced 784 leads at about $23 on the implant side and 73 on the smaller brand. Both wound down in July when they took the function in house. Worth saying plainly that this was a staffing decision on their end rather than a performance one, and they kept the structure we built.

The third is a set of three meal prep brands under one relationship, two of them still running. Same vertical, same kind of offer, partly overlapping geography. Less about optimization on that one and more about keeping the brands out of each other's auctions, which is the specific risk when a group sells similar things to similar people.

Across the whole book it is 34 Meta accounts and about $2.64M in spend over the last twelve months.

The gap worth naming: our largest multi account client is one practice running several accounts, not several separate businesses. The truest match to a business group, the dental one, ended in July. And I do not yet know what your businesses actually sell, which is what decides whether these accounts should be consolidated or deliberately kept apart.
