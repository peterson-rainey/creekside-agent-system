## Draft

Giving Tuesday falls right in the most expensive week on the Meta calendar, so it should drive how September and October get built. Across the Meta accounts our team runs, CPMs were about $28 on November 10 last year, peaked at $58 the Sunday after Black Friday, and were still $54.74 on Giving Tuesday. Reaching a cold audience that day cost roughly double what it did three weeks earlier.

That makes the real work before December 1 filling the warm pools while reach is cheap. A few things decide whether that works:

The Pixel has to be live before the September 22 reels go out. Video viewer audiences can look back at past views, but website visitor audiences only start counting from the day the Pixel is installed. Any site traffic from Wave 1 before then can't be retargeted later.

Where does a donation actually complete? If the donate button sends people to a giving platform on another domain, a Pixel on your site never sees the gift. The visitor audience builds fine, but the Giving Tuesday campaigns end up optimizing toward a click instead of a donation. I'd check that before any audience work.

Lookalike seeds matter more than the lookalike itself. A seed of site visitors finds people who browse. A seed of past donors finds people who give, and Meta needs at least 100 matched people to build one. An uploaded donor file usually beats waiting months for Pixel conversions to pile up.

On the day itself, video viewers and site visitors overlap heavily, so three ad sets running at once will bid against each other unless exclusions are set up. Big budget edits on a peak day can also push ad sets back into learning, so it's worth scheduling the budget ahead of time.

Over the last twelve months our team has run 34 Meta accounts and about $2.6M in spend. Lindsey leads Meta here and would be the one working in your account.

Two questions. Which platform processes your online gifts, and roughly how does the planned Giving Tuesday and December budget compare with what's going behind the September reels?
