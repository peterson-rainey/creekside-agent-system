On the market itself, the lever is fulfilment rather than price. Every furniture advertiser claims competitive pricing and none of them can prove it in a feed. A delivery date is provable, and it removes the exact reason people stall on a bed purchase.

I would start with two campaigns, not six. One broad prospecting campaign optimised to Purchase with the budget concentrated, and one catalog campaign carrying retargeting and range browsing. Furniture is visual and multi-SKU, so the catalog earns its place early rather than as a later addition.

The reason for consolidating is arithmetic. Meta needs roughly 50 purchases per ad set per week to leave the learning phase. Split a starting budget across five ad sets and all five sit in learning permanently, which is usually the point a brand decides Meta does not work for them.

Early testing runs at the creative level inside the prospecting campaign, not by launching new audiences. Three or four genuinely different arguments at a time rather than recrops of one hero image: delivery speed, price against a named alternative, the made-by-us story, and the room shot. Angles get replaced rather than tweaked. Audiences start broad, since furniture buyers do not cluster into tidy interests.

One thing I would check before any of it. A bed is a two to six week decision and Meta reports on a seven day click window by default, so the campaigns doing the real work often read as the weakest ones. If reported ROAS and actual revenue are not moving together month to month, that gets fixed before anything is scaled.
