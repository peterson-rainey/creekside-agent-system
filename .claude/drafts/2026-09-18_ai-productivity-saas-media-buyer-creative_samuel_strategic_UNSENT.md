An AI productivity app will get cheap signups on almost any platform, because "try an AI tool free" is an easy click. The catch is that Meta, TikTok and YouTube will each happily optimize toward those signups, and the people who sign up fastest are rarely the ones who pay. If the paid subscription event isn't being sent back to each platform, all three algorithms end up chasing tire kickers while the dashboards look great.

So week one is tracking before ads: signup and first paid subscription firing server side on Meta and TikTok and imported into Google, so every platform gets judged on cost per paying user, not cost per signup.

The honest tradeoff on scope: three platforms plus creative production on $1,200 spreads thin. Meta needs roughly 50 conversions a week per ad set to exit learning, and a paid subscription is far rarer than a signup. My recommendation is to prove one platform first, usually Meta for a consumer productivity tool, then port the winning angle to TikTok or YouTube once there's something worth porting.

On creative, our team builds the angle and hook matrix and produces the statics and scripted short-form video. To be straight on two points: we haven't shipped AI-generated video ads for a client yet, and TikTok is a smaller part of our book than Meta and Google. If either is a hard requirement, I'm not your best fit.

Rough timeline: tracking and first creative batch in week one, launch in week two, first real read on cost per paying user around day 30.

Relevant work: we run paid acquisition for our own AI product, per-seat subscription with a free trial, so the "cheap trials that never convert" problem is one we deal with on our own money. For a B2B SaaS client, Meta built awareness and Google captured the search demand it created, and inbound leads and ARR doubled within 6 months. For a consumer app, creative testing took cost per install from $7.36 to $3.90 across 2,662 installs.
