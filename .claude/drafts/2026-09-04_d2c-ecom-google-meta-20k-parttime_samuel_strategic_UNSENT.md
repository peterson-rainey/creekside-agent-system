## PROPOSAL (paste-ready, 3,214 chars)

Most D2C accounts running Google and Meta together report one blended ROAS, and that number is usually carried by branded search. On one of our e-commerce accounts the branded search campaign returns 26.95x on $6,085 of spend. The unbranded search and shopping campaigns on the same account over the same period return 4.15x on $14,383 and 4.13x on $8,061. Blended, it reads 8x to 11x. Scale on the blended figure and you scale the campaigns that were already going to convert, while the incremental line stays flat.

So the first question on a launch is not which platform. It is which half of the account is actually buying new customers, and what a new customer is allowed to cost.

How we would open:

1. Split reporting on day one. Branded and unbranded never share a line. Meta prospecting and retargeting never share a line. You need the cold traffic number by itself before any budget decision.
2. Set the CPA ceiling off contribution margin and repeat rate, not off a target ROAS. Target ROAS is an output, not an input.
3. Launch narrow on Meta with a tight creative set, hold structure steady through the learning window, and let creative be the variable. Most launches stall because structure and creative move at the same time.
4. Google captures demand that already exists. Meta creates it. Budget them against different jobs.

On the $20k a month bar, here is exactly what we can and cannot show.

Meta, D2C e-commerce, pulled from the account: a home furnishings brand we ran at $24,861 in May, $23,871 in June, and $40,389 in July of this year. Across the engagement that was $105,010 in spend and 343 purchases at a $306 blended cost per purchase. The useful detail is underneath that. CPM climbed from the high $30s to the mid $80s over those months as we scaled, and cost per purchase held inside a $251 to $334 band the entire way. Absorbing a doubling in auction cost without letting CPA follow it is the actual skill in scaling Meta.

Google, D2C e-commerce, and this is the honest limit: our e-commerce Google accounts run in the $6,000 to $10,000 a month range, not above $20,000. The account I opened with sits there, returning 7.3x to 11.2x blended with 199 conversions in its best month. The Google accounts we run above $20,000 a month are lead generation, not e-commerce. Better you hear that from me now than find it later.

On the team, there are three operators here. The one on our D2C e-commerce side ran the Meta account above and would be on your client's account day to day. Six years on paid ads across the group.

Two things would change the plan:

What monthly media budget range is the client planning, and is that per platform or combined? A launch at $8,000 and a launch at $40,000 are different builds, not the same build scaled.

Which countries is the client selling into, and is there an existing pixel with purchase history, or is this a clean start?

One structural note. We work on a percentage of ad spend rather than an hourly rate, because hourly pays us to spend time and a percentage pays us to spend well. If this role is scoped strictly hourly, worth surfacing before either of us puts more time in.

Happy to walk through the account structure behind either of those numbers.
