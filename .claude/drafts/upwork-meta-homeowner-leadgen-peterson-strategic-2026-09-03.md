Solar and roofing lead gen on Meta rarely breaks because of bad audience research. It breaks because a cheap cost per lead and a qualified homeowner are two different things, and most accounts are quietly optimized for the first one.

Meta collapsed most of its targeting levers into broad delivery years ago, so audience research is worth doing but it mostly informs creative angles, not ad set setup. The pre-launch work that actually moves cost per qualified lead is deciding what a real lead is to your sales team, then wiring that definition back into the account so Meta learns from booked appointments instead of raw form fills. Homeowner verticals are the worst offenders here. A three question instant form will hand you cheap leads all day and your closers will hate every one of them.

On results, the closest thing in our book to what you are describing is a high volume lead form program feeding a call center. 2,558 leads at a $25.31 average cost per lead across roughly $65K in Meta spend over four months, with monthly CPL staying between $18 and $30 even as we scaled monthly spend from $9K to $32K. Different vertical, same mechanic: instant forms, hard qualification, and constant creative rotation to keep CPMs from climbing. We also run a Texas gutter account on Meta currently, though it is early enough that I would not put numbers behind it yet.

On static versus video, for homeowner services I lean static at the bottom of the funnel because the buyer already knows what a new roof or a solar array is. What moves them is the offer, the financing terms, and a believable before and after. Video earns its place cold, where you have to build the problem first, storm damage or a utility bill that keeps climbing, before any offer can land. Video also buys reach cheaper when CPMs run hot, which matters a lot in a seasonal vertical.

What is your current cost per lead, and what does your sales team count as a qualified one? Those two numbers decide almost everything about how I would structure this.
