Is MER being tracked for each site on its own, or blended across the portfolio? I ask because a blended number lets one strong site quietly cover for another that is buying revenue at a loss, and a few high-ticket mattress orders can hide a lot.

Here is how I would run the first 14 days, and where each step comes from.

Days 1 to 4, tracking. Before trusting any ROAS, I match what Meta reports against real store orders site by site, then check Pixel and CAPI deduplication, purchase values, and whether UTMs are clean enough to split revenue by channel. On a replacement parts brand I ran, the campaigns optimized to site visits and add to cart showed 7x to 10x, while the broad purchase campaign carrying about half the budget showed about 5x. The flattering numbers lived where the money wasn't. Anything broken becomes a spec your specialist can build from.

Days 5 to 9, where the revenue actually comes from. For each site I split results into prospecting vs retargeting and full price vs promo. On a high-ticket furniture brand I ran, the best month on paper was 4.17x. Almost 60% of that spend sat inside a 50% off sitewide sale, and retargeting brought in another third of the revenue. A great month in Ads Manager, and a very different question for margin.

Days 10 to 14, after the click. I follow traffic from landing page to completed order, by device and by product. On a luxury mattress brand I run Meta for, the biggest leak turned out not to be in the ads at all: fewer than 1 in 10 shoppers who reached checkout finished. Your Brand Director and UX team get findings like that ranked by revenue at stake, with the specific change attached.

Day 14 is one document: what's broken, what's leaking, and where budget moves first.
