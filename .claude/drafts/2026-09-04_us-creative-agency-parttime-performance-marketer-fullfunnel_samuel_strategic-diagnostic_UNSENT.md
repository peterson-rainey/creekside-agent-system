Most lead quality problems are not targeting problems, they are feedback problems. When qualified, booked and closed outcomes never make it back into Meta and Google, both platforms keep optimizing toward the cheapest form fill they can find, and the account gets progressively worse at the exact thing it is being judged on. Cost per lead drops, quality drops with it, and the creative usually takes the blame.

That is why the order of diagnosis matters more than the tooling. Frequency and CPM by segment separate an audience problem from a creative one. Click to form start separates the page from the ad. Form start to submit separates the offer from the traffic. Below the submit, only the CRM can answer. On one aesthetics account our team found several hundred leads sitting untouched in the CRM while the ads were producing thousands of conversations a month. No creative test would ever have found that, and no amount of ad account work would have fixed it.

A short introduction, then your list.

We are a US based agency, Central time, six years across Google and Meta, currently around 16 live Google accounts and roughly a quarter million a month of Meta spend. Lead generation for US service businesses is the core of the book.

On Meta, Google and CRO. Both platforms hands on, daily. Tracking is where most accounts are quietly broken, so GA4, GTM, pixel with the Conversions API server side, event deduplication and offline conversion import are standard rather than a specialty. Straight with you on CRO: we diagnose funnels and write the recommendations, and we have no published conversion rate lift to point at, because our clients own their pages and their web teams ship the changes. On the page layer I would rather work from your numbers than claim a result we cannot show you.

Three examples.

A dental implant practice running Meta lead forms into a call center, 21 April to 15 July 2026, on a $20,000 per month budget. $64,747 produced 2,558 leads at $25.31. The work that mattered there was not cost reduction. Our team added qualifying questions to the form and deliberately accepted a higher cost per lead for better ones, then throttled spend to match what the call center could actually answer. The constraint was capacity, not CPL, which is only visible if you are reading booked consults rather than form fills.

A bankruptcy firm in Orange County on Google. Restructured into four market specific campaign segments: conversions went from 117 to 229 while cost per conversion fell from $86.09 to $50.29, on $11.5K of spend, only $1.44K more than the prior period.

A custom home and barndominium builder in Tennessee on Google, from 15 leads a month to 60 while cost per lead came down from $454 to $239.
