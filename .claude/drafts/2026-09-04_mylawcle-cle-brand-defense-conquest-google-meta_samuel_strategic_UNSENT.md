## PROPOSAL (3,391 chars)

Your brand-defense problem has two levers and most people only run one.

The paid lever is bidding your own name. The free lever is Google's trademark complaint process. If myLawCLE is a registered mark, filing an authorized-representative complaint with Google forces competitors to strip "myLawCLE" out of their ad headlines and descriptions. It does not stop them bidding on the term, nothing does, but it kills the ad that reads "myLawCLE Alternative" in the headline. That filing costs nothing and usually resolves in a couple of weeks. Running it alongside a defense campaign gets the slot back and weakens their ad at the same time. If the mark is not registered yet, that route is closed and defense becomes purely a bidding problem, which changes the budget math.

The same rule runs in reverse on conquest. You can bid Barbri and Lawline all day. You cannot put their names in the ad copy. Compliant conquest copy sells the comparison without the competitor's name in it. The named comparison lives on the landing page, and that page is what carries the disclaimer.

What we can show you.

Conquest against brand, same account, same period. A competitor-keyword campaign we ran converted at $64.24 per conversion. The brand campaign in that same account converted at $136.96. Conquest was the cheaper of the two. That was healthcare, not CLE.

The honest other half. A second conquest campaign in that same account, different geo, spent $381.58 across 173 clicks and converted zero. Conquest is competitor and geography specific, not a switch you flip. We killed that one rather than let it run.

Brand defense economics. On a client where we run brand and non-brand side by side, brand search sits at $0.35 CPC and $16.90 per conversion. Non-brand in the same account is $1.26 and $104.25. Roughly six times cheaper. That gap is the argument for defending the slot.

Legal. We run Google Search for law firms. One bankruptcy practice went from 117 to 229 leads a month with cost per lead down from $86 to $50.29. Worth saying plainly: that work sells legal services to consumers. Selling to practicing attorneys is a different buyer and we have not done it. Our closest education account is a coaching and education franchise on Google and Meta, not accredited CLE.

No attorney-audience Meta proof either. Prospecting to practicing attorneys on Meta is a targeting problem with no dependable interest layer, so the honest build is broad prospecting judged on cost per enrollment, with retargeting doing the closing. Anyone promising you a clean attorney segment is selling you Meta's job-title targeting, which is thin and getting thinner.

Your 30-day sequence is realistic. Week 1 defense is the easy part. What actually decides week 1 is whether GA4 and Google Ads are counting the same conversion. On accounts coming to us fresh we usually find purchase firing twice, or live and on-demand enrollments collapsed into one action, which makes CPA by campaign meaningless before the first scorecard goes out.

One thing to be straight about rather than have you find it later. We are a small shop, not a solo freelancer. One person builds and runs the account, the same person every week, no rotating juniors and nothing handed off. But we are not going to claim to be a one-person operation when we are not. If that rules us out, better to know now than in week three.
