## ANSWER 1 (paste-ready)

Please show me websites you personally redesigned and explain what you would change about my current website.

None to show. Our team runs paid Meta and Google and doesn't redesign websites, so the rebuild itself would be a separate hire. We did go through your site the way we would before sending ad traffic to it, and a few things stand out.

When your site comes up on Google right now, the headline reads "perfect lashes website." Google sometimes rewrites these, but the page title should still say lash extensions and Chilliwack. Your Google Business Profile carries even more weight for nearby searches, so the reviews and booking link there are worth a look too.

The site doesn't run a Meta pixel or Google tag, and Canva's own help pages say its websites don't use cookies. So as the site stands, no ad can be credited with a booking through it. Until that changes, the cleaner measurement sits in your booking system. If that's Vagaro, it already reports where each appointment was booked.

On the homepage, training is a "book training" button and nothing else: no course length, price, kit or dates. Someone weighing a career change needs a full page, and your strongest proof is already on your site. At least two of your artists started in your own course, and one now manages the salon.

The pricing by artist level is a real strength. A Level 2 classic set at $90 is an easy first-visit offer, and ads can steer new clients to whichever level has open chairs. Keep it, and clean up small typos like "purcahse product," since the brand is meant to feel premium.

## ANSWER 3 (paste-ready)

How do you use metrics to inform your strategy?

By deciding what a result is worth before judging what it costs. On your prices, a new classic client with a Level 3 artist is $120 for the set, then $60 a fill. A client who comes back every three weeks for a year adds roughly $1,000 in fills on top of that set, so the right cost per new client depends on your rebook rate, not the first ticket. That's the first number we'd ask you for.

Each campaign then gets one number it's judged on. Services: cost per booked appointment. Training: cost per inquiry, then how many of those enroll, because a cheap inquiry that never enrolls is expensive. If you book through Vagaro, its Source report helps here, since it shows where appointments were booked and whether each client is new or returning.

Everything else is a warning light. Frequency climbing while click-through falls usually means people are tired of the creative, so new creative goes in before cost per booking rises. Cheap clicks with few bookings point to the booking page, not the ad.

Budget calls get made on at least a week of data, never a single day, and training gets a longer window because students take longer to decide.
