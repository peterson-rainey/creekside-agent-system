Is the property management account already running under Special Ad Category, or has it not been flagged yet? Housing strips age, gender and most detailed targeting, and forces a minimum fifteen mile radius, so whatever audience structure carries the other three accounts cannot simply be copied into that one. It has to be rebuilt, and that is usually where a mixed portfolio quietly leaks money.

The reason that is worth asking first: after 10+ years running Meta accounts, and after building and selling my own e-commerce brand, the failure I see most in multi-industry portfolios is one playbook applied four ways.

On a dental implant account I worked on, we deliberately added qualifying questions to the Meta lead forms and accepted a higher cost per lead in exchange for leads the call center could actually work. That account produced 2,558 leads at $25.31 across 90 days on roughly $65K in spend. When we pulled spend back later, it was to match call center capacity, not because the economics broke. Volume was never the constraint there. Intake was.

On a replacement parts retailer I ran, the account was fragmented across fourteen campaigns. Consolidating to seven took return from 4.87x to 8.07x over the following two months, and CPM actually rose about 11% across that window, so the gain was not bought with cheaper media. Both of those accounts have since ended, so they are closed books I can walk through in full rather than talk around.

Neither one is a marina or a roofing account. What carries over is the diagnosis rather than the vertical: separating lead quality from lead volume, and separating account structure from media cost. Those are the two questions I would put to each account before touching targeting.

Pixel and Conversions API coverage also matters more across four industries than it does inside one, because server side event matching is what holds the quality signal steady when the offer and the landing page change from account to account.
