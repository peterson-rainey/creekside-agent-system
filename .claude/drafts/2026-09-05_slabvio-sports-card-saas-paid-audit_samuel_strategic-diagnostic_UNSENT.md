## PROPOSAL (paste-ready) -- 4,841 chars

The first thing worth resolving is what "$7.42 per result" is actually counting, because it probably is not a trial start. A result in Meta is whatever event that ad set optimizes for, and at your budget the account was almost certainly set to a click or a landing page view rather than a purchase event, since Meta will not reliably deliver on a purchase event that fires once or twice a day. Run the arithmetic against your own spend. Fourteen signups from paid and organic combined, against roughly $250 to $350 a week across two platforms, puts your real cost per signup an order of magnitude above $7.42. Both numbers are true. They are counting different things, and the cheaper one is the one steering your budget decisions.

That matters more than it sounds, because the same gap explains why neither of your two candidate diagnoses can currently be proven. Meta needs roughly fifty optimization events per ad set per week to exit the learning phase. At $10 a day you have never been near that, on either platform, which means the delivery you have been evaluating is Meta guessing rather than Meta optimizing. TikTok's climbing CPM is the same condition seen from the auction side. So the honest position on your question is that at fourteen signups you do not yet have a top of funnel finding or an activation finding. You have a measurement problem sitting in front of both.

With that said, you asked for a gut read, so here it is. Your 43 percent checkout completion on a trial that requires a credit card is not a weak number. It is a strong one. A card gate typically filters hard, and the people who clear it are the most motivated cohort you will ever see. If one in six of that cohort never adds a card inside 24 hours, the drop is not happening at the ad. It is happening between checkout and first value, and a card-gated trial makes that failure expensive in a specific way, because those users are on a billing clock they will silently cancel rather than complain about. So my read agrees with yours directionally, but for a different reason than the one in your post, and with a caveat: the zero percent Facebook activation is a fourteen row sample, not an audience verdict, and I would not let anyone restructure a channel on it.

The other item I would pull forward is that "Track. Analyze. Profit." is itself the compliance exposure. TikTok did not flag a stray ad, it flagged a profit claim, and that claim lives in your brand line rather than in one piece of creative. That is a positioning decision and a disclaimer layer at the same time, and it belongs inside the audit rather than on a roadmap for later.
