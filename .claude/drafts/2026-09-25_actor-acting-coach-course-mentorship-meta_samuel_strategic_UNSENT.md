On a high ticket mentorship, the cheapest application is rarely the best one. If the only signal Meta gets is "application submitted," it learns to find more people who fill out forms easily, and those are often not the actors who enroll. The fix is telling Meta which applicants were actually qualified, so it can learn the difference.

**Where applicant quality gets decided**

After each application is reviewed, it gets marked qualified or not, and that result goes back to Meta through the Conversions API. Meta needs about 50 results in a week before an ad set exits learning, and a mentorship round will not produce 50 enrollments a week. So the campaign still optimizes on applications, while the qualified signal shows which ads and audiences bring in the right actors. Each ad then gets judged on cost per qualified application, not cost per lead.

On tracking, I would confirm the pixel and Conversions API count each application and each course purchase once, not twice, and that purchases on the checkout page actually reach Meta. Then I would compare Meta's numbers against your checkout and application records for the same dates. If they disagree, that gets fixed before any budget decision.

**The course and the mentorship feed each other**

Someone who paid $397 and watched your lessons is already sold on how you teach. That makes course buyers one of the warmest audiences you have for the mentorship, and worth their own ad set before each round opens. It is also worth checking how many past mentees bought the course first.

**Creative direction from the results**

Past ads get grouped by pain point and hook, then ranked on qualified applications and purchases rather than clicks. Before each round, you and your editor get a short brief: which angles to keep, which to retire, and two or three new ones to film. Depending on what the data shows, that might be the actor who keeps getting auditions but no callbacks, or the one who has trained for years and still cannot get seen by agents.

**What our book covers**

No acting or performing arts account on our book yet, worth saying up front. The closest match our team runs is Meta for a company that sells a coaching certification to adults, through a recurring webinar and application-style lead campaigns. The lesson from that account that applies here: campaigns built to generate conversions and campaigns built for reach get reported separately, because blending them hides which one is working. The attached case study covers January to October 2025.

**How we work and bill**

We are a small team rather than a solo freelancer. One strategist owns your account, and you get a written update every two weeks plus a live dashboard you can check anytime: spend, results, what they mean, and what to test next.

We do not bill by the hour. Management is a monthly fee of 20% of ad spend with a $1,500 minimum, plus a one time $1,500 onboarding fee that covers the full review of what you have run so far. There is a 90 day minimum. Between mentorship rounds, the time goes into the course campaigns and the brief for the next round, so each launch starts from a plan instead of from scratch.

**Three questions**

Roughly how does spend during a mentorship round compare to a normal month on the course?

How many mentorship spots open per round, and what share of applicants usually enroll?

What happens after someone applies: a call, or a straight yes or no?

With those, I can tell you whether the first fix is tracking, audiences, or creative.
