Improving lead quality and reducing CPL are two goals that pull against each other, and most Meta lead gen accounts never resolve the tension because they optimize for the wrong event. If your campaigns fire on the shallow Lead event, Meta will go find the cheapest form-fills available, and a share of those will be people who were never going to buy a website build. The fix is a deeper optimization event plus a qualification step ahead of sales. That usually raises nominal CPL while lowering cost per qualified opportunity. If CPL alone is the current scorecard, that is worth settling before any account work starts.

The second thing worth flagging is the audience. Selling website creation to store owners means your buyer already has a site, so the useful signal is behavioral and competitive rather than interest based. What a lead is actually worth to you comes down to build price and close rate, and neither one is in the post.

On proof, two honest notes. We do not have a website design or development client in our book, and I am not going to dress up an adjacent account as one. Our strongest Meta lead gen results also come from selling to consumers rather than to business owners, so owner targeting is a different motion than the one those numbers were built in.

What those numbers do show is volume handling. Our team ran Meta lead forms for a dental practice for 78 days on $64,746.78 and produced 2,558 conversions at a $25.31 CPL. The account was pulled back for one reason, and it was not cost. The client's call center could not answer the volume fast enough. That is the failure I watch for on any lead gen build, because the bottleneck usually sits downstream of the form rather than in the auction. Across the book, our team managed just over $2.6M on Meta across 34 ad accounts in the last twelve months.

Two questions.

Is your current spend closer to early testing, or are you already at a level that produces enough conversion data to optimize on?

And if you have run this offer before, what rough range was the cost per qualified lead, rather than the cost per form-fill?
