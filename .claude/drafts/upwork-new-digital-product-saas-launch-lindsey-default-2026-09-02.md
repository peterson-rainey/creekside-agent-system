What price point are you imagining for this? It decides more about the three month target than the product category does, and it is the variable most media buyers will not raise with you before taking the job.

Here is the arithmetic behind that. If this lands as a subscription at twenty or thirty dollars a month, clearing fees and spend inside ninety days means recovering acquisition cost in about three billing cycles, so blended cost per customer has to come in under roughly sixty to ninety dollars before management is counted at all. On cold Google and Meta traffic, for something with no search volume against its own name, no retargeting pool, and no conversion history for either algorithm to learn from, that is a very hard number to hit in month one. A one-time digital product in the two to five hundred range clears the same window comfortably, because the revenue lands on day one instead of amortizing across a year. Same ad account, same operator, opposite verdict. So the honest version of identifying a product that will be profitable starts with the economics of the offer, not the category.

The second thing that decides it is what month one is actually for. On a zero-state launch there is no pixel history and no conversion data, so pointing Meta at a purchase event that fires four or five times a week means it never exits learning and the spend gets expensive while it guesses. The way through is to optimize toward a dense upstream event, a trial start or a real email capture, hold revenue as the business number, then graduate onto the purchase event once volume supports it. Google runs the opposite direction, because there is no existing demand to capture for a product that does not exist yet, so early Google is category and competitor terms rather than brand.

On relevance, our team has done the zero-to-launch version of this on our own product. We co-founded and launched an AI SaaS on a per-seat subscription with a seven-day trial, and we run its paid acquisition ourselves, so those tradeoffs are ones we have paid for rather than read about. On client work the closest matches are a digital product where we drove 2,662 app installs on Meta and brought cost per install down from $7.36 to $3.90, and an early-stage B2B SaaS just past its seed round where we ran Meta for awareness and Google for demand capture because the search volume was not there yet.

Where I will be straight with you: we have not been hired to pick the product itself. What we can do is pressure-test a shortlist you are weighing against what survives paid acquisition, which comes down to price point, payback window, and whether the buyer is already searching for a solution. Choosing a category without reference to those three is guesswork with a better name on it.

Two things worth knowing before anyone hands you a plan. Roughly what monthly spend range are you working with, and which countries are you selling into? Both change whether ninety days to profitable is the right target or whether the honest one is ninety days to a proven, repeatable cost per customer with profit just behind it.

There is a short video on my profile covering how I approach launches like this. Happy to get into specifics whenever you want.
