Custom mattresses and home goods tend to run on promo calendars, and that is where tracking quietly breaks. On a luxury mattress brand our team runs Google for, Shopify's automatic discounts were not syncing to Merchant Center, so Shopping ads showed prices about $599 above the live site. It looked like a bidding problem. It was a feed problem, and nothing inside the ad platform flagged it.

Here is how our team would run the first 14 days.

Days 1 to 3, one source of truth. Pull real orders and net revenue per site from the store backend, then line up what GA4, Meta and Google each claim for the same window. Adding every channel's claimed revenue against actual revenue is the first honest MER read, and it shows which platform is over-crediting itself.

Days 4 to 6, the tracking pipes. GTM container review, Pixel and CAPI deduplication and event match quality, Google enhanced conversions, and which conversion actions are set to Primary. We have seen Shopify app micro-events log checkout values in the hundreds of millions and feed straight into bidding. Plus UTM naming across every site. Each fix becomes a written spec, ranked by how much budget it is currently misdirecting.

Days 7 to 10, the paid accounts. Split each site into brand vs non-brand, prospecting vs retargeting, and full price vs promo. Brand search and retargeting usually carry the ROAS that makes an account look healthy, so the real question is what cold traffic does on its own.

Days 11 to 13, after the click. Landing page to completed order, by device and by product, plus checkout completion rate and product pages that intermittently fail to load. Your Brand Director and UX team get the leaks ranked by revenue at stake, with the specific change attached.

Day 14, one document: what is broken, what is leaking, and where budget moves first.

One tradeoff on the paid audit. A week on one site gets tracking and the ad accounts done properly. The post-click read needs enough order volume to mean anything, so on a lower-volume site we would say so rather than guess.
