## PROPOSAL

Across that $250k, does a lead that turns into a booked job or a signed customer get written back into Meta and Google, or does it stop in the CRM?

That answer decides what a part time optimizer can actually do for you. If the platforms only ever see the form fill, both algorithms get very good at buying form fills. Cost per lead improves every month and the revenue line does not move. It reads like a creative problem and it is a signal problem, so creative testing on top of a blind conversion signal just finds cheaper versions of the same weak lead.

The clearest version I have run was a dental implant practice, Meta instant forms into a call center, about $20,000 a month. $64,746.78 spent, 2,558 leads at $25.31, April to mid July. The cost per lead was excellent and the client still pulled back, because the front desk could not work that volume. Nobody had defined what a good lead was before we scaled. On service accounts that is usually the real ceiling, not CPM.

On scale, the largest single Meta account I run personally is around $100,000 a month and it is live right now. Service industry, lead generation. So the size of your book does not worry me.

Two things I will not stretch. Google is not mine. I run Meta, and Google sits with a colleague I work with regularly, so on a both platforms seat you are getting half of that directly from me. And I want to know who owns creative and who owns the landing pages before I quote anything, because if I cannot touch either one then my only levers are bidding and account structure, and I would rather say that now than three weeks in.

One practical note so it does not surface late: I work flat monthly per account rather than hourly.

Roughly how many accounts sit inside that $250k, and what has to happen before a lead counts as qualified?
