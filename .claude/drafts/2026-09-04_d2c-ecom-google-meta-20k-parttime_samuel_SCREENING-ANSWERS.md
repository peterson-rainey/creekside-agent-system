## ANSWER 2 — "How do you use metrics to inform your strategy?" (2,247 chars)

Three rules, in order.

First, every campaign gets one decision metric tied to the job it is doing, and blended numbers are never the decision metric. Prospecting is judged on cost per new customer. Retargeting is judged on whether it moved incremental revenue, not on its own ROAS, because retargeting will always look profitable when it is re-buying people who were going to convert anyway. The Google account above is the clearest example. Blended it reads 8x to 11x, which looks like an account you should pour money into. Split it and the cold traffic is 4.1x while branded search at 26.95x is carrying the average. Those two readings lead to opposite budget decisions, and only one of them is real.

Second, the target comes from the client's economics, not from the platform. I want contribution margin per order, repeat purchase rate, and how long the client is willing to wait to get paid back. That gives an allowable cost per acquisition, and the ROAS target is just arithmetic after that. Setting a target ROAS first and reverse engineering a budget is the most common way I see ecommerce accounts scale into unprofitable volume, because the target was picked by feel.

Third, I use leading indicators to diagnose why a number moved before touching anything. Cost per purchase is a lagging metric and it tells you something is wrong without telling you what. CPM tells you whether you are paying more for the same attention. Click through and hook rate tell you whether the creative is fatiguing. Landing page conversion rate tells you the problem is not in the ad account. In the furnishings account, CPA stayed flat while CPM more than doubled, which said the creative was absorbing the auction pressure. That is a signal to keep feeding creative volume, not to cut budget, and the wrong read there would have stalled the account right as it was scaling.

Underneath all three is volume discipline. I set the decision thresholds and the minimum conversion count before launch, and I do not act on a number until it clears them. Most account damage I get hired to undo came from someone reacting to a bad three day window and restarting the learning phase over noise.
