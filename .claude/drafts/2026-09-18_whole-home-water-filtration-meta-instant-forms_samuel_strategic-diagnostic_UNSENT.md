## PROPOSAL

META HOME SERVICES

With a rep calling in under 60 seconds, the Instant Form doesn't need to do the qualifying. Your reps do that better than any form question can. What the form has to do is make sure the person who picks up is a homeowner who can say yes to a whole-home install. That usually takes two or three questions, not six, and the one I'd test first for filtration is water source. A homeowner on well water and one on city water respond to different problems, different hooks and often a different system, so knowing which one it is shapes both the sales call and the next round of creative.

The reporting side has a trap worth checking early. On a gutter account our team ran, the blended CPM read $39.05. Split by objective, the lead campaigns were actually paying $47.77, and about $204 of boosted posts buying cheap reach was pulling the average down. Small dollars, but it's the same pattern that makes an audience look efficient in Ads Manager while producing nothing downstream. Every number worth acting on should trace back to the CRM stage it produced.

That account also kept its proven Instant Form ads in their own campaign and tested new creative separately, with offer-led variations like financing running against city-led ones. That's the structure I'd use for your creative volume: winners stay protected, new concepts get a fair test, and a concept that wins gets rebuilt as variations instead of one edit soaking up the budget.

The closest match to your funnel was a dental implant group with cases in the upper $30Ks, Meta forms going straight into a call center. Over 90 days it produced 2,558 leads at $25.31 each. Our team added qualifying questions, accepted a higher cost per lead, and cut spend when the call center needed room. That last part applies to you as well. Past a certain point, rep capacity sets how fast spend can scale, not Meta.

Home services we've run include gutters, awnings, pest control and paving, with over $20M in managed spend overall. I don't have a verified CAC figure from a home-service Meta account that I can share, so a walkthrough of the reporting on a call will tell you more than a cropped screenshot.

To be upfront, we're a small team rather than a solo buyer. A dedicated Meta specialist runs your account day to day and I stay on strategy and creative direction. Pricing is a percentage of spend: 20% up to $30K a month, 15% on the portion from $30K to $60K, 10% above that, with a $1,500 monthly minimum and a one-time $1,500 onboarding fee that covers the audit and tracking build.

Roughly where does monthly Meta spend sit today, under $10K, $10K to $30K, or above $30K? That decides whether the first month goes into restructuring or straight into testing.
