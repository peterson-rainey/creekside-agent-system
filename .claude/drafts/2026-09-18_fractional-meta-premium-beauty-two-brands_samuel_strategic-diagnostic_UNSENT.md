## PROPOSAL

Beauty creative has a policy trap that costs more than a weak hook. Meta doesn't allow cosmetic ads that are negative about someone's appearance, body or hygiene, so the obvious problem-first angle ("tired of dull skin?") gets ads rejected. Before and after is allowed for cosmetic products shown to adults. That changes where the testing effort goes: the transformation and the product experience carry the ad, not the insecurity.

The first thing I'd check in both accounts is what each campaign is actually optimizing for. On a beauty-tech brand our team ran, a camera with a built-in vanity mirror sold to a young, very online audience, purchase-optimized campaigns paid under $60 a sale. Add-to-cart and checkout campaigns over the same weeks paid more than double. It wasn't a controlled test, but the pattern is common. The shallower event gets through learning faster and looks cheap in Ads Manager, and it teaches Meta to find people who browse.

That account spent $58,298 over about four and a half months for 623 purchases at $93.58 each, and only about one sale in seven came from retargeting. The warm pool runs out fast, so the prospecting creative has to carry the account. That's why creative volume matters more than audience tricks.

It also shapes how new creative goes in. Adding a new ad to an ad set restarts its learning phase, so dripping new UGC or founder videos into a winning ad set one at a time keeps resetting the thing that's working. I'd keep proven ads in their own campaign, launch new concepts in batches in a separate test, and read each concept by its hook and hold rate before spend decides it lost. A concept that wins gets rebuilt as new hooks and formats instead of being left to fatigue.

On tracking, the check I'd run first is whether the Shopify pixel and Conversions API events share an event ID. Without deduplication Meta counts some purchases twice and ROAS reads higher than Shopify does. Then I'd line up Meta-reported purchases against Shopify orders by week for each brand, so the number we optimize toward is one both of us trust.

We're also a small team rather than one buyer. A dedicated Meta specialist runs both accounts day to day, and I stay on strategy and creative direction alongside your creative team.

Roughly where does monthly Meta spend sit for each brand today: under $10K, $10K to $30K, or above $30K? That tells me whether month one goes into restructuring or straight into creative testing.
