## 2. How do you use metrics to inform your strategy?

Two questions first, because most reporting answers neither. Is a lead problem a quality problem or a volume problem, and did performance move because of structure or because media simply got cheaper.

On the first, a dental implant account in our book added qualifying questions to the Meta lead forms deliberately and took a higher cost per lead in exchange for leads the call center could actually work. It produced 2,558 leads at $25.31 over 90 days on about $65,000 in spend. When spend later came down, it was to match call center capacity, not because the lead economics broke. Reading that CPL rise as a media problem would have been the wrong call entirely.

On the second, a replacement parts account was spread across fourteen campaigns. Consolidating to seven took return from 4.87x to 8.07x over the following two months, and CPM rose about 11% across that same window. That last number is the one that matters, because it rules out the easy explanation. Without it you have a coincidence rather than a finding.

So day to day it is less about watching every metric and more about knowing which one is allowed to trigger a change. Across four accounts in four industries those thresholds will not be the same, and the quickest way to lose money is to apply one account's trigger to another account's numbers.
