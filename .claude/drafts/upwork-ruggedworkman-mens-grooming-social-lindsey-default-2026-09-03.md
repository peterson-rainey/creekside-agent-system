Do you know what share of your customers place a second order inside ninety days? For beard oil, shampoo and scrubs that one number decides more about a ninety to one hundred twenty day campaign than the platform mix does, and that window is just long enough to measure it.

The reason to ask first is that consumables get sold twice, and most launch plans only budget for the first sale. When I was running my own e-commerce brand, the first order rarely paid for itself once shipping and the intro discount came out. The second and third orders were the business, and those lived in email rather than in the feed. A content calendar with no replenishment flow under it produces traffic that looks healthy and a margin that does not.

That is also why posts per week is worth tracking but not worth steering by. It moves whether or not anything sells. Over ten years the reporting I have seen hold up is built on cost per first order, ninety day repeat rate, and revenue per subscriber, with posting cadence sitting underneath those as an input.

Straight with you on fit. My lane is paid social and email for e-commerce, not organic content calendars, community management, or YouTube and gaming placements. I ran Meta for a hot tub parts brand selling to hands-on guys who fix their own equipment, roughly $129,000 through that account at an $8.71 CPM across 14.8 million impressions, and what actually moved it was fixing an event setup that rewarded shallow actions and hid which products paid. Men's grooming is not a category I have run, and I would rather say so.

Two things worth knowing before anyone hands you a plan. Roughly what monthly range are you considering for paid, if paid is in scope at all? And is the Shopify catalog already synced to Meta, since shops and product retargeting both depend on it.

I have attached a few relevant results below. There is also a short video on my profile covering how I approach e-commerce accounts like this.
