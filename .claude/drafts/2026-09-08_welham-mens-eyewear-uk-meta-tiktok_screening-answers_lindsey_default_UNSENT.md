## Q2 — One case study from a fashion, eyewear, apparel or accessories brand

Straight answer first: I do not have an eyewear or apparel account with a trophy ROAS to hand you, and I would rather say that than dress up something adjacent as more than it is.

The closest real one is Blush Camera, a DTC brand selling into the same Instagram fashion and lifestyle audience yours lives in. I took it on cold, a new account with no pixel history. What I changed: got it out of the learning phase by consolidating and optimizing on the right event before pushing to purchase, then built a creative cadence to hold frequency down as spend grew. Month one, cost per purchase about $120. Month two, $68. We then scaled spend about 70 percent and cost per purchase moved back to $116, because the account outran its creative supply and CPMs climbed. Blended ROAS sat around 1.4 to 1.5x, with retargeting near 1.8x on a small warm pool. Spend up to about $20K a month over roughly four months. It taught me the thing that will decide your account: on a young brand, creative volume is the ceiling on scale, not targeting.

If you want the cleanest turnaround numbers I have, it is Master Spa Parts, ecommerce rather than fashion, where cost per purchase went from $45 to $20 and conversions from 349 to 665 over four months, on flat frequency and slightly less spend. Different category, same discipline.

## Q4 — First 30 days at £5,000–£20,000/mo (structure, prospecting vs retargeting, testing budget, what you'd stop)

Week one is tracking and a baseline, not spend. I verify the pixel and Conversions API are both firing server-side, events deduplicate cleanly, event match quality is high enough to trust, and the Shopify to Meta event mapping is right. Then I baseline the last 90 days and hand you the written audit and 30-day plan.

Structure: lean enough that the account can actually learn. One prospecting campaign, broad plus a small number of tightly scoped interest and lookalike tests, and one retargeting campaign. Meta needs roughly 50 conversions a week per ad set to leave the learning phase, so I consolidate rather than split your budget across eight ad sets that never get there.

Prospecting versus retargeting: at your spend and on a young account, roughly 80 to 85 percent prospecting and 15 to 20 percent retargeting. The warm pool is small early, and over-funding retargeting flatters blended ROAS while starving the cold engine that actually grows the brand.

Testing budget: I ring-fence about 20 to 25 percent of spend for structured creative testing, a set number of new concepts a week, each with a clear pass or fail on hook rate and cost per result over a spend floor, not a two-day ROAS read. Winners graduate into the scaling campaign, losers are cut on the rule, not on a hunch.

What I would stop: optimizing to purchase before there is purchase signal, splitting budget across too many ad sets, judging creative on one to three day windows, boosting posts as if it were growth, and touching budgets daily. The goal by mid-October is a library of proven winners to scale into Black Friday, not an account still testing at Q4 CPMs.
