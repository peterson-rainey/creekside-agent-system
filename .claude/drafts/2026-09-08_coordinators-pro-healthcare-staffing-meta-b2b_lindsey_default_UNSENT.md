The first thing I would want settled on your account: which special ad category, if any, your Meta campaigns are declared under.

On your site, your buyer is the practice owner, not a candidate, so the argument that you sell a service rather than post a job is a good one. It is not settled. Meta's employment rules are written broadly enough that placement services get argued both ways, and the classifier reads vocabulary, not who is buying. Staffing copy is built from the words it looks for. Hire, hiring, staffing, the job title itself. Those are words in copy, not targeting inputs. Meta has never offered employer or job title as a targeting option in any category.

Two things worth separating. Individual ads can be appealed and false positives do get reversed. What cannot be changed is a campaign's declaration, which is fixed at creation, so a campaign pulled into Employment gets duplicated and rebuilt rather than un-flagged. The rebuild costs you lookalikes, ZIP level targeting and age or gender narrowing. Special Ad Audiences, the old substitute, were retired in 2022, so nothing replaces them. Custom audiences do survive: customer list, site visitors, engagement. On a narrow B2B audience that is the lever left standing, and where I would start.

Track record. The largest Meta account I run is just under $99,000 a month, about $1.19 million over the last twelve months, live today, high ticket elective health. Its conversion data is incomplete, so I quote spend, not a cost per acquisition. Where data will not carry a number, I do not hand you one.

The one that transfers to your funnel: a dental implant account I ran, Meta lead forms feeding a call center. $64,746 over 90 days, 2,558 leads at $25.31. The useful part is not the CPL. The ceiling was never cost, it was how fast the call team could dial, so spend was throttled to capacity and qualifying questions went into the form to buy a better lead at a deliberately higher CPL. That account has since ended. Your funnel ends in a discovery call, so that is the same failure waiting: a good CPL that outruns whoever answers it.

I also worked a mortgage account on Meta inside a special ad category carrying the same restrictions Employment does, $144,984 across the flight, since ended. The rebuild math above comes from that, not a help page.

The gap, before you find it on a call. Nobody on our team has run Meta selling a staffing or back office service to practice owners, and we have no B2B Meta results worth putting in front of you. All of our healthcare work sells to patients, the opposite direction from yours.

Week one, before spend moves, I inventory your Business Manager and send back a written list with owners on it. Which assets sit in a personal profile instead of the business, who holds the pixel, whether your forms and pixel report the same event, and where one person leaving locks you out. I would sit as a user in your Business Manager and your GHL, never as owner, so your assets stay yours. GoHighLevel our team runs at API level, our own location and a client's: pipelines, contact level custom fields, calendars, a server side endpoint that creates contacts from a form. The API enrolls and removes contacts from workflows, which is the half that automates. Sequence logic, copy and timing stay interface work.

On tracking I read event quality and act on it: match rates, duplicate events, which event is actually optimized toward. On a restricted healthcare account, standard events were permitted, custom ones were not, the kind of detail that quietly breaks an optimization event. I have not shipped a server side CAPI build end to end. If you need one I will spec it, sit the developer call and test the events myself rather than hand off a ticket. Given what you place, I would keep health adjacent fields out of Meta and leave GHL as the system of record.

On format, since you are open. I do not sell hours because hours put the access and systems work in a bucket someone has to protect. Priced on spend, there is no bucket. Our fee is monthly as a percentage of ad spend, in brackets: 20 percent on the first $30,000, 15 percent on the portion from $30,000 to $60,000, 10 percent above that, with a $1,500 per platform monthly minimum, a $15,000 monthly cap, and a one time $1,500 per platform onboarding that includes the full audit. A fifth of a week is about 34 hours a month, so that floor likely sits at or under what you had in mind. You get a written update weekly whether or not we have a call, and you never need a scheduled call to reach me.

You asked for a short intro video. I will record one for your account and send it in this thread once it opens. My profile video is worth a watch in the meantime.

Two questions. Is Meta closer to $10,000 a month right now or closer to $50,000, and what is a placed coordinator worth to you over their first year?
