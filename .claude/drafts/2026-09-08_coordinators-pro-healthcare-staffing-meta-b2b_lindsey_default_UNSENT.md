The first thing I would want to know about your account: which special ad category, if any, your Meta campaigns are declared under.

A staffing company sits in an awkward spot there. Your ads sell a service to practice owners, so they are not job opportunity ads and should not fall under Employment. But Meta's classifier reads vocabulary, not the buyer, and staffing creative is built out of exactly the words it reaches for. Hire, hiring, staffing, find your assistant, job titles. Meta also re-classifies retroactively, weeks after launch, off copy and imagery. If a campaign gets pulled into Employment you do not appeal it back. The category is fixed at campaign creation, so the remedy is duplicate and rebuild under the declared category, and you lose lookalikes, ZIP level targeting and any age or gender narrowing on the way through. For a B2B account whose entire edge is a narrow audience of practice owners, that is not a policy footnote. That is your targeting model.

Where I have worked inside that restriction set: a mortgage account on Meta, in a special ad category carrying the same restrictions Employment does, $144,984 across the flight. I am not quoting a cost per lead from it, because 7 of its 13 months have no usable conversion data and I will not build a number on that. What it taught me is what an account looks like after it loses lookalikes and geographic precision, which is the thing worth protecting here.

Now the gap, before you find it on a call. Our healthcare work is patient acquisition. Dental, oral surgery, cosmetic, naturopathic. Ads that reach patients. Yours reach the practice. I have not run Meta selling a staffing or back office service to medical practice owners, and the B2B side of my book is thin. I am not going to dress that up.

What does transfer is the intake shape. A dental implant account I ran, Meta lead forms feeding a call center: $64,746 over 90 days, 2,558 leads at $25.31. The useful part is not the CPL. It is that the ceiling was never cost, it was how fast the call team could dial, so spend was throttled to match capacity and qualifying questions went into the form to buy a better lead at a deliberately higher CPL. That account has since ended. Your funnel ends in a discovery call, so that is the same failure mode waiting: a good CPL that outruns whoever answers it.

The largest Meta account I run is just under $99,000 a month, about $1.19 million over the last twelve months, live today, high ticket elective health. Spend is the honest figure there. Its conversion data is incomplete and I will not build a cost per acquisition out of it.

On the systems side. We run our own GoHighLevel location and I have been in the plumbing, not just the interface: pipelines and stages, contact level custom fields, calendars, and a server side endpoint that creates GHL contacts from a form. Worth knowing before you scope around it: the GHL API will list workflows and add or remove contacts from them, but it cannot author sequence logic, copy, timing or trigger conditions. Those are interface only. Anyone promising to automate your follow up through the API is describing the manual part.

On access, I hold credentials as a user inside your Business Manager and your GHL, never as owner, so your assets stay yours. What I own is the resolution. If a pixel stops receiving, a permission breaks or an asset handoff strands you, that is mine to see through rather than route back to your team.

On tracking, I read event quality and act on it: match rates, duplicate events, which event is actually being optimized toward, whether browser and server agree. On a restricted healthcare account I worked, standard events were permitted and custom ones were not, which is the kind of detail that quietly breaks an optimization event. I have not built a server side CAPI implementation end to end and will not claim it. A net new build is scoped work with whoever holds your dev access.

On format, since you said you are open. I do not bill hourly or by a fraction of a week. Our fee is monthly, as a percentage of ad spend, in brackets: 20 percent on the first $30,000 a month, 15 percent on the portion from $30,000 to $60,000, 10 percent above that, with a $1,500 monthly minimum and a one time $1,500 onboarding that includes the full audit. That is a monthly rate. What it is not is a time budget, and for what you described that works in your favor, because the access, systems and admin work has no separate hour bucket to be protected from. The responsiveness you want is a service level, and I will put it in writing. Same or next business day, faster when something is broken.

Two questions. Roughly what are you spending on Meta each month now, and what is a placed coordinator worth to you over their first year?
