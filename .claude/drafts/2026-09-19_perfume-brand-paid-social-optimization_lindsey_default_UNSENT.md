Which ad set is spending the most right now, and is it bringing in first-time buyers or mostly people who already know the brand? In fragrance, retargeting and past customers often take a big share of the budget and report a strong ROAS, while prospecting quietly gets more expensive. Split new buyers from returning ones and the account usually looks very different from the blended number.

Perfume also has a problem most categories don't: nobody can smell the ad. The image and the first line have to sell a feeling, an occasion or a reason to try, and those are separate angles worth testing on their own. When one wins, I build variations around it rather than swapping in new visuals, because a fresh image carrying a tired angle wears out just as fast.

That's also how you find out which message drives sales. Each test changes one thing and gets a pass/fail line before it launches, so after a few cycles you know which reason to buy is doing the work, not just which ad got lucky.

Two accounts shaped how I work:
- On a consumer camera brand selling to a young, very online audience, purchase-optimized campaigns paid under $60 a sale, while add-to-cart and checkout campaigns over the same weeks paid more than double. It wasn't a controlled test, but it's why I check the Pixel, Conversions API and store events before touching budgets.
- I took over an ecom account running 232 live ads on about $15K a month and cut it from eleven campaigns with spend down to seven. Cost per conversion went from $34.72 the month before I started to $19.89 by April, at about the same CPM. I changed targeting and creative in the same audit, so I can't credit one lever, but fewer, cleaner tests made the winners much easier to see.

Having built and sold my own ecommerce business, I look at an account the way an owner does: what a first order is worth, not just what the click cost.
