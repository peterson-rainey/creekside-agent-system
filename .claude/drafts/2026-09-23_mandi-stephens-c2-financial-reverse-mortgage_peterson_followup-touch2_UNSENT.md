## VARIATION A (recommended)

Mandi,

I sent the recap Saturday. Here is one piece I did not put in it.

When an outside vendor writes a reverse mortgage ad, the accountability does not stay with the vendor. NRMLA holds its members accountable for the indirect actions of the vendors they engage, advertising and lead generation providers included, and has said it intends to enforce its Code of Ethics against members whose lead gen companies run unethical advertising. That is the part worth knowing about the ads you flagged on the call.

NRMLA keeps a list of the practices it treats as unethical, and a couple of them show up constantly in that kind of creative. "No monthly payments" has to be qualified as no monthly mortgage payments, stated just as prominently, since taxes, insurance and upkeep stay with the borrower. Anything implying the loan is a government program, or putting a HUD or FHA name or logo on it, is on the same list. The landing page carries as much weight as the ad, so the disclosures have to live on the page and the copy has to match what was approved.

None of that is legal advice. It is the set of questions worth putting to any vendor you evaluate, us included: who writes the copy, who reviews it before it runs, and where the disclosures sit on the page.

Does reverse ad review run through C2 on your end, or does it sit with you as the originator?
