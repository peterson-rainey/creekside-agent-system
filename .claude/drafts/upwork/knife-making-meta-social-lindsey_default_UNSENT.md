## Draft
When your ads get rejected now, is it the product itself, the way the knives are shown in the creative, or the page the ad sends people to? Those are three different problems. The fix for each is different, and treating them as one is usually why a knife brand ends up guessing, editing, and resubmitting over and over.

When I ran my own e-commerce brand, I learned that restrictions are easier to plan around than to fight. So on a new account I start with a diagnosis before any strategy: which ads were rejected and under which reason, whether the Page or ad account itself has any limits on it, and which creative angles are getting through today.

From there, the approach I'd take is to build the brand around what review allows, not around what gets past it:
1. Lead with the craft. Forging, grinding, finishing, and the maker behind it. Process content tends to be the strongest organic and paid content in handmade goods, and it reads as craftsmanship, not as a weapon.
2. Separate reach from selling. Use video and engagement campaigns to grow a warm audience on Facebook and Instagram, then retarget that audience with product-focused ads and a clean landing page.
3. Build what Meta can't take away. Email list growth from day one, so a rejected ad or a slow week never stops sales.

The roadmap would be a 90-day plan: foundation and tracking in month one, content and audience testing in month two, and scaling what works in month three, with a weekly note on what won and what changes next.

On testing, splitting beats blending. On one furniture brand, prospecting was broken out by product line: one lane ran at 2.36x, another at 1.36x, and a third sat at 0.40x and got cut after 11 days. One blended campaign would have hidden that.

I work alongside a small team, including a dedicated tracking specialist for pixel and server-side setup, so the numbers we read are the numbers that actually happened.
