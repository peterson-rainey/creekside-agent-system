When a seller lead comes in, how long before your team knows it's real: when the appointment gets set, or when the contract gets signed?

I ask because that answer decides what Conversions API can actually teach Meta. Its lead quality optimization only learns from a CRM stage that happens within 28 days of the lead, needs around 200 Instant Form leads a month, and wants that stage converting somewhere between 1% and 40% of leads. A signed contract from a motivated seller often lands weeks or months after the form, so the stage worth sending back is usually the earliest one your acquisitions team trusts. At the $3K end of your range you may not reach 200 leads a month, and then the right setup is contract rate tracked in reporting while Meta optimizes on something earlier.

The closest work I've done is Meta lead gen for a reverse mortgage lender, aimed at older homeowners sitting on equity, under the same Special Ad Category restrictions housing gets: no ZIP targeting, a 15 mile minimum radius, age and gender locked, no lookalikes. Spend went from about $2K to $22K a month while I ran it. With targeting that locked down, the creative and the form questions end up doing the filtering, and that carries straight over to pre-foreclosure, inherited and tired landlord angles.

I haven't run a wholesaler or flip account myself, so I won't hand you a seller CPL or contract rate borrowed from another vertical. I'd set those targets off your deal margin and lead-to-contract rate once the first few weeks of data come in.
