"A full demo calendar and converted signups" is two targets, and the ad platforms cannot serve both at once. Whichever event you hand Meta or Google is the one it optimizes toward, and Demo Booked is the easy one to fill. You get a calendar and a bad show rate. The event worth bidding on is demo held and qualified, or activated signup, pushed back to both platforms as an offline conversion once your CRM knows the answer. Until that loop exists, cost per demo will look excellent while pipeline does not move.

Second thing I would fix before spending anything. The two brands run on opposite clocks. A B2B demo cycle resolves in 30 to 90 days. A digital print converts in the same session or never. If both sit under one budget conversation and one reporting cadence, the SaaS side loses every monthly review, because at day 30 it has spend and no closed revenue while the store has receipts. Separate scorecards, separate budgets, separate judgment windows, agreed before launch, or the pipeline work gets defunded a month before it would have paid.

Third, the store. Digital prints carry a low order value and no natural repeat purchase, which is the hardest shape to hold Purchase optimization on. A Meta ad set needs roughly 50 conversions a week to leave learning, and a $12 file will not hand that to five ad sets. So the first lever is order value, collections and bundles rather than single downloads, with email earning the second and third sale instead of paid buying it again.

On operating inside your tool. Our team runs paid acquisition for our own AI product, a per seat subscription with a free trial, so the trial to paid leak is an argument we have on our own money and not only a client's. No published results on it, so take that as context rather than a case study.

WORK I HAVE OWNED

Email from scratch, DTC. I built the Klaviyo flow set for a DTC ecommerce brand end to end: a four email welcome with purchase exclusion filters from email two onward, a three email abandoned checkout at one hour, twenty three hours and twenty four hours with dynamic line item blocks and a Placed Order equals zero filter, and a three email abandoned cart. The catch that mattered most was not a flow. Onsite tracking was returning zero Added to Cart events, so the cart flow would have fired on nobody. Same build underway on a bedding brand.

Paid social, DTC purchase funnel. A replacement parts store I ran on Meta for nine months: $128,518 spend, 4,440 purchases at $28.95 each, roughly 5.5x blended. Prospecting, retargeting and catalog ran as separate layers with purchasers excluded throughout. That engagement has ended.

B2B SaaS. Here I will be straight with you, because you would find it on a call anyway. Our book holds one seed stage B2B SaaS where Meta carried awareness and Google carried capture as a full funnel. I do not have a documented demo booking or converted signup number I would put in front of you. Our deepest evidenced results sit in lead generation and ecommerce. If another proposal shows you a clean SaaS demo gen case study, ask what the show rate was and what happened at month two.

WHAT I DO NOT HAVE

No photography, print or digital product brand in the book. No SEO or organic social service line, and no results in either I would defend. I write ad copy, email and landing copy. If blog volume and organic social are meant to be real channels rather than support for paid, that is a second seat, and I would rather say so now than discover it in month two.

FIRST 30 DAYS

Week one. Conversion audit on both brands. What actually fires, what is deduplicated, which event each platform is bidding on, and what happens to a signup after it becomes one. Klaviyo and Shopify reconciled against Ads Manager over the same window.

Week two. Build the two scorecards. Magikal gets cost per qualified demo held and cost per activated signup, with offline conversion feedback wired to both platforms. The store gets cost per purchase and order value, with flow revenue reported separately from campaign revenue.

Weeks three and four. Store first, because it can produce signal inside the window: welcome, cart and checkout flows live, then paid social against Purchase. Your generation tools write the email and ad creative for that build, which tests the flagship customer premise where a weak output costs one sale rather than one demo. The Magikal paid build launches against the corrected event, not before.

WHAT I NEED FROM YOU

Your combined monthly ad budget across both brands and roughly how it splits, and the countries the ads actually run in. We work on a percentage of ad spend rather than an hourly rate, so that number decides whether this is a fit at all. If combined spend lands under roughly $5,000 a month, a percentage will not clear a workable fee, and I would rather tell you that this week than three weeks in.
