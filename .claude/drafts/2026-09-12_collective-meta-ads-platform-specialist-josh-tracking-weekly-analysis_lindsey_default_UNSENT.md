When cost per result starts rising week over week, what gets checked first: CPM, or CTR and frequency? I ask because they point to opposite fixes. Rising CPM with steady CTR usually means the auction got pricier and the creative is fine. Falling CTR with climbing frequency means the audience is tired. Treat them as one problem and good ads get cut.

I saw this on a replacement parts store I ran Meta for. October and December spend were nearly identical, about $15,700 each. CPM rose 45% and cost per conversion rose 63%, but CTR went up and frequency dipped over the same months, so it wasn't fatigue. Most of it was auction pressure, and the gap between those two numbers was the account's own drift, which is where the real digging was. That's why I don't pull creative on cost alone in a stretch like that.

Tracking gets the same skepticism. On that account, campaigns optimized to shallow events like website visits and add to cart reported 7x to nearly 10x ROAS, while the broad purchase campaign carrying about half the budget reported about 5x. The flattering numbers lived where the money wasn't. On a dental account I ran, the health category put custom events off the table, so every conversion had to map to a standard event. Before trusting a weekly report, I check that reported conversions match real enrollments and that browser and server events aren't counted twice.

That's also how I'd review Josh's ideas: show him the data behind my answer, so the reasoning is his to reuse next time.
