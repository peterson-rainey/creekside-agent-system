Are all three brands running out of one ad account and one pixel right now, or does each have its own? I ask because B2B AI SaaS, B2C AI SaaS and an agency are three different learning problems, and when they share a pixel the highest-volume signal wins and quietly starves the other two.

The B2B brand is where I would start. I run a B2B SaaS account on Meta in our own book. Over the last twelve months it has spent just over $18,000 and produced three recorded conversions. The traffic is not the issue: 1.4% CTR, $2.28 CPC, $31.88 CPM. The account was asked to optimize toward a demo request that fires a handful of times a year, which is nowhere near what Meta needs to learn. No creative angle fixes that. It gets fixed by moving the optimization event up the funnel to something with real volume and letting sales work a warm list.

The B2C app is the opposite trap. There is enough volume that Meta will buy you the cheapest signal available, installs or signups, and fill the top of the funnel with people who never activate. The pattern I see repeatedly is a shallow event added to escape the learning phase and then never removed, so the account keeps buying the wrong person for months while the CPA looks fine in Ads Manager.

On the agency: my book is deep on consumer brands and small business owners. Selling paid media to other agency principals is not something I have run, and I would rather say that now. If that brand is the priority, someone else is the better hire. If it sits behind the two SaaS brands, I would run it off your own client list as customer-list and lookalike audiences, not interest targeting.

Two things I would want before quoting. What is combined monthly Meta spend across the three, closer to $10K or closer to $50K, and is it split evenly or is one brand carrying it? And for each brand, what is the one event you would judge me on?

Ten years in, the accounts that work are the ones where that answer matches what is actually set as the conversion event in the account. It usually does not.
