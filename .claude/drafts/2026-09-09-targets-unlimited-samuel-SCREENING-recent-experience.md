Most relevant first. Our team currently runs a survival gear ecommerce brand on both Google and Meta, which is the closest match to your catalog on both product mix and policy exposure. On the Google side, July spend was about $8.1K and August about $9.0K. Conversions moved from 141 to 269 across those two months and cost per conversion from $57.85 to $33.35, then eased back to $43.04 through the first nine days of September. The Meta side of that same account is newer and has only run a short flight so far, so I would call that early rather than settled.

Second, a Shopify brand whose Google Ads we run across 49 countries on roughly $6K to $9K a month, built on Shopping and Performance Max. Return on ad spend has held between 7.3x and 10.6x every month since April. It has trended down across that run rather than up, which is the accurate version of it.

On restricted categories specifically, we rebuilt a policy-constrained health account that had been running on duplicate conversion tracking and brand-only targeting. That is the same shape of work your optics SKUs will need, which is sorting what can run from what cannot before any money goes against it.

Between the three of us who would touch this account, the bench covers Google Shopping and feed structure, Meta creative and buying, and ecommerce reporting. Six years in, and no shooting sports account in that history. What transfers is feed architecture and restricted-category account risk rather than category familiarity.
