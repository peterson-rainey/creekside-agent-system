## PROPOSAL

When paid social flattens in fragrance, it's usually one of two problems, and they need opposite fixes. If frequency is climbing on prospecting while click-through falls, the creative is wearing out on the same people. If frequency is flat but cost per purchase keeps rising, the ad sets are often overlapping and competing for the same buyers, or budget has drifted toward retargeting, which reports a strong ROAS while quietly stopping growth. The first thing I'd do is work out which one this account has, ad set by ad set, before rebuilding anything.

That split matters more than most brands expect. On a beauty-tech account our team ran, a camera with a built-in vanity mirror, the brand spent $58,298 over about four and a half months for 623 purchases, and only about one sale in seven came from retargeting. The warm pool ran out fast, so prospecting creative had to carry the account. Purchase-optimized campaigns there also paid under $60 a sale, while add-to-cart and checkout campaigns over the same weeks paid more than double. It wasn't a controlled test, but it's why I check what each campaign is optimizing for before moving budget.

On structure, another ecom account we took over was running 232 live ads on about $15K a month. We cut the campaigns with spend from eleven to seven. Cost per conversion went from $34.72 the month before we started to $19.89 by April at a roughly flat CPM, then $22.91 in May. Targeting and creative changed in the same audit, so I won't credit one lever, but fewer, cleaner ad sets made the winners much easier to read.

Knowing which message sells is mostly bookkeeping. Every ad gets tagged by the reason to buy it's selling: the scent itself, an occasion, gifting, or the brand story. New concepts launch in batches in their own test campaign instead of being dripped into winning ad sets, because adding an ad restarts that ad set's learning. After two or three cycles you can read purchases by angle rather than by ad, so a winning message survives even when its first visual tires. Heading into gifting season, that's worth having in place before the holiday auctions get crowded.
