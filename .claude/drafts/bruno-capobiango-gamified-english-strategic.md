Bruno,

Most subscription accounts are optimized against a blended acquisition cost that is really two very different numbers averaged together, and the average flatters the expensive half.

Concrete version from an account our team runs now. It is Google Search and Shopping, not app inventory, so treat it as the shape of the problem rather than your channel mix. Over the last 180 days branded search returned 492 conversions at $16.99 each. The unbranded campaigns, the ones actually reaching people who did not already know the brand, returned 257 at about $125. The account's blended figure is roughly $55. Nobody acquires a customer for $55 there. If that blended number were defended under pressure, the cuts would land on the only campaigns bringing in people who had never heard of the brand.

The same averaging problem sits one layer deeper on a subscription product, in the event you optimize toward. Signup fires often enough to train the algorithm quickly, so most accounts settle there, and it also fires for everyone who opens the product once and never returns. In the accounts we have seen, the event that actually correlates with retained revenue is a specific in-product milestone rather than the signup itself. Which is why the tracking work comes before the campaign structure, not after it.

If the product is app-first, this gets harder rather than easier: SKAN and ATT limit what event data reaches Meta at all, so the activation signal usually has to be modeled rather than passed through raw. That constraint shapes the campaign structure, so it is worth settling early.

So the first thing I would want to see is your event map and your paid-to-retained curve by cohort.

Straight with you on fit: we have subscription, e-commerce and lead generation work across our three operator books, but no gamified learning product. Different vertical, same growth mechanics, and better you know that now than on the call.
