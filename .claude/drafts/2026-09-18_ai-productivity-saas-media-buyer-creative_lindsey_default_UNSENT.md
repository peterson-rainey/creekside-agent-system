When someone signs up for the app and later starts paying, does Meta ever hear about the second part, or does the ad account only see the signup?

I ask because on a free-to-paid product, that one event decides who the algorithm goes looking for. If it only learns from signups, it finds the people who sign up fastest, and those are rarely the ones who pay. Cost per signup looks great while cost per paying subscriber quietly climbs. Running my own e-commerce business taught me the cheapest conversion on the dashboard and the most valuable customer are rarely the same person. So week one is tracking before ads: signup and first paid subscription both firing server side, so every creative gets judged on paying users.

On creative, a lot of my work is consumer brands selling to a young, very online audience. For one consumer camera brand I split angles like selfie and nostalgia into their own campaigns, and the account spent $58,298 for 623 purchases, about $94 each. For a B2B SaaS platform I ran Meta while our team ran Google to capture the search demand, and inbound leads and ARR doubled within six months across both. Right now I run a Meta account spending around $100K a month.

On scope: I run Meta and the creative strategy myself. Google and YouTube sit with our team's Google specialist, who runs Search, Performance Max and Demand Gen, the campaign type that serves on YouTube. TikTok isn't a platform we run, and I haven't shipped AI-generated video ads for a client, so I won't send samples dressed up as that. On $1,200 I'd still prove Meta first, then bring in Google once there's a winning angle worth carrying over.
