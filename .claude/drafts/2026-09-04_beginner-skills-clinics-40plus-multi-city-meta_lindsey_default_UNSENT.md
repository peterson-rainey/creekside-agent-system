# Beginner skills clinics (adults 40+), multi-city Meta -- strategic consultation

Run the arithmetic first. Two to three weeks at $40 to $100 a day is $560 to $2,100 of media per event, against $1,600 to $4,800 of revenue at eight to sixteen seats. Your worst case spend against a half full room is underwater before anyone touches the account. That one purchase at $361 is not a media buying failure, it is that box closing. Any structure worth recommending has to survive the math, which is why my answers push toward fewer, better fed campaigns.

**1. The learning phase, and what I optimize for.**

The 50 conversions is per ad set, per optimization event, on a rolling seven days. That decides your structure. One ad set per event means four concurrent events are four separate failures to reach it, not one account that misses it. At twenty events you would generate roughly 160 to 320 purchases a month, enough to optimize on. It is just cut into twenty pieces that never teach each other anything.

So I consolidate. City becomes targeting inside a shared structure, not a campaign boundary, so the pixel builds one durable picture of who buys a beginner clinic instead of twenty thin ones that reset. Then I move the bid event up the funnel to something firing ten to twenty times more often than a purchase, and keep purchase as the number I am judged on rather than the one I bid on. The fatal habit at your volume is letting one metric be both the target and the evidence.

**2. A local business across multiple markets.**

The gap first. I do not have a ticketed, fixed date, multi city event business in my book. If that exact pattern is the requirement, weigh me accordingly.

What I do have is your structure problem, live and mine. I run Meta for a prepared meals company selling into a radius around each location, and over nine months took it from one market to five: Dallas, then Plano, Frisco, Denton and McKinney, launched about a month apart, one campaign per market.

The part worth your attention is that the newest, coldest markets produce the best click metrics. McKinney runs about 1.73% click through at $1.13 a click, Denton 1.58% at $0.82. Dallas, the market that carries the revenue, runs 0.87% at $1.84. The cheapest, best looking traffic sits where there is the least proof. Judging expansion on clicks would have moved budget exactly the wrong way. Dallas has absorbed around $16,400 of media against roughly $1,400 in McKinney, and that concentration is deliberate.

What I would do differently, and would do here from day one: consolidate far earlier than feels comfortable, and never let a new market open as its own isolated campaign. Keeping markets separate so the reporting stays readable is the thing that starves them.

**3. A brand new city, first 72 hours.**

Not selling seats. Audience discovery.

Launch into the consolidated structure so the city inherits what the pixel already knows. Go broader than feels right rather than stacking a tight venue radius on a cold market, because narrow geography plus a small budget manufactures the $361 outliers. Hold creative constant against what already works so the market is the only new variable. Bid on the upper funnel event.

Then judge on what has volume inside 72 hours: cost per landing page view, and the rate those visitors reach checkout, benchmarked against your own norms. Purchases in the first three days are close to meaningless, and acting on them is how workable markets get killed. A market is failing when reach is cheap and the checkout rate sits well below baseline, and merely thin when the checkout rate is normal and volume is low.

I would also launch earlier than two to three weeks. The first days are learning, which leaves little selling time, and a 45 year old weighing a $250 Saturday is not an impulse buyer. Four to six weeks fits that decision better.

**Where your problem actually is.**

Your ad metrics are not your problem, and I would rather say that now than bill you to discover it. Strong click through and $0.38 clicks landing on a page with 21% scroll depth and 3% reaching checkout is the signature of an offer and a page not carrying what the ad promised. Media buying changes who arrives, it cannot fix what happens to them. For a 40 plus first timer the missing ingredient is rarely urgency, it is knowing who else like them is in the room. I expect the order to be offer, page, structure, media.
