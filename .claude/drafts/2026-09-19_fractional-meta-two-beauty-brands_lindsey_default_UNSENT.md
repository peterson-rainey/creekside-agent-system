Are both brands running out of the same Meta business, with a shared pixel or overlapping audiences? I ask because two premium beauty brands usually chase the same buyer, and if their prospecting isn't separated, they end up bidding against each other in the same auction. Each brand's CPA then looks worse than it really is, and that's the first thing I'd check before judging either account's ROAS.

Tracking comes next, because it decides what Meta learns from. On a consumer camera brand I ran, selling to a young, very online audience, the purchase-optimized campaigns paid under $60 a sale, while the add-to-cart and checkout campaigns over the same weeks paid more than double. It wasn't a controlled test, but it's why I audit the Pixel, CAPI and Shopify events before touching budgets.

Creative fatigue is often a structure problem first. I took over an ecom account running 232 live ads on about $15K a month and cut it from eleven campaigns with spend down to seven. Cost per conversion went from $34.72 in the month before I started to $19.89 by April and $22.91 in May, at about the same CPM. I changed targeting and creative in that same audit, so I can't credit one lever, but fewer, cleaner tests made it much easier to see which angles were actually winning.

For beauty specifically, some of the hooks that feel strongest are the ones Meta rejects. Copy that points at a flaw in someone's skin or appearance gets flagged, so the angles that scale lean on the result, the ritual, or the founder's reason for making the product. Having built and sold my own ecommerce business, I look at that the way an owner does: why someone buys, not just what the click cost.
