Before the Ad Packs, one question decides more than the packs do. Which event are you sending back to Meta as the conversion, the registration or the purchase?

I ask because the funnel you described is where I have watched this go wrong most often. Point the campaign at the registration and Meta does exactly what it was told. It gets very good at finding people who will register for a free thing and never show up, and cost per registration reads beautifully while cost per customer quietly climbs. It gets diagnosed as a creative problem or a targeting problem when it is neither.

I have the account data that made me believe this rather than just repeat it. On a replacement parts brand I ran on Meta, we had separate campaigns optimized to shallow events sitting next to the broad purchase campaign. Website Visits returned 10.26x, Add to Cart 7.28x, View Content 5.22x. The broad purchase campaign returned 4.99x and carried more than half the account spend. Every flattering number lived where the money was not. That account ran about $131,000 across ten months at roughly 5.5x blended on 4,500 orders, and it has since ended.

On your first question, the Ad Pack is the right unit for the idea, but one ad set holding six creatives does not give six creatives a fair test. Delivery concentrates on whatever wins the first few hundred impressions and the rest never gather enough conversions to be judged. What I would run instead is a separate testing surface with its own budget, so a new creative cannot eat spend that is already producing, and a consolidated scaling campaign that winners graduate into and that does not get reset unless fatigue is actually showing. Judge the pack, not the creative. With one idea per pack the pack is the experiment, and a weak creative inside a winning angle is a production problem.

On the second, I set up and troubleshoot Pixel, CAPI, standard events, UTMs and attribution windows on the accounts I run, and I keep pixel verification as a standing weekly item because breaks show up there about a week before they show up in cost per lead. The failure I find most is both the browser and the server feed sending the same event without a shared event ID, so nothing deduplicates, Meta counts close to double, and the account spends a month learning from people who were never there. On a dental account I ran inside a restricted category, the working judgment was which standard events were permitted at all, since custom events were not. Straight with you on the limits. Deep server side builds we do alongside a specialist rather than owning that stack end to end, and we have no course or masterclass account of my own to point at.

ChatGPT Ads, yes, and one of the three accounts our book runs there is mine. Since June 7, $1,626 spent, a $52.39 CPM, $3.69 CPC and 1.42% CTR, on dental and one experience brand, so order of magnitude only. The honest part is the part most bidders will not tell you. Zero conversions have reported back and every campaign has run on click bidding, so I would not judge it against Meta on CAC. I would fund it as a fixed carve and read it through UTMs and downstream revenue.

One flag on the 30 days. It is enough to prove the tracking and find the angle, not enough to read CAC, because week four registrations have not finished buying yet.

Two things so I can size this. Where are the ads running, and roughly what monthly range are you considering for spend?

There is a short video on my profile covering how I work an account like this one.
