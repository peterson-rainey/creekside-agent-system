Do you know how many of your preorders end up refunded or cancelled before the product ships? I ask because that number decides what the 1% lookalike learns from. If refunds stay in the seed list, Meta goes looking for more people who back out, and on a launch those are usually the least committed buyers you have.

The value ladder has the same problem. When an email signup, a demo and a preorder all reach Meta with loose values, the algorithm chases whatever it can get the most of, and on a preorder funnel that is almost always signups. The gap between the values matters more than the dollar amounts. I'd write that spec with your instrumentation contractor so every event fires with the value we agreed on.

I ran Meta for a consumer camera brand, a physical product in a price range where people think before they buy. Over the same three weeks, warm retargeting came in at $87.61 per purchase and cold prospecting at $102.14. That gap was smaller than most people expect, and the warm pool ran thin fast. On a preorder with a long consideration window, retargeting can't carry the account, so the fallback audience plan has to be ready before the lookalike is. I'd step down by intent: preorder add to cart first, then demo bookings, then engaged signups, each tested on its own.

I'd also check early on whether your preorder checkout sits on a separate domain or platform. When it does, GA4 and Meta disagree for structural reasons, and that has to be mapped before any conversion number is trusted.

To be straight with you: our team hasn't managed a preorder or crowdfunding launch. The pieces here (thin seed audiences, value signals, retargeting on a long decision) are ones we work through constantly.
