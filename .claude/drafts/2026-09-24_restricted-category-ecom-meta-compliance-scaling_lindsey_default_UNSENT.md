Quick question before anything else: when Meta pushes back, is it landing on individual ads, or on the ad account and Page themselves? Those run as two separate systems, and clearing one does not carry across to the other. An ad can come back approved on appeal while the account keeps a standing mark against it, which is usually why a store gets a clean week and then a delivery drop it cannot explain.

The reason I ask is that the restricted work in my book is cosmetic and medical aesthetics, not supplements, CBD, alcohol or weight management. The largest Meta account I run is a cosmetic practice, just under $1.19M in the last twelve months and still running, sitting between $90K and $115K in a month. Copy there lives under Meta's health and cosmetic-procedure rules: 18+ gating, tight limits on before-and-after imagery, and no promising a result inside a timeframe. Most of what gets pulled there starts on the landing page rather than the ad.

On your third question, the straight answer is that I have not had a restricted account reinstated. What I have is the prevention side, keeping accounts inside review rather than recovering them after. If getting a currently restricted asset back is the first job, that is worth knowing now.

The ecom half is the rest of it. Shopify with pixel and CAPI both firing is standard for me, and the first thing I look at is deduplication, because double-counted purchases make an account look healthier than it is. On a parts store I ran, shallow retargeting on add-to-cart and time-on-site cost $31 to $36 a conversion against $22 to $28 on the purchase-optimized campaigns. On a meal prep brand I run, new-customer campaigns exclude every past purchaser so the cold number stays a cold number.
