## PROPOSAL (paste-ready)

When one Google Ads setup is running both B2B lead generation and ecommerce, the first thing worth checking is what each campaign is allowed to count as a conversion. If purchases and form fills share the same account-default goals, a campaign bidding for conversions treats a sale and a contact form as the same win, and spend drifts toward whichever is cheaper to get.

B2B adds a second layer. A form fill is not a buyer. Unless qualified leads or closed deals come back from the CRM, Smart Bidding learns to find people who fill in forms, which in B2B often means students, job seekers, vendors and companies too small to buy. Cost per lead can improve inside the platform while the pipeline stays flat.

Ecommerce has its own version, usually an account-wide ROAS or cost per sale that blends brand and non-brand together. On a Shopify electronics brand we built and ran on Google, branded search came in at $16.90 per purchase and non-branded search at $104.25. The two ran side by side for about ten months on nearly the same spend. A single account-level number would have hidden a sixfold gap between searches for the brand name and everything else.

In lead gen, the choice of metric can flip the answer. On a commercial paving account our team runs, selling to property managers and general contractors, Search and Performance Max ran in the same city over the same dates at similar spend. Performance Max clicks cost about a fifth as much, and its leads cost about 2.2 times more, $289 each against $131. Judged on clicks, PMax wins. Judged on leads, Search does.

Often the fix is in the tracking, not the campaigns. On a luxury mattress brand our team runs Google for, five Google Shopping app micro-events had been set as primary conversions, and begin checkout alone had logged $214 million in conversion value. Primary actions are what Smart Bidding optimizes toward, so those moved to secondary and the purchase became the only primary action.

With your account, the first pass is the goals and primary actions behind every campaign. Then results get split by brand and non-brand, lead gen and ecommerce, and new versus returning customers, and lined up against CRM records and actual store orders. Whatever gap shows up there decides what gets tested first.

On the B2B side we have also run a software company selling to service businesses, where Meta built awareness for a category those buyers did not know existed and Google captured the searches that followed. We work as a small agency team rather than a single hire, so a Google Ads specialist on our team would run the account day to day, a Meta specialist would handle paid social, and I stay on strategy.

Two questions. Do qualified leads or closed deals make it back into Google Ads from your CRM today, or does bidding stop at the form fill? And since the right first move at $8K a month is different from the right one at $40K, roughly where does monthly spend sit across the B2B and ecommerce sides, under $10K, $10K to $30K, or above $30K?
