A weekly investments webinar and a private meeting in your office are two different offers, and running both through one campaign is where most of the budget quietly goes. Meta will find the cheaper conversion, which is always the registration, so the webinar fills while the meeting calendar stays thin.

The weekly cadence creates a second problem that is easy to miss. If every session gets its own campaign named for that week's date, the account restarts learning every seven days and never builds enough conversion history to optimize on. An evergreen registration campaign usually beats it, with the date handled on the landing page and in the creative rather than in the account structure.

The number that decides this is show rate, and it gets watched too late. Meta optimizes toward whatever event you send back to it, so if that event is the registration, it gets very good at finding people who register and never attend. Registration, attendance and booked meeting need to come back as three separate events, then you optimize on the last one. Cost per registration is easy to move. Cost per client is the one that matters.

Worth flagging before any budget goes live: your site leads with a 34.88% three year CAGR. Return claims are the most common rejection reason in this category, and review covers the landing page, not just the ad. The strongest hook you already own is probably the one that will not clear, so the creative has to carry the education instead.
