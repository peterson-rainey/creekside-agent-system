Most VSL-to-application funnels that book calls but bleed CAC are optimizing to the wrong event. Meta gets pointed at the application submit, because that is the last thing that fires on your side, so the system goes and finds people who reliably fill out applications. Cost per application holds flat while qualified rate quietly falls, and the damage surfaces two steps later at the sales call. The second thing worth checking before anyone touches creative is whether budget has collapsed onto one angle. On a coaching certification account our team runs now, prospecting is split so each angle holds its own campaign and budget cannot drift onto the fastest converter, which is usually the one pulling the least qualified applicant.

Where our work sits, honestly: we manage Google and Meta media. Campaign structure, tracking, creative angles, retargeting and the qualification logic feeding your CRM are squarely what we do. VSL scripting and landing page design are not services we sell, and we hold no published conversion-rate before-and-after, so treat our input on the page and the video as message-match and offer diagnosis, not CRO work with a track record behind it.

**1. Closest funnel we have managed**

Two, and neither is a complete match, so here is the honest split.

Closest on shape: a high-ticket coaching certification, Meta only, running today. Recurring weekly webinar into registration, then an application, then an enrollment call. Angle-split prospecting, scheduled activation and intraday deactivation around each webinar date. Cost per registration there has degraded materially over the past two quarters on flat spend, which is the decay pattern your audit is looking for, so I would rather describe the mechanics than quote a better number from an earlier window.

Closest on booked-call volume with real figures: a dental implant practice, Meta forms into a call center, 21 April to 15 July 2026. $64,746.78 spend, 2,558 qualified leads, $25.31 cost per lead. Instructive for a different reason, covered in question 4.

What we cannot give you: show rate, customers closed and CAC are not in our reporting on either account. Revenue closes in the client's CRM and is not passed back to the platform, so we manage to cost per qualified lead and the downstream numbers stay with the client. If in-funnel CAC evidence is your bar, we do not clear it.

**2. First metrics before recommending anything**

Cost per application and cost per qualified booked call side by side, over 90 days, weekly. If those two lines diverge, the problem is targeting or qualification, not creative or budget. Then: application-to-qualified rate by campaign and angle, VSL watch depth against application rate, booked-to-held rate, spend concentration across ad sets, frequency by audience, and what event the pixel actually optimizes versus what you count as a real lead. I also want the sales team's own read on lead quality by week, because it usually contradicts the dashboard and the contradiction is the finding.

**3. Relevant funnel**

Our own acquisition funnel, built August 2026: a six-question qualification form that routes applicants by answer, an SSR booking page, and a post-booking VSL page, with click IDs written to the CRM record at submit rather than held in session, plus pre-call warm-up and no-show recovery sequences. Stated plainly, that is our own property, not a client engagement, and it carries no published performance results. Two failure modes from building it are worth your time: duplicate contact merging can overwrite a stored click ID, and third-party booking tools drop the parameter at handoff.

**4. Why funnels book calls and still produce bad CAC**

Optimizing to the application rather than a qualified-call event fed back to Meta. Budget concentrating on the angle with the loosest qualification. Retargeting taking credit for demand prospecting already paid for, which makes blended CAC look fine while incremental cost climbs. Qualification thresholds set to protect volume rather than the calendar. And the one people miss most: sales capacity, not media. On the dental account above we pulled spend back while cost per lead was still $25, because the call centre could not work the volume. Leads getting stale in a queue looks identical to a media problem in the dashboard and is not one.
