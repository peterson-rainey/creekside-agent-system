When we agree those performance targets, will they sit on blended ROAS, or on what it costs to win a customer who has never bought from you?

I ask because on a repeat-purchase product those two numbers drift apart fast. Returning buyers convert cheaply, broad and Advantage+ campaigns will keep spending on them unless existing customers are defined and held back, and at your budget that slice can pass for growth for a long time. On a meal-prep brand I run, I set up new-customer campaigns to exclude past purchasers for that reason, so the cold number stays a cold number.

Supplement case studies: I have not run a supplement brand, and I won't dress another category up as one. Closest I have: an online replacement-parts brand selling across the contiguous US, average order around $160.
- Starting point: February 2026, spend spread across 14 campaigns, several of them thin retargeting on shallow events like add to cart and time on site. Cost per conversion $31.75.
- What I changed: cut to 7 campaigns and folded that retargeting into a single purchase-optimized campaign.
- Result: $19.89 in April, then $22.91 in May. CPM also ran about 10% lower in those months than the six before, so not all of that was the restructure. It spent about $13K a month and has since ended.

For scale, the largest Meta account I run spends roughly $100K a month, a cosmetic dental practice, so lead gen rather than DTC.
