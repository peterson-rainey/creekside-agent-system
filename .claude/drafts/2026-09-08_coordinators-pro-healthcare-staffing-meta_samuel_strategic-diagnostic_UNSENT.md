Worth knowing before anyone quotes you a plan: the audience you are buying is one of the most expensive on Meta, and the reason is structural rather than fixable. Meta has never exposed employer or job title as a targeting input, in any category. Practice owners, medical directors and office managers are not natively addressable there. Every account selling into that audience runs on interest proxies, behavioral signals, and lookalikes built off its own converter list, which means the quality of that seed list does more work than any targeting setting will.

That shows up in the price. Across the thirty Meta accounts we run, the book averages a $26.68 CPM. The account in our book that most resembles yours has run at $47.62 lifetime and $53.92 over the last ninety days, so roughly double. That gap, not the targeting menu, is what governs what a budget can buy here, and it is the number I would want any plan built around.

That account is MedWriter, an AI assistant sold to solo practitioners and group practices on the promise of saving hours of daily admin work. Same buyer as yours, close to the same pitch. Our team has run it continuously for twelve months on about $10K a month, $117,895 in spend, and it is live today.

Practically, that says where the leverage is. If targeting cannot find practice owners on its own, the two levers left are the seed list and creative volume. The practices you have already placed coordinators with are the strongest lookalike source you own and are usually the most underused asset in an account like this. Testing cadence tends to move more than another round of interest stacking.

The second piece is the return leg into GoHighLevel. Meta optimizes toward whatever you report back to it, so if the pixel and CAPI stop at form submitted, the system gets very good at finding people who submit forms. The signal that matters to you is the practice that actually signs. Pushing GHL stage changes back as offline conversions is what closes that loop, and on an audience this expensive it is closer to essential than nice to have.

One thing to confirm early, because it is specific to staffing and it is quietly the largest risk in the account. Meta's employment rules reach employment agency services, not only ads that post a job, so the category can attach to what a business is rather than to how an ad is worded. The reasonable pushback is that you do not post jobs, and that may not settle it. Inside that category you lose lookalikes entirely, geo snaps to a fifteen mile minimum radius with no ZIP precision, and age locks to a broad band. Losing lookalikes is the real damage, since that is the one scalable route to your buyer. Checking which box your campaigns actually sit in is item five on our standard Meta audit.

On timing, since you are hiring in September. Across the accounts we run, Meta CPM went from $18.84 in September to $32.41 in December last year, up about 72 percent into Q4. Whatever the plan is, it should be built knowing media gets more expensive right as it launches.

Being straight about fit. In healthcare we also run patient acquisition at $30.65 and $97.39 per lead, though reaching patients is a different motion from selling into practices and I would not stretch it. What we do not have is a staffing agency in the book.

One thing on structure, because it speaks to what you wrote. The failure you are describing, where hands-on work gets boundaried away, is usually manufactured by the pricing model rather than by the person. When the fee is a bucket of hours, every access issue and integration fix competes with strategy for the same bucket, and the incentive to call something out of scope is built in. We price monthly as a percentage of ad spend, so nothing meters against a Business Manager permissions problem and there is no hour budget to protect. I would rather size that against your real spend than quote at a number blind, which is why the question below matters. The responsiveness you are asking for is a service level and belongs in writing either way.

A first month would be a Business Manager ownership and permissions audit so the assets are provably yours, a check on which special ad category your campaigns are declared under, a pixel and CAPI event review against what you count as a real lead, and a GHL stage mapping so Meta learns from closed business instead of form fills.

Two things would sharpen this. What monthly range are you spending on Meta right now, and once a practice raises a hand, who does the follow up and how fast does it happen?
