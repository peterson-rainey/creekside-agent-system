A luxury resort in Brazil selling to U.S. travelers has a measurement problem before it has an ads problem. The booking usually lands weeks after the first ad, and Meta's standard attribution counts a click for 7 days at most, so the campaign that started the trip often gets no credit for the booking. Judge Meta on in-platform bookings alone and it is easy to cut what is working.

**Where the booking actually gets recorded**

Most resorts take bookings through a third party booking engine on a separate domain. Unless tracking follows the guest across that handoff, Google and Meta see the click, lose the booking, and optimize toward the wrong people. I would check that before any campaign goes live.

The second is your own name on Google. Online travel agencies can bid on a resort's brand name, so a guest who has already chosen you can still book through an OTA and cost you the commission. A small brand campaign is usually worth running for that alone.

**How the two platforms split the work**

Meta is where a U.S. traveler first sees the place, and that is where your photo and video earn their keep. Google catches the people already searching for the resort or the region. Retargeting keeps you in front of them through the planning window. While full bookings build up, inquiries and booking engine starts are the early signal.

**What our book does and does not cover**

No hotel or resort account on our book yet, worth saying up front. The closest travel account our team ran was a US luggage storage booking service on Google: 1,050 bookings at $2.44 each across 30+ cities. That is a cheap, same day decision, so the cost does not transfer, but the tracking discipline does. A closer match for a high ticket, considered purchase is a high end furniture brand we ran on Meta: about $105,000 over four months at roughly $306 per purchase, now a past client.

**On testing the partnership**

Because bookings lag the ad, a 30 day test mostly measures the lag. Ninety days is when the numbers start to mean something.

**Three questions**

Which booking engine do you use, and is it on your own domain?

What does a typical stay cost, and how far ahead do U.S. guests usually book?

Beyond the U.S., which markets matter most to you?

Once I have those, I can tell you whether to lead with Meta or Google, or fix tracking first.
