## Proposal body

Construction search traffic looks bigger than it actually is. A chunk of the clicks on builder and contractor terms come from people pricing DIY kits, comparing financing, or researching plans, not people ready to sign with a builder. Volume in the account looks healthy while the leads coming out the other end are a mixed bag, and that gap is usually structural rather than a bidding problem.

We ran into this directly with a custom home and barndominium builder in Middle Tennessee, on Google Search. The barndominium side pulled heavy volume, but most of it was people searching barndominium loans, pre-built kits, and DIY plans. We split that into its own campaign with its own budget and keyword set, used exact match for full length terms and phrase match for shorter roots, and kept a running negative list cutting the loan and DIY searches out. Leads went from 15 to 60 and cost per lead dropped from $454 to $239.

On Local Services, we ran LSA for a home services client in Tennessee that produced over 230 phone leads and over 70 message leads across 12 months alongside their Google Ads campaigns, and we have an active LSA account running now. The setup carries more weight than most people expect. The COI has to name the exact legal entity, the Google Business Profile category and job types have to match what you actually want to be called for, service areas are worth setting at the zip level rather than a radius, and the dispute window is there for leads that clearly do not match. Skip those and budget leaks into calls you cannot use.

Worth knowing going in, construction clicks are not cheap. We manage a paving contractor whose main Search campaign has run $17.37 CPC over the last 12 months. In their strongest 12 day stretch they hit close to 3 leads a day against a goal of 1, with cost per lead down 31 percent, from $185 to $127.

That same account is the clearest example of why click price is the wrong thing to watch. A Search campaign and a Performance Max campaign ran over the same four months at nearly the same spend. Search came in at $131 cost per lead on a $14.53 CPC. Performance Max came in at $268 cost per lead on a $3.00 CPC, roughly a fifth of the click cost. Cheap clicks and cheap leads are not the same thing, and on an account where a single click runs north of $17, that difference decides the budget.
