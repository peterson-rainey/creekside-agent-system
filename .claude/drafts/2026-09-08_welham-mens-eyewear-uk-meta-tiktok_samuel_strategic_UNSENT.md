## PROPOSAL (paste-ready)

The account you take into Black Friday is won or lost in September, not November. Q4 is when Meta auction costs peak, so it is the worst month of the year to still be figuring out which creative and which audiences actually work. A young account that reaches BF still testing pays for that education at the highest CPMs of the year, with no runway left to act on what it learns. The real deadline is mid October: an account whose winners are already proven and just need budget.

On a young account the first constraint is almost never targeting, it is signal. Meta wants roughly 50 conversions a week per ad set to leave the learning phase, and a store with thin pixel history rarely gets there optimizing straight to purchase, so early on you either consolidate or optimize to a shallower event and move up once the data is real. Here is that pattern from an account our team ran, a brand launched cold into the same Instagram fashion and lifestyle audience yours lives in. Month one, cost per purchase sat around $120. Month two, $68, once the account had signal and enough creative in rotation to hold frequency down. We pushed spend about 70 percent and CPA snapped back to $116, not from targeting, from running out of fresh creative while CPMs climbed. That is the real ceiling on scale for a brand like yours, and it is why the buyer and the creative team cannot work in separate rooms. Our job is to tell your strategist which angle is holding CPA at volume and which one won a test and then died at scale.

Structure follows the same logic. Prospecting and retargeting get separate budgets and separate scaling rules, and on a young account retargeting is a thin slice, on that brand it held about 1.8x on roughly a tenth of the spend, so the account has to earn its keep on cold economics first.

Premium is where this gets sharp. We scaled a design-led premium brand on Meta from about $17K a month to past $40K over four months. Purchases grew, so on the dashboard it read like clean scaling. Underneath, CPM roughly doubled, from the mid thirties to near $80, and cost per purchase drifted from about $247 to $313. Nothing was broken. The audience was saturating faster than we could ship new angles. A premium margin can absorb a higher CPM, but only if the prospecting pool and the creative throughput keep pace, and knowing whether it is creative or auction moving your number is most of the job. That is the difference between scaling horizontally and vertically, and being able to say why before you touch the budget.

None of it counts if the numbers are wrong. Before we scale we confirm the pixel and Conversions API are both firing, deduplication is clean, and event match quality is high enough to trust, then set attribution windows on purpose rather than taking the default.

The buyer who would own your account is Lindsey, 10-plus years on ecommerce paid social, and she personally ran every account above. Not agency overflow, the same hands on the account.

Straight with you on TikTok, since you listed it firm. Meta is where our depth and our numbers are. We run TikTok as the second channel, and what carries over is exactly what matters here, the testing discipline and the signal quality, because TikTok burns creative faster than Meta, not slower. If you need a proven day one TikTok track record rather than the second channel built on the same method, better you hear that from us now than in week two.

Rather than blurred screenshots, we would put Ads Manager on a screen share and let you pull any date range yourself, which is also how the weekly read works: what happened, what it cost, and what changes next. On rate, we bill a percentage of ad spend, not hourly and not a flat retainer, so our incentive tracks yours: 20 percent to start, dropping to 15 then 10 as monthly spend scales, plus a one-time onboarding that covers the audit and the 30-day plan. At £10K a month that is £2,000. Is the £5K to £20K where you are now, or the ceiling you are scaling toward?
