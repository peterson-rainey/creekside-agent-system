Before the seven answers, one thing about the competitor evidence, because it changes where I would start.

The Meta Ad Library is not a neutral sample. Outside the UK and EU it only reliably surfaces ads that are currently active, and expired ones drop out of view unless you run the per-country filters rather than the default search. So the competitor set you are looking at is made of survivors. The ads that ran the same aggressive angle and got pulled are largely invisible to you. The Library will also tell you when an advertiser was actually caught: a card that reads as later disabled for not following Advertising Standards is an enforcement record, not a win. Pulling those is free, and it usually turns "why are they allowed and we are not" into "which of them are actually surviving, and for how long."

That matters because the second thing worth saying plainly is that ad-level review and advertiser standing are two separate systems. Getting a rejected ad overturned on appeal does not clear an account-level restriction, and clearing a restriction does not make previously rejected creative approvable. Teams burn months because they fix one and assume it carries to the other. Your symptom list, previously approvable creative now rejected plus compliant creative approved but converting worse, reads like two different problems stacked, and they need to be separated before any creative work is worth doing.

Now the questions, straight.

1. Restricted categories we have worked in: financial services on Meta (reverse mortgage lead generation, retirement and estate planning, prop trading education), medical aesthetics and cosmetic procedures, peptides and supplements, and naturopathic health on the Google side.

2. Sports betting, sports picks, handicapping, iGaming, fantasy: no. We have not run a gambling or picks account, and I am not going to dress up an adjacent vertical as one. The closest real analogue in our book is financial, which your post lists as qualifying. A trading education account in our book did $4,642.81 in eight days across four campaigns, and the US prospecting campaign came in at $17.25 per conversion on $1,587.40 while the UK version of the same offer came in at $179.22 per conversion on $2,150.63. Same creative family, same operator, roughly a ten times spread by market. On the larger end, a reverse mortgage lead account ran $139,913.88 over 309 days.

3. High rejection rates: the approval-constrained work in our book is medical aesthetics, where what gets an ad through turns on clause-level detail rather than tone. Age gating at eighteen plus, before-and-after imagery being permitted only under that gate, and the outright ban on promising a result within a timeframe. In our experience, what moves approval rates there is not softer copy. It is identifying which specific clause a rejection is keyed to, then rebuilding the claim so it says the same commercial thing without triggering it. I would expect your category to behave the same way, at a different set of clauses.

4. Gambling and gaming authorization: no, we have not been through Meta's gaming authorization process. If your category requires it, that is a gap on my side and you should weigh it. What I would do is establish first whether Profit Beasts actually falls inside it, because a paid picks and community business is not automatically classified as real money gaming, and being classified into the wrong bucket would by itself produce exactly the rejection pattern you describe.
