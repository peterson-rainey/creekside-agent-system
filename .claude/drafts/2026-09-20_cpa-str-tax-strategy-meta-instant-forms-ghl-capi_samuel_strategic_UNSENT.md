## PROPOSAL

Sending Qualified and Closed back through Conversions API is the right call, but Meta can't optimize toward a stage it rarely sees. Its conversion leads goal only runs on instant forms and needs at least 200 leads a month, with the optimized stage converting between 1% and 40% within 28 days. So month one optimizes to the lead while your qualifying questions screen, then switches to Qualified once volume clears that bar. Closed with value gets sent from day one, but early on it's too rare to steer delivery.

On the Special Ad Category, Meta's own examples for financial products and services are insurance, bank accounts, investment services and payment services. CPA tax strategy isn't on that list, so I'd agree it likely doesn't apply. The copy decides it as much as the offer, though. Pitch the rental as an investment and you drift toward investment services, so the copy stays on the tax outcome.

With income off the table, the form filters and the hook targets. A line about what a W2 earner owes in April finds the right person better than an interest does, so I'd start broad and test real estate investing interests as a second ad set, since they likely pull in existing landlords who aren't W2 earners.

All four hooks launch together. Adding an ad is the kind of edit that sends an ad set back into learning, so drip-feeding them resets the test. The static gets its own ad set, because Meta favors whichever ad it predicts is cheapest and a photo next to four videos may barely spend. For friction, keep the calendar button and have your GHL contractor text the booking link to qualified leads who don't book from the screen.

The closest work to your funnel was a dental implant group with cases in the upper $30Ks, Meta forms feeding a call center: 2,558 leads at $25.31 each over 90 days. Our team added qualifying questions, accepted a higher cost per lead, and cut spend when the call center needed room.

Launch is about two weeks, gated mostly on pushing a test lead end to end into GHL. A dedicated Meta specialist runs it day to day, our tracking specialist handles CAPI with your contractor, and I stay on strategy and creative. We don't bill hourly. The first month is the heavy lift, then creative and budget moves a few times a week plus a weekly written note on cost per qualified booking and what changed. Weekly calls the first month, biweekly after.

Pricing is 20% of spend up to $30K a month, lower above that, with a $1,500 monthly minimum plus a one-time $1,500 onboarding fee. Starting around $7,500 a month in spend, month one is $3,000 on our side, then $1,500 until spend passes $7,500.

How many booked calls a week can your team take right now? That caps how fast spend should climb more than Meta does.
