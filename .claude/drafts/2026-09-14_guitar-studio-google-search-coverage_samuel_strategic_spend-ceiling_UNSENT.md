Sign-ups matter more than CPC. That's where Maximize Conversions falls short: it can only chase the sign-ups Google sees, and your phone and text students are invisible to it. Any search where people tend to call looks weaker to it than it is, and on a fixed daily budget every expensive click leaves fewer searches it can afford.

First, I'd check whether the campaign is losing impression share to budget or to rank. Budget losses mean the money runs out before the searches do. Rank losses mean you're losing the auction on bid or ad quality, and more budget won't fix those. That's one screenshot, and it tells you which problem you have before you hire anyone.

For structure, the eight proven searches get their own campaign on exact match, with a budget sized so it's never the constraint. Anything else worth testing sits in a separate capped campaign with those eight as exact negatives, so it can't borrow their money.

For bidding, I'd run the proven campaign on target impression share, top of page, with a maximum CPC limit. It bids for the outcome you described and ignores conversion data, so the tracking gap stops steering your bids. I'd set a high top-of-page target rather than chase 100% absolute top, where prices usually climb fastest. The limit should come from what a student is worth, not from past CPCs. At roughly one student per 18 clicks, a $50 click still buys a student for about $900.

The tradeoff is cost. If a competitor starts bidding hard on the same searches, coverage or price has to give, and your limit decides which.

On and off becomes a switch, since nothing in that campaign depends on conversion history. Pause it when you're full, and the first week back is a search terms check and a look at whether the limit still fits current prices.

Call reporting on call assets counts calls from the ad without touching your page, though texts and calls from the page still won't count. Our team manages a local practice account that imports booked appointments back into Google, so tracking beyond the website isn't new to us.

A coverage target and CPC limit are only useful if they fit what you're comfortable spending. While you're filling spots, is that closer to what you spend now, about double, or more?
