Most seminar accounts do not have a targeting problem. They have a budget-allocation problem that every dashboard hides.

We manage a Meta account right now for a retirement planning practice running exactly your model: small dated in-person events, one campaign per event, evening start times. Over the last twelve months, 2025-09-23 through 2026-09-22, that account spent $51,293 across four objective classes. $39,737 went to lead campaigns. The other $11,557, roughly 22 percent of the budget, went to traffic, engagement and awareness campaigns that cannot register anyone for anything.

Here is the part that keeps it alive. Over that same year the traffic campaigns beat the lead campaigns on every number a client actually looks at. Click-through 1.81 percent against 1.52 percent. Cost per click $0.48 against $2.06. CPM $8.77 against $31.28. The engagement campaigns were cheaper still at a $4.76 CPM. So every month the cheap campaigns look like the winners, the expensive one looks like the problem, and budget drifts toward objectives that fill a report instead of a room. To be straight about what that comparison is: different objectives buy different auctions, and the spend behind each class is very different, so it is a rate comparison and not a matched test. That is why it persuades people, not a reason to trust it.

On your actual target, cost per qualified registrant. I would push back on making that the bid target inside Meta, and it is the one thing I would want to settle before launch. Meta needs roughly 50 conversions per week per ad set to optimize reliably. A small in-person seminar might produce 30 to 60 registrants in total. If the conversion event fires only for registrants who pass the qualifier, the auction is starved and Meta is bidding close to blind. What works is optimizing toward the registration itself, enforcing qualification through the form, the audience and the creative, and holding cost per qualified registrant as the reporting number we manage to rather than the number we hand the algorithm. Same target, applied in a different place.

One thing worth flagging on the landing page. Meta's conversion leads goal, the setting most people reach for when lead quality is the complaint, is available only with Instant Forms, not with an off-site page. If we go landing page plus qualifying form, which is usually the right call for business owners, quality has to come from form design and from the data we send back after screening. That is a real tradeoff, not a detail.

How we would run it. Each event gets a registrant dashboard you can open before the room fills, showing who registered, what they answered on the qualifier and where they came from. Written reporting every two weeks while campaigns are live, with that dashboard running continuously, so nothing waits on a scheduled call. Our team has run this vertical, including estate planning, Social Security and taxes-in-retirement event themes, and one operator carries the account start to finish rather than passing it around.

Two things I want to know. What does a typical monthly budget look like across events, roughly in the five to fifteen thousand range, above thirty, or somewhere else? And how many seats are you actually trying to fill per seminar? Seat count changes the structure more than budget does.
