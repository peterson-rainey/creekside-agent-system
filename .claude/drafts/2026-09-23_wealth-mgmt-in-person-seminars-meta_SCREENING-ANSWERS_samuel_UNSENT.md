### Q1

The closest one is live right now. We run Meta for a retirement planning practice that fills small in-person evening seminars, which is your model almost exactly. Over the last twelve months that account ran 12 separate dated events, roughly one a month, on about $51,000 in Meta spend. The themes will sound familiar: Social Security, wills and living trusts, taxes in retirement, local estate planning. Each event gets its own campaign and its own tight geo, because nobody drives 40 minutes on a Tuesday night.

What I took from it is mostly about where the money leaks. Roughly 22 percent of that year went to traffic, engagement and awareness campaigns that cannot register anyone, and they survived because they beat the lead campaigns on click-through rate, cost per click and CPM. Cheap clicks read as success in every monthly report. They do not put anybody in a chair. I would want to look at your account for the same pattern in the first week.

The other project worth naming is a law firm on Google, not Meta, and I flag the platform because it matters. That engagement was measured on cost per qualified case rather than cost per lead: 102 cases over six months at $950.83 blended. That figure sits against total ad spend across channels and the split behind it is not broken out. That account has since ended. I bring it up because it is the same measurement problem you are describing. The hard part is never generating form fills, it is agreeing what counts as qualified and then getting that definition back into the reporting without breaking how the platform bids.

Tight audiences and B2B lead gen are most of what our team does. Business owners with an exit on the horizon are a harder read than most, since there is no interest category for someone quietly thinking about selling. That gets solved with creative that names the situation and a qualifier that does the filtering, not with a list.

### Q2

None. I do not hold Meta Blueprint or any other paid-media certification, and nobody on our team does.

What I would offer instead is six years of running Meta and Google lead generation, and a live screen share of a real account where you can interrupt and ask why a given call was made rather than watch a narrated version. In this category the useful test is not a badge. It is whether someone reads the policy surface correctly. One example that catches most people: on Meta, advertiser standing and ad-level review are two separate systems, so getting an ad approved tells you nothing about the health of the account running it. That distinction decides whether a seminar campaign delivers in the week before the event, which is the only week that matters when the room has a fixed date.
