Jann, you already know the optics get rejected. What tends to get missed is that the rejections are not symmetrical, and that asymmetry decides your channel mix for you.

Google prohibits scopes and sights outright as gun components under its Dangerous Products policy. Meta treats target-focused optics as accessories and permits them, subject to an 18 and over audience floor. So the optics revenue has exactly one paid home, and it is Meta. Google carries the targets, stands, archery, clay, apparel and the catfish line, which is the bulk of the catalog and all of it clean. Most operators treat this as two separate compliance headaches to be managed around. It is really one media plan, and it falls straight out of the catalog once the SKUs are sorted by ruling rather than by product category.

The practical piece is the Shopping feed. Submitting optics in the same feed as everything else is not a line item rejection risk, it is a Merchant Center account risk, which is a different order of problem. Segmenting the feed by policy class before any budget gets set keeps the compliant majority of the catalog from being held hostage to the ambiguous slice.

On social, the 18 and over floor narrows the audience before a word of copy is written, so creative carries more of the load here than it would in an unrestricted catalog. The other constraint is that assets in this category get re-reviewed, and not always consistently, so the build has to assume any single ad can be pulled without warning. That means variants in rotation from the start rather than a hero ad everything depends on.

On the operating pattern, our team runs a survival gear ecommerce brand on both Google and Meta. On the Google side, July spend was about $8.1K and August about $9.0K. Conversions moved from 141 to 269 across those two months and cost per conversion from $57.85 to $33.35, then eased back to $43.04 through the first nine days of September. We also manage a Shopify brand doing seven figures a month on Google across 49 countries. None of that is shooting sports, and the skill that actually transfers is not having sold scopes before, it is keeping an account alive while a slice of the catalog is permanently policy flagged.
