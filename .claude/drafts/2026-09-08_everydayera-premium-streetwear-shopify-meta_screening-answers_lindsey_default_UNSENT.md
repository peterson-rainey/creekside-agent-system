## 4. Recommended starting ad budget

The honest number comes from the learning phase, not from a package tier. Meta needs roughly 50 purchases a week per ad set to optimize properly. At a realistic first-order cost for premium streetwear, somewhere in the $40 to $60 range once you are past the first few weeks, that math points to $8,000 to $10,000 a month in a single consolidated prospecting ad set.

If that is above where you want to start, the workable floor is $5,000 to $6,000 a month, but the structure changes. We would optimize on a shallower event for the first three or four weeks to build signal, then ladder up to purchase once there is enough of it. That is slower and it is the honest version.

Below about $5,000 a month I would not take it on, because you would be paying me to manage an account that cannot gather enough data to improve, and neither of us would be happy in month three.

## 5. Ad creative and video testing

I direct and manage it, and I run the testing structure. I write the briefs, decide what gets tested against what, and set the pass or fail rules.

On production, the version that works best for a brand at your stage is that you or your creators shoot to my briefs, because streetwear creative lives or dies on whether it looks native to the person posting it, and outsourced polish usually reads as an ad. Where you need production support I can arrange it, but I would not pretend that is the strong half of what you are hiring.

The testing itself is structured, not vibes. A set number of new concepts a week, each judged on hook rate and cost per result over a real spend floor, not a two-day ROAS read. Winners graduate into the scaling campaign, losers get cut on the rule rather than on a hunch. Ring-fence about 20 to 25 percent of spend for it.

## 6. Meta Pixel and Conversions API

Yes, and it is the first thing I touch, before a dollar moves.

On Shopify the cleanest path is the native Meta sales channel handling the server-side events, with the pixel still firing browser-side. The part most people skip is verifying the two are deduplicating properly on event ID, because when they are not you get double-counted purchases, a ROAS that looks great and a business that is not growing. I check Event Match Quality on your key events and fix the customer data being passed if it is weak, since that is what actually decides how much signal survives iOS.

I also confirm the Shopify to Meta event mapping is right, that your purchase event is firing on the order and not the thank-you page load, and that there are no duplicate pixels left over from a theme or an app.

## 7. How often campaigns get optimized

Monitored daily, changed weekly, and the gap between those two is deliberate.

I look at the account every business day for anything that needs an immediate call, a creative that has broken, spend running away, delivery collapsing. Structural changes go on a weekly cadence: what scales, what gets cut, what new creative enters rotation.

The reason I do not touch budgets daily is that every meaningful edit restarts the learning phase, so an account that gets adjusted every morning never leaves it. Frequent fiddling feels like management and is usually the thing keeping performance flat. You get a written weekly update either way, so you always know what changed and why.

## 8. What the first 30 days look like

Week one is tracking and a baseline, not spend. Pixel and CAPI verified, deduplication confirmed, event match quality checked, Shopify event mapping corrected, catalog and product feed set up properly. You get a written audit and the 30-day plan at the end of it.

Week two we launch, lean on purpose. One prospecting campaign, broad plus a small number of tightly scoped interest and lookalike tests, and one retargeting campaign. At your stage that is roughly 80 to 85 percent prospecting and 15 to 20 percent retargeting, because the warm pool is small early and over-funding retargeting flatters blended ROAS while starving the engine that actually builds the brand.

Weeks three and four are creative iteration and the event ladder. New concepts in on cadence, losers cut, and if we started on a shallower event we move up to purchase once the signal supports it.

What I am not promising is profitable scale inside 30 days on a cold pixel. What I am promising is that by day 30 you have clean tracking, a structure that can learn, and the first read on which creative angles your buyer responds to.

## 9. Contract and minimum commitment

Ninety days, then month to month with 30 days notice.

The 90 days is not a lock-in tactic, it is the shortest window where the work can actually be judged. Month one is tracking and learning phase, month two is the first honest read on creative, month three is where the compounding starts. Judging a cold-launched account at day 45 tells you about the learning phase, not about the account.

I do not run free trials or paid pilots for this reason. If the fit is wrong we will both know well before 90 days, and I am not going to hold you hostage with the paperwork.

## 10. Access needed to Shopify and Meta

Nothing that involves you sending me a password, ever. All of it is permission-based access you grant and can revoke.

On Meta, partner access to your ad account at admin level through Business Manager, plus access to the Facebook Page, the Instagram account, the pixel or dataset, and the product catalog. If the Business Manager does not exist yet I will walk you through creating it so the assets stay owned by you rather than by me, which matters the day we stop working together.

On Shopify, a staff account with orders, analytics and online store permissions. Orders so I can reconcile what Meta reports against what actually sold, analytics for the funnel, online store to confirm the pixel and sales channel are configured. I do not need payouts, banking or customer PII access, and I would rather not have it.
