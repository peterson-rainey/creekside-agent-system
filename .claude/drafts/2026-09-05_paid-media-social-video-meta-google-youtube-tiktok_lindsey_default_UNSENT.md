## PROPOSAL (paste-ready, 3,112 chars)

Which system owns the appointment outcome, and does anything push that outcome back into the ad platforms?

Asking because lead to appointment conversion and CAC are both on your success list, and none of the four platforms can see either one. They see a form fill and stop there. So unless appointment status is being uploaded back in, through CAPI on the Meta side and offline conversion import against GCLID on the Google side, the algorithms optimize toward whoever fills out a form fastest. That is close to the opposite of qualified. The second problem is that Meta, TikTok and YouTube all self attribute on view through, so with one video library running across all of them, the four dashboards will report more leads in total than your CRM actually received.

Worth settling before creative volume goes up, because more variations tested against an ungraded back half just produces more of whatever the pixel already likes.

The closest thing in my book to what you are describing is a dental group I ran Meta for. 2,558 leads at $25.31 each across 78 active days, on roughly $64,700, lead forms feeding a call center. The number I would actually point at is not the CPL. We pulled spend back while the CPL was still holding, because the call center could not work the volume it was getting. That is the failure mode when nobody is grading the back half.

On the consumer side I ran a camera brand, $58,298 into 623 purchases. I also run Meta for a cosmetic practice, a little over $1.19 million in spend across the last twelve months, so holding acquisition cost while scaling is familiar ground.

Now the parts I will not oversell.

On YouTube I would rather give you our actual numbers than a pitch. View objective video campaigns in our team's Google accounts have produced zero conversions. YouTube inventory Demand Gen has run on four accounts, one of them converted at roughly $204 a lead and it is paused now, and the other three returned nothing. Demand Gen overall does produce for us, but the converting share of it sits in Discover and Gmail placements and in remarketing, not in YouTube short form. If YouTube lead generation is a core requirement here rather than a nice to have, that is a real gap and you should weigh it.

TikTok I run secondary to Meta, usually somewhere in the two to eight thousand a month range, never as the primary engine. There is no headline number from it I would stand behind. Worth flagging that if you mean TikTok Shop, with affiliates and live selling, that is a different discipline and not one I have operated.
