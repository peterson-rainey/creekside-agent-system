Is a pillowcase buyer coming back for the bonnet and the sheet set, or is almost every order a brand-new customer you paid full price to reach? I ask because that one number decides where the next dollar should go. If repeat and add-on orders are thin, more ad spend just buys the same expensive first purchase over and over, and the bundle and post-purchase work is what makes scaling possible.

To answer you directly: both, and I'm stronger on the ads. I built and sold my own e-commerce business, so the store side is hands-on for me, but paid social is where most of my 10+ years went.

One example: a consumer camera brand selling to a young, very online audience. I managed $58,298 in Meta spend there over about four months for 623 purchases at $93.58 each. Retargeting came in cheaper at $70.73 against $92.85 on prospecting, but the warm pool was small, so it was never a lever for scale.

For volume, a replacement parts store I ran spent $124,990 on Meta over roughly eight months for 4,327 purchases at $28.89 each.

On the store side, the first thing I check is whether tracking actually sees the cart. On a Klaviyo build for an e-commerce brand, we found onsite tracking was recording zero Added to Cart events, so the abandoned cart flow would have fired on nobody. Upsells and bundles only earn their keep once that data is clean.
