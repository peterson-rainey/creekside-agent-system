## PROPOSAL (paste-ready) -- 2,795 chars

Before a rate sheet is worth building, the Meta leg needs a decision that carries a lead time. HIV messaging aimed at a policy audience will read as a social issue ad in the US, which means advertiser authorization, ID verification, and a "Paid for by" disclaimer on every placement. Those ads also sit in the public Ad Library for seven years, which matters when the spokesperson is a named physician. Authorization gates launch rather than delaying a review, so on a six week flight it is the likeliest thing to eat week one.

Worth separating that from a restriction people often assume applies here and does not. The 15 mile radius floor and the loss of lookalike audiences belong to the housing, employment and credit categories. Social issue campaigns keep both. So geo and interest layering survives, and the real ceiling sits somewhere else: Meta has never exposed employer or job title as a targeting input. There is no version of a Reels buy that addresses legislative staff as legislative staff. Whatever reaches them reaches a much broader population around them, and the buy has to be priced and graded on that basis.

Which is why the wrap readout metric is the thing to settle first. Share of delivery reaching government and policy segments is reportable on LinkedIn, which exposes employer and title. Meta and Google expose neither at viewer level. If that number goes in front of the end client at week six, the Meta and Google legs need an agreed proxy documented before launch, and grading it credibly tends to mean a voter file or panel overlay run against delivery after the fact rather than anything the platforms report natively. Set that at launch or it will not be gradeable.

On scope, our team is Meta and Google Demand Gen. LinkedIn is not our book and neither is TikTok, and for an audience this senior we would rather say that than promise job function targeting we do not run. Those legs belong with a public affairs specialist, as does policymaker and advocacy audience buying generally.

The regulated health side is where we are useful. Our team runs a practicing physician's Meta account at over a million in spend and thirty million impressions across the past year, where creative clears health and body image review on every refresh. Average CPM there is $38.82, which is competitive delivery rather than cheap reach. On Google, our Demand Gen book covers the Shorts, Discover and Gmail inventory this format needs, and our team has won a Google health policy disapproval appeal under the Health In Personalized Advertising flag.

Rate sheets for the two platforms we would own are straightforward once the authorization question is answered, since that changes the Meta number.
