Most AI platform accounts I look at are optimizing toward the wrong event, and the ad platforms are doing exactly what they were told to do.

The pattern is this. Signup or trial-start gets set as the conversion because it fires fast and gives the algorithm volume. But on a subscription product the money event is activation and month-two retention, which lands 14 to 30 days later and usually never gets sent back to Meta or Google. So both platforms spend the budget finding the cheapest possible signups. Volume looks healthy, the dashboard looks fine, and paid retention quietly decays. Before touching targeting or creative, the first thing worth checking is which event each platform is bidding on, and whether anything downstream of the signup ever makes it back into the account.

The second thing I would want to look at is whether Meta and Google are being held to the same number. Your post asks both to increase conversions and return on ad spend, and for a product like yours those two platforms are not doing the same job. Nobody searches for a category they do not know exists yet, so Meta is where demand gets built. Google captures people already shopping, which for AI marketing tools means a results page full of comparison posts, "best AI tool for X" listicles, and free-tier hunters. Judged on last-click cost per acquisition, Meta will always look like the loser and usually gets cut first, which then starves Google of the branded and competitor searches that were making it look good. Separate KPIs per platform, or the account eats itself.

Third, return on ad spend. On a subscription, first-order ROAS measures how patient you are more than how good the ads are. A $200 per month product at 60% six-month retention is completely different acquisition math than the same product at 25%, and the customer acquisition cost you can afford moves by two to three times. I would want your payback window before recommending a single bid change.

On fit. We run paid acquisition for our own AI product, per-seat subscription with a free trial, the same funnel shape you are describing. The trial-to-paid leak and the "leads are cheap but they do not convert" conversation are ones our team has on our own money, not only on a client's. The closest match in our client book is a seed-stage B2B SaaS we ran across both platforms, Meta carrying awareness and Google carrying capture. I will be straight with you that our deepest documented results sit in lead generation and e-commerce rather than SaaS. I would rather say that now than have you find it on a call.

What the first 30 days look like:

Week 1. Conversion and event audit across both platforms. Which event each one optimizes to, what is actually firing, what is being deduplicated, and what happens to a signup after it becomes a signup.

Week 2. Split the KPIs. Meta gets a cost per qualified trial, Google gets a cost per acquisition on capture terms, and we agree on what "qualified" actually means before either number matters.

Weeks 3 and 4. Restructure both accounts against that corrected signal, then begin creative and search term testing.

Two things I need before I can quote properly:

First, current monthly ad spend across both platforms, and whether the next 90 days is meant to hold roughly there, run two to three times that, or land somewhere between. We work on a percentage of ad spend rather than an hourly rate, so that number sets the price.

Second, which countries the ads actually run in, not where the company is based.

We work on a 90-day initial term. A subscription product with a 30-day payback window cannot be honestly judged in 30 days, and I would rather set that expectation up front than manage against it later.
